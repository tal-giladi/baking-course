# R1-13 · Quick loaf: banana bread (creaming method)

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on the established creaming method for quick loaves and loaf cakes, as described in the professional texts cited below. Banana, sugar and fat levels sit in the middle of common practice, and the leavening is split between a small amount of soda (for the mild acidity of banana, brown sugar and yogurt) and baking powder (for most of the lift). It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make one banana loaf by the creaming method. Cream butter at 18–20 °C with sugar to a measured specific gravity, add eggs without curdling, fold in the dry ingredients briefly, and bake to a measured internal temperature. Use the loaf's split, crumb and base to judge creaming and doneness.

## Expected result

- One loaf ~23 × 13 cm, 8–9 cm tall at the centre, with a single split along the top.
- Deep golden-brown to mahogany crust (brown sugar and banana brown quickly), slightly crisp on day 1, soft and tacky on day 2.
- Moist, fine, even crumb with small cells; uniformly set from top to base: **no dense, wet band** above the base.
- Clear banana and caramel flavour; no bitter or soapy aftertaste.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain (all-purpose) flour | 100.0 | 250 |
| Unsalted butter, at 18–20 °C | 40.0 | 100 |
| Light brown sugar | 60.0 | 150 |
| Whole eggs, out of shell (about 2) | 40.0 | 100 |
| Very ripe banana, peeled and mashed (3–4 bananas) | 140.0 | 350 |
| Plain yogurt (or cultured buttermilk) | 20.0 | 50 |
| Vanilla extract | 1.0 | 2.5 |
| Baking powder | 2.0 | 5 |
| Baking soda | 0.8 | 2 |
| Fine salt | 1.2 | 3 |
| **Total** | **405.0** | **1012.5** |

**Yield:** ~1010 g batter → one loaf in a 23 × 13 × 7 cm loaf pan (nominal 900 g / 2 lb tin), filling it a little over half. Baked weight ~880–920 g (bake loss ~10–13 %).
**Banana:** weigh after peeling. "Very ripe" means the peel is heavily speckled or mostly brown and the flesh is soft and sweet: much of the starch has converted to sugar. Under-ripe banana gives less flavour and a drier loaf.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 fits a 17 × 9 cm mini-loaf pan (bake ~40–45 min). ×2 makes two loaves; cream in a stand mixer, and bake both on the same rack with at least 5 cm between pans.
**Optional:** fold in 75 g toasted, chopped walnuts (30 %) with the last addition of flour.

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g) and a 0.1 g scale for soda and baking powder.
- Stand mixer with paddle, or hand mixer and a large bowl.
- Fine sieve; fork or potato masher; flexible spatula.
- 23 × 13 × 7 cm metal loaf pan; parchment; soft butter for greasing.
- Specific-gravity cup (100–250 mL, known water weight).
- Probe thermometer; oven thermometer; skewer; cooling rack; foil.

## Mise en place

1. Take butter out 1.5–3 h ahead, or slice it and leave 30–60 min. **Probe it: 18–20 °C.**
2. Eggs to 18–22 °C (5 min in warm tap water); beat them in a jug.
3. Oven rack in the lower-middle position. Preheat to **175 °C (350 °F)** for ≥20 min.
4. Grease the pan, then line the base and long sides with one strip of parchment overhanging by 3 cm (lifting handles).
5. Mash the banana with a fork to a coarse purée with a few pea-size lumps. Stir in the yogurt and vanilla.
6. Sift the baking powder and soda with about 50 g of the flour, then whisk with the rest of the flour and the salt for 30 s.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Cream (t = 0).** Paddle butter and brown sugar on **medium speed for 4 min** (3–5 min), scraping down at 2 min. Endpoint: lighter in colour (tan to pale caramel), visibly more volume, fluffy and holding soft ridges. Brown sugar is moist and creams to a slightly denser mix than white sugar.

**Step 2 — Measure SG of the creamed mix.** Expect roughly **0.85–1.0**. Record it and the mixture's temperature (**19–22 °C**). If SG is above ~1.05 and the mixture is lumpy, the butter was too cold: let the bowl stand 10 min and cream 1 min more. If it is shiny and slack, the butter was too warm: chill the bowl 10 min, then continue.

**Step 3 — Eggs in 3 additions.** On medium, add about a third of the beaten egg; beat **20–30 s** until smooth and uniform again; repeat. The mixture should look glossy and cohesive, like thick mayonnaise. If it turns grainy and wet (curdled), beat in 1 spoonful of the measured flour and continue; note it in your log.

**Step 4 — Banana mixture.** On low, add the banana–yogurt–vanilla mixture and mix **15–20 s** until just combined. The batter may look slightly broken at this stage because of the banana's water: normal, and it comes together with the flour.

**Step 5 — Dry ingredients in 2 additions.** On **low, about 10 s each**, or fold by hand in 15–20 strokes each, just until no dry flour is visible. Finish with 2–3 folds of the spatula from the bottom of the bowl. Stop there. Add walnuts with the last few folds if using.

**Step 6 — Measure SG of the batter.** Expect roughly **0.95–1.1**. Record it.

**Step 7 — Pan.** Scrape into the pan (~1010 g), level the top. For a clean central split, pipe or spread a thin line of soft butter (~3 g) down the centre of the batter.

**Step 8 — Bake at 175 °C (350 °F) for 55–65 min.** Rotate the pan 180° at 35 min if your oven has a hot side. If the top is dark at 40–45 min, lay a sheet of foil loosely over it.

**Step 9 — Doneness.** From 50 min: probe the centre of the loaf through the top, into the middle of the depth. **Done at 94–98 °C**; a skewer comes out with no wet batter (moist crumbs are fine); the split looks dry, not glossy; the top springs back. Do not pull on time alone: banana ripeness and pan material shift the bake by ±10 min.

**Step 10 — Cool.** 10 min in the pan on a rack, then lift out by the parchment and cool completely on the rack, **at least 2 h**. Cutting a hot quick loaf compresses the still-soft crumb into a gummy band. Flavour and texture are best the next day, wrapped at room temperature. Keeps 3–4 days wrapped; freeze slices for longer.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature at creaming | 18–20 °C (probe) | Plastic window: holds air. Colder: lumps; warmer: greasy, dense loaf |
| Creaming | 3–5 min medium; SG ~0.85–1.0 | Creaming makes the cells that gas later expands |
| Egg temperature and addition rate | 18–22 °C, 3 additions, smooth after each | Prevents curdling, which loses air and gives a coarser crumb |
| Leaveners | Soda 2.0 g and powder 5.0 g, weighed to 0.1 g and sifted | Excess soda: soapy, dark, coarse, sinking; lumps: specks |
| Mixing after flour | ≤ 20 s on low | Gluten development: tunnels, toughness, peaked top |
| Internal temperature | 94–98 °C | Below ~93 °C the centre is still wet: gummy streak, sinking on cooling |
| Cooling before slicing | ≥ 2 h | Hot crumb smears into a gummy band |

## Scientific explanation

Creaming butter at 18–20 °C with sugar traps air in the fat-crystal network; these cells are the nuclei of the crumb ([08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)). The eggs are emulsified into the fat, so the water phase is dispersed as fine droplets and the air cells survive. Banana, brown sugar (molasses) and yogurt are mildly acidic; the small dose of soda (0.8 %) reacts with that acid on mixing, and baking powder supplies most of the gas, partly on mixing and mostly in the oven ([08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)). Because the batter is deep and heavy (banana is 140 % of flour), heat penetrates slowly: the crust sets long before the centre finishes rising, which is why the top splits. The high sugar content (brown sugar plus the banana's own sugars) delays starch gelatinization and protein setting and holds water, so the centre stays soft and moist — and also why the loaf needs to reach 94–98 °C before the crumb is fully set. Soda raises the pH slightly, which speeds Maillard browning and gives the dark crust and brown crumb typical of banana bread ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)); too much soda turns that into bitterness and a coarse, collapsing crumb.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Banana | 140 → 180 % | More flavour, moister, denser; higher risk of gummy streak and sinking. Add ~5 min bake, keep internal ≥ 95 °C |
| Sugar | Brown → white granulated | Lighter colour, creams to a lower SG, less caramel flavour; slightly less acid for the soda |
| Fat | Creamed butter → 80 g oil (muffin method) | Moister, more tender, coarser and less even crumb; stays soft longer |
| Leavening | Soda 0.8 → 1.6 % | Darker, coarser, more domed then sinking; soapy note |
| Creaming time | 1 min → 6 min | Higher SG → lower SG; denser → lighter, finer crumb (up to a limit) |
| Oven | 175 °C → 160 °C | Flatter top, less split, paler crust, more even colour; bake ~10–15 min longer |
| Add-ins | + 30 % walnuts or chocolate | Heavier; may need 5 min longer |
