# R1-12 · Muffins (muffin method)

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on the established muffin method as described in the professional texts cited below. Proportions sit in the middle of standard professional ranges so that the method, not an unusual formula, decides the result. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe. The base is a lemon and blueberry muffin; the blueberries are optional.

## Objective

Make 12 domed muffins by the muffin method: dry and wet ingredients prepared separately and combined in 10–15 folds to a lumpy batter. Control the dome with fill level and a high-start oven profile. Record stroke count, batter specific gravity and internal temperature, and use the cut face to judge mixing.

## Expected result

- 12 muffins, ~7 cm across the base, with a rounded dome standing 1.5–2.5 cm above the rim of the cup and a slight overhang.
- Golden-brown, lightly crisp top with a fine crackle; paler sides.
- Moist, tender, slightly irregular crumb with small round cells; **no long vertical tunnels**; berries distributed through the height, not all on the bottom.
- Clean lemon and butter flavour; no bitter or soapy aftertaste.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain (all-purpose) flour, ~10–11.5 % protein | 100.0 | 300 |
| Caster or fine granulated sugar | 45.0 | 135 |
| Baking powder (double-acting or SAPP-based) | 4.0 | 12 |
| Fine salt | 1.0 | 3 |
| Finely grated lemon zest (about 1 lemon) | 1.0 | 3 |
| Whole eggs, out of shell (about 2) | 33.3 | 100 |
| Whole milk | 70.0 | 210 |
| Unsalted butter, melted | 30.0 | 90 |
| Blueberries, fresh or frozen (optional) | 50.0 | 150 |
| **Total** | **334.3** | **1003** |

**Yield:** ~1003 g batter with berries → **12 muffins at ~83 g** (allow ~5 g left in the bowl). Without berries, ~853 g → 10 muffins at ~85 g, or 12 slightly flatter muffins at ~70 g.
**True hydration:** water from milk (~87 % → 183 g), egg (~75 % → 75 g) and butter (~16 % → 14 g) ≈ 272 g, or **~91 %** of flour: a batter, far wetter than any bread dough.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (150 g flour) gives 6 muffins; weigh the baking powder on a 0.1 g scale (6.0 g). ×2 (600 g flour) for 24 muffins; mix in a wide bowl so you can still fold in 15–20 strokes.

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g) and a 0.1 g scale for the leavener.
- Fine sieve; two mixing bowls; balloon whisk; wide flexible spatula.
- Microplane or fine grater for zest.
- 12-cup muffin tin (cups ~7 cm across the top, ~100–120 mL), paper liners or butter and flour for greasing.
- Ice-cream scoop (~70–80 mL) or spoon; a small cup of known volume for specific gravity.
- Probe thermometer; oven thermometer; timer; cooling rack.

## Mise en place

1. Oven rack in the middle. Preheat to **220 °C (425 °F)** for at least 20 min, confirmed with an oven thermometer.
2. Line or grease the tin.
3. Melt the butter and let it cool to **35–45 °C** (warm, not hot: hot butter can begin to cook the egg).
4. Bring eggs and milk to **18–22 °C** (eggs in warm tap water for 5 min; milk 15 s in a microwave). Cold milk hardens melted butter into flecks.
5. If using frozen blueberries, keep them frozen until the moment you fold them in; fresh berries rinsed and dried. Toss berries with 5 g of the measured flour.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Dry mix.** In the large bowl, sift the baking powder with about 50 g of the flour, then add the rest of the flour, the sugar, salt and zest. Whisk for 30 s. Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingertips first if you want more lemon aroma (the oils transfer to the sugar).

**Step 2 — Wet mix.** In the second bowl, whisk the eggs until the white and yolk are uniform (~20 s), whisk in the milk, then the melted butter in a steady stream while whisking. Check temperature: **20–26 °C**.

**Step 3 — Combine: 10–15 folds.** Make a well in the dry mix, pour in the wet mix all at once. Fold with the spatula: scrape across the bottom, lift and fold over, turn the bowl a quarter. Count. Stop when **no pockets of dry flour** remain, at 10–15 folds. The batter is thick, matt and visibly lumpy.

**Step 4 — Berries.** Add the floured berries after about 8 folds, so they are incorporated within the 10–15 total, not with extra folds after.

**Step 5 — Specific gravity.** Fill your SG cup level with batter, weigh, and divide by the water weight of the same cup ([08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)). Expect roughly 1.0–1.1. Return the batter to the bowl without stirring.

**Step 6 — Portion.** Scoop **~83 g** into each cup (weigh the first two to calibrate your scoop). Cups should be full to the rim. Optional: sprinkle 1–2 g coarse sugar on each.
*Optional rest:* cover the filled tin and refrigerate 30 min to 24 h (double-acting or SAPP powder only). Bake straight from the fridge, adding 2–4 min.

**Step 7 — Bake with a high start.** Load at **220 °C (425 °F)** and bake **5 min**. Without opening the door, turn down to **190 °C (375 °F)** and bake **13–16 min more** (total 18–21 min). Alternative: a constant **200 °C (390 °F)** for 20–23 min; the dome will be slightly lower. Rotate the tin 180° at 12 min if your oven map shows a hot side.

**Step 8 — Doneness.** Tops golden-brown and springing back when pressed lightly in the centre; a skewer in the centre comes out with no wet batter (berry juice is fine); **internal temperature 93–97 °C**.

**Step 9 — Cool.** Leave in the tin **5 min** (the structure firms), then lift out onto a rack. Left longer, steam condenses in the cups and the bases go soggy. Cool ≥15 min before cutting one in half vertically for evaluation.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Baking powder | 12.0 g ± 0.2 g, sifted | Too little: flat and dense. Too much: coarse, sunken, bitter. Clumps: holes and specks |
| Wet ingredients temperature | 20–26 °C combined | Cold liquid sets the butter into flecks; hot liquid starts cooking the egg |
| Mixing | 10–15 folds, stop while lumpy | Each extra fold develops gluten: tunnels, peaks, toughness |
| Fill | ~83 g, to the rim | Underfilled cups cannot dome above the rim |
| Oven profile | 220 °C × 5 min → 190 °C, verified with oven thermometer | High start sets edges while the centre rises: dome |
| Internal temperature | 93–97 °C | Below: gummy centre, sinking. Well above: dry |

## Common failures

- Long vertical holes, chewy crumb → [tunnels in muffins](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=tunnels-in-muffins)
- Pointed or cracked peak → [peaked or cracked muffin tops](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=peaked-or-cracked-muffin-tops)
- No dome, flat tops → [flat muffins with no dome](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=flat-muffins-with-no-dome)
- Green-grey streaks around berries, bitter taste → [discoloured crumb or coloured specks](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=discoloured-crumb-or-coloured-specks)
- Dark or burnt bases → [burnt bottoms](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=burnt-bottoms)
- All the berries at the bottom → batter too thin or overmixed; toss berries in flour, use frozen berries straight from the freezer

## Scientific explanation

Liquid fat coats the flour proteins on contact and sugar competes for the water, both slowing gluten formation, but the batter is so wet (~91 % true hydration) that gluten still forms quickly under agitation ([08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)). Stopping at a lumpy batter keeps the gluten network weak and discontinuous, so the baked crumb is tender and the gas cells expand where they are rather than merging into tunnels. The fast acid in the baking powder reacts during mixing and seeds the batter with CO₂; the slow acid reacts once the batter warms in the oven ([08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)). The 220 °C start heats the tin and the outer batter quickly, so the edges and surface set early while the centre is still fluid and expanding with CO₂, thermal expansion and steam; the centre is pushed above the set rim into a dome. Lowering to 190 °C lets the centre reach 93–97 °C — starch gelatinized, egg and flour proteins set — before the sugar-rich top overbrowns ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)). Blueberry anthocyanins stay purple-red in this near-neutral batter; excess soda would turn them green-grey ([SC-05](stage-1/science/sc-05-acids-bases-ph.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fat | Melted butter → 80 g neutral oil + 10 g extra milk | More tender, moister when cold, stays soft longer; less butter flavour and colour |
| Sugar | 45 → 60 % | Sweeter, more tender, darker top, flatter dome, keeps moist longer |
| Liquid | Milk → buttermilk (210 g) with 1.5 g soda and baking powder reduced to 7.5 g | Tangier, more tender, finer crumb; same lift ([08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)) |
| Mixing | 12 → 60 folds | Peaked, tunnelled, chewy ([X1-22](experiments/X1-22-muffin-overmixing.md)) |
| Batter rest | 0 → 12–24 h at 4 °C | Taller dome, thicker batter, slightly more even crumb |
| Oven | High-start → constant 180 °C | Flatter tops, paler, more even crumb, longer bake |
| Flour | 30 % of the flour → wholemeal | Denser, nuttier, needs ~+10 g milk; rest the batter 20 min |
| Add-ins | Blueberries → 100 g chocolate chunks | Less water from fruit: slightly drier, taller muffin |
