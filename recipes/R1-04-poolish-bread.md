# R1-04 · Poolish bread and baguettes

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established poolish technique, as described in the professional bread texts cited below. The overall formula is kept deliberately close to R1-01 (70 % hydration, 2 % salt) so that the preferment is the main difference you taste and feel. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a lean bread in which 40 % of the flour is fermented overnight as a poolish. Judge poolish ripeness, mix and ferment a final dough at a DDT of 24 °C, shape bâtards and short (~35 cm) baguettes, and bake them on a stone or steel with steam. Compare the result directly with your R1-01 reference loaf.

## Expected result

- Two bâtards (~440–455 g baked each) or four short baguettes (~35 cm, ~210–220 g baked each), or a mix.
- Thin, crackly crust, deep golden with reddish-brown tones, and "ears" along the scores.
- Creamy-coloured crumb that is more open and irregular than R1-01, with a glossy cell wall.
- Sweet, wheaty, slightly nutty aroma; fuller flavour than R1-01, with no sourness and no alcohol.
- The dough is noticeably more extensible than R1-01: it stretches to baguette length without tearing.

## Formula

Three-column presentation (see [05.1](stage-1/module-05/lesson-01.md)). All total-formula percentages are on total flour. Poolish percentages are on poolish flour. PFF = 40 %.

| Ingredient | Total formula % | Total formula g | Poolish % | Poolish g | Final dough g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (11.5–13 % protein) | 100.0 | 600.0 | 100.0 | 240.0 | 360.0 |
| Water | 70.0 | 420.0 | 100.0 | 240.0 | 180.0 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 12.0 | — | — | 12.0 |
| Instant yeast | 0.44 | 2.64 | 0.1 | 0.24 | 2.4 |
| Poolish | — | — | — | — | 480.24 |
| **Total** | **172.44** | **1034.64** | **200.1** | **480.24** | **1034.64** |

**Yield:** ~1035 g dough → 2 bâtards at 515 g, or 4 baguettes at 255 g (plus ~15 g lost to the bowl and bench), or 1 bâtard + 2 baguettes. Baked and cooled weight is about 12–15 % less for bâtards and 15–18 % less for baguettes (more crust per gram).
**Hydration:** 70 % overall. The final dough on its own is 50 % (180 / 360); it looks dry until the poolish is worked in.
**Poolish yeast (0.24 g):** dissolve 1.0 g instant yeast in 99 g water at ~25 °C to make a 1 % solution. Stir well and use **24 g** of it as part of the poolish water (so: 216 g plain water + 24 g solution). Discard the rest.
**Yeast alternative:** fresh yeast at about 3× the instant weight (poolish 0.7 g, final dough 7 g). Conversions depend on the brand ([03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md)).
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (300 g flour) gives one bâtard or two baguettes and works well. ×2 (1200 g flour) for 8 baguettes; bake in batches and retard the waiting shaped pieces at 3–5 °C for up to 1 h.

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g) and a 0.1 g scale for the final-dough yeast, or use a 10 % solution.
- Probe thermometer; min/max thermometer for the overnight poolish if you have one.
- 1–1.5 L straight-sided container with a lid for the poolish; mark the level.
- 2–3 L straight-sided container for bulk.
- Bowl scraper, bench scraper.
- Linen couche (or a heavy, well-floured cotton tea towel) and a flipping board (a 10 × 40 cm strip of thick card or thin plywood).
- Baking stone or steel on the middle-low rack. Alternatives: an inverted heavy baking tray preheated as a "stone", or a perforated baguette tray.
- Steam setup: a heavy tray or cast-iron pan on the lowest rack (a tray of lava rocks or clean steel chain works well), plus ~150 g boiling water. See [04.1](stage-1/module-04/lesson-01.md).
- Lame or razor blade, parchment, peel or a rimless tray, oven gloves, cooling rack.
- Optional: oval covered pot for a bâtard.

## Mise en place

**Evening before (12–16 h before mixing):**

1. Make the 1 % yeast solution.
2. In the poolish container, mix 240 g flour, 216 g water and 24 g yeast solution until no dry flour remains (about 1 min with a spoon or spatula). Use water at a temperature that gives a mixed poolish at **20–22 °C**. With flour at 21 °C, water at ~21–23 °C does it; there is almost no friction with a spoon.
3. Mark the level, cover, and leave at **20–22 °C** for 12–16 h. If the room will be warmer (24–25 °C), halve the solution to 12 g (0.06 %, 228 g plain water) or plan to mix earlier.

**Morning:**

4. Check the poolish is ripe (Procedure step 1).
5. Measure the flour, room and poolish temperatures. Calculate the final-dough water temperature for a **DDT of 24 °C**. With a preferment, use the four-factor version: water = 4 × DDT − flour − room − poolish − friction. Example: flour 21, room 22, poolish 21, friction 2 → water = 96 − 21 − 22 − 21 − 2 = **30 °C**.
6. Weigh final-dough flour and yeast into the mixing bowl and whisk. Weigh salt separately. Weigh the final water and hold back 15 g.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Judge the poolish (t = −5 min).** Ripe poolish:
- has risen to **2–3× its starting volume** and is **domed, or just starting to recede** in the centre; a faint ring on the container wall above the surface shows it has peaked slightly;
- is covered in bubbles of many sizes, some breaking at the surface, with a wrinkled, "pock-marked" look;
- smells sweet, wheaty and mildly alcoholic, like beer, with no sharp vinegar note;
- pours in thick, stringy ribbons when you tilt the container.

If it is still rising and domed with few surface bubbles, give it 1–2 h more at 24 °C. If it has collapsed well below its high-water mark, smells sharply of alcohol or acid, and is thin and soupy, it is over-ripe: see [poolish or biga over-ripe](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=poolish-or-biga-over-ripe) and continue with a shorter bulk.

**Step 2 — Disperse the poolish (t = 0).** Pour 165 g of the final water over the poolish and loosen it with a spatula so it slides out. Tip it into the bowl with the flour and yeast.

**Step 3 — Mix to shaggy.** Mix by hand or on low speed for 1–2 min until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest **20 min**.

**Step 4 — Add salt and develop.** Sprinkle on the salt and the reserved 15 g water. Pinch through for 2 min, then slap-and-fold or squeeze-and-fold for **3–5 min** (stand mixer: speed 1 for 2 min, speed 2 for 2–3 min). Stop at *moderate* development: smooth, cohesive, and a membrane that stretches thin before tearing. Poolish dough reaches this sooner than R1-01; do not overmix, or you lose the extensibility you paid for.

**Step 5 — Check temperature.** Target **24 °C** (23–25 °C acceptable). Record it.

**Step 6 — Bulk fermentation, about 1.5–2 h at 24 °C.** Move to the oiled, marked container. Fold at **30 and 60 min** (one set each, 4 folds, as in R1-01). The dough should become smooth, airy and slightly domed.

**Step 7 — End of bulk.** When the dough is **+50–75 %** in volume, domed, bubbly at the sides, and jiggles as one mass. Poolish doughs usually reach this 15–30 min faster than you expect from the total yeast; watch from 75 min onward.

**Step 8 — Divide.** Tip out onto a lightly floured bench. Divide with the scraper into 2 × 515 g (bâtards) or 4 × 255 g (baguettes). Minimize cuts; add a small piece to adjust weight rather than cutting many times.

**Step 9 — Pre-shape.** Bâtards: a loose round. Baguettes: a loose cylinder about 12–15 cm long: flatten gently into a rectangle, fold the top third down and the bottom third up, and roll seam-down. Keep gas in; the aim is uniform pieces with light tension.

**Step 10 — Bench rest, 20–30 min**, covered. Baguette pre-shapes must relax enough to extend without tearing: press one lightly, and if it resists and springs straight back, wait 5–10 min more.

**Step 11 — Shape** ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)).
- *Bâtard:* as in R1-01, a tight oval ~25 cm long.
- *Baguette (35 cm):* flatten gently seam-up into a rectangle. Fold the top third down and seal with the heel of your hand. Rotate 180° and repeat. Fold in half lengthways and seal the seam firmly with the heel of your hand along the whole length. Roll to length from the centre outwards with both hands, applying light pressure and moving the hands apart, in 2–3 passes, to **~35 cm**. Taper the ends slightly. Check that the loaf fits your oven's stone diagonally or straight across; most home ovens take 35–38 cm.

**Step 12 — Final proof, about 40–60 min at 24 °C.** Place seam-up in the floured couche, pleating the cloth between pieces so they hold their sides. Cover. Start preheating now (step 13). Proof is done when the poke test gives a slow, partial spring-back and the pieces have grown ~50–75 % ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)). Baguettes proof slightly faster than bâtards. *Alternative:* retard the shaped pieces at 3–5 °C for up to 12 h, covered, and bake from the fridge.

**Step 13 — Preheat.** Stone or steel on the middle-low rack, steam tray on the lowest rack, **250 °C (480 °F)** for at least 45–60 min.

**Step 14 — Transfer and score.** Use the flipping board to roll each piece seam-down onto parchment on a peel. Score:
- *Baguette:* 3 cuts, each ~10–12 cm long, nearly parallel to the length of the loaf, overlapping by about one third, blade at ~30° to the surface, ~5–6 mm deep.
- *Bâtard:* one long cut at 30–45°, 6–8 mm deep, or 2–3 overlapping diagonal cuts.

**Step 15 — Load and steam.** Slide the loaves on their parchment onto the stone. Pour ~150 g boiling water into the steam tray and close the door at once.

**Step 16 — Bake.**
- *Baguettes:* 12 min with steam at 250 °C (480 °F). Remove the steam tray and the parchment, drop to **240 °C (465 °F)**, and bake another **10–14 min**. Total 22–26 min.
- *Bâtards:* 15 min with steam at 250 °C. Vent, drop to **230 °C (445 °F)**, and bake another **18–25 min**. Total 33–40 min.
- *Pot option for a bâtard:* as R1-01, 20 min covered at 250 °C, then 15–20 min uncovered at 230 °C.
- **Done when:** internal temperature **≥96 °C**, crust deep golden-brown with darker, crisp ears, and a hollow sound when tapped underneath. Baguettes should crackle audibly as they cool.

**Step 17 — Cool.** On a rack: baguettes ≥30 min, bâtards ≥1 h. Baguettes are best on the day. Freeze leftovers wrapped within a few hours.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** The stone, steam tray and oven walls are at 250 °C. Pour the steam water from a kettle or long-handled jug, keep your face back from the door, and wear long sleeves. Do not pour water onto a hot glass oven door or oven light; cold water can crack them. Never place a glass dish in the oven as a steam tray.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Poolish yeast | 0.24 g via 24 g of a fresh 1 % solution | A 0.1 g error is ±40 %: it moves ripeness by hours |
| Poolish temperature overnight | 20–22 °C | Warmer means over-ripe by morning; colder means under-ripe |
| Poolish ripeness at use | Domed or just starting to recede, bubbly, sweet-alcoholic smell | Under: little flavour. Over: slack dough, paler crust, faster bulk |
| Final DDT | 24 °C ± 1 | Sets the bulk time |
| Mixing | Moderate development, 3–5 min by hand | Overmixing costs extensibility and the open crumb |
| Bench rest before baguette shaping | Dough relaxes before rolling | A tight pre-shape tears when extended |
| Oven and steam | 250 °C, stone preheated ≥45 min, steam in the first 12–15 min | Steam delays crust set: better spring, open scores and shine |
| Internal temperature | ≥96 °C | Set crumb; baguettes that are pulled pale go soft within an hour |

## Common failures

- Poolish collapsed and soupy at use → [poolish or biga over-ripe](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=poolish-or-biga-over-ripe)
- Poolish flat and floury → [poolish or biga under-ripe](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=poolish-or-biga-under-ripe)
- Slack dough that won't hold a baguette shape → [weak, slack dough](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=weak-slack-dough)
- Baguette tears while rolling → shorten mixing, lengthen bench rest; see [03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)
- Scores don't open, no ears → [poor oven spring](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=poor-oven-spring) and [X1-10 Steam](experiments/X1-10-steam.md)
- Pale crust → [pale crust](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md?id=pale-crust)
- Crust goes soft within an hour → [crust goes soft after cooling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md?id=crust-goes-soft-after-cooling)

## Scientific explanation

During the 12–16 h poolish, a small yeast population grows while flour amylases turn damaged starch into maltose and dextrins ([05.1](stage-1/module-05/lesson-01.md)). Fermentation products (organic acids, alcohols, esters) accumulate, and the poolish flour's gluten is fully hydrated and relaxed by time and protease activity, without being mechanically developed. At ripeness the yeast population is large and active, and there is still some sugar. Mixed into the final dough, it brings a fast start to fermentation, a pool of flavour compounds, and a large proportion of extensible gluten. The final dough needs little mixing. It ferments in about 1.5–2 h despite a total yeast of only 0.44 %, and it stretches easily into long shapes. Residual sugars and a slightly lower pH contribute to a well-coloured, often reddish crust through Maillard reactions ([04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md)). The crumb is more open than R1-01 because an extensible dough lets cells expand further before the structure sets. Steam in the first minutes keeps the surface soft so the scores can open, then condenses on the crust and gelatinizes surface starch, which gives the shine and the thin, crackling crust.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| PFF | 40 → 20 % | Less flavour and extensibility; dough closer to R1-01; raise final yeast to ~3.5 g |
| PFF | 40 → 50 % | More flavour and extensibility; slacker dough; bulk faster; lower final yeast to ~1.8 g |
| Poolish time | 16 h → 8 h with 0.3 % yeast | Less flavour, similar extensibility; same-day schedule possible |
| Poolish ripeness | Just peaked → well past peak | Slacker dough, faster bulk, paler crust, sharper flavour |
| Hydration | 70 → 74 % | More open crumb, harder shaping; add water as a late bassinage |
| Final proof | Same day → 12 h retard at 3–5 °C | Blistered crust, more flavour, easier scoring, flexible schedule |
| Flour | Add 10 % wholemeal wheat | More flavour and colour; ferments faster; reduce yeast slightly and add ~2 % water |
