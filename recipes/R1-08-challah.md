# R1-08 · Challah

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established technique: an egg- and oil-enriched, honey-sweetened braided bread in the Ashkenazi Jewish tradition, made dairy-free (pareve) as the tradition requires. The percentages sit inside the normal range for challah and are set to give a stiff dough that braids cleanly. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe. Braiding notation follows the positional convention explained in [06.3](stage-1/module-06/lesson-03.md).

## Objective

Make one large six-strand challah (or two three-strand loaves) with clean, well-defined strands, a deep mahogany, glossy crust with no split joins, and a soft, yellow, close, shreddable crumb. Control dough stiffness, strand length and proof so the braid holds, and control crust colour with oven temperature and a double egg wash.

## Expected result

- One loaf, about 820–850 g after cooling, about 30–34 cm long, 12–14 cm wide and 9–11 cm tall. Or two loaves of about 410–425 g each.
- Glossy, even, deep golden-brown to mahogany crust. Strands clearly separated, with no raw-looking pale dough showing at the joins.
- Close, even, yellow crumb that tears into soft strands along the braid. Tender, not cakey, not dry.
- Mildly sweet, eggy flavour with a little honey. No yeasty or boozy note.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (11.5–13 % protein) | 100.0 | 500 |
| Water | 32.0 | 160 |
| Whole eggs (about 2 large), beaten | 20.0 | 100 |
| Egg yolks (about 2) | 8.0 | 40 |
| Neutral oil (sunflower, rapeseed/canola) | 10.0 | 50 |
| Sugar | 5.0 | 25 |
| Honey | 5.0 | 25 |
| Fine salt | 1.8 | 9 |
| Instant yeast (osmotolerant preferred) | 1.4 | 7 |
| **Total** | **183.2** | **916** |
| *Egg wash (not in total)* | | *1 whole egg + 1 yolk + pinch of salt, beaten and strained* |
| *Topping (optional)* | | *Sesame or poppy seeds, about 10 g* |

**Yield:** 916 g of dough → one six-strand loaf (6 × 150 g strands) or two three-strand loaves (2 × 3 × 150 g). About 820–850 g total after baking and cooling.
**True hydration:** water 160 + whole egg 100 × 0.75 + yolk 40 × 0.50 + honey 25 × 0.17 = 259 g → **52 %**. This is deliberately stiff: challah needs a dough that holds a rolled strand without flattening. Eggs, yolk fat and oil make it feel much softer than a lean dough at 52 %.
**Total sweetener:** 5 % sugar plus 5 % honey (about 82 % sugars) ≈ 9 % sugars; in the water phase ≈ 45 ÷ (45 + 259) ≈ 15 %. Standard instant yeast works; osmotolerant gives a more reliable timeline.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (458 g) makes one three-strand loaf. ×2 makes two six-strand loaves; divide into 12 × 150 g strands.
**Allergens:** egg, wheat (gluten), sesame if used.

## Equipment

- Digital scale (1 g), probe thermometer.
- Stand mixer with dough hook (recommended for a stiff dough), or a clear bench for hand kneading.
- Straight-sided 2–3 L container, marked.
- Bench scraper, ruler or tape measure, pastry brush (soft, silicone or natural bristle).
- Two stacked baking sheets (the second one insulates the base), parchment.
- Oven thermometer. Foil for tenting.

## Mise en place

1. Target a final dough temperature of **25 °C** (acceptable 24–26 °C). A stiff dough in a stand mixer heats quickly: in a 20–22 °C kitchen, water at 15–20 °C is a typical starting point for a machine mix, and 28–30 °C for a hand mix. Use your friction factor ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)).
2. Weigh the flour, sugar, salt and yeast into the mixer bowl and stir. Separately, whisk the eggs, yolks, oil, honey and water together until the honey dissolves.
3. Make the egg wash, strain it through a fine sieve (strained wash flows evenly and doesn't streak), cover and refrigerate it. You will use it twice.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Mix (t = 0).** Add the liquids to the dry ingredients.
- *Mixer:* speed 1 for 3 min until no dry flour remains. Then speed 2 for **6–8 min**.
- *Hand:* combine in a bowl, then knead on an unfloured bench, pushing away with the heel of your hand and folding back, for **10–12 min**. A stiff dough is easier to knead by hand than a soft one; don't add flour.

**Endpoint:** the dough is smooth, satiny and firm, not sticky, and slightly tacky at most. A stretched piece forms a membrane thin enough to see light through before it tears (good development). If it tears at a thick stage, mix 2 min more and test again.

**Step 2 — Check the temperature.** Target 25 °C, acceptable 24–26 °C. Record it.

**Step 3 — Bulk fermentation (about 1.5–2 h at 25 °C).** Place in the lightly oiled marked container, cover, and keep at 24–26 °C. An optional fold at 45 min evens out the temperature and strengthens the dough. **Done when** the volume is **+75–100 %**, the top is domed, and the dough feels light and airy when pressed.
*Alternative:* after 45 min, refrigerate for 8–16 h. Divide and pre-shape cold; strands roll more easily after 30 min at room temperature.

**Step 4 — Divide and pre-shape.** Degas gently. Divide into **6 × 150 g** (±2 g); for two small loaves, 6 × 150 g as two sets of 3. Roll each loosely into a 12–15 cm log. Cover and rest **15–20 min**. The logs must relax, or the strands will shrink back while you roll them.

**Step 5 — Roll strands.** On an **unfloured** bench (a little friction helps), roll each log from the centre outward with flat palms to:
- six-strand: **40–45 cm** long;
- three-strand: **30–35 cm** long.

Keep each strand slightly thicker in the middle and tapered towards the ends; this gives the loaf a raised centre and tapered ends. Roll all strands to the same length, and measure them. If a strand keeps springing back, move on to the next and come back to it after 5 min. Pop any large surface bubbles. Strands that tear or look lumpy were not rested or were rolled with flour on the bench.

**Step 6 — Braid.** Line the strands up side by side, pinch them firmly together at the top, and fan them out. Tuck the joined end under slightly. Positions are numbered **1 to n from left to right**, and are re-numbered after every move. "*a* over *b*" means: pick up the strand now at position *a*, lay it over the others, and place it so that it becomes position *b*.

- **Three-strand:** 1 over 2, 3 over 2; repeat to the end. For an even loaf, start from the middle, braid to one end, turn the loaf around and braid the other half.
- **Four-strand:** 4 over 2, 1 over 3, 2 over 3; repeat.
- **Six-strand:** start once with **6 over 1**. Then repeat **2 over 6, 1 over 3, 5 over 1, 6 over 4** until the strands run out.

Braid snugly but without stretching the strands. A braid that is too tight tears at the joins in the oven, and one that is too loose spreads and blurs. Pinch the ends together and tuck both ends under. Transfer the loaf to the parchment-lined, doubled baking sheet. Adjust the shape with your hands so it is symmetrical.

**Step 7 — First egg wash.** Brush the whole loaf thinly, including down into the joins and the sides, with a soft brush. This first coat seals the surface and sets as a base layer during proof.

**Step 8 — Final proof (about 45–75 min at 25–26 °C).** Leave uncovered (the egg wash keeps the surface moist), or cover loosely with an oiled film that doesn't touch it. **Done when** the loaf has increased by about **75 %**, the strands have swollen so that the gaps between them are closing but still visible, and a gentle poke on the side springs back slowly. Under-proofed braids tear open at the joins; over-proofed braids lose definition and spread.

**Step 9 — Preheat** to **180 °C (355 °F)** conventional for the six-strand loaf, or **185–190 °C (365–375 °F)** for the three-strand loaves. Rack in the centre. Preheat at least 20 min and check with the oven thermometer.

**Step 10 — Second egg wash.** Brush again, thinly and evenly, just before baking, paying attention to the joins and the lower sides. Wipe up any pooled wash at the base with a dry brush. Sprinkle with seeds if you are using them.

**Step 11 — Bake.**
- Six-strand, 900 g: **35–40 min** at 180 °C.
- Three-strand, 2 × 450 g: **25–30 min** at 185–190 °C.

Check colour at 15–20 min. If the top is already deep golden-brown, tent it loosely with foil and continue. Rotate the tray halfway through if your oven browns unevenly. **Done when:** the internal temperature is **88–93 °C** (probe inserted from the end into the thickest part), the crust is evenly deep golden-brown to mahogany, the joins are coloured (no pale, raw-looking dough in the cracks), and the base is golden, not dark brown.

**Step 12 — Cool** on a rack for at least 1 h. Store sealed in a bag at room temperature once cool; challah is at its best within 2 days and makes excellent toast and French toast on day 3.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Raw dough and egg wash contain raw egg. Keep the wash refrigerated between coats, discard the leftover, and wash the brush in hot soapy water. Sesame is a major allergen in many jurisdictions; label the loaf if you share it.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| True hydration | ~52 % (stiff) | Softer dough gives strands that flatten and a braid that blurs |
| Development | Membrane you can see light through | Weak dough tears at the joins and gives a dense, tight crumb |
| Dough temperature | 24–26 °C | Predictable 1.5–2 h bulk |
| Strand rest | 15–20 min before rolling | Unrested strands shrink back and tear |
| Strands | Equal weight (±2 g) and equal length | Unequal strands give a lopsided loaf |
| Braid tension | Snug, not stretched | Tight: tearing at joins. Loose: spreading |
| Final proof | ~+75 %, gaps between strands closing | Under: joins burst. Over: loss of definition |
| Egg wash | Two thin, strained coats, no pooling | Streaks, blotches and uneven colour |
| Oven | 180–190 °C, doubled sheet, tent if needed | Egg and sugar crust browns fast; base can scorch |
| Internal temperature | 88–93 °C | Lower: gummy centre. Higher: dry crumb |

## Common failures

- Joins tear open, showing pale dough → [braids splitting or tearing](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=braids-splitting-or-tearing)
- Streaky or blotchy shine → [egg wash streaks or blotches](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=egg-wash-streaks-or-blotches)
- Dark top, pale joins or pale sides → [uneven colour on challah](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=uneven-colour-on-challah)
- Crust dark, centre raw → [crust too dark before the inside is done](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=crust-too-dark-before-the-inside-is-done)

## Scientific explanation

Challah is structured by two protein systems: gluten developed during a long mix of a stiff dough, and egg proteins that coagulate from about 62–70 °C during baking ([SC-03](stage-1/science/sc-03-proteins.md), [09.1](stage-1/module-09/lesson-01.md)). The low true hydration (52 %) gives strands with enough viscosity and elasticity to keep their rolled cross-section through proof and oven spring. Yolk lipids and lecithin, together with the oil, lubricate and shorten the gluten, so the crumb is tender and close rather than chewy and open, and the dough feels softer than its water content suggests. Oil can go in with the liquids because the long mechanical work of a stiff dough develops the gluten despite the coating fat. The ~9 % sugars (sucrose and honey's glucose and fructose) feed the yeast early, slow it somewhat by osmotic stress, and remain in the crust with the egg proteins. That combination browns fast by the Maillard reaction ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)). Honey's fructose browns at a lower temperature than sucrose, which is why the oven is 180–190 °C rather than the 230 °C of lean bread. The egg wash puts a thin layer of protein, fat and pigment on the surface. The first coat dries during proof into a continuous film; the second adds gloss and colour. Salt in the wash loosens the egg proteins so it spreads evenly.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Yolks | 8 → 16 % (reduce whole egg to 12 % and add 2 % water) | Richer, yellower, more tender crumb; darker crust |
| Oil | 10 → 6 % | Chewier, more bread-like crumb; dries faster |
| Oil → butter | 10 % oil → 12 % butter (not pareve) | Firmer when cool, buttery flavour, slightly more colour |
| Sweetener | 10 → 15 % (honey or sugar) | Sweeter, softer, darker crust much faster: lower oven to 170–175 °C; raise osmotolerant yeast to ~1.8 % |
| True hydration | 52 → 56 % | Softer crumb, but strands flatten and the braid blurs |
| Strand number | 3 → 6 | Taller, more rounded loaf; more joins to proof correctly |
| Shape | Straight braid → round (a long single rope coiled into a spiral) | Traditional festival form; bake 5–10 min longer |
