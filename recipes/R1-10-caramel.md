# R1-10 · Dry caramel, wet caramel and caramel sauce

<span class="tag established">Established technique</span>

Dry caramel, wet caramel and cream-deglazed caramel sauce are established professional techniques described in every standard pastry text. The quantities here are an educational working set chosen for a home kitchen; the sauce ratio (1 : 1 sugar to cream, 20 % butter) is one common point within a wide professional range. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Cook sucrose to a chosen colour by two methods, recognize the colour stages, prevent crystallization, stop the cooking on time, and turn a medium-amber caramel into a smooth sauce of controlled thickness, safely.

## Expected result

- **A. Dry caramel shards:** a thin, clear, glassy sheet, light-to-medium amber, no white crystals, no burnt smell. Snaps cleanly.
- **B. Wet caramel colour ladder:** paper drips at light, medium and dark amber that you can taste and keep as a reference.
- **C. Caramel sauce:** ~400 g of glossy, smooth, medium-amber sauce with a balanced caramel flavour, no bitterness, no graininess. At room temperature it flows slowly off a spoon; from the refrigerator it is thick and spoonable.

## Formula

Percentages are on sugar weight (sugar = 100 %), the usual way to express confectionery formulas.

**A. Dry caramel**

| Ingredient | % of sugar | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Granulated sugar | 100 | 100 |
| **Total** | **100** | **100** |

Yield: ~100 g caramel; a sheet about 20 × 25 cm when poured thin.

**B. Wet caramel (colour ladder, or base for the sauce)**

| Ingredient | % of sugar | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Granulated sugar | 100 | 200 |
| Water | 30 | 60 |
| Glucose syrup (38–45 DE) | 10 | 20 |
| **Total** | **140** | **280** |

Yield: ~215 g of caramel once the water has boiled off (200 g sugar + ~16 g glucose solids). The glucose syrup is optional but strongly recommended while you learn; it makes crystallization much less likely.

**C. Caramel sauce**

| Ingredient | % of sugar | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Granulated sugar (cooked dry, or wet as in B) | 100 | 200 |
| Whipping cream, 35 % fat, warmed to 60–80 °C | 100 | 200 |
| Unsalted butter, cubed, at room temperature | 20 | 40 |
| Fine salt | 0.75 | 1.5 |
| **Total** | **220.75** | **441.5** |

Yield: ~400–410 g sauce (about 30–40 g of water is lost as steam during deglazing and simmering; more if you cook it thicker). If you use the wet method for C, add 60 g water and 20 g glucose syrup; they boil off or become part of the sugar.
**Salted caramel:** raise the salt to 2.5–4 g (1.25–2 % of sugar), preferably flaky sea salt added at the end.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (100 g sugar) works in an 18 cm pan but overcooks quickly; watch colour closely. ×2 (400 g sugar) needs a 24 cm pan at least 10 cm deep. Do not go beyond ×2 in a home pan: the steam burst when the cream goes in grows with batch size.

## Equipment

- Heavy stainless-steel saucepan, light-coloured inside, 18–20 cm diameter, at least 10 cm deep (≥ 2 L capacity). A dark non-stick pan hides the colour; tin-lined copper is traditional but not needed.
- Heatproof silicone spatula or wooden spoon; whisk.
- Probe or candy thermometer reading to at least 200 °C, calibrated in boiling water.
- Pastry brush and a cup of water (for washing down the walls).
- Silicone mat or parchment on a tray (for shards and drips); a white plate or white paper.
- Small saucepan or microwave jug to warm the cream.
- Heatproof jar for the sauce.
- **Bowl of ice water** beside the stove; oven gloves.

## Mise en place

1. Clear the stove area. Long sleeves, closed shoes, hair tied back. No children or pets in the kitchen.
2. Set the bowl of ice water within reach of the hand you do not stir with.
3. Weigh everything. Cube the butter. Warm the cream to **60–80 °C** (steaming, small bubbles at the edge) and keep it warm.
4. Check the pan is spotless and dry: sugar dust, fat or crumbs in the pan seed crystals.
5. Lay out the silicone mat and label 3 spots on white paper: "light", "medium", "dark".

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Caramel at 170–190 °C (340–375 °F) causes severe burns and sticks to skin. Never touch or taste it from the pan; drip it onto paper and wait. If it touches skin, plunge the area in the ice water or cold running water and keep cooling for **20 minutes**; do not pull off stuck sugar; get medical help for burns larger than a coin or on the face, hands or joints. Add the cream **warm, off the heat, in three parts, standing back**: the mixture rises violently and releases scalding steam. Keep the handle turned inward.

</div>

## Procedure

### A. Dry caramel (shards)

1. **Heat the pan** on medium heat (about 60 % of the burner's maximum) for 1 minute. Sprinkle a thin layer of about **one third** of the 100 g sugar evenly over the base.
2. **First melt, 2–4 min.** Watch the sugar at the edges turn clear and liquid. Do not stir yet. When about half the layer has melted, gently push the dry sugar into the liquid with the spatula.
3. **Add the rest in two stages.** When the first layer is almost all liquid and turning straw-coloured, sprinkle on the next third; stir gently to submerge it. Repeat with the last third. Lumps will form; keep stirring slowly and they re-melt within 1–2 min.
4. **Colour, 1–3 min.** Once everything is liquid, stop stirring and swirl the pan to even the colour. Target **light to medium amber, ~170–175 °C (340–345 °F)**. Take it off the heat one shade before your target: the pan carries it further.
5. **Pour** at once onto the silicone mat in a thin layer; tilt the tray to spread it. It hardens in 2–3 min. Snap into shards when cool. Store airtight with a desiccant sachet or in a dry tin; it goes sticky in humid air within hours.
6. **Clean up:** fill the pan with water and bring it to a boil; the caramel dissolves.

### B. Wet caramel (colour ladder)

7. **Dissolve.** Put the water in the pan, then the sugar, then the glucose syrup in the centre. Stir over medium heat until the syrup is completely clear, with no crystals on the spoon or at the bottom, **before** it boils (3–5 min).
8. **Wash down.** When it boils, stop stirring. Brush the inside walls with the wet pastry brush just above the syrup line, or put a lid on for 1–2 min so condensation washes the walls.
9. **Boil, 8–12 min.** Boil on medium-high without stirring. The bubbles become smaller and slower as the water leaves; the temperature passes 110 °C, 130 °C, 150 °C. If you use a thermometer, clip it in or hold it in the centre without scraping the pan.
10. **First colour, ~160 °C.** The syrup turns pale straw at the edges first. Swirl the pan (do not stir) to even it out.
11. **Colour ladder.** With a clean spoon, drip a small drop onto the labelled paper at **light amber ~165–170 °C (330–340 °F)**, **medium amber ~175–180 °C (345–355 °F)** and **dark amber ~185–190 °C (365–375 °F)**. Record the time and reading for each. Each stage arrives only 30–60 s after the previous one.
12. **Stop** at dark amber: pour the rest onto the silicone mat, or dip the pan base in cold water for 2–3 s (stainless steel only).
13. Taste the cooled drips in order. Photograph them on white paper with a daylight reference.

### C. Caramel sauce

14. **Cook the caramel** by the dry method (steps 1–4 with 200 g sugar in 3–4 additions) or the wet method (steps 7–10 with 200 g sugar, 60 g water, 20 g glucose). Take it to **medium amber, ~175–180 °C (345–355 °F)**, and take the pan **off the heat**.
15. **Deglaze in three parts.** Standing back, pour in about one third of the warm cream. It will foam up to 3–4 times its volume and steam hard. Whisk until the bubbling subsides (10–15 s). Add the second third, whisk; then the last third.
16. **Return to low heat.** If any caramel has set into lumps, stir over low heat until they dissolve, 1–3 min. Then simmer, whisking, until the sauce reaches **~105 °C** for a pourable sauce (**106–108 °C** for a thick spoonable one), 1–3 min.
17. **Off the heat, add the butter** and whisk until it is fully emulsified and the sauce is glossy (about 1 min). Whisk in the salt.
18. **Cool and store.** Pour into a clean heatproof jar. Cool to room temperature (1–2 h) uncovered, then cover and refrigerate. Keeps about 2 weeks refrigerated. Rewarm gently (water bath or 10–15 s bursts in the microwave, stirring) to use.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Clean pan and tools | No sugar dust, fat or crumbs | Foreign particles and stray crystals seed crystallization |
| Wet caramel: sugar fully dissolved before boiling | Clear syrup, no crystals on the spoon | Undissolved crystals seed crystallization once the syrup is supersaturated |
| Wet caramel: no stirring after the boil | Swirl only; walls washed down | Stirring and wall crystals trigger crystallization |
| Colour at removal from heat | One shade lighter than target (e.g. remove at ~172 °C for 175–180 °C) | 5–10 °C carry-over in a heavy pan |
| Maximum colour | ≤ ~190 °C (375 °F) | Above this the caramel is bitter and acrid |
| Cream temperature | 60–80 °C | Cold cream seizes the caramel and makes the steam burst more violent |
| Sauce final temperature | 103–105 °C pourable; 106–108 °C thick | Sets sugar concentration and so viscosity |
| Safety set-up | Ice water, long sleeves, deep pan, cream added off the heat | Sugar burns are severe |

## Common failures

- Wet caramel turned white and sandy → [caramel crystallized or grainy](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=caramel-crystallized-or-grainy)
- Bitter, acrid, very dark → [caramel burnt or bitter](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=caramel-burnt-or-bitter)
- Hard lump when the cream went in → [caramel sauce seized when cream added](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=caramel-sauce-seized-when-cream-added)
- Sauce too runny or too stiff after chilling → [caramel sauce too thick or too thin](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=caramel-sauce-too-thick-or-too-thin)
- Dry caramel with dark spots and unmelted sugar at the same time → heat too high or sugar layer too thick; add in more stages on lower heat
- Shards go sticky → stored in humid air; airtight with desiccant

## Scientific explanation

Sucrose heated dry, or after its water has boiled away, decomposes: it splits into glucose and fructose, which lose water and link into a large family of compounds. Small volatile ones give the aroma (buttery, fruity, toasty notes); organic acids make caramel slightly sour; larger polymers give the brown colour and, as cooking continues, bitterness. Colour and flavour are therefore linked: the darker, the less sweet and the more bitter ([07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md), [SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)).

In the wet method, the water only delays this: the syrup boils at rising temperatures as it concentrates, and caramelization begins once almost all the water is gone (~160 °C). Near the end the syrup is highly **supersaturated**, the condition in which a single seed can set off crystallization of the whole pan. Glucose syrup, a mix of glucose, maltose and longer chains, puts different molecules between the sucrose molecules and makes it much harder for them to line up into crystals ([SC-13](stage-1/science/sc-13-crystallization.md)).

Adding cream dilutes the caramel with water and fat and cools it by 70–80 °C in seconds. If the cream is cold, part of the caramel drops below its setting point before it can dissolve, forming a glassy lump; gentle heat dissolves it again. Simmering the sauce then boils off water: the final temperature is a direct measure of sugar concentration and so of thickness. Milk protein and lactose from the cream and butter add a Maillard note while the sauce simmers, and the butter fat, emulsified by the cream's proteins, gives gloss and a round mouthfeel.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Final caramel colour | Darker (185 °C) | Less sweet, more bitter, more complex; darker sauce |
| | Lighter (165 °C) | Sweeter, milder, paler sauce |
| Glucose syrup | 0 → 20 % of sugar | Much lower crystallization risk; slightly softer, chewier set caramel; slightly less sweet |
| Acid (lemon juice, a few drops) instead of glucose | Add | Some inversion, lower crystallization risk; browns a little faster |
| Cream in sauce | Less (150 g) | Thicker, more intense sauce |
| | More (250 g) | Thinner, milder sauce |
| Butter | 0 → 80 g | More gloss and richness; softer set when cold |
| Final sauce temperature | Higher | Thicker when cool; at ~115–120 °C it approaches a soft caramel candy |
| Method | Dry instead of wet | Faster, deeper flavour, more risk of uneven colour |

## Experiment suggestions

- **Colour ladder tasting:** make three sauces from caramel at 170, 180 and 188 °C and taste them blind with a friend; rank sweetness and bitterness.
- **Crystallization control:** 100 g wet caramel with and without glucose syrup, both stirred once at 130 °C; compare. The full version is [X1-36](experiments/X1-36-sugar-crystallization.md).
- **Viscosity vs temperature:** split a sauce batch and cook the halves to 104 °C and 108 °C; after 24 h refrigerated, time how long 20 g takes to flow 10 cm down a tilted plate.
