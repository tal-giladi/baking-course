# R1-38 · Brownies — chocolate in a baked batter

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a melted-method fudgy brownie with proportions in the normal range for the style (high chocolate and butter, sugar above flour, very little flour), designed so that you can move it towards cakey by changing one variable at a time. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Bake a fudgy brownie with a thin, shiny, crackled top and a dense, moist interior, using melted chocolate and butter, whipped eggs and sugar, and minimal flour; judge doneness from edges, surface and centre; and understand which variables move a brownie between fudgy and cakey.

## Expected result

- 16 squares of 5 × 5 cm, about 2 cm tall.
- **Top:** a thin, glossy, paper-like crust that crackles when cut, lighter than the interior.
- **Interior:** dense, moist, fudgy; holds together when cut; slightly glossy cut face; no raw, wet streak and no dry, cakey crumb.
- **Edges:** set and slightly chewy, not hard or burnt.
- Deep chocolate flavour, balanced sweetness and salt.

## Formula

Baker's % is on flour; because flour is so low here, the percentages are large. That is the point: a brownie is a chocolate-and-sugar system held together by egg, with flour as a minor structural ingredient.

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain (all-purpose) flour | 100 | 75 |
| Cocoa powder, Dutch-processed | 26.7 | 20 |
| Dark chocolate 60–70 %, chopped | 226.7 | 170 |
| Unsalted butter | 186.7 | 140 |
| Caster sugar (fine granulated) | 333.3 | 250 |
| Whole eggs (3 large, out of shell), ~20 °C | 200 | 150 |
| Fine salt | 4.0 | 3 |
| Vanilla extract | 6.7 | 5 |
| **Total** | **1,084.1** (rounding) | **813** |

**Yield:** one 20 × 20 cm pan, ~780 g baked (~4 % bake loss), 16 squares of ~49 g.
**Leavening:** none. Lift comes only from air whipped into the eggs and steam; that is why it is dense.
**Scaling:** by pan area at the same depth. 18 × 18 cm = ×0.81; 23 × 23 cm = ×1.32; 23 × 33 cm = ×1.9 (~1,543 g batter). ×2 in two 20 cm pans bakes like the original.

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g); probe thermometer; oven thermometer.
- 20 × 20 cm metal pan (light-coloured aluminium preferred), 5 cm deep, lined with a parchment "sling" overhanging two sides.
- Heatproof bowl and saucepan (bain-marie) or microwave.
- Mixing bowl (3 L) and a balloon whisk, or a hand mixer / stand mixer with whisk.
- Sieve; spatula; cooling rack; long sharp knife and a jug of hot water.

## Mise en place

1. Oven to **175 °C (350 °F)**, conventional, rack in the middle (fan: 160–165 °C). Check with an oven thermometer; allow 20 min to preheat.
2. Line the pan: butter it lightly, lay a parchment strip across the base and up two sides (the sling), butter the parchment.
3. Eggs out of the refrigerator 30 min before, or in warm water 5 min: **~20 °C**.
4. Sift flour and cocoa together.
5. Chop the chocolate (≤ 1 cm); cut the butter into cubes.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Melt chocolate and butter (4–6 min).** In the bowl over barely simmering water (heat off once the water is hot; bowl not touching the water) or in the microwave at 50 % power in 30 s bursts, melt chocolate and butter together, stirring, to a smooth, glossy liquid at **45–50 °C**. Take it off the heat.

**Step 2 — Whip eggs and sugar (3–4 min).** Whisk eggs, sugar, salt and vanilla at medium-high speed (hand mixer) or briskly by hand. **Endpoint:** paler, slightly thickened and foamy, volume up by about half, falling from the whisk in a thick stream that disappears into the surface within 2–3 s (short of a full ribbon). Most of the sugar should feel dissolved: a drop rubbed between finger and thumb is only faintly gritty. This is the step that makes the shiny top.

**Step 3 — Combine (1 min).** Pour the warm chocolate mixture (**40–45 °C**) into the eggs while whisking gently at low speed. **Endpoint:** homogeneous, glossy, dark batter, noticeably thicker. The warmth helps dissolve the remaining sugar.

**Step 4 — Add the dry ingredients (1 min).** Sift the flour and cocoa over the batter; fold with the spatula, about 20–30 strokes, **just until no dry streaks remain**. Do not beat: the batter should be thick and glossy.

**Step 5 — Pan (1 min).** Scrape into the lined pan, level with the spatula into the corners, tap the pan once on the bench to release large bubbles. Batter depth ~2 cm.

**Step 6 — Bake (24–28 min; start checking at 22 min).** Bake at 175 °C. **Doneness cues** (use all of them):
- **Edges:** set, slightly raised and pulling away from the paper by 1–2 mm; 2 cm from the edge the brownie is firm.
- **Top:** the whole surface is dull-shiny and crackled, including the centre; no wet, glossy batter visible in the middle.
- **Centre:** a gentle shake of the pan shows only a very slight wobble in the middle 5 cm, not a liquid ripple. A toothpick inserted **halfway between centre and edge** comes out with **moist, sticky crumbs**, not wet batter; at the very centre, thick fudgy smears are expected.
- If you probe the centre, **record** the temperature at which you pulled it and the result after cooling, and use it as your own reference for this pan and oven.

Rotate the pan 180° at ~15 min if your oven map ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) shows a hot side. Overbaking by 3–5 minutes is the most common way to turn a fudgy brownie cakey and dry.

**Step 7 — Cool (at least 2 h).** Cool in the pan on a rack for **at least 1 h** at room temperature; the centre finishes setting from residual heat (carry-over) as cocoa butter solidifies. For clean squares, chill another 1 h.

**Step 8 — Cut.** Lift out with the sling. Trim the edges if you want sharp squares. Cut into 4 × 4 with a long knife dipped in hot water and wiped between cuts.

**Step 9 — Store.** Airtight, room temperature, 3–4 days; wrapped and frozen, 2 months.

**Safety:** Raw batter contains raw egg and raw flour; do not taste it ([FDA-flour], [FDA-eggs]). Melted chocolate and butter at 50 °C and a 175 °C pan: use oven gloves. Allergens: egg, milk, wheat, soya (lecithin in chocolate), possible nut traces.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate–butter melt | 45–50 °C, fully smooth | Too hot scorches chocolate and cooks the eggs on contact |
| Eggs | ~20 °C | Cold eggs whip poorly and set the chocolate fat into lumps |
| Egg–sugar whipping | 3–4 min: paler, thickened, sugar mostly dissolved, short of a ribbon | Dissolved sugar and a light foam make the shiny crust; much more whipping makes it cakey |
| Flour mixing | Fold just to no dry streaks | Minimal gluten: fudgy, not bready |
| Oven | 175 °C, verified | Edges and centre set at the right relative rate |
| Doneness | Edges set, whole top matte-crackled, slight centre wobble, moist crumbs midway | Overbaking is the main cause of dry, cakey brownies |
| Cooling | ≥ 1 h in pan | Carry-over setting; cocoa butter solidifies |

## Scientific explanation

A brownie is a sugar-and-fat-rich batter with very little flour, held together mainly by **egg coagulation** and by **cocoa butter and butterfat** that solidify on cooling ([16.1](stage-1/module-16/lesson-01.md)). Sugar at more than three times the flour weight, dissolved in the egg water, raises the setting temperature of the egg proteins and keeps the crumb moist and dense ([07.1](stage-1/module-07/lesson-01.md), [09.1](stage-1/module-09/lesson-01.md)); fat from chocolate and butter coats the few starch granules and proteins, limiting gluten and giving tenderness. The cocoa particles and the chocolate's non-fat solids absorb water and act as a structural filler. **Fudgy vs cakey** is the balance between these tenderizers (fat, chocolate, sugar) and the builders and aerators (flour, whipped egg, leavening): more fat and chocolate relative to flour, less air and a shorter bake give fudgy; more flour, eggs whipped to a full ribbon or baking powder, and a longer bake give cakey ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)). The **shiny crackled top** is a thin layer of egg-and-dissolved-sugar foam that rises to the surface and dries into a meringue-like film during baking; it forms when sugar is well dissolved in the whipped eggs (helped by the warm chocolate) and fails when sugar stays crystalline or the eggs are not whipped. As the brownie cools, the fats crystallize, so a brownie is softer warm and firmer and fudgier after chilling.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | 75 → 110 g | Cakier, more crumb, less fudgy |
| Chocolate | 170 → 120 g (+15 g cocoa) | Less fudgy, drier, more cocoa-bitter, less rich |
| Egg whipping | 3–4 min → 8–10 min (full ribbon) | Taller, lighter, cakier; very shiny thick crust |
| Egg whipping | Just stirred in | Denser, fudgier; dull top, little or no crust |
| Sugar | Caster → half light brown sugar | Chewier, moister, more caramel flavour; duller top |
| Leavening | Add 2 g baking powder | Cakey rise, open crumb |
| Bake time | −4 min / +5 min | Very gooey centre / dry, cakey, hard edges |
| Pan | Metal → glass or ceramic | Slower edges, then overcooked edges before the centre sets; reduce to 165 °C |
| Cocoa | Dutch → natural | Slightly redder, sharper, lighter; no leavening effect here because there is no soda |

## Experiment suggestions

- Egg whipping time: 0 / 3 / 8 min, everything else identical: height, crust, fudginess.
- Flour level: 55 / 75 / 110 g in three small 15 × 15 cm pans (×0.56 formula).
- Bake time series: pull three small pans at 18, 22 and 26 min; cut after cooling.
- Chocolate % at the same weight: 55 % vs 70 %: sweetness, set, fudginess.
