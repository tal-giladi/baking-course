# R1-06 · Sourdough country loaf

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established pain au levain / country-loaf technique as described in the professional bread texts below. The numbers are chosen to be forgiving for a first sourdough: moderate hydration, a modest levain, and a cold retard that makes the schedule flexible and the loaf easy to score. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make one leavened-only-by-levain country loaf. Build a levain from your R1-05 starter, run a warm bulk fermentation (~25–26 °C) judged by volume and dough cues, retard the shaped loaf overnight at 3–5 °C, and bake it from cold in a covered pot to ≥96 °C internal.

## Expected result

- One round or oval loaf, ~760–790 g after cooling, 20–22 cm across and 9–11 cm tall (boule).
- Deep reddish-brown crust, thin and crisp when cool, with small blisters from the retard and a pronounced ear on the main score.
- Open, irregular, glossy crumb with cells from 2 mm to about 1.5 cm, evenly distributed from crust to base, with no dense layer and no single tunnel.
- Balanced, gentle acidity: clearly sourdough, but more wheat than vinegar. Keeps noticeably better than R1-01: good on day 3.

## Formula

PFF = 15 % (75 g of the 500 g total flour is in the levain). Levain at 100 % hydration.

| Ingredient | Total formula % | Total formula g | Levain % | Levain g | Final dough g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (12–13 % protein) | 90.0 | 450 | 100.0 | 75 | 375 |
| Wholemeal wheat flour | 10.0 | 50 | — | — | 50 |
| Water | 74.0 | 370 | 100.0 | 75 | 295 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 10 | — | — | 10 |
| Levain | — | — | — | — | 150 |
| **Total** | **176.0** | **880** | **200.0** | **150** | **880** |

**Levain build (the morning of mixing):** 20 g ripe starter + 65 g bread flour + 65 g water = 150 g. The seed brings 10 g flour and 10 g water, so the levain contains 75 g flour and 75 g water.
**Yield:** one loaf, 880 g dough → ~760–790 g baked and cooled.
**Hydration:** 74 %. For your first sourdough, or with weaker flour, drop to 70 % (350 g total water; final-dough water 275 g; total 860 g). Once you are comfortable, go to 76–78 % by adding water late (bassinage).
**Levain as a percentage:** 150 g is 30 % of total flour, or 35 % of final-dough flour (150 / 425). PFF is 15 %.
**Scaling:** ×2 for two loaves (build 40 g + 130 g + 130 g levain = 300 g). ×0.5 is not recommended in a large pot; a 440 g loaf spreads in a 24 cm pot.

## Equipment

As [R1-01](recipes/R1-01-lean-loaf.md): scale, probe thermometer, 2–3 L straight-sided container with a marked level, bowl and bench scrapers, banneton (20 cm round or 25 cm oval) with rice flour, cast-iron or enamelled pot with lid (24–26 cm), lame, parchment, oven gloves, rack. Plus:

- A straight-sided jar for the levain, with a rubber band.
- Somewhere at 25–26 °C for bulk (oven with light on and door ajar, a cooler with a jar of warm water, a proofing box). Check it with a thermometer.
- A fridge shelf at 3–5 °C, checked with a thermometer.
- Optional: pH meter.

## Mise en place

1. **1–2 days before:** refresh the starter from the fridge with two 1:2:2 feeds at 24–26 °C ([R1-05](recipes/R1-05-levain.md) step 10). It must peak on time.
2. **Day 1, ~08:00:** build the levain (Procedure step 1).
3. **Day 1, ~12:30:** measure flour and room temperatures. Calculate water for a **DDT of 26 °C**. Include the levain as a fourth factor if you add it at mixing: water = 4 × DDT − flour − room − levain − friction. Example: flour 21, room 22, levain 25, friction 2 → water = 104 − 21 − 22 − 25 − 2 = **34 °C**. The autolyse in step 2 cools, so aim for the top of the range.
4. Weigh final-dough flours into the mixing bowl. Weigh 275 g water for the autolyse and 20 g water with the salt in a small cup.

## Procedure

Times assume 25–26 °C throughout. Record actual temperatures and times.

**Step 1 — Build the levain (t = −5 to −7 h).** Mix 20 g ripe starter + 65 g bread flour + 65 g water (~27 °C) in the jar. Mark the level. Keep at 25–26 °C. Ready in **5–7 h** when it has **at least doubled**, is domed or just at the top of its rise, has bubbles throughout, and smells pleasantly sour and fruity. pH ~3.8–4.2 if measured. At 21 °C, allow 8–10 h, or build it the night before at 1:5:5 (see Variables).

**Step 2 — Autolyse (t = −45 min).** Mix the 375 g bread flour, 50 g wholemeal and 275 g water to a shaggy mass with no dry flour. Cover and rest **30–60 min**.

**Step 3 — Add the levain (t = 0).** Spread the ripe levain over the dough. Pinch and fold it through with a wet hand for 2–3 min until uniform. Rest 15 min.

**Step 4 — Salt and mix (t = 15 min).** Add the salt and the 20 g water. Pinch through for 2 min, then slap-and-fold or squeeze-and-fold for **3–5 min** until the dough is smooth and cohesive and forms a thin membrane that tears before it is fully translucent (moderate development). Stand mixer: speed 1 for 3 min, speed 2 for 2–3 min.

**Step 5 — Check temperature.** Target **25–26 °C** (24–27 acceptable). Record it. Move to the oiled, marked container.

**Step 6 — Bulk with folds (t = 20 min to ~4.5–5.5 h).** At 25–26 °C:
- Fold at **30, 60, 90 and 120 min**: one set of 4 stretch-and-folds, or a coil fold (lift the centre, let the ends tuck under, rotate 90°, repeat). Stop folding early if the dough already holds a tall, domed shape.
- Then leave it undisturbed.

**Step 7 — End of bulk.** Sourdough bulk is slower and less obvious than yeasted bulk. Typically **4–6 h** from adding the levain at 25–26 °C. End it when **all** of these hold:
- volume **+50–75 %** from the mark (use the low end if your kitchen is warm or you plan a long retard);
- domed surface, with bubbles at the top and sides, some large;
- the edges where the dough meets the container are convex, not flat;
- the dough jiggles as one airy mass and releases cleanly from the container;
- it smells pleasantly tangy, not sharp.

If it is still dense and flat-sided after 6 h, the dough is cooler than you think or the levain was weak: extend and check every 30 min rather than moving on by the clock.

**Step 8 — Pre-shape.** Tip onto an unfloured bench. Using the bench scraper, shape a loose round with light tension. Note how it behaves: a well-fermented sourdough holds a rounded shape and slowly relaxes; a slack puddle means over-fermented or under-developed.

**Step 9 — Bench rest, 20–30 min**, uncovered, until relaxed and slightly flattened.

**Step 10 — Shape.** Lightly flour the top, flip, and shape a tight boule or bâtard ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)). A stitched bâtard or a boule with a firm final roll builds the tension this wetter dough needs. Place seam-up in the rice-floured banneton.

**Step 11 — Short rest then retard.** Cover (a shower cap or a bag). Leave **15–30 min** at room temperature, then refrigerate at **3–5 °C for 8–16 h**. Check the fridge temperature where the loaf sits.

**Step 12 — Preheat (next morning).** Pot and lid in the oven at **250 °C (480 °F)** for at least 45 min. Leave the loaf in the fridge until the oven is ready.

**Step 13 — Check proof.** From cold, the loaf should have grown visibly (~+30–50 % since shaping), feel lighter, and the poke test should leave a dent that springs back slowly. Cold dough springs back more than warm dough ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)). If it is tight, dense and clearly smaller than the banneton, leave it at room temperature for 30–90 min and check again.

**Step 14 — Score and load.** Turn out onto parchment. Score one long cut at 30–45°, ~1 cm deep, on a bâtard, or a square or a single curved cut on a boule. Cold dough cuts cleanly. Lower into the pot and cover.

**Step 15 — Bake.** **20 min covered at 250 °C (480 °F)**, then remove the lid, lower to **230 °C (445 °F)**, and bake **20–25 min** more until the crust is deep reddish-brown and the ear is dark. Sourdough colours more slowly than yeasted dough: be patient and bake to colour. **Done at ≥96 °C internal** and a hollow sound from the base.

**Step 16 — Cool ≥2 h** on a rack before cutting. Sourdough crumb is moister and sets more slowly than R1-01. Store cut-side down on a board for 1–2 days, then in a bag; or slice and freeze.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** The pot and lid are at 250 °C and the handles stay hot for a long time. Use dry, thick oven gloves, lower the loaf on parchment, and put the removed lid somewhere visible on a heat-proof surface with a glove on top as a reminder. Steam bursts out when you lift the lid; open it away from your face.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Starter and levain maturity | Starter peaking on time; levain doubled, domed, used at peak | An immature or over-ripe levain is the most common cause of dense, sour or slack sourdough |
| DDT | 25–26 °C | Sourdough is very temperature-sensitive; at 22 °C bulk may take 7–8 h |
| Folds | 3–4 sets in the first 2 h | Builds the strength that a slow, wet, acidifying dough needs |
| End of bulk | +50–75 %, domed, convex edges, jiggles | Under: dense crumb and tunnel holes. Over: slack, flat, sour, pale |
| Shaping tension | Taut skin, sealed seam | Controls height and spread through the long retard |
| Retard temperature | 3–5 °C, 8–16 h | Warmer fridge over-proofs the loaf; flavour and scoring depend on it |
| Pot preheat | 250 °C, ≥45 min | Oven spring |
| Internal temperature | ≥96 °C | Sourdough crumb is moist; underbaking reads as gummy |
| Cooling | ≥2 h | Cutting early gives a gummy crumb |

## Scientific explanation

The levain brings a large population of acid-tolerant wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria, already active, plus acids ([05.3](stage-1/module-05/lesson-03.md)). In the final dough the yeasts produce CO₂ more slowly than commercial yeast, so bulk takes 4–6 h even at 25–26 °C. Meanwhile the bacteria keep producing lactic and acetic acid; the dough pH falls from about 5.5–6 towards 4 over bulk and the retard. Moderate acidity tightens gluten early, which, with the folds, gives the dough strength. Over long times, acidity and protease activity soften the network, so an over-fermented sourdough becomes slack and sticky. The retard at 3–5 °C slows yeast more than it slows acid production, and it allows acetic acid in particular to accumulate. That adds flavour and a firmer, cold dough that scores cleanly. Because fermentation has run for so long, less residual sugar is left for Maillard browning, and the low pH slows the Maillard reaction too. So sourdough needs a full, confident bake to colour ([04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md)). The acids and other fermentation products slow mould growth and staling, which is why the loaf keeps well ([Arora 2021]).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| PFF | 15 → 10 % | Slower bulk (5–7 h), milder flavour, more time before over-fermentation |
| PFF | 15 → 20 % | Faster bulk (3.5–4.5 h), more acidity, less tolerance to delay |
| Levain timing | Same-day 1:3:3 → overnight 1:5:5 at ~21 °C (14 g + 68 g + 68 g = 150 g, 10–12 h) | Levain ready at breakfast; slightly milder |
| Levain hydration | 100 % → stiff 55 % | Tangier (more acetic), stronger dough; raise final-dough water to keep 74 % overall |
| Hydration | 74 → 78 % | More open crumb; needs stronger shaping; more risk of spreading |
| Wholemeal | 10 → 25 % | More flavour, faster bulk, more sour, denser crumb; add ~3–4 % water |
| Retard | 8 → 16 h | More sourness (particularly acetic), more blistering, risk of over-proof if the fridge is >5 °C |
| Bulk temperature | 26 → 22 °C | Bulk ~1.5× longer, more acetic character |

## Experiment suggestions

- [X1-14 Levain](experiments/X1-14-levain.md): measure your culture's peak times so you can schedule this loaf accurately.
- Split a double batch after shaping: retard one loaf 8 h and one 16 h. Taste blind.
- Bake one loaf with a same-day proof (2–3 h at 25 °C, no retard) against a retarded one: compare sourness, crust and ease of scoring.
