# R1-40 · Chocolate tart

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a fully baked sucrée (or chocolate sucrée) shell filled with a set dark-chocolate ganache, with a baked chocolate custard filling as the alternative and an optional pouring glaze. This is an **assembly recipe**: the shell comes from [R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md), the ganache method from [R1-36](recipes/R1-36-ganache.md) and chocolate tempering, for the seal and décor, from [R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md). Quantities are for one 22 cm tart, at standard professional proportions, written for this course.

## Objective

Make a chocolate tart with a crisp shell, a smooth, glossy filling that self-levels without bubbles or cracks and slices cleanly at 18–22 °C, by controlling the ganache emulsion, its pouring temperature and the setting conditions (or, for the baked version, the bake end-point).

## Expected result

- **Whole tart (22 cm × 2 cm):** about 870 g with glaze. Level, mirror-like or satin surface filling the shell to 2–3 mm below the rim; no bubbles, cracks, grease sheen or bloom; clean golden (or dark cocoa) rim.
- **Slice (1/10, about 87 g) at 18–22 °C:** clean, straight cut face; ganache smooth, dense and melting, like firm butter at room temperature; shell crisp.
- **Taste:** deep chocolate, balanced bitterness, a trace of salt; not greasy.
- **Baked option:** a softer, silkier filling with a matt, even top that trembles slightly when the tart is nudged at room temperature.

## Equipment

- 22 cm × 2 cm tart ring, tray, parchment, weights (from [R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md)).
- Scale (1 g); probe thermometer.
- Small saucepan; heatproof bowl or tall jug; silicone spatula; hand (stick) blender (strongly recommended).
- Pastry brush for the chocolate seal.
- A level shelf at 16–20 °C (or refrigerator) for setting; a pin or blowtorch for bubbles.
- Long, thin knife, jug of hot water and a cloth for slicing.

## Mise en place

- **Day before or morning:** shell lined, blind baked to a **full bake** and cooled ([R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md) steps 6–11). For chocolate sucrée, judge doneness by time (weighted 15–18 min, then 10–12 min without), a dry matt base and a toasted smell, not by colour.
- Chocolate chopped to 5 mm pieces (or pistoles) in the bowl or jug; butter cubed and at 18–20 °C.
- Shell at room temperature (18–22 °C), on its serving board, on a level surface.

## Procedure

### Seal the shell

1. Melt (or temper, [R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)) 30 g of the dark chocolate. Brush a thin, complete film over the inside of the cooled shell, corner first. Let set 10 min at ≤ 20 °C. Tempered chocolate sets hard and glossy; melted, untempered chocolate sets softer but still works as a barrier.

### Filling A: ganache

2. **Heat** the cream with the glucose syrup and salt to **80–85 °C** (steaming, small bubbles at the edge). Do not boil hard.
3. **Pour** the hot cream over the chocolate in one go. Wait **1 min** so the chocolate starts to melt.
4. **Emulsify.** Stir from the centre in small, tight circles with a spatula, widening gradually: the mixture first looks split and grainy, then turns glossy and elastic from the centre outward. Or use a hand blender, head fully submerged and tilted to avoid drawing in air, 20–30 s. **Endpoint:** uniformly glossy, smooth, no streaks of fat or chocolate specks; temperature about 45–50 °C.
5. **Butter.** When the ganache has cooled to **35–40 °C**, add the soft butter and blend (submerged) until fully incorporated and glossy.
6. **Pour.** At **35–40 °C**, pour into the centre of the shell from low height, filling to **2–3 mm below the rim** (you will use about 560 g; pour any excess into a ramekin). Tilt the tart gently to level. Tap the board once on the bench to lift bubbles; prick surface bubbles with a pin or pass a blowtorch briefly 20–30 cm above the surface.
7. **Set** undisturbed at **16–20 °C for 2–3 h** until firm to a light touch at the centre. (If the kitchen is warmer than 22 °C, refrigerate 30–60 min, then return to room temperature, covered loosely, for at least 30 min before glazing or serving.)

### Filling B: baked chocolate custard (alternative to steps 2–7)

2b. Preheat the oven to **150 °C (300 °F)**, still, rack in the middle. Put the sealed shell on a tray.
3b. Heat cream, milk, sugar and salt to **80–85 °C**; pour over the chocolate and emulsify as in step 4.
4b. Cool to **40–45 °C**, then whisk in the beaten eggs gently (without whipping in air) until smooth. Strain if you see any threads.
5b. Pour into the shell to 2–3 mm below the rim; pop bubbles.
6b. Bake **15–25 min**. **Endpoint:** the outer 3–4 cm is set and matt; the centre 5–6 cm wobbles as a single unit, like a set jelly, when the tray is nudged; centre **68–72 °C** on a probe.
7b. Cool at room temperature at least **1 h** (the centre sets further). Serve at room temperature the same day, or refrigerate up to 2 days and bring back to room temperature for 45–60 min.

### Glaze (optional, Filling A only)

8. Chill the set tart **20–30 min** so the surface is firm and cool (about 10–15 °C).
9. Heat cream, water and glucose to a simmer; pour over the 40 g chocolate; emulsify with a submerged blender. Cool to **32–35 °C**.
10. Pour about **75 g** onto the centre of the tart in one movement; tilt the tart to spread it to the edges (do not touch it with a spatula). Leave to set 20 min at room temperature.

### Décor and slicing

11. Decorate sparingly: a tempered chocolate disc or shard ([R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)), a few flakes of sea salt, cocoa nibs, or a caramel shard ([R1-10](recipes/R1-10-caramel.md)).
12. **Slice at 16–20 °C** with a long, thin knife dipped in **60–70 °C** water and wiped dry: one downward cut, then press through the shell. Clean, dip and wipe between cuts. Mark 10 portions with a guide.

### Holding

13. **Filling A:** best on the day it is set and the next; keep covered at a cool room temperature (≤ 20 °C) for up to 24 h, or refrigerated up to 3 days (bring back to 18–22 °C for 45–60 min before serving, still covered, to avoid condensation). **Filling B:** refrigerated, 2 days.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shell | Fully baked, sealed with chocolate, at room temperature | Crisp base for 2–3 days; a warm shell melts the seal and the ganache edge |
| Cream temperature | 80–85 °C | Melts the chocolate fully without overheating (above ~55 °C in the finished mix, fat separation is more likely) |
| Emulsion | Glossy and elastic from the centre; blender head submerged | A fine, stable emulsion is smooth and shiny; coarse or broken is grainy and oily ([16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md)) |
| Butter addition | 35–40 °C ganache; butter at 18–20 °C | Emulsified rather than melted into oil |
| **Pour temperature** | **35–40 °C** | Self-levels; avoids skin, cracks, separation and melting the seal |
| Setting | 2–3 h at 16–20 °C | Slow setting gives the best gloss; no condensation |
| Baked filling end-point | 150 °C oven; centre wobbles; 68–72 °C | Eggs set without curdling; no doming or cracks |
| Service temperature | 18–22 °C | Sliceable, melting, full flavour |

## Scientific explanation

A ganache is an emulsion and a suspension at once: droplets of the cream's water phase (carrying dissolved sugar, milk proteins and glucose) dispersed among cocoa-solid particles in melted cocoa butter and milk fat ([16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md), [SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)). Stirring from the centre or blending breaks the water phase into fine droplets and coats them with cocoa particles and milk proteins; a fine emulsion reflects light evenly and looks glossy, a coarse one looks dull and feels grainy. On cooling, the cocoa butter crystallizes partly and the milk fat firms, and the mass sets. Because the cocoa butter in a ganache is not tempered, the set is soft and the ganache is sliceable rather than snapping. Added butter lowers the firmness and melting point of the fat phase and gives a softer, more melting slice; glucose syrup binds water, improves shine and slows drying ([Beckett]).

The tart stays crisp because the ganache's water is bound by sugar and cocoa solids (aw well below that of pastry cream) and the water phase is surrounded by fat, which slows its exchange with the shell; the chocolate seal adds a further fat barrier ([SC-01](stage-1/science/sc-01-water.md), [17.1](stage-1/module-17/lesson-01.md)).

The pouring temperature controls the surface. At 35–40 °C the ganache is fluid enough to level but close to its setting range, so it sets evenly from the whole surface. Poured much hotter, the surface cools and sets while the interior is still warm and contracting, which cracks it, and the hot fat melts the seal and edge of the shell. In Filling B, the egg proteins coagulate around 68–72 °C in this rich, sweet mixture and set it to a soft custard; baking at 150 °C keeps the edge from overshooting while the centre catches up, as in any baked custard ([12.1](stage-1/module-12/lesson-01.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate:cream ratio | 1:1 → 1:0.8 | Firmer, richer, more bitter slice; slower to level at the same temperature |
| Chocolate cocoa % | 60 → 70 % | Firmer set, more bitter, less sweet |
| Butter | 0 → 10 → 15 % of chocolate + cream | Firmer, cleaner → softer, more melting, more gloss; above ~15 % greasy |
| Glucose syrup | 0 → 10 % of chocolate | More shine, smoother, slightly softer, keeps longer |
| Pour temperature | 35 → 50 °C | More bubbles rise; risk of skin, cracks and oily sheen |
| Setting | Room temperature → refrigerator | Faster; duller surface and risk of condensation |
| Filling | Ganache → baked custard | Softer, silkier, lighter-tasting; shorter life, refrigerated |
| Infusion | Steep tea, coffee beans, spices or citrus zest in the hot cream 10–20 min, strain, top up to weight | Flavoured ganache ([R1-36](recipes/R1-36-ganache.md)) |
