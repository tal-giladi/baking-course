# R1-23 · Crème d'amande (frangipane)

<span class="tag established">Established technique</span>

Crème d'amande at equal weights of butter, sugar, almond flour and egg is a classical French preparation, used in this proportion across professional practice; the small flour addition and the pear tart assembly are written for this course. Frangipane, crème d'amande combined with crème pâtissière, is given as a variant for use after [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md).

## Objective

Make a smooth, stable almond cream by controlled creaming, bake it in a par-baked sucrée shell with pears, and judge doneness by texture and centre temperature. This is the M11 practical: it brings together the short dough, the lined and par-baked shell, and a baked filling.

## Expected result

- **Crème d'amande (raw):** smooth, pale, spreadable, like soft buttercream; not airy, not split.
- **Tart (22 cm):** the cream has puffed around the pears to just below the rim, surface evenly golden-brown, centre springs back when pressed; cut face moist, fine and cake-like, not wet or raw in the middle; base crisp and golden. Pears tender, glazed. 8 slices.

## Formula

### Crème d'amande

Baker's percentages here use almond flour as 100 %, the usual convention for nut creams.

| Ingredient | % (almond flour = 100) | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Unsalted butter, 18–20 °C | 100 | 100 |
| Caster sugar (or icing sugar) | 100 | 100 |
| Almond flour (finely ground blanched almonds) | 100 | 100 |
| Whole egg, room temperature, beaten | 100 | 100 |
| Plain flour (or cornstarch) | 10 | 10 |
| **Total** | **410** | **410** |

Optional: 10 g dark rum or 1 g vanilla paste, or the finely grated zest of half an orange, added with the eggs.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 50 g each of butter, sugar, almond flour and egg, 5 g flour (205 g; one 16 cm tart or 6 tartlets of 8 cm at ~30 g). ×2 = 200 g each, 20 g flour (820 g; two 22 cm tarts). Crème d'amande keeps 3 days refrigerated, covered, or 1 month frozen; soften at room temperature and stir smooth before use.

### Pear tart assembly

| Component | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pâte sucrée ([R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md)), lined in a 22 cm × 2 cm ring and par-baked | ~250 g dough |
| Crème d'amande (above) | 410 g |
| Pear halves, poached (below) or canned in light syrup, drained | 6 halves, ~360 g drained |
| Flaked almonds (optional) | 15 g |
| Apricot jam or nappage, for glazing | 40 g, plus 10 g water |

**Poached pears (if using fresh):** 3 ripe but firm pears (about 550 g), peeled, halved and cored. Simmer in 500 g water, 150 g sugar, 15 g lemon juice and a split vanilla pod (or 2 g paste) at a bare simmer, covered with a paper cartouche, for **10–20 minutes** until a knife tip meets slight resistance at the centre. Cool in the syrup (can be done a day ahead).

<div class="callout safety">

**Allergens:** tree nuts (almond), egg, milk, gluten. **Raw egg:** the cream contains raw egg until baked; bake until the centre reaches at least 85 °C, and do not taste the raw cream ([FDA-eggs]).

</div>

## Equipment

- Scale; probe thermometer.
- Stand mixer with paddle, or bowl and stiff spatula.
- Sieve (for almond flour and sugar if lumpy).
- Offset spatula or piping bag with a 12 mm plain tip.
- Small knife and cutting board; paper towels.
- Pastry brush; small saucepan for the glaze.

## Mise en place

- Butter out of the refrigerator 30–60 minutes before, to **18–20 °C**: soft, dents easily, not shiny.
- Eggs at room temperature (18–22 °C), beaten and weighed.
- Sift the almond flour with the flour.
- Shell: par-baked and cooled ([R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md) steps 6–11), left in its ring on a tray lined with parchment or a perforated mat.
- Pears drained on paper towels, cut side down, for at least 15 minutes; pat dry.
- Oven preheated to **170 °C (340 °F)** still, 160 °C fan, rack in the lower-middle position.

## Procedure

1. **Butter and sugar.** Paddle the butter and sugar on **low to medium-low for 1–2 minutes**, until smooth and slightly paler. Do not whip to a light, fluffy cake-batter stage.
2. **Almond flour.** Add half the almond flour–flour mix and combine on low.
3. **Eggs.** Add the egg in 3–4 additions on low, scraping the bowl each time, alternating with the rest of the almond mix if it starts to look curdled. **Endpoint:** smooth, glossy, homogeneous, spreadable. If it splits (looks grainy, wet), the butter or egg is too cold: mix a little longer on low with the bowl warmed in your hands, or add a spoonful of the almond mix.
4. **Flavour.** Mix in rum, vanilla or zest if using. Total mixing after the eggs: under 2 minutes.
5. **Fill.** Spread or pipe the cream into the shell in an even layer, **no more than half to two-thirds of the wall height** (about 1–1.2 cm deep in a 2 cm ring). Level with an offset spatula.
6. **Pears.** Slice each pear half crosswise into 3–4 mm slices, keeping the half together; slide a palette knife underneath, press gently to fan it, and set it on the cream, narrow ends toward the centre, like the spokes of a wheel. Press gently so the pears sit about halfway into the cream. Scatter flaked almonds between them if using.
7. **Bake.** On the tray, **170 °C for 40–50 minutes**, rotating once at 25 minutes. **Endpoint:** cream puffed around the pears and evenly golden-brown to the centre; the centre springs back when pressed lightly; a skewer in the cream between pears comes out clean; **centre ≥ 85 °C**. If the rim darkens before the centre is done, shield the edge with a foil ring.
8. **Cool** 15 minutes on the tray, lift off the ring, and cool on a rack to room temperature (about 1 hour).
9. **Glaze.** Heat the apricot jam with the water until fluid (strain if chunky) and brush thinly over the pears and surface. Serve the same day for the crispest base; keeps 2 days, covered, in a cool place.

### Variant: frangipane (after M12)

Once you have made crème pâtissière ([R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md), [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md)): whisk 200 g cold pastry cream smooth and fold it into 400 g crème d'amande (**2 : 1** by weight). Use immediately or refrigerate up to 2 days. Frangipane is softer, moister and more custardy than crème d'amande and rises a little less; bake it to the same endpoint. It is the classic filling of the galette des rois and pithiviers made with puff pastry in M15.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature | 18–20 °C | Cold: lumpy, splits when eggs go in; warm/melted: greasy, heavy, leaks fat |
| Egg temperature and addition | Room temperature, in 3–4 additions | Keeps the emulsion; a split cream bakes greasy and uneven |
| Aeration | Smooth, not fluffy; ≤ 2 min after eggs | Excess air rises, then collapses, cracks and overflows |
| Fill level | ≤ 1/2–2/3 of wall height | The cream rises by about a third |
| Fruit | Well drained and patted dry | Free juice makes the cream and base wet |
| Doneness | Springs back, clean skewer, centre ≥ 85 °C | Egg set (texture and safety); underbaked centre is wet and sinks |

## Common failures

- **Cream rises high, then sinks or overflows.** See [Frangipane sinks or overflows](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=frangipane-sinks-or-overflows).
- **Wet, soggy base under the cream.** See [Soggy bottom](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=soggy-bottom).
- **Greasy surface, fat pooling.** Butter too warm or cream split. See [Greasy pastry, butter leaking](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=greasy-pastry-butter-leaking).
- **Rim dark before the centre is set.** See [Overbrowned edges](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=overbrowned-edges).

## Scientific explanation

Crème d'amande is an emulsion-based batter with almond flour as its main dry ingredient. Creaming the butter with sugar gives a continuous fat phase with some air; yolk lecithin and protein let the egg water disperse into it as small droplets ([SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md), [09.3](stage-1/module-09/lesson-03.md)). Almond flour, about half fat and a fifth protein, gives body and holds moisture, but has no gluten-forming protein and little starch. In the oven the air and steam expand and the cream puffs; the egg proteins coagulate (from about 62 °C for some white proteins, most set by 80 °C, [09.1](stage-1/module-09/lesson-01.md)) and fix the puffed structure. The small amount of flour adds starch that gelatinizes and binds free water from the eggs and fruit, so the cream holds its rise and does not weep.

The balance explains the two classic failures. **Too much air** (overcreaming) gives a cream that rises far above its set structure; when the gas cools and contracts, the weak, egg-only structure collapses, so it sinks and cracks, or it overflows the shell before it sets. **Too little baking** leaves the centre below the coagulation range: it is wet, sinks on cooling, and is unsafe with raw egg.

Frangipane adds pastry cream, whose gelatinized starch and milk make the filling moister and softer and reduce the rise.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Creaming time | 1 → 6 min | More rise, more collapse, coarser texture, more overflow risk |
| Flour | 0 → 20 % of almond weight | More stable, less rise and sink, slightly drier and more cake-like |
| Sugar type | Caster → icing | Smoother, slightly denser |
| Almond flour | Blanched → skin-on (almond meal) | Darker, more rustic, stronger flavour |
| Nut | Almond → hazelnut or pistachio (part or all) | Flavour and colour change; pistachio is often used at 30–50 % of the nut weight |
| Pastry cream (frangipane) | 0 → 50 % of crème d'amande weight | Softer, moister, less rise |
| Bake temperature | 160 → 180 °C | Hotter: faster colour, higher risk of dark rim and raw centre |

## Experiment suggestions

- Creaming time: make two half batches, one mixed 1 minute and one beaten 6 minutes on medium-high, bake in identical tartlet shells, and measure peak rise (mm above the rim at 20 minutes) and final height after cooling.
- Flour level: 0, 10 and 20 % of the almond weight, baked in tartlets with the same drained fruit; compare how much juice pools.
- For the moisture-barrier question (sealed shell or not), see [X1-43](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md) in M17.
