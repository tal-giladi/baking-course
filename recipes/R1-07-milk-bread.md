# R1-07 · Milk bread with tangzhong

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established technique: a soft enriched pan loaf of the Japanese "Hokkaido" style, using a tangzhong (flour cooked with five times its weight of water). The percentages sit in the normal range for soft milk breads and were chosen to make the effect of the tangzhong easy to see and to leave room for [X1-16](experiments/X1-16-tangzhong.md). It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a tall, very soft, fine-crumbed pan loaf that stays soft for three days. Cook a tangzhong to its endpoint, compute the true hydration of a dough whose liquid is milk and egg, add butter after the gluten is developed, shape four rolled pieces, and bake to an internal temperature of 88–93 °C without over-browning.

## Expected result

- One loaf, about 570–590 g after cooling, in a 22 × 11 cm loaf pan, with four rounded lobes rising 3–5 cm above the rim.
- Thin, soft, even, glossy golden-brown crust. No dark ring at the base.
- White, fine, even crumb that pulls apart in soft, feathery strands along the spiral of each rolled piece. It springs back fully when pressed.
- Mildly sweet, milky flavour. On day 3, stored sealed, the crumb should still be soft enough to eat untoasted.

## Formula

All percentages are on **total flour** (300 g), including the 21 g of flour cooked into the tangzhong.

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Tangzhong** | | |
| Bread flour | 7.0 | 21 |
| Water | 35.0 | 105 |
| **Final dough** | | |
| Bread flour (11.5–13 % protein) | 93.0 | 279 |
| Whole milk (pasteurized; see note) | 30.0 | 90 |
| Whole egg, beaten | 15.0 | 45 |
| Caster or fine granulated sugar | 10.0 | 30 |
| Milk powder (nonfat) | 3.0 | 9 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 6 |
| Instant yeast | 1.0 | 3 |
| Unsalted butter, soft (18–22 °C) | 10.0 | 30 |
| **Total** | **206.0** | **618** |
| *Egg wash (not in total)* | | *1 egg + 10 g milk + pinch of salt, strained; you use about a third* |

**Yield:** 618 g of dough → one 22 × 11 cm loaf (4 × ~152 g pieces), about 570–590 g baked and cooled.
**True hydration:** water 105 + milk 90 × 0.87 + egg 45 × 0.75 + butter 30 × 0.16 = 221.9 g → **74 %** of flour (worked in [06.1](stage-1/module-06/lesson-01.md)).
**Sugar in the water phase:** 30 ÷ (30 + 221.9) ≈ 12 %. Standard instant yeast works at this level. Osmotolerant instant yeast, at the same 3 g, gives a slightly faster rise.
**Milk:** pasteurized milk can be used as it is. If you use raw milk, scald it to 82–85 °C and cool it (see [06.1](stage-1/module-06/lesson-01.md)). The milk powder can be omitted; the loaf will be a little paler and less milky.
**Scaling:** ×2 for two loaves (600 g flour, 1236 g dough, tangzhong 42 g flour + 210 g water). ×0.5 is not recommended for a loaf, but a ×0.5 batch (309 g) makes 6 rolls.

## Equipment

- Digital scale (1 g), and a 0.1 g scale for the yeast if you have one.
- Probe thermometer.
- Small saucepan (heavy base) and a silicone spatula or small whisk.
- Stand mixer with dough hook (recommended), or a bench and scraper for hand mixing.
- Straight-sided 2 L container with lid, marked at the starting level.
- Loaf pan, **22 × 11 cm** at the top, 6–7 cm deep (about 1.5 L), metal. Grease it with butter or line it with a parchment sling.
- Rolling pin, bench scraper, pastry brush, cooling rack.
- Optional: foil for tenting.

## Mise en place

1. **Tangzhong (at least 1 h before mixing, or the day before).** Whisk 21 g flour into 105 g cold water in the saucepan until smooth. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly and scraping the base, until the paste reaches **65 °C** (acceptable 65–70 °C). At that point it thickens suddenly to a soft pudding, and the spatula leaves trails that hold for a second. This takes 2–4 min. Scrape into a bowl, press cling film onto the surface, and cool to room temperature (below 30 °C). Weigh it: if it is below 120 g, add water back to **126 g** so evaporation doesn't change the hydration. Refrigerated, it keeps for 24 h; bring it back to room temperature, or allow for its temperature in the next step.
2. **Liquid temperature.** Target a final dough temperature of **26 °C**. As a starting point: in a 20–23 °C kitchen with a stand mixer (about 12–15 min of mixing), use milk at 12–18 °C, straight from the fridge in a warm kitchen. By hand, use milk at about 28–32 °C. Record the result and refine your friction factor ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)).
3. Weigh the flour, sugar, milk powder and salt into the mixer bowl. Weigh the yeast separately. Beat the egg and weigh 45 g. Cut the butter into 4 pieces and leave it to soften to 18–22 °C (it should bend and dent easily without being oily).
4. Grease the pan. Make the egg wash, strain it and refrigerate it.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Combine (t = 0).** Add the yeast to the dry ingredients and stir. Add the tangzhong, milk and egg.
- *Mixer:* speed 1 (low) for 3 min, until no dry flour remains and a rough, sticky dough forms.
- *Hand:* mix in a bowl with a scraper for 2 min until shaggy, then tip onto the bench.

**Step 2 — Develop the gluten (t = 3 to 10 min).**
- *Mixer:* speed 2 (medium-low; about 4 on a KitchenAid) for 5–7 min. The dough starts to pull away from the sides of the bowl and slaps against them. Stretch a piece: it should form a thin, somewhat translucent membrane that tears with a ragged edge. That is moderate development.
- *Hand:* slap-and-fold for 8–10 min. The dough starts very sticky and becomes smooth and elastic. Use a scraper, not extra flour.

**Step 3 — Add butter (t ≈ 10 min).**
- *Mixer:* on speed 1, add the butter one piece at a time, waiting until each is absorbed (about 1 min). The dough will break into slippery pieces and then come back together. Then run speed 2 for 4–6 min.
- *Hand:* spread the butter over the dough, fold it in, squeeze through, then slap-and-fold for 5–8 min. It will be slippery for the first 2–3 min.

**Endpoint:** the dough is smooth, glossy and soft, and it cleans the bowl (the mixer bowl is almost clean at the bottom). A stretched piece forms a **thin, translucent windowpane** that tears with a smooth, round-edged hole. Total mixing time: about 12–15 min by mixer, 15–20 min by hand.

**Step 4 — Check the dough temperature.** Target **26 °C** (acceptable 24–27 °C). If the dough is above 28 °C, bulk will be fast; watch the volume rather than the clock.

**Step 5 — Bulk fermentation (about 60–90 min at 26 °C).** Round the dough, place it in the lightly greased marked container, cover, and keep it at 25–27 °C. No folds are needed. **Done when** the volume has increased by **75–100 %**, the top is domed, and a floured finger pressed 1 cm in leaves a dent that springs back slowly. Enriched soft doughs are normally taken further than lean doughs (compare +50–75 % in [03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)), because you want a light, open-textured, soft crumb rather than an open irregular one.
*Alternative:* bulk 30 min at room temperature, then refrigerate for 8–16 h. Shape cold; proof will take 30–60 min longer.

**Step 6 — Divide and pre-round.** Tip the dough onto a lightly floured bench, degas it gently by pressing flat, and divide it into **4 × 152 g** (±2 g). Round each piece loosely, seam down. Cover and rest for **15 min**, until the balls relax and flatten slightly.

**Step 7 — Shape (4 rolled pieces).** For each piece:
1. Roll into an oval about **25 × 12 cm**, pressing out large bubbles.
2. Fold both long sides to the centre so they overlap slightly, giving a strip about **9 cm wide** and 25 cm long. Press the seam.
3. Roll the strip lightly to even it out, then roll it up tightly from one short end into a cylinder about 9 cm long and 5 cm in diameter. Pinch the seam.
4. Place the cylinder seam-down in the pan, with the spiral facing the long sides of the pan.

The four cylinders sit side by side along the 22 cm length, touching lightly. This rolling gives the loaf its characteristic spiral, feathery crumb, and the tension helps it rise tall.

**Step 8 — Final proof (about 50–80 min at 26–28 °C).** Cover loosely with oiled film. Proof somewhere warm but no higher than 30 °C: an oven with the light on and the door ajar, with a thermometer inside, is fine. **Done when** the highest point of the lobes is about **1 cm below the rim** of a 6–7 cm deep pan, and a gentle poke on the side leaves a dent that springs back slowly. Start preheating the oven about 30 min before you expect to bake.

**Step 9 — Preheat.** **175 °C (350 °F)** conventional (160–165 °C fan), rack in the lower third so the top of the loaf is near the middle of the oven. Preheat at least 20 min, and check with an oven thermometer.

**Step 10 — Egg wash.** Brush a thin coat over the tops with a soft brush, using light strokes and no pooling in the gaps between the lobes. Pooled egg wash glues the lobes together and bakes into dark patches.

**Step 11 — Bake, 30–35 min.** Check at 15–18 min. If the top is already deep golden, tent it loosely with foil. **Done when:** the internal temperature is **88–93 °C** (probe through the side into the centre of a middle lobe), the top is evenly deep golden-brown, and the sides are golden when you tip the loaf out. Pale sides mean the loaf needs 3–5 more minutes, out of the pan, directly on the rack.

**Step 12 — Cool.** Turn out of the pan within 5 min. Otherwise steam condenses on the sides and they soften and wrinkle. Optionally brush the top with melted butter for a softer, shinier crust. Cool on its side on a rack for **at least 1.5 h** before slicing. Store sealed in a bag at room temperature once completely cool. Don't refrigerate it: that speeds staling ([04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md)).

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** The tangzhong sticks and scorches quickly and spits when it thickens. Stir constantly and keep your hand off the rim of the pan. Handle the hot loaf pan with dry gloves. Raw dough contains raw egg and raw flour; don't taste it.

</div>

### Variant: soft dinner rolls

Make the same dough. At Step 6, divide it into **12 × 51 g**, round each piece tightly, and place them in a buttered 20 × 20 cm square pan in 4 rows of 3 (or on a parchment-lined tray, 3 cm apart, for rolls with crust all round). Proof 40–60 min at 26–28 °C until the rolls touch and have increased by about 75 %. Egg wash, then bake at **180 °C (355 °F)** for 18–22 min to 88–92 °C internal. Brush with butter as they come out.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tangzhong endpoint | 65–70 °C, thick pudding with trails; mass topped up to 126 g | Below ~60 °C little starch gelatinizes; lost water changes hydration |
| Butter addition | After moderate development (Step 2 endpoint) | Butter added early coats proteins and the dough takes much longer to develop |
| Final mix | Thin translucent windowpane, glossy, 24–27 °C | Under-mixed soft dough gives a lower loaf with a coarse, cottony crumb |
| End of bulk | +75–100 % | Under: tight, lower loaf. Over: weak, sour, pale |
| Shaping | 4 × 152 g, tight cylinders, seam down | Equal pieces rise evenly; the rolling creates the spiral crumb |
| Final proof | Top ~1 cm below the rim | Under: torn sides, dense base. Over: collapse, wrinkled top |
| Oven | 175 °C, lower third | Hotter ovens burn a 10 %-sugar, milk and egg crust before the centre sets |
| Internal temperature | 88–93 °C | Below 88 °C: gummy centre, sides cave in. Well above 93 °C: dry crumb |
| Demoulding | Within 5 min | Trapped steam makes the sides soft and wrinkled |

## Common failures

- Top dark before the centre is done → [crust too dark before the inside is done](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=crust-too-dark-before-the-inside-is-done)
- Sides cave in or loaf sinks as it cools → [loaf collapses or caves in](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=loaf-collapses-or-caves-in)
- Torn, ragged sides where the lobes meet the pan → [torn or burst sides](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=torn-or-burst-sides)

- Slow rise → [slow or no rise in sweet dough](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=slow-or-no-rise-in-sweet-dough)

## Scientific explanation

Cooking 7 % of the flour with five times its weight of water to 65–70 °C gelatinizes much of that flour's starch: the granules take up water, swell and lose their crystalline order, and the paste thickens ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md)). Gelatinized starch binds far more water than native starch at dough temperature, so the dough can carry 74 % true hydration while handling like a lower-hydration dough, and the baked crumb holds more water. The result is a softer, moister crumb that feels fresh for longer, even though the staling mechanisms (starch retrogradation and moisture redistribution) still run ([06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md), [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md)). Sugar at 10 % feeds the yeast early and then slows it slightly by osmotic stress, which is why the yeast is 1 % rather than the 0.8 % of the lean loaf. Butter added after the gluten has formed lubricates and shortens the strands, giving a tender, easily torn crumb. Egg proteins and yolk lecithin reinforce the structure and refine the crumb. Lactose from the milk and milk powder is not fermented by baker's yeast, so it remains, with the residual sucrose, to brown the crust. That is why the oven runs at 175 °C and the loaf is pulled at 88–93 °C, before the crust over-browns and the crumb dries.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tangzhong flour | 7 → 0 % (all flour raw, same water) | Stickier dough, slightly more volume, noticeably firmer crumb by day 2–3 ([X1-16](experiments/X1-16-tangzhong.md)) |
| Tangzhong flour | 7 → 10 % (water 5× the flour; reduce milk to keep true hydration) | Softer, moister, slightly denser, more "mochi"-like crumb |
| Tangzhong → yudane | 15 % flour scalded with 15 % boiling water; reduce milk to keep true hydration | Similar softness, slightly chewier, more pronounced sweetness |
| Sugar | 10 → 5 % | Faster rise, paler crust, less tender. A good sandwich loaf |
| Butter | 10 → 5 % | Slightly higher volume, less tender, stales sooner |
| Liquid | Milk → water (add milk powder to 6 %) | Nearly identical loaf; easier to standardize |
| Egg | 15 → 0 % (replace with 39 g milk, which carries the same 34 g of water) | Whiter, slightly less rich crumb, paler crust (wash with milk instead) |
| Pullman lid | Bake covered for 25 min, then uncovered 5–10 min | Square shokupan-style loaf with a tighter, more even crumb |
