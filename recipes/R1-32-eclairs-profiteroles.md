# R1-32 · Éclairs and profiteroles with craquelin

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: coffee éclairs glazed with fondant, and craquelin-topped profiteroles filled with crème diplomate, assembled from course components at standard professional proportions.

## Objective

Produce two finished choux products from one batch of paste, to a specification: straight, evenly cracked éclairs of equal length and weight, filled by weight and glazed with a thin, glossy fondant; and round, uniform profiteroles under a fine craquelin crackle. Practise piping to weight, controlling the surface, baking to dryness, filling and glazing, and scheduling so the shells are crisp when eaten.

## Expected result

- **8 coffee éclairs**, 13–14 cm long after baking, about 3.5–4 cm wide and 3.5–4 cm tall, straight, with fine parallel cracks, deep golden-brown. Each filled with 40–50 g coffee pastry cream and topped with a smooth, opaque, glossy coffee fondant in an even strip with clean edges. About 85–95 g each.
- **24 profiteroles**, about 5 cm across and 4 cm tall, round and symmetrical under an even, fine-crackled brown craquelin, each filled with 18–20 g crème diplomate and dusted with icing sugar.
- Shells crisp when eaten; filling cold; no soggy base.

## Formula

### Components

| Component | Recipe | Quantity to make | Used here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choux paste | [R1-31](recipes/R1-31-pate-a-choux.md), one batch | ~740 g | 360 g éclairs + 336 g profiteroles |
| Crème pâtissière | [R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md), one batch, made the day before | ~750 g | 350 g for éclairs (flavoured with coffee) + 400 g for diplomate |
| Crème diplomate | In [R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md) (400 g pastry cream + 200 g whipped cream + gelatin) | ~600 g | 24 × 18–20 g = 430–480 g |
| Craquelin | Below | 160 g | 24 discs (plus spares) |
| Coffee fondant | Below | ~280 g | ~15 g per éclair; the rest is dipping depth |

### Craquelin

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain / all-purpose flour | 100 | 50 |
| Light brown sugar | 120 | 60 |
| Unsalted butter, soft (18–20 °C) | 100 | 50 |
| **Total** | **320** | **160** |

Ratio butter : sugar : flour = 1 : 1.2 : 1. Rolled at 1.5 mm it gives 40 or more discs of 3.5 cm after rerolling scraps; freeze the spares for another batch.

### Coffee filling for the éclairs

| Ingredient | % of pastry cream | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Crème pâtissière (R1-26), cold | 100 | 350 |
| Instant coffee | 1.4 | 5 |
| Hot water, to dissolve | 1.4 | 5 |
| **Total** | **102.8** | **360** |

### Coffee fondant

| Ingredient | % of fondant | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Pâtissier's fondant (ready-made) | 100 | 250 |
| 1:1 sugar syrup ([R1-28](recipes/R1-28-sugar-syrups.md)) | 6–10 | 15–25 |
| Instant coffee (or 6–8 g coffee extract, and omit the water) | 1.6 | 4 |
| Hot water, to dissolve the coffee | 1.6 | 4 |
| **Total** | **109.2–113.2** | **273–283** |

**Chocolate alternative** for the éclairs: a dipping ganache of 100 g dark chocolate (55–70 %), 100 g cream (35 %) and 10 g glucose syrup or honey; heat the cream to a simmer, pour over the chopped chocolate, wait 1 min, stir from the centre until glossy, and use at **30–35 °C**. The mechanism is in [16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md) and [R1-36](recipes/R1-36-ganache.md). With a chocolate glaze, use a chocolate pastry cream (60 g dark chocolate stirred into the 350 g pastry cream while it is still hot when you make R1-26, in place of the coffee).

### Piece specification

| Piece | Tip | Raw paste | Baked shell (approx.) | Filling | Finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Éclair | 16–18 mm French star | 12–13 cm long, **45 g** | 25–29 g | 40–50 g coffee pastry cream | ~15 g fondant |
| Profiterole | 12–14 mm plain | 3.5 cm, **14 g** + 3.5 cm craquelin disc | 9–11 g | 18–20 g diplomate | Icing sugar |

**Yield:** 8 éclairs and 24 profiteroles. **×0.5:** make a full R1-31 batch anyway (a half batch is harder to control) and freeze half the piped pieces raw. **×2:** two separate batches of R1-31 are more reliable than one double batch by hand; the craquelin and fondant scale directly.

## Equipment

- Everything in [R1-31](recipes/R1-31-pate-a-choux.md) and [R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md).
- 16–18 mm French star tip; 12–14 mm plain tip; 5–6 mm plain tip for making holes; 6–8 mm plain or Bismarck tip for filling; 2–3 piping bags.
- Two flat baking trays; parchment or perforated mats; pencil and ruler.
- Rolling pin; two sheets of parchment; 1.5 mm rolling guides (or two stacked 1 mm guides trimmed, or careful measurement); 3.5 cm round cutter.
- Small bowl for fondant over a pan of barely simmering water; probe thermometer.
- Skewer; wire rack; fine sieve for icing sugar.

## Mise en place

**Day before**

- Make [R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md) and chill it in its tray.
- **Craquelin:** mix the soft butter and brown sugar with a spatula until smooth (about 1 min), add the flour and mix just until a uniform dough forms. Roll between two sheets of parchment to **1.5 mm** (1–2 mm), slide onto a tray and freeze flat for at least 1 h (it keeps frozen 1 month).
- Draw guides on the underside of two sheets of parchment: 8 éclair lanes 12.5 cm long, 5 cm apart, on one; 24 circles of 3.5 cm, 4 cm apart, on the other.

**On the day**

- Oven at **200 °C (390 °F)** conventional or **180 °C (355 °F)** fan, middle shelf, preheated 30 min.
- Cut 24 craquelin discs from the frozen sheet with the 3.5 cm cutter; keep them frozen on parchment.
- Prepare egg wash from the leftover egg of R1-31 with a pinch of salt.

## Procedure

### Paste, piping and baking

1. **Make the paste** following [R1-31](recipes/R1-31-pate-a-choux.md), steps 1–10. Record the panade loss, egg weight and paste temperature.
2. **Pipe the éclairs.** French star tip, bag at 45°, tip 1 cm above the tray. Pipe 8 éclairs of **12–13 cm**, keeping even pressure and speed; stop squeezing, then flick back over the end. Pipe the first two on the tray on the scale: **45 g ± 2 g**. Tap down the ends with a wet finger. Brush very lightly with egg wash, without letting it run onto the parchment.
3. **Pipe the profiteroles.** Plain tip, bag vertical, 1 cm above the tray. Pipe 24 balls of **3.5 cm, 14 g ± 2 g**, flicking off sideways. Flatten any points with a wet finger. Lay a **frozen craquelin disc** centred on each. Put the tray in the **freezer** while the éclairs bake.
4. **Bake the éclairs.** Conventional: **200 °C for 15 min**, then **170 °C (340 °F)** for **20–30 min** (total 35–45 min). Fan: **175–180 °C** constant, **30–40 min**. **Do not open the door for the first 25 min.** For the last 5 min, prop the door open 1–2 cm with a wooden spoon.
5. **Check doneness:** deep golden-brown including in the cracks, sides rigid, light. The tray of 8 lost about **35–45 %** of its 360 g of paste (baked shells ~200–235 g). If in doubt, give 5 more minutes at 170 °C: an over-dry éclair is still good; an underbaked one collapses.
6. **Vent.** Pierce two holes in the base of each éclair with a skewer; cool on a rack for at least 30 min. Bring the oven back to 200 °C (180 °C fan).
7. **Bake the profiteroles** from the freezer (craquelin discs in place). Conventional: **200 °C for 12 min**, then **170 °C** for **17–22 min** (total 29–34 min). Fan: **170–175 °C**, **28–32 min**. Door closed for the first 20 min. **Done:** craquelin evenly brown and crackled, shell golden under it, rigid, bake loss 35–45 %. Pierce one hole in each base; cool on a rack.

### Fillings

8. **Coffee pastry cream.** Dissolve the instant coffee in the hot water and let it cool. Beat the 350 g cold pastry cream for **20–40 s** until smooth, then whisk in the coffee. Taste and adjust. Bag with the 6–8 mm or Bismarck tip.
9. **Diplomate.** Make the crème diplomate from the remaining 400 g of pastry cream following [R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md), steps 12–16. It begins to set within 30–60 min: make it just before filling. Bag with the second filling tip.

### Filling

10. **Holes.** Enlarge the vent holes in each éclair to 3 holes along the base with the 5–6 mm tip, twisting it in; one hole in each profiterole.
11. **Fill the éclairs by weight.** Tare an éclair on the scale, insert the tip in the end hole and fill until cream appears at the middle hole, then the other end. **Target: 40–50 g.** Scrape the base flat.
12. **Fill the profiteroles:** **18–20 g** diplomate each, until you feel resistance and cream shows at the hole. Stand them craquelin up. Refrigerate while you glaze.

### Glazing

13. **Warm the fondant.** Put 250 g fondant in a small bowl over barely simmering water and stir until it reaches **35 °C**. Add the dissolved coffee, then the syrup **5 g at a time**, until the fondant coats the back of a spoon opaquely and just flows off it. **Keep it at 35–37 °C; never above 38 °C.**
14. **Dip each éclair.** Hold it base up at an angle, dip the top into the fondant to about one third of its height, lift it out vertically and let the excess run back for 2–3 s, then run a clean finger around the edge of the glaze to cut it off cleanly. Turn upright. Re-warm the fondant (35–37 °C) every 2–3 éclairs, adding a gram or two of syrup only if it thickens after warming.
15. **Set** the glazed éclairs for 5 min at room temperature (the fondant sets glossy in 1–2 min), then refrigerate.
16. **Finish the profiteroles:** just before serving, dust lightly with icing sugar through a fine sieve.
17. **Serve** within **1–4 h** of filling, cold (the fondant and pastry cream at ≤ 5 °C until service; take them out 10–15 min before for the best texture).

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Filled choux are high-risk foods: keep at **≤ 5 °C**, total time out of the refrigerator under 2 h, eat within 24 h ([FDA-FoodCode]). Gelatin in the diplomate is animal-derived. Hot panade and steam from pierced shells burn. Allergens: gluten, egg, milk.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paste | All [R1-31](recipes/R1-31-pate-a-choux.md) CCPs; V test | Everything downstream depends on it |
| Piece weight | Éclairs 45 ± 2 g; profiteroles 14 ± 2 g | Uniform bake and uniform product |
| Craquelin | 1–2 mm, frozen, same diameter as the piece | Even crackle and round rise; thick discs flatten the piece |
| Egg wash | Thin; none on the tray | Wash on the tray glues the base and causes lopsided rise |
| Oven door | Closed ≥ 20–25 min | Unset walls collapse |
| Doneness | Colour in the cracks; rigid; bake loss 35–45 % | Wet walls collapse and go soggy fast |
| Venting | Pierce bases immediately | Steam inside condenses and softens the shell |
| Filling | By weight; cold filling into cold shells | Consistent product; warm filling softens shells |
| Fondant | 35–37 °C, thinned with syrup | Above ~38 °C the glaze sets dull and brittle |
| Time and temperature after filling | ≤ 5 °C; served within 1–4 h of filling | Moisture migration; food safety |

## Scientific explanation

The French star tip and the craquelin both manage where the choux surface gives way under steam pressure ([14.2](stage-1/module-14/lesson-02.md)). The star tip's ridges are parallel lines of weakness, so the éclair cracks finely along its length instead of bursting at one point. The craquelin disc, a sweet crumble dough, is cohesive and heavy enough to hold the surface of the ball together while it expands; it cracks into a fine, even pattern and keeps the rise symmetrical, and it shades the top so the paste stays extensible slightly longer. Its sugar and butter brown into a crisp crust.

After baking, the shell is a dry, porous structure of set egg protein and dried starch gel, with very low water activity. Pastry cream and diplomate have high water activity. Water migrates from filling to shell until they approach equilibrium, softening the shell over hours, faster at room temperature; hence filling late, drying the shells hard and keeping them cold ([X1-43](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md) measures the same effect in tarts).

Fondant is a dense mass of tiny sucrose crystals in saturated syrup ([13.1](stage-1/module-13/lesson-01.md), [SC-13](stage-1/science/sc-13-crystallization.md)). Warming to 35–37 °C makes it fluid without dissolving the crystals; thinning with syrup adjusts viscosity without heat. Overheated, the finest crystals dissolve and the sugar recrystallizes onto fewer, larger crystals as it cools: the glaze sets dull and brittle.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tip | Plain → French star (or fork marks) | Random cracks → fine parallel cracks, straighter éclairs |
| Craquelin thickness | 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 mm | Uneven cracks → fine crackle → heavier crust → flattened, dense pieces |
| Craquelin sugar | Brown → white; part flour replaced by cocoa | Darker, more toffee → paler; chocolate craquelin |
| Oven profile | See [X1-38](experiments/X1-38-choux-oven.md) | Height, colour, dryness, collapse |
| Filling | Pastry cream → diplomate → Chantilly | Firmest, longest-lasting → lighter → lightest, shortest life |
| Filling time before serving | 1 h → 4 h → overnight | Crisp → slightly softened → soft and chewy |
| Fondant temperature | 30 → 36 → 45 °C | Thick, heavy coat → thin, glossy → dull, brittle |
| Glaze | Fondant → ganache | Sweet, opaque, firm → richer, softer, less sweet ([R1-36](recipes/R1-36-ganache.md)) |

## Experiment suggestions

- Run [X1-38 Choux: oven temperature and venting](experiments/X1-38-choux-oven.md) with profiteroles piped and frozen from this recipe.
- Craquelin thickness: 0 (egg wash), 1, 2 and 3 mm discs on 14 g balls from one paste, baked on one tray; measure height, diameter and roundness (height ÷ diameter).
- Soggy clock: fill 6 profiteroles at once; cut one open at 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 and 24 h (kept at ≤ 5 °C) and score shell crispness 0–3.
- Fondant temperature: glaze three éclairs at 32, 36 and 42 °C; photograph the gloss at 10 min and 4 h.
