# R1-22 · Flaky pie dough and fruit pie

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: an all-butter flaky pie dough and a cornstarch-thickened blueberry filling, in proportions within the ranges of standard professional and home practice. The formulation is written to make two control variables visible: butter piece size (flakiness) and starch percentage plus centre temperature (filling set).

## Objective

Make a double-crust fruit pie with a flaky, tender, fully baked crust (including the bottom) and a filling that holds a clean slice after cooling. You will keep butter in discrete flakes from mixing to oven, calculate the thickener from the fruit weight, and bake to the correct endpoint: thick bubbling in the centre.

## Expected result

- **Crust:** deep golden top with visible flaky layers at the edges and vents; bottom crust golden and crisp, not pale or wet; tender, shatters under a fork, not chewy.
- **Filling:** glossy, fruit pieces intact, juices set to a soft gel that holds a slice with only slight slumping after 3–4 hours of cooling. Bright, not cloudy or gummy.
- **Pie:** 23 cm, about 1.6 kg, 8 slices.

## Formula

### Flaky pie dough (double crust)

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain / all-purpose flour (9.5–11.5 % protein) | 100 | 300 |
| Unsalted butter, cold (4–7 °C) | 70 | 210 |
| Fine salt | 2 | 6 |
| Caster sugar | 2 | 6 |
| Ice water (1–4 °C) | 28 | 84 |
| **Total** | **202** | **606** |

**Yield:** 606 g → bottom crust 320 g, top crust 286 g. Hydration counts added water only (28 %); hold back 15 g and add only if needed.

### Blueberry filling (percentages on fruit weight)

| Ingredient | % of fruit | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Blueberries, fresh (or frozen, not thawed) | 100 | 900 |
| Granulated sugar | 16.7 | 150 |
| Cornstarch (fresh fruit 4 %; frozen fruit 5 % = 45 g) | 4 | 36 |
| Lemon juice | 1.7 | 15 |
| Finely grated lemon zest | 0.2 | 2 |
| Fine salt | 0.1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **122.7** | **1,104** |

For other fruit, use the thickener table in [11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md): for example, 900 g sliced apples need 14–27 g cornstarch (1.5–3 %), 900 g sour cherries 45 g (5 %).

### Finish

Egg wash: 1 egg (50 g) beaten with a pinch of salt. Coarse or demerara sugar: 10 g.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 dough (303 g) makes a single 23 cm bottom crust or a 26 cm free-form galette with half the filling (552 g). ×2 = two pies: 600 g flour, 420 g butter, 12 g salt, 12 g sugar, 168 g water (1,212 g dough); 1,800 g fruit, 300 g sugar, 72 g cornstarch, 30 g lemon juice, 4 g zest, 2 g salt (2,208 g filling).

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g (0.1 g useful for starch); probe thermometer.
- Large bowl and pastry blender, or food processor, or bench scraper and fingers.
- Rolling pin; ruler; bench scraper; small sharp knife.
- 23 cm pie plate, about 4 cm deep (glass lets you see the base; metal browns faster; ceramic is slowest).
- Heavy baking tray (preheated in the oven, to catch drips and give bottom heat); foil for shielding.

## Mise en place

- Cut the butter into 1.5–2 cm cubes; refrigerate 20 minutes, or freeze 10 minutes (target 4–7 °C).
- Ice water with ice cubes; weigh 84 g at the moment of use.
- Pick over and weigh the fruit. Calculate and record the cornstarch percentage you are using.

## Procedure

### Day 1: dough

1. **Dry mix.** Whisk flour, salt and sugar in a large bowl.
2. **Cut in the butter.** Toss the butter cubes in the flour. Press each cube flat between thumb and fingers, or cut in with a pastry blender, or pulse 4–6 × 1 s in a processor. **Endpoint:** flour looks slightly coarse and yellowish; butter pieces from **pea-sized to hazelnut-sized (5–10 mm)**, most flattened into flakes. Mixture ≤ 12 °C. Stop earlier rather than later.
3. **Add water.** Sprinkle about 70 g of the ice water over the mixture while tossing with a fork (or pulse 3–4 times). Squeeze a handful: **endpoint: it holds together when pressed, with some dry crumbs remaining in the bowl.** Add the remaining water 5 g at a time if needed. Record the total.
4. **Gather.** Tip onto the bench and press into a rough rectangle with the scraper. It will look shaggy with visible butter streaks. That is correct.
5. **Fold (for layers).** Roll the rectangle to about 1.5 cm thick, fold into thirds like a letter, rotate 90°, roll lightly and fold again. Two folds at most. If the butter softens, chill 15 minutes between folds.
6. **Divide and rest.** Divide into **320 g** (bottom) and **286 g** (top). Flatten each into a disc 2–2.5 cm thick, wrap and refrigerate **at least 2 hours, ideally overnight** (keeps 2 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen).

### Day 2: pie

7. **Oven.** Rack in the lowest position with a heavy tray on it. Preheat to **220 °C (430 °F)** still (205 °C fan) for at least 30 minutes.
8. **Roll the bottom.** Temper the 320 g disc 5 minutes. Roll on a floured bench to **3–4 mm** and about **31 cm**, quarter-turning often. Ease it into the plate without stretching, letting it settle into the corner. Leave a 1.5–2 cm overhang. Refrigerate while you make the filling.
9. **Filling.** Whisk the sugar, cornstarch, salt and zest together (this separates the starch grains so they do not lump), toss with the fruit and lemon juice. Frozen fruit: use straight from the freezer and 5 % starch. Mix just before filling so the juice does not collect.
10. **Roll the top** to **3–4 mm** and about 28 cm. Cut 5–6 vents of 2–3 cm, or cut 2.5 cm strips for a lattice.
11. **Assemble.** Pour the filling into the bottom crust and level it (it should be mounded slightly in the centre). Lay on the top crust. Trim both crusts to 1.5 cm overhang, fold the edges under together so they rest on the rim, and crimp. Chill the pie **20–30 minutes** (until the dough is firm).
12. **Finish.** Brush the top thinly with egg wash (avoid pooling in the crimp), sprinkle with coarse sugar.
13. **Bake, hot.** Place the pie on the preheated tray. **20 minutes at 220 °C** until the crust is set and starting to colour.
14. **Bake, lower.** Reduce to **190 °C (375 °F)** still (175 °C fan) and bake **40–50 minutes** more. Shield the edge with a foil ring if it is dark before the end. **Endpoint:** crust deep golden all over; through the vents, the filling **bubbles thickly and slowly in the centre**, not just at the edge. Centre filling ≥ 95 °C if you probe it. Record total time.
15. **Cool** on a rack **at least 3–4 hours**, to below 30 °C at the centre, before cutting.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature and size | 4–7 °C; 5–10 mm flattened flakes | Solid flakes become steam-lifted layers; soft butter smears and gives a mealy or greasy crust |
| Dough temperature | ≤ 15 °C at every stage | Keeps flakes intact; chill whenever the butter looks shiny |
| Water | 25–30 % of flour, recorded | More gluten above this: tough, shrinks; below: cracks, falls apart |
| Rest | ≥ 2 h, ideally overnight | Relaxes gluten, evens hydration, firms butter |
| Starch dose | Per fruit type and state (here 4 % fresh, 5 % frozen) | Juice volume varies with fruit; too little runs, too much is gummy |
| Bottom heat | Lowest rack, preheated tray, 220 °C start | Bakes the base before the filling soaks it |
| Endpoint | Thick bubbling at centre vents; ≥ 95 °C | Starch only thickens fully near boiling; centre is last to get there |
| Cooling | 3–4 h | The starch gel sets as it cools |

## Scientific explanation

**Crust.** Flattened cold butter pieces divide the dough into thin layers separated by fat. Rolling and the two folds stretch the flakes into sheets and stack them. In the hot oven the dough between the flakes starts to set while the butter melts; the butter's own water (about 16 %) and water from the adjacent dough flash to steam, pushing the layers apart, and the melted fat fries the thin leaves crisp ([11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md), [SC-11](stage-1/science/sc-11-phase-changes-gases.md)). The high starting temperature (220 °C) makes this happen quickly, before the butter can simply melt out. Flour not coated by fat forms some gluten with the water, which holds the layers together; that is why pie dough needs more water than brisée and a proper rest.

**Filling.** As the fruit heats, its cells rupture and sugar draws juice out osmotically. Cornstarch granules suspended in that juice absorb water and swell as they reach gelatinization temperature, which the dissolved sugar raises; full thickening in a fruit filling needs close to boiling ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md)). The centre of a deep pie is the last point to heat, so bubbling there is the evidence that all the starch has thickened. On cooling, amylose chains re-associate and the paste sets further into a soft gel. The lemon juice adds brightness; its acid weakens cornstarch slightly under long heating, which the 4 % dose already allows for.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Butter piece size | Fine → walnut-sized | Fine: tender, mealy, no layers; large: very flaky, higher lift, less tender ([X1-31](experiments/X1-31-pie-fat-size.md)) |
| Number of folds | 0 → 2 | More, more regular layers; beyond 2–3, butter smears |
| Water | 24 → 32 % | Easier to roll; tougher, more shrinkage |
| Fat | Part shortening (up to 50 % of fat) | More tender and flaky, easier to handle (wider plastic range); less butter flavour |
| Cornstarch | 3 → 6 % of fruit | Looser to firmer set; above ~6 % pasty and cloudy |
| Thickener type | Cornstarch → tapioca | Clearer, glossier, freeze-stable; soak coarse tapioca 15 min |
| Pre-cooking the juice | Macerate, drain, boil juice with starch | Reliable set independent of bake; less boil-over |
| Start temperature | 200 → 230 °C | Hotter: more lift, better base; edges brown faster |
