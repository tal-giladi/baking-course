# R1-02 · Lean rolls and pan loaf

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established lean-dough technique. It uses the same system as [R1-01](recipes/R1-01-lean-loaf.md) at a slightly lower hydration, so the dough is easy to divide and shape and holds a defined form in a pan or on a tray.

## Objective

Divide one lean dough by weight and shape it two ways: one tin-baked pan loaf and a set of free-standing rolls. Practise dividing to ±2 %, rolling tight balls, and judging pan proof by the crown height.

## Expected result

- **Pan loaf:** in a 22 × 11 cm pan (~7 cm deep). Evenly domed top rising about 3–5 cm above the rim, golden-brown all over including the sides and base. Fine, even crumb with no large holes or dense streak. Slices cleanly.
- **Rolls:** 5 round rolls, ~7–8 cm across, uniform in size (±2 g baked), crisp thin crust, soft open crumb.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (11.5–13 % protein) | 100.0 | 600 |
| Water | 65.0 | 390 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 12 |
| Instant yeast | 1.0 | 6 |
| **Total** | **168.0** | **1008** |

**Division:** pan loaf 650 g + 5 rolls × 68 g (340 g) = 990 g. The remaining ~18 g (1.8 %) allows for dough lost in the bowl and on the bench. Alternatively make **14 rolls × 70 g** (980 g) or **15 rolls × 65 g** (975 g).
**Hydration:** 65 %. Lower than R1-01, so the dough is firmer, easier to shape and holds a tight roll.
**Yeast alternatives:** fresh yeast 18 g (3.0 %) or active dry 7–8 g (~1.2 %). Conversions depend on the brand.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 300 / 195 / 6 / 3 g = 504 g → 7 rolls × 70 g, or one small pan loaf only. ×2 = 1200 / 780 / 24 / 12 g = 2016 g → 2 pan loaves + 10 rolls.

## Equipment

Scale (1 g, and 0.1 g for yeast), probe thermometer, straight-sided container (3 L), bench scraper, **loaf pan 22 × 11 cm, ~7 cm deep** (metal, lightly greased), baking tray with parchment, spray bottle or steam tray, cooling rack, lame. Optional: stand mixer.

## Mise en place

1. Calculate the water temperature for a **DDT of 25 °C** ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)). This firmer dough gains more friction heat when mixed by machine, so use your measured friction factor.
2. Grease the pan with a thin film of oil or butter. Line the tray with parchment.
3. Plan the oven: the rolls and pan loaf both bake with the oven at 230 °C (445 °F) at the start. The pan loaf then finishes at 210 °C (410 °F). One oven is enough if you shape the rolls ~15 min after the pan loaf, so the rolls proof faster and go in first (see Step 9).

## Procedure

1. **Mix.** Combine the flour, yeast and 380 g of the water to a shaggy mass (1–2 min). Rest 15 min. Add the salt and the remaining 10 g water.
   - *Stand mixer:* speed 1 for 2 min, then speed 2 for 5–7 min, until the dough is smooth, clears the bowl, and stretches into a thin, mostly translucent membrane. This is good development, more than R1-01, because an even, fine crumb in a pan loaf needs it.
   - *By hand:* 8–10 min of slap-and-fold to the same endpoint, or 5 min plus one extra fold during bulk.
2. **Dough temperature:** 24–26 °C. Record it.
3. **Bulk fermentation, about 1–1.5 h at 24–25 °C**, in a marked, straight-sided container. Do one set of folds at 30 min. The end point is **+60–75 % volume**, a domed surface, and a dough that feels airy and holds its shape. At 1 % yeast this dough is faster than R1-01, so check from 50 min.
4. **Divide** on an unfloured bench: one 650 g piece and five 68 g pieces, each ±1 g for the rolls and ±10 g for the loaf. Use at most one top-up piece per portion and tuck it into the centre.
5. **Pre-shape.** Make a loose round of the 650 g piece. Roll the small pieces into loose balls. Cover.
6. **Bench rest, 15–20 min**, until the pieces have relaxed and slightly flattened.
7. **Shape the pan loaf.** Press it into a rectangle ~22 × 30 cm. Roll it up tightly from a short side, sealing with the heel of your hand at each turn, into a cylinder ~20–21 cm long. Place it seam-down in the pan with the ends touching the pan's ends. Press it lightly flat.
8. **Shape the rolls**, about 15 min after the pan loaf. Cup your hand over each piece and circle it on unfloured bench until it forms a tight smooth ball (5–10 circles). Set them seam-down on the parchment, **4 cm apart**.
9. **Final proof at 24–25 °C, covered.**
   - Rolls: **35–50 min**, until about 1.5–1.75× their volume. The poke test should give a slow, partial spring-back.
   - Pan loaf: **45–70 min**, until the crown is **1–2 cm above the rim**. The poke test should give a slow, partial spring-back.
   - Preheat the oven to **230 °C (445 °F)**, with a steam tray on the lowest rack, at least 30 min before the rolls are due.
10. **Score and bake the rolls.** Optionally cut a single 5 mm slash or a cross. Load the tray, pour ~100 g boiling water into the steam tray (or spray the oven walls), and close the door. Bake **15–18 min at 230 °C (445 °F)**, removing the steam tray after 8 min, until golden-brown and **≥95 °C internal**. Cool on a rack.
11. **Bake the pan loaf.** Optionally score it with one central lengthwise cut 5 mm deep. Load it into the oven at **230 °C (445 °F)** with fresh steam. After 10 min, lower to **210 °C (410 °F)** and bake a further 25–30 min, about 35–40 min in total. Turn it out and check that the **internal temperature is ≥96 °C** and that the sides and base are golden. If the sides are pale, return the loaf *out of the pan* to the oven rack for 5 min.
12. **Cool.** Turn the pan loaf out of the pan immediately. Left in the pan, it steams and the sides go soggy. Cool it on a rack **≥1.5 h** before slicing. Rolls need 20–30 min.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Metal pans at 210–230 °C burn through thin tea towels. Use dry oven gloves to turn the loaf out. Keep your face back when pouring water into a hot steam tray.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Piece weight | Rolls ±1 g, loaf ±10 g | Uniform size gives uniform proof and bake |
| Mixing development | Thin, mostly translucent membrane | Fine even crumb in the pan loaf. Tight rolls |
| Dough temperature | 24–26 °C | Keeps the 1 % yeast dough on a 1–1.5 h bulk |
| Bench rest | 15–20 min | Enough relaxation to roll tight without tearing |
| Pan-loaf proof | Crown 1–2 cm above rim | Lower: dense and bursting at the sides. Higher: collapsed or mushroom top, holes under the crust |
| Internal temperature | Rolls ≥95 °C, loaf ≥96 °C | Starch fully set |
| Out of pan immediately | ≤2 min after baking | Prevents soggy sides |

## Common failures

- Pan loaf with a big cavity under the top crust → [large holes under the crust](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=large-holes-under-the-crust-flying-crust)
- Pan loaf splits along one side → [blowouts on the side](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=blowouts-on-the-side)
- Flat, spreading rolls → [loaf spreads sideways](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=loaf-spreads-sideways)
- Dense, tight crumb → [dense, tight crumb](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=dense-tight-crumb)
- Pale pan-loaf sides or a burnt top → [bread-baking](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md)

## Scientific explanation

At 65 % hydration there is less free water, so the dough is firmer and more elastic ([02.3](stage-1/module-02/lesson-03.md)). It holds a tightly rolled shape and resists spreading, which suits free-standing rolls, and it yields a finer crumb. More mixing gives a more fully developed, uniform gluten network that stretches evenly into many small cells rather than a few large ones. In the pan, the walls stop the dough spreading, so all the expansion goes upward. Proofing to a defined crown height therefore stands in for the poke test. The pan also slows heat transfer to the sides and base compared with a hearth loaf. That is why the pan loaf bakes at a lower finishing temperature for longer: the centre reaches ≥96 °C before the top over-browns, and the sides still colour ([04.1](stage-1/module-04/lesson-01.md)). Rolls have a small mass and a large surface, so they bake quickly and hot.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hydration | 65 → 70 % | More open crumb and softer rolls. Harder to roll tight. Rolls spread more |
| Mixing | Less (R1-01 level) | More irregular crumb in the pan loaf and less volume |
| Roll spacing | 4 cm → 1 cm | Pull-apart rolls with soft sides that join during proof and bake |
| Add 3–5 % oil or butter | — | Softer crumb and crust, slower staling. Now a slightly enriched dough (M06) |
| Pan-loaf dough weight | 650 → 750 g in the same pan | Taller crown. Risk of mushroom top and flying crust if over-proofed |

## Experiment suggestions
