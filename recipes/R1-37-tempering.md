# R1-37 · Tempered chocolate by seeding

<span class="tag established">Established technique</span>

The seeding method is established professional technique, described in standard texts and in chocolate manufacturers' guidance. The quantities and the three applications (discs, dipped items, a moulded bar) are chosen for a home kitchen; the temperatures are standard professional ranges and the manufacturer's printed curve for your chocolate takes precedence.

## Objective

Temper 500 g of dark couverture by seeding, verify temper with a test strip, keep it in temper for 20–30 minutes of work, and produce dipped items, discs on acetate and a moulded bar that are glossy, snap cleanly and release from acetate and mould.

## Expected result

- Test strip sets at ~20 °C in **3–5 min**: even gloss, no streaks, releases from parchment, snaps.
- Discs on acetate: mirror-glossy underside, clean release, a crisp, audible snap when broken, no fingerprints from brief handling.
- Moulded bar: visibly shrinks from the mould edges and drops out when the mould is turned over; glossy face.
- Dipped items: thin, even coat with a small, neat foot; glossy; no streaks after 24 h.
- No bloom after 7 days at a stable 15–20 °C.

## Formula

| Ingredient | % of total | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Dark couverture 55–70 %, to melt | 75 | 375 |
| Dark couverture, same product, **tempered** (callets or finely chopped from a glossy bar): the seed | 25 | 125 |
| **Total** | **100** | **500** |

Plus **extra seed**, 25–50 g, on standby. Seed must be well tempered (glossy, snapping, unbloomed chocolate from the pack).

**Items to dip or decorate** (not part of the formula): 100–150 g candied orange peel strips, dried apricots or toasted whole almonds/hazelnuts (cooled to room temperature); 30–40 g mixed nuts and dried fruit for topping discs (mendiants).

**Yield:** about 450 g of usable chocolate (30–50 g stays on the bowl and tools): ~20 discs of 4 g, 150 g dipped items, one 80–100 g moulded bar, with some left for test strips. Leftover chocolate is set on parchment, broken up and reused (it is already tempered: it can be your next seed).

**Scaling:** 500 g is the practical minimum: smaller masses lose heat too fast to hold at working temperature. ×2 (1 kg) holds temperature better and is easier for dipping. Keep the seed at 20–25 % of the total.

**Milk and white chocolate:** same proportions; different temperatures (below). Follow the pack if it differs.

| | Melt to | Working temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Dark | 45–50 °C | 31–32 °C |
| Milk | 40–45 °C | 29–30 °C |
| White | 40–45 °C | 28–29 °C |

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g); a fast-reading **probe thermometer** (an infrared thermometer reads only the surface: stir first, and confirm with the probe).
- Microwave and a plastic or glass bowl (2 L), or a bain-marie; everything **bone dry**.
- Heatproof spatula; small offset palette knife; bench scraper.
- Dipping fork (or a table fork); parchment paper; acetate sheets or strips (or clean, smooth plastic document sleeves).
- Polycarbonate bar mould (or a small smooth plastic container as a stand-in), polished with cotton wool.
- Piping bag (optional), small spoon; hairdryer (optional) or a heating pad for holding temperature.
- Room thermometer. Work at **18–22 °C**.

## Mise en place

1. Check the room: 18–22 °C, no steam (no kettle, dishwasher or boiling pans nearby).
2. Clean and dry all tools. Polish the mould cavities with cotton wool.
3. Weigh 375 g chocolate into the bowl; weigh 125 g seed and 25–50 g extra seed separately. Chop seed finely if not in callets.
4. Lay parchment and acetate on a tray. Arrange the items to dip (dry, at room temperature) and the mendiant toppings.
5. Record the chocolate's brand, %, and the tempering curve from the pack.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Melt (5–8 min).** Microwave at 50 % power in 30 s bursts, stirring well between bursts (or bain-marie with the heat off, bowl not touching the water). Continue until completely melted and **45–50 °C** throughout (probe after stirring). **Endpoint:** completely fluid, no lumps, 45–50 °C. Do not exceed 50 °C for dark (45 °C for milk and white).

**Step 2 — Seed (1 min).** Add about two thirds of the seed (~85 g) and stir continuously and thoroughly, scraping the sides. The temperature falls quickly.

**Step 3 — Stir down (5–15 min).** Keep stirring with the spatula, folding the chocolate against the bowl, taking a probe reading every minute. When the added seed has mostly melted and the temperature is still above ~34 °C, add the remaining seed (~40 g). **Endpoint:** **31–32 °C** (dark), fluid and glossy. If unmelted seed pieces remain at 32 °C, lift them out with a fork and set them aside (or pulse 5 s with a stick blender). If the chocolate reaches 32 °C with all seed melted, add 10–15 g of the extra seed, stir 1 min and remove any remaining pieces.

**Step 4 — Test (5 min).** Dip the tip of a knife or a strip of parchment, scrape one side, and leave it at ~20 °C. Meanwhile keep stirring the bowl every 30 s.
- **Sets in 3–5 min, glossy, no streaks, snaps:** in temper. Go to step 5.
- **Still tacky at 5–8 min, or sets streaky:** not in temper. Add 25 g extra seed, stir 2 min, re-test.
- **Sets in under 2 min, chocolate thickening:** over-crystallized. Warm 3–5 s (microwave or hairdryer on the surface), stir, re-test.

**Step 5 — Discs and mendiants (5 min).** With a spoon or a piping bag, deposit 4 g drops on acetate, 4 cm apart. Tap the tray to spread them into 3–4 cm discs. Top each with 2–3 pieces of nut and fruit before they set. Leave to set at 15–20 °C.

**Step 6 — Moulded bar (5 min).** Fill a bar cavity to the top, tap the mould firmly on the bench for 30–60 s until no more bubbles rise, and scrape the top level with the scraper. Set at 15–18 °C, or put it in the fridge for 10–15 min once the surface has set (not before), then out to room temperature.

**Step 7 — Dipping (10–15 min).** Stir the chocolate and check it is still at working temperature. Dip each item fully (or two thirds, for peel), lift with the fork, tap the fork on the rim 3–4 times to thin the coat, scrape the base across the rim, and slide onto parchment. **Endpoint:** thin, even coat; small foot; sets glossy within 5 min.

**Step 8 — Keep it in temper throughout.** Stir every 1–2 minutes. Check the temperature every 5 min. As it cools below working temperature and thickens, warm by 1 °C at a time (3–5 s bursts, stirring immediately). Never let dark chocolate pass **32 °C** (milk 30 °C, white 29 °C). Re-test with a strip if in doubt.

**Step 9 — Unmould and store (after 30–60 min).** Peel discs off the acetate. Turn the bar mould over: a tempered bar drops out or releases with a light tap. Store in a closed box at **15–18 °C**, away from odours, not in the fridge.

**Step 10 — Leftovers.** Spread the remaining chocolate thinly on parchment, let it set, break it up and store it: it is your next seed.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** A microwave heats chocolate unevenly and it keeps its shape while molten inside; stir between every burst to avoid scorching and hot spots. Allergens: chocolate may contain milk and soya (lecithin) and is often processed with nuts; nuts used for dipping are declarable allergens.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dryness | No water, no steam | A few drops seize the chocolate |
| Melt temperature | Dark 45–50 °C (milk/white 40–45 °C), never above | Erases all crystals; overheating thickens and scorches, especially milk and white |
| Seed | 20–25 % of total, well-tempered chocolate of the same product | Supplies form V nuclei; wrong or untempered seed gives no temper |
| Working temperature | Dark 31–32 °C, milk 29–30 °C, white 28–29 °C, or the pack's curve | Window where form V survives and unstable forms melt |
| Test strip | Sets 3–5 min at ~20 °C, glossy, snaps | The only proof of temper |
| Room and centres | Room 18–22 °C; items dry, ~20 °C | Cold items shock-set unstable forms and cause condensation; warm rooms slow setting |
| Storage | 15–18 °C, stable, dry | Prevents fat and sugar bloom |

## Scientific explanation

Cocoa butter can crystallize in six forms; only form V (melting ~33–34 °C) gives gloss, snap, contraction from the mould and a clean melt ([16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md), [SC-13](stage-1/science/sc-13-crystallization.md)). Left alone, cooling chocolate nucleates the unstable forms first because they form faster. Melting to 45–50 °C erases every crystal. The seed is chocolate already full of form V crystals; as it partly melts into the warm mass, fragments of form V remain and act as templates. Stirring spreads them through the chocolate and keeps it cooling evenly. At 31–32 °C (dark) form V is stable while forms I–IV melt, so the only crystals present are the ones you want. When the chocolate is deposited and cools further, those crystals template the whole mass into form V: many small, uniform crystals that pack densely (gloss, snap), shrink slightly (release), and resist the slow recrystallization that causes bloom. Milk fat lowers every temperature by about 2–3 °C, which is why milk and white chocolates are worked cooler.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Seed amount | 25 % → 10 % | Slower cooling, may not temper (test strip slow); more stirring |
| Seed amount | 25 % → 35 % | Faster; many unmelted pieces to remove; risk of over-crystallizing |
| Working temperature (dark) | 31 °C → 33–34 °C | Seed melts: slow set, streaks, loss of temper |
| Working temperature (dark) | 31 °C → 28–29 °C | Over-crystallized, thick coats, dull and thick finish |
| Seed quality | Tempered callets → bloomed or untempered chocolate | Temper unreliable or absent |
| Room | 20 °C → 27 °C | Slow set, streaking, weak contraction |
| Setting | 15–18 °C → freezer | Unstable forms, condensation, sugar bloom |
| Chocolate type | Dark → milk/white | Work 2–3 °C cooler; softer set |
