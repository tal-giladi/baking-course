# R1-33 · Puff pastry: classic and blitz

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a classic French puff pastry (pâte feuilletée) with an envelope lock-in and six single turns, and a blitz (rough) puff in which butter chunks are folded into layers without a plaque. Proportions sit in the middle of standard professional ranges. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a classic puff pastry with 729 nominal fat layers, keeping the butter plastic (block 10–15 °C) through every turn, and bake test pieces that rise evenly to at least five times their rolled thickness with straight sides and distinct leaves. Make a blitz puff from the same flour and butter and compare lift, evenness and time.

## Expected result

- **Classic:** about 958 g of pastry. A 3 mm sheet cut into 6 × 6 cm squares rises to roughly **18–30 mm**, straight up, with vertical sides, a flat top and fine, clearly separated leaves visible at the cut edge. Crisp and shattering, deep golden-brown, dry throughout (no doughy centre), tasting of butter and salt, not greasy.
- **Blitz:** about 696 g. Rises to roughly 60–80 % of the classic height, less evenly (some bulges and slightly tilted sides), with coarser, less regular layers. Very good for straws, palmiers, pie tops and bases; not for tall vol-au-vent.

## Formula

### Classic puff pastry

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Détrempe** | | |
| Plain / all-purpose flour (10–11.5 % protein), or 50:50 bread and pastry flour | 100.0 | 400 |
| Water, cold (4–8 °C) | 50.0 | 200 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 8 |
| Unsalted butter, soft (18–20 °C) or melted and cooled | 12.5 | 50 |
| *Détrempe subtotal* | *164.5* | *658* |
| **Beurrage** | | |
| Unsalted butter, ≥ 82 % fat | 75.0 | 300 |
| **Total** | **239.5** | **958** |
| *Optional: white vinegar or lemon juice (not in total)* | *0.5* | *2* |

**Butter:** 350 g total = 87.5 % of flour; beurrage = 300 ÷ 958 ≈ **31 %** of the finished pastry, 300 ÷ 658 ≈ 46 % of the détrempe weight.
**True hydration of the détrempe:** water 200 + butter 50 × 0.16 = 208 g → **52 %**. A firm dough.
**Water range:** flours vary by a few percent in absorption; hold back 20 g of the water and add it only if the dough is dry and crumbly after 1 minute.
**Layers:** envelope lock-in + 6 single turns → 3⁶ = **729** fat layers (convention in [15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)).
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (479 g, plaque 150 g at 14 × 14 cm) is easy to laminate by hand and enough for 12 squares or 20 cheese straws. ×2 is hard to roll by hand; make two blocks instead.

### Blitz (rough) puff pastry

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain / all-purpose flour | 100.0 | 300 |
| Unsalted butter, ≥ 82 % fat, cold (4–7 °C), 2 cm cubes | 80.0 | 240 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 6 |
| Water, ice-cold (1–4 °C) | 50.0 | 150 |
| **Total** | **232.0** | **696** |

**Scaling:** ×0.5 (348 g) works well for a first try.
**Allergens (both):** wheat (gluten), milk (butter).

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g), probe thermometer, ruler, rolling pin (a heavy straight pin, 45–50 cm, is best).
- Rolling guides: 3 mm and 7–8 mm (strips of wood, stacked coins or purpose-made guide sticks) for even sheets.
- Parchment, cling film, pastry brush (dry, for flour; a second one for egg wash), bench scraper.
- A long, very sharp knife (or a pizza wheel), round cutters (7 cm and 5 cm for bouchées).
- Two heavy baking trays, a wire rack.

## Mise en place

1. **Plan the time.** Classic: détrempe (15 min) → rest ≥ 1 h, ideally overnight → plaque (10 min) → lock-in and 6 turns in three pairs with 30 min rests (about 2.5 h) → rest ≥ 1 h (or up to 2 days) → sheet, cut, rest 30 min → bake. Blitz: about 2 h in total.
2. Measure the kitchen temperature. Above 24 °C: chill the flour and water, work quickly and rest more often.
3. Clear a flat shelf in the refrigerator big enough for a 20 × 20 cm block on a tray.

## Procedure

### Classic puff pastry

**1. Détrempe (5 min).** Mix flour and salt. Add the water (and vinegar, if using) and the soft butter. Mix by hand or with the mixer's paddle on low for **1–2 minutes**, only until no dry flour remains and the dough holds together. **Stop before it is smooth and elastic**: a rough, slightly shaggy dough is right; the turns will develop it. Dough temperature about 15–18 °C.

**2. Shape and rest.** Form into a ball, cut a deep cross in the top (the four lobes relax outwards and make the later square easier), wrap, and refrigerate **at least 1 hour, ideally overnight (8–16 h) at 3–5 °C**. A long rest hydrates the flour evenly and relaxes the gluten.

**3. Plaque (10 min).** Lay the 300 g of cold butter in 1 cm slices on parchment, cover, beat with the pin until pliable, then fold the parchment into a **20 × 20 cm** packet and roll the butter to fill it evenly: 326 cm³ ÷ 400 cm² ≈ **8 mm** thick. Refrigerate flat. 10–20 minutes before lock-in, bring it to **12–15 °C**: it should bend without cracking.

**4. Roll the détrempe (5 min).** On a lightly floured bench, open the cross into a square and roll it to about **29 × 29 cm** (about 6–7 mm thick), leaving the centre very slightly thicker than the corners. Brush off excess flour. The dough should be at about 4–10 °C and match the butter's firmness ([15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)).

**5. Envelope lock-in.** Place the butter square on the dough rotated 45°, so its corners point to the middles of the dough's sides (the dough's corners stick out beyond the butter's sides as four triangles). Fold each dough corner to the centre so that they meet and slightly overlap, covering the butter completely. Pinch the seams closed. Tap the block gently all over with the pin to seal the dough to the butter. Result: a sealed parcel about 20 × 20 cm. One fat layer, two dough layers.

**6. Turns 1 and 2 (single turns).**
- Roll the block to about **20 × 60 cm** (about 7 mm), working from the centre outwards, keeping the sides straight and the corners square. Brush off flour.
- Fold in three like a letter: bottom third up, top third down over it, edges aligned. Brush off flour between the layers as you fold. You have a 20 × 20 cm block. **Turn 1.**
- Rotate 90° so the folded edge is on your left. Roll again to 20 × 60 cm and fold in three. **Turn 2.**
- Mark the block with **two fingertip dents**, wrap, refrigerate **30 minutes**.
- Record the block temperature before and after each turn. Target **10–15 °C**; if it passes 16 °C mid-turn, stop, wrap and chill 15 minutes, then finish.

**7. Turns 3 and 4.** Same as step 6, starting with the folded edge on your left. Four dents. Refrigerate **30 minutes**.

**8. Turns 5 and 6.** Same again. Six dents. Wrap and refrigerate **at least 1 hour** (up to 48 h), or freeze (up to 1 month; thaw overnight in the refrigerator).

**Alternative schedule:** 4 double turns (4⁴ = 256 fat layers), rested 30 minutes after each. Faster, and in practice the rise is similar.

**9. Sheet (dough at 6–10 °C).** Roll to **3 mm** for flat items and straws, **4–5 mm** for bouchées and vol-au-vent, using guides. Roll in both directions so the sheet is relaxed in both. Lift the sheet and let it settle on the bench (it will shrink a little); if it shrinks more than 5 %, rest it 15 minutes and roll again.

**10. Cut.** Use a very sharp knife pressed **straight down** (or a straight-down cutter): **do not drag or saw**, which pulls the layers down and seals the edge. For squares, trim all four edges first: a folded edge has no open layers and will not rise. Place pieces on parchment on a tray; for rounds, turn each piece upside down (the cleaner-cut face goes on top).

**11. Rest cut pieces (30 minutes, refrigerated).** This relaxes the gluten stretched by sheeting and cutting, and reduces shrinkage and tilting. Firm, cold pieces also rise straighter.

**12. Finish.**
- **Egg wash** (1 egg + pinch of salt, beaten): only on the **top surface**. Any wash that runs down the cut edge glues the layers and that side will not rise.
- **Docking:** for flat sheets that must stay flat (tart bases, mille-feuille sheets), prick all over with a fork or docker: the holes vent the steam. For maximum rise (squares, vol-au-vent), do not dock.
- Chill 10 minutes after washing.

**13. Bake.** Preheat to **220 °C (430 °F)** conventional, rack in the middle, for at least 20 minutes, with the baking tray inside if possible. Bake at **210 °C (410 °F)** for about **12–15 minutes**, until the rise is complete and the surface golden; then lower to **180–190 °C (355–375 °F)** for another **8–15 minutes** to dry the inside. Do not open the oven in the first 10 minutes.
- **Done:** deep golden-brown on top, sides and base; the pieces are light when lifted; break one open: the inside leaves are dry and crisp, not doughy or translucent.
- Squares 6 × 6 cm at 3 mm: about 20–25 min in total. Cheese straws: 200 °C, 12–16 min.

**14. Cool** on a rack. Puff pastry is best within 6–8 hours; re-crisp at 160 °C for 5 minutes.

### Blitz puff pastry

**1. Mix (5 min).** Toss the cold butter cubes with the flour and salt. Add the ice water and fold with a scraper until a rough, shaggy dough forms in which the **butter cubes stay whole** (you should see chunks 1–2 cm). Do not knead. Temperature ≤ 15 °C.

**2. Rest (20 minutes, refrigerated),** pressed into a rough rectangle 2–3 cm thick, wrapped.

**3. First fold.** On a well-floured bench, roll to about **20 × 50 cm**. It will look like a mess, with butter streaks and loose pieces; scrape up anything that falls off and press it back. Fold in three (single turn). Rotate 90°.

**4. Folds 2–4.** Give a second single turn immediately, then wrap and refrigerate **30 minutes**. Give turns 3 and 4, then rest **at least 1 hour**. After turn 4 the dough should be smooth, with butter visible only as long flat marbling.

**5. Sheet, cut, bake** as for classic (steps 9–14). Blitz tends to lift unevenly: rest cut pieces a full 30 minutes.

**Safety:** A very sharp knife is safer than a dull one on laminated dough because it needs no pressure. Cut away from your hand and keep the tip on the bench. Puff pastry releases a burst of steam when the oven door opens: stand back. Raw flour is not safe to taste ([FDA-flour]).

## Uses

| Product | Sheet | Cut and finish | Bake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cheese straws** (paillettes) | 3 mm | Egg wash, grated hard cheese (e.g. 60 g Parmesan or Comté per 300 g pastry) and paprika pressed in; cut 1.5 × 15 cm strips; twist | 200 °C, 12–16 min |
| **Palmiers** | 3 mm, rolled in sugar instead of flour for the last two turns | Sprinkle more sugar; roll both long sides to the centre; chill; slice 1 cm | 200 °C, 8 min, flip, 5–8 min (sugar caramelizes: watch closely) |
| **Bouchées / vol-au-vent** | 4–5 mm | Two 7 cm rounds per case: one whole (base), one with a 5 cm centre removed (ring); egg-wash the base edge only where the ring sits, place the ring, egg-wash the ring top | 210 °C then 190 °C, 20–25 min; scoop out any soft centre |
| **Flat sheets** (tart bases, mille-feuille) | 2.5–3 mm | Dock well | 200 °C; for mille-feuille, weight with a rack and a second tray (Stage 2 and [M17](stage-1/module-17/README.md)) |
| **Pithivier / galette des rois** (preview) | 3 mm, two discs | Crème d'amande or frangipane ([R1-23](recipes/R1-23-creme-damande.md)) in the centre, sealed, scored | Stage 2 |

**Scraps** (*rognures*): stack them flat, in their original orientation, and press together (never ball them up), rest 30 minutes and roll. They rise less and more irregularly: use them for straws, tart bases and palmiers.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Détrempe mixing | Just combined, rough, not elastic | Turns develop gluten further; an overmixed détrempe fights every turn and shrinks |
| Détrempe rest | ≥ 1 h, ideally overnight, 3–5 °C | Even hydration, relaxed gluten, cold dough |
| Plaque | 20 × 20 cm, 8 mm even, 12–15 °C at lock-in | Even layers; plastic, not brittle |
| Firmness match | Dough and butter resist a fingertip about equally | Butter and dough must thin at the same rate |
| Block temperature during turns | 10–15 °C; rest if > 16 °C | Warm butter smears into the dough; cold butter shatters |
| Rests | 30 min after every two singles (or after each double) | Re-firm butter; relax gluten |
| Rectangle | Straight sides, square corners, flour brushed off | Uneven edges = uneven layers and lopsided rise |
| Cutting | Sharp blade, straight down; trim folded edges | Crushed or folded edges do not rise |
| Rest after cutting | 30 min refrigerated | Prevents shrinkage and tilting |
| Egg wash | Top only | Wash on edges glues layers |
| Oven | 210–220 °C start, then 180–190 °C to dry | Hot start sets leaves while steam is trapped; lower finish dries the inside |

## Scientific explanation

Puff pastry is leavened only by steam. The 729 nominal fat layers of the classic six-turn schedule are thinner at 3 mm than a starch granule ([15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)), so many have merged, but enough continuous films survive to separate the dough leaves and trap the steam produced when the dough's water (52 % true hydration) and the butter's water (about 16 %) reach 100 °C. The hot start sets the outer leaves while the steam pushes them apart; the melted butter fries each leaf crisp; the lower finish drives out the remaining water so the inner leaves dry and set rather than collapsing back into a paste when the pastry cools. The butter in the détrempe (12.5 %) shortens the gluten, making the dough extensible enough to roll through six turns and closer in firmness to the plaque. The optional vinegar is traditional; any effect on gluten at 0.5 % is small, and rest does far more ([Figoni], [Suas]). Blitz puff replaces the single plaque with discontinuous chunks that are flattened into overlapping streaks by the folds: more barriers than pie dough ([11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md)) but less regular than a plaque, so the rise is lower and less even.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Number of turns | 6 → 4 singles | Taller but less even rise, bigger blisters, more leakage; 6 → 8: lower rise, finer and more uniform ([X1-39](experiments/X1-39-puff-folds.md)) |
| Beurrage | 75 → 50 % | Less rise, less flaky, sturdier; 75 → 100 % ("full puff"): more rise and richness, harder to laminate, more leakage risk |
| Butter in détrempe | 12.5 → 0 % | Tougher détrempe, more shrinkage, harder to match firmness |
| Sheet thickness | 3 → 5 mm | Taller pieces, longer bake needed to dry the centre |
| Oven | 210 → 180 °C start | Butter leaks, less rise, paler |
| Fat | Butter → puff pastry margarine | Much easier lamination and higher rise, waxy mouthfeel, loses butter flavour |
| Method | Classic → blitz | Faster; 60–80 % of the rise, less even |
| Lock-in | Envelope → English (butter on 2/3, letter fold) | Doubles the fat-layer count for the same turns (2 × 3⁶ = 1,458) |

## Experiment suggestions

- [X1-39 Number of folds vs puff pastry lift](experiments/X1-39-puff-folds.md): 2, 4, 6 and 8 singles from one block.
- Classic vs blitz, same flour and butter, 6 × 6 cm squares at 3 mm: height, evenness, time spent.
- Edge test: cut one square with a sharp knife straight down, one by dragging a dull knife, one with egg wash run down the sides. Measure height on each side.
