# R1-35 · Danish pastries

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a laminated yeast dough richer than croissant (egg and more milk in the détrempe, 60 % roll-in butter, three single turns), shaped into pinwheels, envelopes, combs and snails and filled with a bake-stable pastry cream, fruit or almond cream, then glazed with apricot nappage. Proportions sit within standard professional ranges for Danish dough. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe, and it follows the French/international pâtisserie style rather than any one national tradition.

## Objective

Laminate an egg-enriched yeast dough with 27 fat layers, shape it into four classic Danish forms, fill them with components that survive the oven, and bake them to a deep golden colour with a crisp, flaky, fully baked base under the filling, then finish with a thin, glossy nappage.

## Expected result

- About **20 pastries**: 9 filled squares (pinwheels, envelopes, combs) of about 53 g dough + 20–30 g filling, and 11 snails of about 45 g each.
- Deep golden-brown, glossy; well-defined flaky layers at every edge; shapes hold (points of pinwheels still pointed, snails still round).
- Tender, rich, slightly sweet, lightly cardamom-scented crumb (optional), flakier and softer than croissant, with a less open honeycomb.
- Pastry cream set and moist, slightly browned on top, not boiled over or runny; fruit intact.
- The base under the filling is baked through and crisp, not pale or gummy.

## Formula

### Dough

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Détrempe** | | |
| Flour, 11–12 % protein (all-purpose, or 50:50 bread and all-purpose) | 100.0 | 400 |
| Whole milk, cold | 40.0 | 160 |
| Whole egg, cold, beaten (about 1.5 large eggs) | 20.0 | 80 |
| Sugar | 12.0 | 48 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 8 |
| Instant yeast, osmotolerant (gold) | 1.5 | 6 |
| Unsalted butter, soft | 8.0 | 32 |
| *Détrempe subtotal* | *183.5* | *734* |
| **Beurrage** | | |
| Unsalted butter, ≥ 82 % fat | 60.0 | 240 |
| **Total** | **243.5** | **974** |
| *Optional: ground cardamom (not in total)* | *0.5* | *2* |

**True hydration:** milk 160 × 0.87 + egg 80 × 0.75 + détrempe butter 32 × 0.16 = 139.2 + 60 + 5.1 = 204.3 g → **51 %**. Slightly firmer than croissant dough.
**Sugar in the water phase:** 48 ÷ (48 + 204) ≈ 19 %: use osmotolerant yeast (with standard instant yeast, 2 % and a longer proof).
**Beurrage:** 240 ÷ 974 ≈ **25 %** of the laminated dough; 60 % of flour vs 55 % in [R1-34](recipes/R1-34-croissant.md).
**Layers:** open lock-in + 3 single turns → 3³ = **27** fat layers.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (487 g, plaque 120 g at 13 × 13 cm) gives about 10 pastries.
**Allergens:** wheat (gluten), milk, egg; almonds if using crème d'amande.

## Equipment

As [R1-34](recipes/R1-34-croissant.md), plus: two piping bags (one with a 10 mm plain tip), a small offset spatula, a sieve, a small saucepan and a pastry brush for glaze.

## Mise en place

1. **Timeline (two days).**
   - Day 1: pastry cream (30 min + chill), détrempe (20 min) → 30 min at room temperature → flatten and refrigerate 8–16 h. Plaque.
   - Day 2: lock-in and three single turns with 30 min rests (about 2 h) → rest 1 h → sheet, cut, shape, fill (1 h) → proof 1.5–2 h → bake 15–20 min → glaze.
2. **DDT 22–24 °C.** Liquid temperature as in R1-34 (the egg counts as liquid).
3. **Pastry cream:** make it on day 1 with the raised cornstarch, boil for a full 2 minutes, cover the surface and refrigerate. On day 2, whisk it smooth just before use; it should be thick enough to hold a peak from a spoon.

## Procedure

**1. Détrempe (DDT 22–24 °C).** Mix as for croissant ([R1-34](recipes/R1-34-croissant.md) step 1): speed 1 for 3 min, speed 2 for 3–4 min, to **moderate development** (smooth, cohesive, tears before a windowpane). The egg makes it slightly softer and stickier than croissant dough. Rest 30 minutes covered, flatten to **20 × 25 cm, 2 cm thick**, wrap and refrigerate **8–16 h at 3–5 °C**.

**2. Plaque.** 240 g butter to **18 × 18 cm** (261 cm³ ÷ 324 cm² ≈ **8 mm**). Refrigerate; bring to **12–15 °C** before lock-in.

**3. Lock-in.** Roll the détrempe (4–8 °C) to **20 × 38 cm**. Open lock-in as for croissant: plaque in the centre, ends folded to meet in the middle, seams pinched. Block about 20 × 19 cm.

**4. Three single turns.** Roll to about **20 × 57 cm**, fold in three; rotate 90°. Wrap and refrigerate **30 minutes** after each turn (the egg-rich dough warms and softens faster than croissant dough; do not give two turns in a row unless the block is still ≤ 13 °C after the first). Block temperature **10–15 °C** at every roll. After the third turn, rest **1 hour**.
- *Alternative:* 1 double + 1 single (12 fat layers) for a more open, bread-like, croissant-style Danish.

**5. Sheet.** Cut the block in half.
- **Half A (squares):** roll to **34 × 34 cm, 4 mm**, rest 10 minutes in the refrigerator, trim to **33 × 33 cm** and cut **9 squares of 11 × 11 cm**.
- **Half B (snails):** roll to about **33 × 36 cm, 4 mm**, with a long side facing you.

**6. Shape and fill.** Keep the pieces cold; work one tray at a time.

- **Pinwheel (3 pieces).** Cut from each corner towards the centre, about 4.5 cm, leaving an uncut centre of about 3 cm. Fold every second point into the centre and press it down firmly (a dab of egg wash under the points helps them stay). Fill after the proof (step 8).
- **Envelope (3 pieces).** Fold all four corners into the centre and press firmly; press a well in the centre with two fingers. Fill after the proof.
- **Comb (3 pieces).** Pipe a 2 cm line of pastry cream (or crème d'amande) along the middle of the square, leaving 1 cm at each end. Egg-wash one edge, fold the square in half over the filling, and press the edges together firmly. Cut 4–5 slits, 2.5 cm long, into the *sealed* edge (not the fold), and curve the pastry slightly so the "teeth" open.
- **Snails (11 pieces, half B).** Spread 165 g of pastry cream thinly over the sheet (or all the cinnamon butter), leaving a 2 cm strip bare along the far edge; scatter the raisins. Egg-wash the bare strip. Roll from the near edge into a tight log, seam underneath. Chill 15 minutes, then cut into **11 slices, 3 cm thick**, with a sharp knife (or thread). Lay them flat, tuck the loose end under.

Space everything at least 5 cm apart on parchment-lined trays.

**7. Proof (1.5–2 h at 24–27 °C, humid; never above 28 °C).** Egg-wash lightly first. Proof to about **1.5–2×** volume: puffy, layers visible, slight jiggle. **Slightly less than croissant**: a fully proofed Danish loses its shape when filled and pressed, and the fillings slide.

**8. Fill the pinwheels and envelopes.** Preheat the oven to **200 °C (390 °F) conventional** or **190 °C (375 °F) fan**. Gently press the well in each centre again with a floured finger. Pipe **25 g** of pastry cream into each, and top with an apricot half (cut side down) or **15 g** of cherries. Egg-wash the exposed dough only, not the filling.

**9. Bake 15–20 minutes** (snails 18–22 min) until deep golden-brown, including the edges of the base when you lift one with a spatula. Rotate the tray at 10 minutes. The pastry cream should be puffed and lightly browned at its edges. Pale sides or a pale base: bake 2–3 minutes more.

**10. Glaze.** Bring the nappage to a boil. Brush a thin layer over the pastries (dough and fruit) while both glaze and pastries are hot, within 5 minutes of baking. The hot glaze sets to a thin, glossy, non-sticky film as it cools. Optional: when cool, drizzle icing across the snails and combs.

**11. Cool** on a rack 20 minutes. Best within 8 hours. Pastries containing pastry cream must be refrigerated if held longer than about 2 hours at room temperature, and eaten within 24 hours ([FDA-FoodCode]).

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Pastry cream is a high-risk food: cook it to a full boil, chill it quickly, keep it cold until it is piped, and do not hold filled pastries at room temperature for more than about 2 hours ([FDA-FoodCode]). Boiling jam glaze is at about 105 °C and sticks to skin; brush it, never touch it. Allergens: milk, egg, wheat, almonds (crème d'amande).

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Détrempe mix and DDT | Moderate development; 22–24 °C | As croissant; egg-rich dough overdevelops easily |
| Plaque and lock-in | 18 × 18 cm, 8 mm, 12–15 °C; dough 4–8 °C | Plastic butter, matched firmness |
| Turns | 3 singles with 30 min rests; block 10–15 °C | Richer, softer dough warms quickly |
| Sheet | 4 mm, cold, relaxed | Even shapes; shapes hold |
| Pastry cream | 11 % cornstarch on milk, full 2-min boil, cold and thick | Holds in the oven without running or boiling over |
| Fruit | Drained and dry | Free juice soaks the base and thins the cream |
| Proof | 1.5–2×, 24–27 °C | Shapes hold; fillings stay in place |
| Bake | Deep golden including base | Base under a wet filling is the last part to bake |
| Nappage | Boiling glaze on hot pastries, thin | Thin glossy film that sets; thick glaze is sticky |
| Holding | ≤ 2 h at room temperature, then refrigerate | Pastry-cream safety |

## Scientific explanation

Danish dough sits between croissant and brioche. The egg (20 %) and extra milk bring fat, emulsifiers and protein that tenderize the dough layers and give a softer, cake-like tenderness, while 60 % roll-in butter in 27 layers keeps the flakiness. More layers than croissant (27 vs 12) makes each dough leaf thinner, so the crumb is finer and more tender, less of a honeycomb ([15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)). Fillings add water and weight to a structure that must still rise: pastry cream is a starch gel ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md), [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md)) that, reheated in the oven, softens and loses water as steam; with too little starch it runs and boils over, so the cornstarch is raised to 11 % of the milk to keep a thicker gel after evaporation. Proofing to only 1.5–2× keeps the dough strong enough to hold a filled well. The hot nappage is a pectin-and-sugar gel from the jam that sets as it cools and gives shine, a moisture barrier and a slightly acidic contrast.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Egg | 20 → 0 % (milk 40 → 56 %) | Croissant-like, less tender, paler |
| Beurrage | 60 → 75 % | Flakier, richer; harder to keep cold; more leakage |
| Turns | 3S (27) → 1D + 1S (12) | More open, bread-like, taller |
| Sugar | 12 → 16 % | Sweeter, faster browning, slower proof |
| Cornstarch in pastry cream | 9 → 11 % | Holds shape in the oven; below 9 % it runs; above 12 % pasty |
| Proof | 1.5–2× → 2.5× | More volume, but fillings slide, shapes open |
| Filling | Pastry cream → crème d'amande | Rises and browns; more stable; nutty |
| Cardamom | 0 → 0.5 % | Scandinavian-style aroma |

## Experiment suggestions

- Pastry cream stability: bake three pinwheels each with cream at 8 %, 10 % and 12 % cornstarch; score spread, boiling over and texture.
- Proof level: proof envelopes to 1.5× and 2.5× and compare shape retention.
- Filling timing: fill before vs after the proof.
