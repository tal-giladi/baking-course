# R1-36 · Ganache: piping, glazing and truffles

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: three dark-chocolate ganaches at the standard ratios (1 : 1, 1.5 : 1 and 2 : 1 chocolate to cream), with glucose syrup and butter at conventional levels, arranged so that you can see how ratio alone moves a ganache from pourable glaze to rollable truffle. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe. Written for a 55–66 % dark couverture; milk and white chocolate conversions are given below.

## Objective

Make a stable, glossy chocolate emulsion by the hot-cream method, at a ratio chosen for its job; control the emulsifying and using temperatures; crystallize a firm ganache for piping and truffles; and recognize and rescue a split ganache.

## Expected result

- **A · Glaze (1 : 1):** at 30–35 °C pours in a smooth, even curtain; sets on a chilled surface to a glossy, satin coat 2–3 mm thick with no bubbles or streaks; soft and spoonable at 20 °C.
- **B · Piping (1.5 : 1):** after crystallizing, pipes a sharp star rosette that holds its ridges at 20 °C; smooth, creamy, melts quickly.
- **C · Truffle (2 : 1):** after 12 h at 16–18 °C, firm enough to scoop and roll; cuts cleanly; no oily sheen, no graininess; melts cleanly in the mouth.

## Formula

Ganache is formulated as **% of chocolate weight** (the chocolate is the base, as flour is in bread).

**A · Glaze (1 : 1)**

| Ingredient | % of chocolate | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Dark chocolate 55–66 %, callets or chopped ≤ 1 cm | 100 | 150 |
| Whipping cream, 35 % fat | 100 | 150 |
| Glucose syrup (DE ~40, or light corn syrup) | 10 | 15 |
| **Total** | **210** | **315** |

Yield ~310 g: glazes the top and sides of a 20 cm cake or a 22–24 cm tart. Water ≈ 150 × 0.58 + 15 × 0.2 = 90 g → **~29 %**.

**B · Piping (1.5 : 1)**

| Ingredient | % of chocolate | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Dark chocolate 55–66 % | 100 | 180 |
| Whipping cream, 35 % fat | 66.7 | 120 |
| Glucose syrup or invert sugar | 8.3 | 15 |
| Unsalted butter, soft (~20 °C) | 11.1 | 20 |
| **Total** | **186.1** | **335** |

Yield ~330 g: fills ~30 macarons or 12 tartlets, or pipes 40–50 small rosettes. Water ≈ 69.6 + 3 + 3.2 = 76 g → **~23 %**.

**C · Truffle (2 : 1)**

| Ingredient | % of chocolate | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Dark chocolate 55–66 % | 100 | 200 |
| Whipping cream, 35 % fat | 50 | 100 |
| Invert sugar or glucose syrup | 5 | 10 |
| Unsalted butter, soft (~20 °C) | 7.5 | 15 |
| **Total** | **162.5** | **325** |

Yield: ~30 truffles of 10 g, plus coating. Water ≈ 58 + 2 + 2.4 = 62 g → **~19 %**.

**Coating for truffles:** 50 g cocoa powder (Dutch-processed for colour), or ~250 g dark chocolate tempered by [R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md).

**Scaling:** ×2 works for all three. ×0.5 is too small for a stick blender (use a spatula in a small bowl). For a chocolate above ~68 %, reduce the chocolate by 10–15 % or raise the glucose/invert to 10 % to avoid splitting.

**Milk and white conversions** (same cream, same method; emulsify and use 2–3 °C cooler):

| Formula | Dark | Milk (~33–40 %) | White |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · Glaze | 150 g : 150 g cream | 300 g : 150 g | 375 g : 150 g |
| B · Piping | 180 g : 120 g | 300 g : 120 g | 360 g : 120 g |
| C · Truffle | 200 g : 100 g | 275 g : 100 g | 300 g : 100 g |

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g); probe thermometer.
- Small saucepan or microwave-safe jug for the cream.
- Tall, narrow container just wider than the blender head (1 L jug), or a deep bowl for spatula mixing.
- Immersion (stick) blender; heatproof spatula.
- Fine sieve (glaze).
- Cling film; a shallow tray or a 15 × 15 cm frame lined with film for truffle ganache.
- Piping bags; 8–10 mm plain and star tips; a small scoop (10–15 ml) or a teaspoon; gloves for rolling.
- A cake round or tart and a wire rack over a tray (glaze).

## Mise en place

1. Weigh the chocolate into the jug or bowl; break any large pieces to ≤ 1 cm.
2. Weigh cream and glucose/invert together into the saucepan (the syrup dissolves as the cream heats).
3. Cut the butter into 1 cm cubes and leave at room temperature (~20 °C, soft but not greasy).
4. For the glaze: chill the cake or tart to be glazed (≤ 8 °C) and set it on a rack over a tray.
5. Line the truffle tray or frame with film.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Heat the cream (2–3 min).** Bring the cream and glucose/invert to a bare simmer, **~80 °C**: small bubbles round the edge, not a rolling boil. Weigh it again if it boiled: top up with cream to the original weight.

**Step 2 — Pour and rest (1 min).** Pour all the cream over the chocolate at once. Tap the container to settle; make sure all the chocolate is covered. Leave it for **1 minute** without stirring.

**Step 3 — Emulsify (1–2 min).** Spatula: stir in small, fast circles at the **centre** until a glossy, elastic kernel forms (it looks like thick, shiny paint), then widen the circles gradually until the whole mass is glossy. Stick blender: tilt the blender into the container, blade **fully submerged**, run on low without lifting, moving slowly around the base for 30–60 s. **Endpoint:** smooth, glossy and elastic, no unmelted pieces, **35–45 °C** (probe). If pieces remain below 35 °C, heat 5–10 s in the microwave and blend again.

**Step 4 — Butter (B and C, 30 s).** When the ganache is **35–40 °C**, add the soft butter and blend (submerged) until no streaks remain.

**Step 5 — Finish according to formula.**

**A · Glaze.**
1. Strain through a fine sieve into a jug to remove bubbles and any lumps; tap the jug.
2. Cool, stirring gently now and then (no whisking), to **30–35 °C**: 32 °C is a good starting point.
3. Pour over the chilled object in one continuous movement, starting in the centre and spiralling out so that it runs over the edges. Do **not** go back over it with a palette knife except one quick pass on the top within the first 5 s, if needed.
4. Let it set 10–15 min at room temperature, or 10 min in the fridge. **Endpoint:** even, glossy coat 2–3 mm thick, no bubbles, no drips thicker than the coat.
5. Leftover glaze keeps, covered, 1 week refrigerated; re-melt to 32 °C, blending to remove air.

**B · Piping.**
1. Pour into a shallow container, press film onto the surface.
2. **Option 1 (immediate):** cool, stirring every 5 min, until thick enough to hold a shape (~26–28 °C), fill the bag and pipe into shells or tartlets: it sets in place in 30–60 min at 18 °C.
3. **Option 2 (crystallized):** leave covered at **16–18 °C for 12 h**. Beat briefly with a spatula to a smooth, soft paste (a few seconds; do not whip unless you want a lighter, whipped texture), fill a bag with a star tip and pipe. **Endpoint:** rosettes hold sharp ridges for 1 h at 20 °C.

**C · Truffle.**
1. Pour into the lined tray or frame (1–1.5 cm deep); press film onto the surface.
2. Crystallize at **16–18 °C for 12 h** (a cool room or a wine fridge; the refrigerator is a fallback that gives a coarser, slightly duller texture).
3. **Endpoint:** firm, cuts cleanly, a fingertip leaves a mark without sticking.
4. Scoop 10 g portions (or pipe 10 g mounds with a plain 10 mm tip onto parchment and let them firm 30 min), then roll between gloved palms quickly into balls. If they soften, chill 10 min.
5. Coat: roll in cocoa powder, or dip in tempered chocolate ([R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)) and roll in cocoa while still wet. Truffle centres for dipping should be at ~18–20 °C, not fridge-cold (cold centres crack the shell as they warm and expand).

### Rescue: split or grainy ganache

If it looks oily, curdled or grainy, or fat pools at the edge:

1. Bring it to **32–35 °C** (microwave 5 s bursts, stirring).
2. Add **5–10 %** of its weight of warm milk or cream (35–40 °C).
3. Blend with the stick blender, head submerged, until glossy and elastic.
4. If still broken: blend 20–30 g warm milk in a clean jug and add the broken ganache gradually while blending.
5. Record the cause.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Ganache is a dairy product with high water activity. Keep it covered; hold at cool room temperature (≤ 18 °C) for no more than 2 days, refrigerate for up to a week, freeze (well wrapped) for up to 3 months ([FDA-FoodCode]). Label with the date. Allergens: milk, and soya if the chocolate contains soya lecithin; check for nut traces on the pack.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate piece size | ≤ 1 cm or callets | Melts evenly from the cream's heat; no lumps to overheat later |
| Cream temperature | ~80 °C, not a hard boil; weight maintained | Enough heat to melt; excess heat or evaporation splits the ganache |
| Emulsifying temperature | 35–45 °C; glossy and elastic | Fat liquid and dispersible; droplets not coalescing |
| Butter addition | Soft butter at 35–40 °C | Disperses without melting to oil |
| Blender | Submerged, tilted, no air | Small droplets, no bubbles |
| Glaze use | 30–35 °C onto a chilled object | Coat thickness and gloss |
| Crystallization | 16–18 °C, 12 h, covered, undisturbed | Fine, stable crystals: smooth, pipeable, rollable |

## Common failures

- Oily, curdled or split → [Ganache split or greasy](troubleshooting/chocolate.md?id=ganache-split-or-greasy)
- Sandy or grainy after setting → [Grainy ganache](troubleshooting/chocolate.md?id=grainy-ganache)
- Won't hold a piped shape; too hard to roll or pipe → [Ganache too soft or too firm](troubleshooting/chocolate.md?id=ganache-too-soft-or-too-firm)
- Truffle shells crack or bloom → [Grey streaks or haze after setting](troubleshooting/chocolate.md?id=grey-streaks-or-haze-after-setting)

## Scientific explanation

Hot cream melts the cocoa butter and dissolves the chocolate's sugar in its water. Stirred or blended at 35–45 °C, the mixture becomes an oil-in-water emulsion: fat droplets (cocoa butter, milk fat, butter) dispersed in a sugar syrup, stabilized by milk proteins, lecithin and cocoa particles, with cocoa particles suspended in the same syrup ([16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md), [SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)). Starting at the centre creates a small zone where the ratio of liquid to chocolate is high enough to emulsify first; it then takes up the rest, like the start of a mayonnaise ([09.3](stage-1/module-09/lesson-03.md)). On cooling, cocoa butter crystallizes inside the droplets and cocoa particles absorb water, so the ganache sets; the more chocolate relative to cream, the more fat and particles and the less water, the firmer the result. Slow, still crystallization at 16–18 °C gives many fine crystals and a smooth texture; fast cooling or stirring while setting gives coarse crystals and graininess. Glucose and invert sugar add dissolved solids to the water phase: more viscosity, gloss, slower sugar crystallization and lower water activity. A ganache splits when fat overwhelms the water phase or when heat and agitation make droplets coalesce; warm liquid and re-blending restores the water phase and re-disperses the fat.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio | 1 : 1 → 2 : 1 | Pourable glaze → firm truffle; more split risk with high-% chocolate |
| Chocolate % | 58 % → 72 % at same ratio | Firmer, more bitter; higher split risk; reduce chocolate 10–15 % |
| Glucose/invert | 0 → 10 % | Softer, glossier, longer shelf life, less graininess over days |
| Butter | 0 → 15 % | Silkier, faster melt, softer at room temperature; more fat to hold |
| Cream fat | 35 % → 18–20 % (single/light cream) | More water, softer and less rich; firmer ratios possible |
| Emulsifying tool | Spatula → stick blender | Smoother, more stable, longer shelf life |
| Setting | 16–18 °C 12 h → fridge 2 h | Faster; coarser, duller, harder to pipe |
| Infusion | Add coffee, spice, tea, zest to the cream | Flavour; re-weigh the cream after straining |
