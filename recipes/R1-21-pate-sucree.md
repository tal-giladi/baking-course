# R1-21 · Pâte sucrée

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a sweet French short dough with icing sugar, whole egg and a little almond flour, in the proportions used in standard professional practice for fruit, lemon and chocolate tarts. The main method is crémage; sablage is given as the alternative so you can compare them in [X1-30](experiments/X1-30-sablage-cremage.md).

## Objective

Make a sweet, fine-textured short dough that lines a straight-sided ring cleanly and bakes to a crisp shell with a clean snap and minimal shrinkage. You will control butter temperature, mixing time after the flour goes in, and the bake temperature that keeps a sugared dough from overbrowning.

## Expected result

- **Raw dough:** smooth, cohesive, cool and slightly soft, like firm modelling clay; no streaks of butter or pockets of flour.
- **Baked shell (22 cm × 2 cm ring):** straight walls within 2 mm of the ring height, sharp base corner, even golden colour on the rim, walls and centre of the base; fine, even crumb; breaks with a clean snap and melts rather than chews. Sweet, buttery, lightly nutty.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Plain / all-purpose flour (9.5–11.5 % protein) or T55/T45 | 100 | 250 |
| Unsalted butter | 60 | 150 |
| Icing sugar (confectioners' sugar) | 38 | 95 |
| Almond flour (finely ground blanched almonds) | 12 | 30 |
| Whole egg, beaten | 20 | 50 |
| Fine salt | 0.8 | 2 |
| **Total** | **230.8** | **577** |

**Yield:** 577 g dough. Two 22 cm × 2 cm shells (about 250 g of dough rolled for each, trim returned) with about 75 g left for 2–3 small tartlets or test pieces.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 125 g flour, 75 g butter, 47.5 g icing sugar, 15 g almond flour, 25 g egg, 1 g salt (288.5 g, one 22 cm shell). ×2 = 500 g flour, 300 g butter, 190 g icing sugar, 60 g almond flour, 100 g egg, 4 g salt (1,154 g).

**No almond flour (nut allergy):** replace the 30 g almond flour with 30 g more flour (280 g flour in total; total dough still 577 g). The shell is slightly firmer and less tender.

**Allergens:** gluten, egg, milk, tree nuts (almond). Label shells made from this dough accordingly.

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g; probe thermometer.
- Stand mixer with paddle, or a bowl and a stiff spatula.
- Sieve (for icing sugar and almond flour); bench scraper; rolling pin; 2.5–3 mm guides if available; ruler.
- 22 cm × 2 cm tart ring (or a fluted pan with removable base) on a tray with parchment or a perforated mat.
- Parchment, baking weights, pastry brush, fork or docker.

## Mise en place

- Butter at **16–18 °C**: soft enough that a finger dents it easily but still matt and cool, not shiny. Cut into 2 cm pieces. (For the sablage method below: cold butter, 4–8 °C, 1 cm cubes.)
- Egg at room temperature (18–22 °C), beaten and weighed.
- Sift the icing sugar and almond flour together. Weigh the flour and salt together.

## Procedure

### Crémage (main method)

1. **Butter and sugar.** Paddle the butter with the icing sugar–almond mix on **low speed, 1–2 minutes**, until smooth and uniform. Do not beat on medium to lighten it: air makes the shell rise and bubble.
2. **Egg.** Add the egg in 3 additions on low speed, scraping the bowl each time. **Endpoint:** smooth, glossy, not curdled. If it looks split, the butter or egg is too cold: keep mixing on low for 30 s, or warm the bowl with your hands.
3. **Flour.** Add the flour and salt all at once. Mix on **lowest speed for 20–40 seconds**, stopping when no dry flour is visible. Finish by hand with the scraper. **Do not mix further:** this is where gluten forms.
4. **Fraisage (optional).** If the dough has streaks, smear it once across the bench with the heel of your hand and gather it.
5. **Shape and rest.** Divide into two discs of about 285 g, flatten each to 2 cm, wrap, and refrigerate **at least 2 hours, ideally overnight**. Dough temperature at wrapping ≤ 20 °C. Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated or 3 months frozen.

### Sablage (alternative)

1. Mix flour, salt, icing sugar and almond flour. Add cold butter cubes (4–8 °C) and rub in with fingertips, or paddle on low for 2–3 minutes, until **no butter pieces remain and the mixture looks like fine damp sand**. Mixture ≤ 18 °C.
2. Add the egg and mix on low (or with a scraper) only until the dough clumps. Fraisage once. Shape and rest as above. Expect a shorter, more fragile dough.

### Lining and baking

6. **Temper.** Let the dough stand 5–10 minutes until the core is **8–12 °C** and it bends without cracking.
7. **Roll.** Between two sheets of parchment or on a lightly floured bench, roll to **2.5–3 mm** and about **29 cm** across. If the dough becomes soft or sticky, slide it onto a tray and chill 10–15 minutes. Sucrée softens fast: work quickly.
8. **Line** the ring without stretching, pressing a sharp corner at the base and firm contact with the wall ([11.2](stage-1/module-11/lesson-02.md)). Trim flush with the rim, or leave 2 mm above it to rasp level after baking. Dock the base every 1–2 cm.
9. **Chill.** Freeze the lined shell **20–30 minutes** (or refrigerate 30–45 minutes). Preheat the oven to **165–170 °C (330–340 °F)** still, or 155–160 °C fan, with a tray on the middle rack.
10. **Blind bake, weighted.** Line with crumpled parchment and fill to the rim with weights. Bake **15–18 minutes**, until the rim is set and just turning pale gold.
11. **Remove weights and finish.** **Par-bake** (for fillings that will be baked, such as [R1-23](recipes/R1-23-creme-damande.md)): 3–5 minutes more, until the base is dry and matt. **Full bake:** 8–12 minutes more, until evenly golden, including the centre of the base.
12. **Seal (fully baked shells for moist fillings).** Brush the inside with beaten egg (or yolk with a little cream) and bake **2–3 minutes** until set and glossy.
13. **Cool** 10 minutes on the tray, lift off the ring, cool on a rack. If you left an edge, rasp it level with a fine grater once cold. Store fully baked shells airtight at room temperature for 2 days, or frozen for 1 month.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature (crémage) | 16–18 °C | Soft enough to blend with sugar and egg without aeration; too warm goes greasy, too cold splits |
| Mixing after flour | ≤ 40 s on lowest speed | Gluten forms quickly once flour meets the egg water: tough dough, shrinkage |
| Rest | ≥ 2 h, ideally overnight | Even hydration, relaxed gluten, firm butter |
| Rolling temperature | Core 8–12 °C | Colder cracks; warmer sticks and stretches |
| Thickness | 2.5–3 mm, even | Even colour and crispness |
| Lined shell | Frozen 20–30 min before baking | Butter stays solid while the structure starts to set |
| Oven | 165–170 °C still | Sugar browns fast; hotter overbrowns the rim before the base bakes |

## Common failures

- **Walls shrink or slump.** See [Shrinking tart shell](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=shrinking-tart-shell) and [Slumped sides in blind baking](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=slumped-sides-in-blind-baking).
- **Dough sticks to everything and tears.** Too warm. See [Dough sticks when rolling](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md?id=dough-sticks-when-rolling).

## Scientific explanation

Sucrée is a sugar-and-fat-rich short dough whose structure comes more from egg protein and a sugar–starch matrix than from gluten. The icing sugar dissolves in the small amount of egg water; that sugar solution competes with flour proteins for water and delays their hydration, so little gluten can form ([11.1](stage-1/module-11/lesson-01.md)). The fine sugar particles also give the even, close crumb and clean surface that granulated sugar cannot. The whole egg supplies the only water in the dough and coagulating proteins that set the walls in the oven. Almond flour adds fat and non-gluten protein, which tenderizes and flavours the dough.

In crémage, the butter, sugar and egg form an emulsion first; the flour then meets that emulsion. Brief mixing gives a smooth, cohesive dough with enough gluten to line well. Long mixing develops the gluten that makes the shell tough and makes it shrink. In sablage, fat coats the flour before the egg arrives: less gluten, a more tender and sandy texture, a more fragile dough ([X1-30](experiments/X1-30-sablage-cremage.md)).

Sugar lowers the temperature at which the shell browns noticeably: caramelization and Maillard browning begin on the dry rim well before the thicker base has baked through. That is why sucrée bakes at 165–170 °C, not 180–190 °C ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Crémage → sablage | More tender, sandier, less shrinkage, more fragile |
| Mixing after flour | 20 s → 3 min | Tougher, more elastic, more shrinkage |
| Icing sugar | 30 → 45 % | More tender, sweeter, faster browning, softer at the bench |
| Granulated instead of icing sugar | Swap | Coarser, speckled, more spread, crunchier |
| Almond flour | 0 → 20 % (flour reduced by the same) | More tender, richer, more fragile |
| Egg | Whole egg → yolks (same weight) | Richer, more tender, deeper colour, less water |
| Butter | 50 → 70 % | More tender; softens faster; more slump without freezing |
