# R1-19 · Chiffon cake

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established technique. The chiffon cake (an oil-and-yolk batter lightened with meringue and a little baking powder, baked in an ungreased tube pan and cooled inverted) is a well-established cake type; these proportions sit in the normal range for chiffon and were sized for a 20 cm tube pan and for measurement. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a tall, light, moist chiffon cake in a 20 cm tube pan. Build a fluid oil-yolk base, whip a meringue to firm-but-not-dry peaks, fold with a measured loss of air, bake in an ungreased pan and cool it inverted so it keeps its height. Explain each of those choices from [10.3](stage-1/module-10/lesson-03.md).

## Expected result

- One ring cake, 20 cm diameter, about **9–11 cm tall** after cooling (no more than about 5–8 % shrinkage from its height at the end of the bake), about 560–590 g.
- Thin golden-brown top, often with dry-looking cracks; sides lightly coloured where they were attached to the pan.
- Very light, soft, moist, even crumb with small cells; no white meringue lumps, no dense band at the base, no large holes.
- Mild vanilla flavour; stays soft when refrigerated.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Base batter** | | |
| Cake flour or soft plain flour (8–10 % protein) | 100.0 | 120 |
| Caster or fine granulated sugar | 50.0 | 60 |
| Baking powder (double-acting) | 3.3 | 4 |
| Fine salt | 1.25 | 1.5 |
| Neutral oil (sunflower, rapeseed/canola) | 50.0 | 60 |
| Egg yolks (about 4–5 large) | 62.5 | 75 |
| Water, at 20–25 °C | 70.8 | 85 |
| Vanilla extract | 3.3 | 4 |
| **Meringue** | | |
| Egg whites (about 5 large), at 18–22 °C | 125.0 | 150 |
| Cream of tartar (or 3 g lemon juice) | 0.8 | 1 |
| Caster or fine granulated sugar | 75.0 | 90 |
| **Total** | **542.1** | **650.5** |

**Yield:** 650.5 g of batter → one 20 cm tube pan (ungreased aluminium, at least 9 cm tall, about 2–2.5 L; fill to about ⅔), about 560–590 g baked.
**Eggs:** 5 large eggs usually give about 150 g of whites and 85–95 g of yolks; weigh both to the gram and keep the extra yolk for another use, so that the fat and water in the base stay as written.
**Balance note:** total sugar 125 % of flour and liquids (water + yolks + whites) 258 % is far outside the creamed-cake rules of [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md): a chiffon's structure comes mainly from the meringue's egg-white protein, not from the flour.
**Flavour variants:** replace the water with the same weight of fresh orange juice and add the zest of 1 orange; or dissolve 4 g instant coffee in the water.
**Scaling:** ×1.5 (975.8 g) for a 23–25 cm tube pan, bake 50–60 min. ×0.5 is not recommended: small tube pans are rare, and the cake needs height to hang.

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g, and 0.1 g for the baking powder, salt and cream of tartar if available).
- Stand mixer with whisk (or hand mixer), perfectly clean and grease-free bowl for the whites.
- Second large bowl and a balloon whisk for the base; large silicone spatula.
- **20 cm tube pan (angel-food type)**, plain aluminium, **not nonstick, not greased**, ideally with a removable base and feet (or a bottle whose neck fits the tube).
- Probe thermometer; oven thermometer; SG cup.
- Thin, flexible knife for release.

## Mise en place

1. **Oven.** Rack in the lower third so the top of the pan sits at mid-oven. Preheat to **165 °C (330 °F)** conventional, fan off, for at least 20 min.
2. **Pan.** Clean and dry. Do **not** grease or line it. Wipe it with a paper towel and a little vinegar if it has any greasy film.
3. **Eggs.** Separate the eggs while cold (the yolks break less easily), with no trace of yolk in the whites. Weigh 150 g whites into the mixer bowl and 75 g yolks into the base bowl. Let the whites warm to **18–22 °C** (about 30 min).
4. **Dry mix.** Sift the flour, 60 g sugar, baking powder and salt into the base bowl or onto parchment.
5. Weigh the oil, water and vanilla into a jug.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Base batter (2 min).** Whisk the yolks, oil, water and vanilla together until homogeneous and slightly thick (about 30 s). Add the sifted dry ingredients and whisk just until smooth and glossy, with no lumps (30–45 s). It is a thin, pourable batter; it does not need aeration. Set aside.

**Step 2 — Meringue (5–7 min).** Whip the whites with the cream of tartar on medium speed until foamy and opaque with soft, bubbly texture (about 1 min). Increase to medium-high and add the 90 g sugar gradually, one spoonful every 10–15 s. Continue whipping until **firm, glossy peaks** that stand up with a tip that bends slightly; the meringue slides only slowly when the bowl is tilted. **Stop before it looks dry, grainy or clumpy** ([09.2](stage-1/module-09/lesson-02.md)). Optional: measure meringue SG.

**Step 3 — Fold (2–3 min).** Whisk **one-third** of the meringue vigorously into the base batter with the balloon whisk to lighten it. Add the second third and fold gently with the whisk or spatula (cut, sweep, lift, turn the bowl), 10–15 strokes. Add the last third and fold with the spatula until no white streaks remain, checking the bottom of the bowl. The batter should be light, thick and uniform.

**Step 4 — Measure.** Fill the SG cup gently and level it. **Target SG about 0.45–0.55** (indicative). Return the batter to the bowl.

**Step 5 — Pan (1 min).** Pour the batter into the ungreased pan in a slow stream, moving around the ring. Run a thin spatula or chopstick once around through the batter to pop large air pockets, then smooth the top. Tap the pan once, gently.

**Step 6 — Bake, 40–50 min at 165 °C.** Do not open the door for the first 30 min. The cake rises above the rim and the top cracks.

**Step 7 — Doneness.** **Done when:** the top is golden brown, the cracks look dry rather than wet and shiny, the top springs back fully when pressed, a skewer inserted halfway between tube and wall comes out clean, and a probe there reads about **95–99 °C** (indicative).

**Step 8 — Invert immediately.** Turn the pan upside down at once: onto its feet, or with the tube over the neck of a sturdy bottle. The cake should hang without touching the counter. Cool **fully inverted for at least 1.5–2 h**, until the pan is completely cool. Measure the height when you invert (through the cracks, or at the rim) and again before release, if you can.

**Step 9 — Release.** Turn the pan upright. Run a thin, flexible knife around the outside wall, pressing the blade against the pan, then around the tube. Lift out by the tube (removable base), run the knife under the base, and invert onto a plate. Serve plain, with whipped cream ([R1-27](recipes/R1-27-creme-chantilly.md)) or a light glaze. Store covered at room temperature 2–3 days or refrigerated up to 4 days; freeze wrapped up to about 1 month.

**Safety:** Inverting a hot, heavy tube pan is the main burn risk: use dry oven gloves and have the bottle or cooling space ready before you open the oven. The batter contains raw egg; do not taste it. Contains wheat and egg.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Egg separation | No yolk in the whites; clean, grease-free bowl | Fat prevents the meringue from forming |
| Meringue endpoint | Firm, glossy peaks that bend at the tip; not dry | Under: weak cake that shrinks. Over: lumps and holes, poor folding |
| Lightening fold | ⅓ of meringue whisked in firmly | Matches densities so the rest folds in without deflating |
| Batter SG | ~0.45–0.55 | Objective check of folding loss |
| Pan | Ungreased, not nonstick | The batter must grip to climb and to hang when inverted |
| Doneness | Dry cracks, springback, ~95–99 °C | Underbaked chiffon falls out or collapses when inverted |
| Cooling | Inverted immediately, ≥ 1.5 h | Upright, the hot crumb compresses under its own weight |

## Scientific explanation

The meringue supplies most of the structure and aeration: egg-white proteins unfold at the surface of air bubbles and form stable films, with sugar thickening the liquid between them ([09.2](stage-1/module-09/lesson-02.md), [SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)). The base batter carries a lot of water and oil, which would weigh down and weaken a pure egg foam, so a little baking powder is added: its CO₂ inflates the meringue's existing cells in the oven ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md), [08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)). Yolk lecithin and proteins emulsify the oil into the water phase before the meringue is added, so the oil is present as small, coated droplets rather than free fat that would burst the foam. Because oil stays liquid when cold, the crumb stays soft even when refrigerated. The tube conducts heat into the centre of a tall cake so it sets before over-expanding. At the end of the bake the crumb is at 95–99 °C, its starch gel still weak and its cells full of steam that is about to condense: hung upside down from the ungreased walls it has gripped, the cake is stretched by gravity rather than compressed while the structure cools and firms.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pan | Ungreased aluminium → greased or nonstick | Lower rise; cake slides or falls out when inverted; shrinkage |
| Cooling | Inverted → upright | 15–30 % loss of height, denser base |
| Meringue | Firm peaks → soft peaks | Weaker structure, more shrinkage, coarser cells |
| Meringue | Firm peaks → dry, over-whipped | White lumps, holes, lower volume |
| Baking powder | 3.3 → 0 % | Lower, slightly denser cake |
| Oil | 50 → 35 % | Less moist and tender, springier |
| Water | → orange juice | Flavour; slightly more tender (acid, sugar); watch browning |
| Oven | 165 → 180 °C | Faster rise, more cracking, darker crust, risk of wet centre if pulled by colour |

## Experiment suggestions

- Inverted vs upright cooling: two cakes (or bake one in two small tube pans if available); measure height at removal and after 2 h.
- Meringue stiffness series (soft, firm, dry peaks): batter SG, cake height, crumb.
- Compare with a génoise ([R1-18](recipes/R1-18-genoise.md)) after 24 h in the refrigerator: firmness by finger press and by a weight on a cube of crumb. See [X1-24](experiments/X1-24-egg-white-foam.md) for meringue variables.
