# R1-17 · Pound cake (quatre-quarts)

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established technique. The quatre-quarts ("four quarters": equal weights of flour, butter, sugar and eggs) is a classical French cake found in many professional texts; the proportions are traditional and the batch size, temperatures and control points were chosen for a home kitchen and for measurement. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a creamed pound cake with a fine, close, tender crumb and a single clean split along the top. Control butter temperature and creaming time, add eggs without breaking the emulsion, measure batter specific gravity, bake slowly to an internal temperature of 93–99 °C, and connect the formula to the balance rules of [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md).

## Expected result

- One loaf cake in a 22 × 11 cm pan, about 710–730 g after cooling, 7–9 cm tall at the split.
- Even golden-brown crust, a single central split with a slightly paler, dry interior surface.
- Close, fine, even crumb, pale yellow, moist and buttery; no tunnels, no gummy streak below the split.
- Rich butter flavour, lightly sweet, with vanilla (or lemon). Better on day 2 than on day 1.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Cake flour or soft plain flour (8–10 % protein); all-purpose also works | 100.0 | 200 |
| Caster or fine granulated sugar | 100.0 | 200 |
| Unsalted butter, at 18–20 °C | 100.0 | 200 |
| Whole eggs, out of shell (about 4 large), at 18–22 °C | 100.0 | 200 |
| Fine salt | 0.75 | 1.5 |
| Vanilla extract | 2.0 | 4 |
| Baking powder (optional, see note) | 1.0 | 2 |
| **Total** | **403.75** | **807.5** |
| *Soft butter for the piped line (not in total)* | | *5* |
| *Butter and flour for the pan (not in total)* | | *as needed* |

**Yield:** 807.5 g of batter → one 22 × 11 cm loaf (pan about 1.5 L, 6–7 cm deep), about 710–730 g baked (bake loss typically 10–12 %).
**Balance check:** sugar = flour (limit of the regular-cake rule), eggs = fat, liquids (eggs) = sugar. See [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md).
**Baking powder:** the traditional cake is leavened only by creamed air and steam. 1 % baking powder gives a slightly lighter, more forgiving cake; omit it when you want the creaming to do all the work (as in [X1-26](experiments/X1-26-creaming-time.md)).
**Lemon variant:** replace the vanilla with the finely grated zest of 1 lemon (about 2–3 g), rubbed into the sugar before creaming.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (403.75 g) fills a mini loaf pan of about 15 × 8 cm or a 15 cm round; bake 35–45 min. ×2 fills two loaf pans; do not bake a ×2 batch in one deep pan (the centre will lag badly).

## Equipment

- Scale (1 g), and a 0.1 g scale for the salt and baking powder if you have one.
- Stand mixer with paddle (or a hand mixer and a deep bowl).
- Probe thermometer; oven thermometer.
- Loaf pan **22 × 11 cm** at the top, 6–7 cm deep, light-coloured metal.
- Sieve, silicone spatula, bench scraper.
- Specific gravity cup (100–250 ml straight-sided cup; water weight known, see [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)).
- Small piping bag or paper cone; thin wooden skewer; cooling rack.

## Mise en place

1. **Temper ingredients (about 1 h ahead).** Cut the butter into 2 cm cubes and leave it until a probe in a cube reads **18–20 °C**: it dents easily under a finger without being shiny or greasy. Put the eggs (in shell) in a bowl of lukewarm water (about 35 °C) for 10 min, or leave them out for 1 h, to reach **18–22 °C**.
2. **Oven.** Rack in the lower-middle position so the top of the cake sits at mid-oven. Preheat to **165 °C (330 °F)** conventional, or 150–155 °C fan, for at least 20 min. Check with an oven thermometer.
3. **Pan.** Brush the pan with soft butter, dust with flour and tap out all excess; or butter it and line the base and long sides with a parchment sling.
4. **Dry mix.** Sift the flour, salt and baking powder (if used) together twice.
5. **Eggs.** Crack into a jug, beat lightly with a fork until uniform, weigh 200 g, and add the vanilla.
6. **Piping butter.** Put 5 g very soft butter (about 22–25 °C, paste-like) in a small piping bag or paper cone.

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Cream (t = 0 to 6 min).** Put the butter in the mixer bowl and beat with the paddle on medium (KitchenAid 4) for **1 min** until smooth. Add the sugar and beat on medium for **4–6 min**, scraping the bowl and paddle at 2 and 4 min. **Endpoint:** the mixture is pale ivory, visibly increased in volume (about 1.5–2 times), fluffy, and holds soft peaks on the paddle; it still feels slightly grainy between your fingers. Its temperature should be **19–22 °C**.

**Step 2 — Add eggs (t ≈ 6 to 12 min).** With the mixer on medium, add the eggs in **6–8 additions** of about 25–35 g, beating **30–45 s** after each until the batter is smooth and glossy again before the next. Scrape the bowl after every second addition. **Endpoint:** smooth, thick, glossy batter; no watery droplets; temperature **20–23 °C**. If it starts to look grainy and wet (curdled), stop adding egg and fix the temperature first ([10.2](stage-1/module-10/lesson-02.md)): warm the outside of the bowl briefly if the eggs were cold, chill it 5 min if the butter is too soft.

**Step 3 — Add flour (1 min).** On the lowest speed, add the sifted dry ingredients in **two parts**, mixing only until no dry flour is visible (15–20 s each). Remove the bowl and finish with 3–5 strokes of a spatula from the bottom up. Do not mix further.

**Step 4 — Measure.** Fill the SG cup level and weigh it. **Target SG about 0.8–0.9** (indicative; record yours). Batter temperature **20–24 °C**. Return the batter in the cup to the bowl with a gentle fold.

**Step 5 — Pan (2 min).** Scrape the batter into the pan (weigh the filled pan if you want bake loss: tare the empty pan first). Spread it level, then make the surface slightly lower in the centre with the back of a spoon (the centre rises most). Tap the pan once on the bench to remove large bubbles. **Pipe a line of soft butter** down the centre along the length of the cake, stopping 2 cm from each end.

**Step 6 — Bake, 50–65 min at 165 °C.** Do not open the door for the first 35 min. At 35 min, check colour; if the top is golden already, lay a sheet of foil loosely over it. **Alternative to the butter line:** at 12–15 min, when a thin skin has formed, pull the rack out and run a thin knife dipped in oil 1 cm deep along the centre, closing the door within 20 s.

**Step 7 — Doneness.** Check from 50 min. **Done when:** a probe in the centre of the thickest part, under the split, reads **93–99 °C** (aim for about 96 °C); a thin skewer inserted into the split comes out clean or with a few dry crumbs; the inside of the split looks dry, not glossy; the cake has started to pull away from the sides.

**Step 8 — Cool.** Cool in the pan on a rack for **10–15 min**, then run a thin knife around the edges, turn out and cool upright on the rack for at least **1.5 h** (centre below 30 °C) before wrapping.

**Step 9 — Store.** Wrap tightly in film when fully cool. Keep at room temperature for up to **4–5 days**; flavour and texture are best on day 2. Freeze wrapped for up to about 2 months. Do not refrigerate a plain pound cake ([10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md)).

**Safety:** Raw batter contains raw egg and raw flour: do not taste it ([FDA-eggs], [FDA-flour]). Contains wheat, egg and milk. Use dry oven gloves; the loaf pan stays hot for several minutes after unmoulding.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature | 18–20 °C at the start of creaming | Too cold: lumps, little air. Too warm: greasy, cells cannot be held |
| Creaming time | 4–6 min at medium, pale and fluffy | Sets the number of air cells, which sets crumb and volume |
| Egg temperature and rate | 18–22 °C; 6–8 additions, smooth each time | Cold or fast additions break the emulsion; air is lost |
| Mixing after flour | Just combined (under 1 min) | Longer mixing develops gluten: tunnels, peaked top, chewy crumb |
| Batter SG | about 0.8–0.9 (your target ± 0.03) | Objective check of aeration |
| Oven | 160–170 °C, measured | Hotter: dark thick crust, big dome, raw centre |
| Internal temperature | 93–99 °C, skewer clean | Below 93 °C: gummy streak under the split, sinking |
| Cooling | 10–15 min in pan, then rack | Earlier: breaks. Later: steam softens sides, sticks |

## Scientific explanation

Creaming at 18–20 °C uses the edges of sugar crystals to cut air into plastic butter, whose fat crystals stabilize the air cells ([10.2](stage-1/module-10/lesson-02.md), [SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md)). Eggs are then emulsified into this fat-continuous foam with the help of yolk lecithin and proteins ([SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)). The resulting cells are the only nuclei in the batter: in the oven, air expansion and steam (and CO₂, if baking powder is used) inflate them, but create no new ones ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)). Equal weights of sugar and flour dissolve enough sugar into the eggs' water to delay starch gelatinization and egg coagulation until late in the bake, which gives the tender, close crumb; the equal weight of eggs supplies the protein that sets the structure and the water the starch needs. The deep, dense batter needs a moderate oven so the heat front reaches the centre before the crust over-browns, and the piped butter line keeps one strip of the surface fluid longest, so the inevitable crack forms there. The high fat and sugar content slows staling ([10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Creaming time | 6 → 2 min | Higher SG, lower, denser, coarser cake ([X1-26](experiments/X1-26-creaming-time.md)) |
| Mixing method | Creaming → reverse creaming | Finer, more tender, flatter crumb; slightly less volume ([X1-27](experiments/X1-27-reverse-creaming.md)) |
| Baking powder | 0 → 1 % | Slightly lighter and more open; more forgiving of under-creaming |
| Flour | Cake flour → all-purpose | Slightly firmer, more domed, a little coarser ([X1-28](experiments/X1-28-cake-flour.md)) |
| Sugar | 100 → 120 % | Moister, sweeter, darker crust; risk of sunken centre ([X1-29](experiments/X1-29-cake-formula-balance.md)) |
| Eggs | Replace 50 g whole egg with 50 g yolks | Richer, more tender, yellower, slightly lower |
| Fat | Part butter → part neutral oil (25 % of fat) | Softer, moister crumb when cool; less aeration at creaming |
| Oven | 165 → 180 °C | Shorter bake, bigger dome, thicker darker crust, risk of raw centre |
