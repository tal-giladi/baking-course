# R1-27 · Crème Chantilly

<span class="tag established">Established technique</span>

Established technique: sweetened, vanilla-flavoured whipped cream at the standard professional sugar level of 8–10 % of the cream. The stabilized variants use standard professional doses. Quantities are written for this course.

## Objective

Whip cream to a chosen stage (soft peak for folding, medium–firm peak for piping) by controlling temperature and stopping at a measurable endpoint, measure overrun, and choose a stabilizer when the cream must hold for a day.

## Expected result

About **1.0–1.1 L** of white, glossy, satiny whipped cream at medium–firm peak (overrun about 90–110 %). It holds a sharp star-tip rosette with defined ridges, feels light and cold on the tongue with no graininess, and tastes of fresh cream and vanilla, lightly sweet. Unstabilized, it holds its shape for 2–4 h in the refrigerator; stabilized, for 24–48 h with little or no liquid draining.

## Formula

Percentages are of the cream.

| Ingredient | % of cream | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Cream, 35–40 % fat, pasteurized (not UHT if you have a choice), at 2–5 °C | 100 | 500 |
| Icing (powdered) sugar | 8 | 40 |
| Vanilla extract (or seeds of ½ pod) | 1 | 5 |
| **Total** | **109** | **545** |

**Yield:** 545 g; about 1.05 L at 100 % overrun. Enough to fill and cover a 20 cm two-layer sponge, or to fill about 20 profiteroles and top them.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 250 g cream, 20 g icing sugar, 2.5 g vanilla (272.5 g); use a smaller bowl so the whisk reaches the cream. ×2 = 1,000 g cream, 80 g icing sugar, 10 g vanilla (1,090 g); a 5 L mixer bowl handles this; watch the last stage closely because the bowl warms.

**Sweetness:** 8 % is lightly sweet (for pairing with sweet components); 10 % (50 g) is classic for serving on its own.

### Stabilized variants (choose one)

| Variant | Change to the formula | Grams (on 500 g cream base) |
|---|---|---|
| Gelatin | Add powdered gelatin 0.6 % + cold water 3 % | 3 g gelatin bloomed in 15 g water (total 563 g) |
| Mascarpone | Replace 20 % of the cream with mascarpone | 400 g cream + 100 g mascarpone (total 545 g) |
| Milk powder | Add skimmed milk powder 4 % | 20 g, sifted with the icing sugar (total 565 g) |

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g (0.1 g for gelatin); probe thermometer.
- Stand mixer with whisk, or a large balloon whisk and a stainless bowl (3–4 L).
- A larger bowl with ice and water to set the whipping bowl in (warm kitchens or hand whisking).
- Flexible spatula; sieve for icing sugar.
- For overrun: a straight-sided cup or ramekin (150–250 ml) and an offset spatula to level it.
- Piping bag with a medium star tip for the test rosette.

## Mise en place

- Chill the mixer bowl and whisk in the freezer or refrigerator for **10–15 min**. Keep the cream in the coldest part of the refrigerator until the moment you use it.
- Sift the icing sugar. For the gelatin variant, bloom the gelatin in the cold water for 10 min.
- **Overrun reference:** fill the cup level with liquid cream, weigh it, and record the net weight of cream (tare the cup first). Pour it back.
- Record the room temperature and the cream temperature (target **≤ 5 °C**).

## Procedure

1. **Start whipping.** Pour the cold cream into the cold bowl. Whisk at **medium** speed (mixer speed 4–6 of 10) for about **30–60 s** until frothy and starting to thicken.
2. **Add sugar and vanilla at soft trails.** When the whisk leaves trails that fade slowly (about 1–2 min), add the icing sugar and vanilla. (Mascarpone variant: beforehand, loosen the mascarpone with the sugar and 50 g of the cream until smooth, then add it now.)
3. **Gelatin variant:** melt the bloomed gelatin to **40–50 °C**, whisk in about 30 g of the cream to temper it (it should be about 30–35 °C), then, with the mixer running at medium, pour it in a thin stream into the cream at the soft-trail stage.
4. **Whip to soft peak** at medium-high: about **1–2 min** more. The cream is glossy; a peak lifted on the whisk flops over. **Stop here for folding** (diplomate, mousses, topping a dessert).
5. **Finish to medium–firm peak for piping.** Reduce to low speed or finish by hand with a balloon whisk, checking every **10–15 s**. **Endpoint:** the peak holds and only its tip bends; the surface is still smooth and satiny, not matte. Total whipping time is typically **2–4 min** with a mixer, **4–6 min** by hand.
6. **Measure.** Fill the reference cup level with whipped cream (no air pockets; tap it down gently) and weigh. Overrun % = (weight of cup of liquid − weight of cup of whipped) ÷ weight of cup of whipped × 100. Target at medium–firm peak: **90–110 %**. Record the final cream temperature (it should still be **≤ 8 °C**).
7. **Use or store.** Pipe or fill immediately. Store covered at **≤ 5 °C**. Unstabilized: use within **2–4 h** (re-whisk briefly by hand if it has loosened). Stabilized: within **24–48 h**.
8. **If slightly overwhipped** (surface matte, texture starting to roughen): fold in **25–50 g** cold liquid cream by hand with a whisk until glossy again.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fat content | 35–40 % (≥ 30 % minimum) | Enough fat to form the partially coalesced network |
| Cream temperature | ≤ 5 °C at the start, ≤ 8 °C at the end; chilled bowl | Fat globules must be partly crystalline for partial coalescence |
| Sugar timing | At soft trails | Early sugar slows foaming; late sugar may not dissolve |
| Stage | Soft peak for folding; medium–firm for piping | Folding adds work; stiff cream turns grainy |
| Last stage | Low speed or by hand, check every 10–15 s | Firm → grainy can take 10–20 s |
| Gelatin | Tempered, 30–35 °C, streamed into moving cream | Cold cream sets cold gelatin into strings |
| Storage | ≤ 5 °C; 2–4 h plain, 24–48 h stabilized | Weeping over time; food safety |

## Common failures

- **Will not whip, or whips then collapses.** See [Whipped cream will not whip](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md?id=whipped-cream-will-not-whip).
- **Grainy, curdled or turning to butter.** See [Overwhipped or grainy whipped cream](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md?id=overwhipped-or-grainy-whipped-cream).
- **Liquid pooling under piped cream or in the container.** See [Whipped cream weeping](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md?id=whipped-cream-weeping).
- **Gritty.** Granulated sugar that did not dissolve; use icing sugar or caster sugar added early.

## Scientific explanation

Whipping first traps air bubbles stabilized by milk proteins. As whipping continues, fat globules collide with the bubbles and with each other; the air interface strips part of their membranes, and fat crystals protruding from the cold, partly solid globules pierce neighbouring globules so that they stick without merging (partial coalescence). The clusters build a fat network that coats the bubbles and fills the space between them, and the foam stiffens ([12.3](stage-1/module-12/lesson-03.md), [SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)). The mechanism depends on the ratio of solid to liquid fat, so it depends on temperature: warm cream has too much liquid fat, globules fully merge, and the foam is weak and greasy ([SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md)).

Over-whipping continues the coalescence until fat clusters become butter grains that push the air and water out: the emulsion inverts. The dissolved sugar thickens the liquid between bubbles, slowing drainage. Gelatin gels that liquid when cold; mascarpone adds fat and solids to the network; milk powder adds protein and lactose to the serum. Each slows weeping.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fat content | 30 → 40 % | Slower to overwhip → faster whipping, firmer, more stable, less airy |
| Cream temperature | 3 → 12 °C | Best stability → slow, low volume, greasy |
| Sugar | 0 → 12 % | Sweeter, slightly lower overrun, slower drainage |
| Whipping speed | Low → high | High: faster, coarser bubbles, easier to overshoot; low: finer, more stable foam |
| Stabilizer | None / gelatin / mascarpone / milk powder | Holding time from hours to 1–2 days (see formula) |
| Heat treatment | Pasteurized → UHT | Slower, softer, weeps sooner |
| Flavour | Cocoa (5–8 %, sifted with sugar), coffee extract, liqueur (≤ 5 %) | Cocoa firms it; alcohol and extra liquid soften it |

## Experiment suggestions

- **Temperature series:** whip three 150 g portions of the same cream at 4, 8 and 12 °C (warm in a water bath, check with the probe). Record time to soft peak, overrun and weeping in a sieve over a cup after 2 h.
- **Fat series:** 30 % vs 35 % vs 40 % cream (or blend 48 % double cream with milk to set the fat level) at the same temperature.
- **Stabilizer comparison:** plain vs gelatin vs mascarpone; pipe 3 rosettes each and weigh the liquid drained after 24 h.
