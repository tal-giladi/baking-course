# R1-34 · Croissant and pain au chocolat

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a French all-butter croissant with a lightly enriched, short-mixed détrempe retarded overnight, an open (butt-joint) lock-in, one double and one single turn, sheeted and shaped by hand. Proportions sit in the middle of standard professional ranges and are sized for a home kitchen. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Laminate a yeasted dough without losing the butter layers to heat, sheet it by hand to 4 mm, shape croissants and pains au chocolat, proof them at 24–27 °C to 2–2.5 times their size, and bake them to a deep brown with an open, regular honeycomb crumb and no butter pooling on the tray.

## Expected result

- **Croissants:** 55–60 g raw, about 13–15 cm long and 6–8 cm tall after baking, with 3–4 distinct, well-separated steps along the body, pointed or slightly curved ends, and a tip tucked underneath. **Pains au chocolat:** about 53 g raw plus two batons, rectangular, rising to about 5–6 cm.
- Crust: deep golden-brown to mahogany, including the underside and the creases between the steps; glossy from the egg wash; crisp and flaky, shedding thin shards when you press it.
- Very light in the hand (a baked croissant weighs about 85–90 % of its raw weight but has several times its volume).
- Crumb, cut lengthwise: an **open honeycomb** of large, elongated cells separated by thin, continuous, slightly glossy walls, radiating from the centre; no dense, doughy or bready core.
- Flavour of browned butter and lightly fermented dough, slightly sweet, not yeasty.
- Tray after baking: a thin film of butter at most, no pools.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Détrempe** | | |
| Bread flour (12–13 % protein) | 50.0 | 250 |
| Plain / all-purpose flour (10–11 % protein) | 50.0 | 250 |
| Water, cold | 28.0 | 140 |
| Whole milk, cold | 28.0 | 140 |
| Sugar | 11.0 | 55 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 10 |
| Instant yeast, osmotolerant (gold) preferred | 1.2 | 6 |
| Unsalted butter, soft | 6.0 | 30 |
| *Détrempe subtotal* | *176.2* | *881* |
| **Beurrage** | | |
| Unsalted butter, ≥ 82 % fat | 55.0 | 275 |
| **Total** | **231.2** | **1,156** |
| *Egg wash (not in total)* | | *1 egg + 1 yolk + pinch of salt, beaten and strained* |
| *Pain au chocolat (not in total)* | | *20 chocolate batons (about 8 cm, 4–5 g each), about 90 g* |

**Flour:** the 50:50 blend gives about 11.5–12 % protein. A single flour of 11.5–12.5 % protein (a strong all-purpose or French T45 *gruau*) works too.
**Liquid:** water + milk = 56 % of flour. **True hydration:** water 140 + milk 140 × 0.87 + détrempe butter 30 × 0.16 = 266.6 g → **53 %**. A firm, smooth dough when cold.
**Sugar in the water phase:** 55 ÷ (55 + 267) ≈ 17 %: osmotolerant yeast is the better choice. With standard instant yeast, use 1.5 % (7.5 g) and expect a slightly longer proof. **Fresh yeast:** about 3× the instant weight, 3.6 % (18 g), crumbled into the liquid.
**Beurrage:** 275 ÷ 1,156 ≈ **24 %** of the laminated dough.
**Layers:** open lock-in + 1 double + 1 single → 4 × 3 = **12** fat layers ([15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)). Alternative: 3 singles → 27.
**Yield:** about 1,150 g of laminated dough → **18 croissants** (55–60 g), or **9 croissants + 10 pains au chocolat**, with about 120–170 g of trim.
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (578 g, 250 g flour, plaque 138 g at 13.5 × 13.5 cm) is easier to laminate by hand and gives 9 croissants; it is the batch used in [X1-40](experiments/X1-40-croissant-temperature.md). ×2 is too big to sheet by hand on a home bench; make two blocks.
**Allergens:** wheat (gluten), milk, egg (wash); chocolate may contain soy and traces of nuts.

## Equipment

- Stand mixer with dough hook (or by hand: see step 1).
- Scale (1 g, and 0.1 g for the yeast if possible), probe thermometer, ruler (at least 50 cm) or tape measure.
- Heavy rolling pin, 4 mm guides if you have them, pastry brush (dry) and a soft brush for egg wash.
- Parchment, cling film, a tray that fits the refrigerator.
- Sharp long knife or pizza wheel.
- Two baking trays lined with parchment.
- A proofing space at 24–27 °C with humidity: a large lidded plastic box or the oven (off) with a thermometer and a bowl of hot water, replaced every 45 minutes. Never above 28 °C.

## Mise en place

1. **Timeline (two days).**
   - Day 1, evening: détrempe (20 min) → 30 min at room temperature → flatten and refrigerate 8–16 h. Make the plaque.
   - Day 2: lock-in and double turn (20 min) → rest 30 min → single turn (10 min) → rest 1–2 h → sheet, cut, shape (40 min) → proof 2–3 h → bake 20 min. About 6 hours on day 2, 1.5 h of it hands-on.
2. **Water temperature for DDT 22–24 °C.** For a short mix with little friction, water temperature ≈ 3 × DDT − flour temperature − room temperature − friction (about 3–5 °C for this short mix) ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)). Treat the milk as part of the water. For flour 20 °C, room 21 °C, friction 4 °C, DDT 23 °C: 69 − 20 − 21 − 4 = **24 °C** liquid.
3. Clear refrigerator space for a 20 × 30 cm tray.

## Procedure

### Day 1

**1. Mix the détrempe (DDT 22–24 °C).** Combine flours, sugar and yeast in the mixer bowl; add water, milk and the soft butter; add the salt on top. Speed 1 for **3 minutes** until no dry flour remains, then speed 2 for **3–4 minutes**. **Endpoint: moderate development.** The dough is cohesive and fairly smooth, pulls away from the bowl, and a piece stretches into a thick, opaque membrane that tears before it becomes translucent. **Do not mix to a full windowpane**: the turns and sheeting will develop the gluten further, and a fully developed détrempe fights the rolling pin and shrinks. By hand: mix in a bowl until combined, then knead on the bench for 5–7 minutes to the same endpoint. Record the dough temperature.

**2. Short rest (30 minutes at room temperature),** covered. Fermentation starts; the gluten relaxes.

**3. Flatten and retard (8–16 h at 3–5 °C).** Press the dough into a rectangle about **20 × 25 cm, 2 cm thick**, on a floured tray, wrap tightly and refrigerate. A flat shape chills through in about an hour, which stops fermentation running on in a warm centre. The dough will have risen a little by morning.

**4. Butter plaque.** Beat 275 g of cold butter between parchment into a pliable slab, then roll it to fill a parchment packet of **19 × 19 cm** (299 cm³ ÷ 361 cm² ≈ **8 mm**). Refrigerate flat.

### Day 2

**5. Temper the plaque (10–20 minutes).** Take the plaque out and let it reach **12–15 °C**: it bends without cracking. The détrempe comes out of the refrigerator only when you are ready to roll it (it should be 4–8 °C). Press both with a finger: they should resist about equally ([15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md)).

**6. Open lock-in.** Roll the détrempe to **21 × 40 cm** (about 9 mm). Place the plaque in the centre across the width, leaving about 1 cm of dough on each side. Fold both ends of the dough over the butter so they **meet in the middle without overlapping** (butt joint). Pinch the centre seam and both open sides closed. You have a block of about 21 × 20 cm: one fat layer.

**7. Double turn (book fold).** Rotate the block so the seam runs away from you. Roll to about **20 × 70 cm** (about 8 mm), from the centre outwards, keeping the sides straight. Brush off flour. Fold the bottom end up to about one-third of the way; fold the top end down to meet it (the ends butt together slightly off-centre); then fold the whole thing in half along that seam, like closing a book. Block about 20 × 17 cm, 4 layers of the previous stack: **4 fat layers**. Mark it with one fingertip dent. Wrap and refrigerate **30 minutes**.
- Check the block temperature before and after rolling: target **10–15 °C**. If the butter shows through or smears, stop, wrap and chill 15 minutes.

**8. Single turn (letter fold).** Rotate 90° so the fold is on your left. Roll to about **20 × 55 cm**. Brush off flour. Fold in three. Block about 20 × 18 cm: **12 fat layers**. Two dents. Wrap and refrigerate **1–2 hours** (the dough relaxes and the butter re-firms; fermentation continues slowly).
- *Alternative:* three single turns (27 fat layers), with 30 minutes' rest after each. Finer layers, slightly less open honeycomb.

**9. Sheet to 4 mm.** Cut the block in half across its length and return one half to the refrigerator.
- **For croissants:** roll the half, in both directions, to a rectangle about **26 × 52 cm and 4 mm thick**. The last few passes should be gentle; lift the sheet off the bench and let it relax back for 1–2 minutes (it will shrink; roll it back gently). If it keeps springing back, refrigerate it flat on a tray for 15 minutes and continue. Keep the dough cold: if it becomes soft and sticky or the kitchen is warm, chill it between steps.
- **For pains au chocolat:** roll the other half to about **30 × 44 cm, 4 mm**.
- *Home technique:* rolling-pin strokes from the centre outwards, first along the length then across. Rotate the sheet 90° now and then to keep it square. Lift and flour lightly underneath (and brush off the top) so it can shrink freely. Measure; don't guess.

**10. Cut.**
- **Croissants:** trim the long edges straight so the strip is exactly **25 cm** wide. Along one long edge, mark every **10 cm**; along the other, mark every 10 cm starting at 5 cm. Cut from mark to mark with a straight-down press of the knife or a pizza wheel: a row of alternating triangles, **10 cm base × 25 cm tall**. Weigh the first two: target **55–60 g**. Lighter means the sheet is too thin; roll the next sheet a little thicker.
- **Pains au chocolat:** trim to 30 × 40 cm and cut into **10 rectangles of 8 × 15 cm** (two rows of five). About 53 g each.

**11. Shape.**
- **Croissant:** cut a **1.5 cm notch** in the centre of the base (optional: it lets the base spread into wider "legs"). Holding the base, stretch the triangle gently to about **28 cm** by pulling the tip while running your other hand down the length. Roll from the base towards the tip with both hands, **lightly, without pressing down** (pressing seals the layers), with a little tension so the roll is snug. You should get **3–4 steps**. Place on the tray **with the tip underneath**, touching the tray, so it cannot unroll. Straight croissants are the French all-butter convention; curve the ends if you like.
- **Pain au chocolat:** place a baton 1 cm from a short end; fold the dough over it; place the second baton at the fold; roll the rest over, finishing **seam-down**.
- Space the pieces **at least 5 cm apart**, 8–9 per tray: they more than double in the proof.

**12. First egg wash.** Brush lightly, from the centre outwards along the steps, **not into the cut edges**. It keeps the surface moist in the proof.

**13. Proof (2–3 hours at 24–27 °C, 75–85 % relative humidity; never above 28 °C).** Put the trays in the proofing box with a bowl of hot water, lid on (or loosely covered so nothing touches the dough). Put a thermometer next to them. **Done when:**
- volume is about **2–2.5×** the shaped size;
- the pieces **jiggle** like set jelly when you shake the tray gently;
- the layers are clearly visible at the sides, separated slightly, with the dough between them looking puffy and airy;
- a gentle press with a floured finger leaves a dent that springs back very slowly.
Cold dough spends the first 45–60 minutes just warming up; judge after 1.5 h, then every 20 minutes. **Butter on the tray during the proof** means the proof is too warm: lower it at once.

**14. Preheat.** Fan oven to **200 °C (390 °F)**, or conventional to **210 °C (410 °F)**, for at least 20 minutes, rack in the middle. Take the trays out of the proofing box about 10 minutes before baking so the surface dries slightly (a dry skin takes the second wash better).

**15. Second egg wash.** Very gently: fully proofed croissants deflate if you press them. Wash the top surfaces only.

**16. Bake.** Load and reduce to **190 °C fan (375 °F)** or **200 °C conventional (390 °F)**. Bake **16–20 minutes**, rotating the tray at 10 minutes (one tray at a time in a home oven gives the most even result; with two trays in a fan oven, swap them at 10 minutes).
- **Done:** deep golden-brown to mahogany everywhere, including the creases between the steps and the underside; the pieces feel very light and sound hollow. Pale creases mean the inner layers are still wet: give 2–3 minutes more (tent with foil if the tops are dark).
- Bake the second tray the same way; keep it in the refrigerator loosely covered if the proof finishes much earlier than the oven is free (see [15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md) on holding).

**17. Cool** on a rack at least 20 minutes (the structure sets as the steam leaves). Cut one lengthwise with a serrated knife and photograph the crumb with a ruler.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Raw dough contains raw flour and (after washing) raw egg: do not taste it ([FDA-flour], [FDA-eggs]). A proofing oven with a bowl of hot water must be emptied before you preheat. Butter that has dripped onto the oven floor can smoke: bake on trays with a lip or parchment that extends to the edges.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Détrempe DDT | 22–24 °C | Controlled fermentation before the retard |
| Détrempe mix | Moderate development, no full windowpane | Turns and sheeting finish the gluten; overmixing causes shrinkage and tearing |
| Retard | 8–16 h at 3–5 °C, flattened | Cold, relaxed, flavoured dough of the right firmness for lock-in |
| Plaque | 19 × 19 cm, 8 mm, 12–15 °C at lock-in | Even layers; plastic, not brittle |
| Firmness match | Dough 4–8 °C, butter 12–15 °C, equal resistance | Layers thin together |
| Block during turns | 10–15 °C; rest if > 16 °C | Warm butter smears (bready crumb); cold butter shatters |
| Turns | 1 double + 1 single (12 fat layers) | Dough leaves thick enough to form honeycomb |
| Sheeting | 4 mm, relaxed, cold | Weight and step definition; shrink-free triangles |
| Shaping | Light rolling, no pressing; tip underneath | Pressing seals layers; loose tip unrolls |
| Proof | 24–27 °C, humid, 2–2.5×, jiggle; never > 28 °C | Butter melts above ~28 °C: layers fuse, butter leaks |
| Oven | 190 °C fan / 200 °C conventional after a hotter preheat | Set leaves while steam lifts; full colour without burning |
| Doneness | Deep colour including creases and base | Pale creases = wet inner layers, collapse on cooling |

## Scientific explanation

A croissant is a laminated bread. Each of its 13 dough layers is a thin sheet of lightly enriched, fermented dough, separated from its neighbours by 12 layers of butter. During the proof, yeast gas inflates the dough layers; the butter layers, if they are still solid and plastic (below about 28 °C), stretch with them and keep the dough layers apart ([SC-08](stage-1/science/sc-08-microbiology.md), [SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md)). In the oven, gas expansion and steam from the dough's water (53 % true hydration) and the butter's water (about 16 %) lift the layers apart while the melting butter fries them ([SC-11](stage-1/science/sc-11-phase-changes-gases.md)). The honeycomb is the result: large cells formed where adjacent gas-filled dough layers were separated by fat, walled by the set, fat-fried dough. If the butter layers are lost, either smeared into the dough during lamination or melted during a warm proof, the dough layers fuse into one thicker dough and the crumb becomes that of a buttery brioche: fine, even and bready. Sugar (11 %), milk and the egg wash give Maillard browning ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)); the moderate gluten development and the 6 % butter in the détrempe keep the dough extensible enough to sheet to 4 mm and roll without tearing ([15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Turns | 1D + 1S (12) → 3S (27) | Finer, more even layers; smaller honeycomb cells; slightly less height |
| Turns | → 4S (81) or more | Layers fuse; brioche-like crumb |
| Beurrage | 55 → 45 % | Less flaky, more bready, easier to laminate |
| Beurrage | 55 → 65 % | Flakier, richer, more fragile; harder to keep cold; more leakage risk |
| Sugar | 11 → 6 % | Paler, less sweet, faster proof; more like a "pain" croissant |
| Détrempe mixing | Moderate → windowpane | More shrinkage and resistance when sheeting |
| Proof temperature | 25 → 31 °C | Faster proof but butter softens: leakage, lower volume, bready crumb ([X1-40](experiments/X1-40-croissant-temperature.md)) |
| Proof level | 2–2.5× → 1.5× | Tight, dense, butter leaks from tight layers during bake |
| Sheet thickness | 4 → 3 mm | More, smaller steps; lighter croissant; thinner dough leaves |
| Flour | Blend → strong bread flour only | More volume, chewier, more shrinkage |
| Preferment | Add 20–30 % of the flour as poolish or pâte fermentée | More flavour and extensibility (Stage 2) |
