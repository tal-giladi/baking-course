# R1-39 · Fruit tart

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: the classical French fresh fruit tart (tarte aux fruits) of a fully baked, sealed pâte sucrée shell, crème pâtissière and glazed fresh fruit. This is an **assembly recipe**: the components are made from their own recipes, linked below, and this page gives the quantities for one 22 cm tart, the order of work, and the control points at assembly. Component weights are standard professional proportions, written for this course.

## Objective

Assemble a fruit tart whose shell is still crisp at service, whose cream holds a clean vertical cut, and whose fruit is glossy and tightly arranged, by controlling three things: how fully the shell is baked and sealed, the water brought in by the fruit, and the time between filling and service.

## Expected result

- **Whole tart (22 cm × 2 cm):** about 1,100 g. Straight golden walls; fruit arranged in tight, even rings rising 1–2 cm above the rim, with a thin, even, glossy glaze and no pooling; no cream visible between fruit except by design.
- **Slice (1/8, about 135–140 g):** clean cut showing a 3 mm shell, a level band of cream about 1 cm deep, fruit on top. The base breaks with a snap under the fork; the cream is cold, smooth and holds its cut face without slumping; no juice pool on the plate.
- **Taste:** buttery shell, vanilla cream, sharp fruit; sweet but not cloying.

## Formula

### Components for one 22 cm tart

**Yield:** one 22 cm tart, about 1,100 g; 8 portions of about 137 g.

**Optional barrier:** 15 g cocoa butter (or tempered dark or white chocolate, [R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)), brushed over the egg-wash seal: use it whenever the tart will be held more than about 4 h after filling. Adds about 15 g (1.4 %) to the tart.

### Fruit (example mix, 450 g prepared)

| Fruit | Grams prepared | Preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Strawberries | 250 | Hulled, halved lengthways, cut within 1 h of assembly |
| Raspberries | 125 | Not washed unless needed; picked over |
| Blueberries | 75 | Washed, dried on paper towel |
| **Total** | **450** | |

Any firm fruit works: apricot or plum halves (poached if hard), figs, peeled citrus segments (drained on paper for 30 min), kiwi slices, grapes, mango. See the protease note under Critical control points if you use diplomate.

### Apricot glaze

| Ingredient | % of jam | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Apricot jam (smooth or sieved) | 100 | 100 |
| Water | 15 | 15 |
| **Total** | **115** | **115** |

Use 60–80 g on the tart; the rest keeps refrigerated for weeks. A commercial neutral glaze (nappage neutre) can replace it: dilute and heat exactly as its maker specifies.

### Crème diplomate option

For a lighter filling served within the day, replace the 350 g crème pâtissière with 350 g crème diplomate ([R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md)): 240 g pâtissière + 120 g cream whipped to soft peak + 2.4 g gelatin bloomed in 12 g water (374.4 g made). It must set in the shell 1–2 h at ≤ 5 °C before the fruit goes on.

**Scaling by ring area** (multiply every component): 18 cm ×0.67; 20 cm ×0.83; 24 cm ×1.19; 26 cm ×1.40. For 8 cm tartlets, the ×1 quantities of cream, fruit and glaze fill about 7–8 tartlets, but tartlets need about 40 % more shell per portion (make the whole [R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md) batch).

<div class="callout safety">

**Allergens:** gluten, egg, milk, tree nuts (almond, in the sucrée). With diplomate: gelatin (animal-derived). A cream-filled product: keep at ≤ 5 °C and eat within 24 h.

## Equipment

- 22 cm × 2 cm tart ring (or fluted tin with removable base), tray, parchment, baking weights (from [R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md)).
- Scale (1 g); probe thermometer.
- Pastry brushes: one for egg wash, one soft brush for glaze (and one for cocoa butter if used).
- Piping bag with a 12–14 mm plain tip; small offset spatula.
- Paring knife and cutting board; paper towels; small saucepan and fine sieve for the glaze.
- Whisk or stand mixer with paddle to re-texture the cream.
- A flat, level space in the refrigerator; a cake board or flat plate for the finished tart.

## Mise en place

- **Two days before to the day before:** sucrée dough made and rested at least 2 h (ideally overnight) ([R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md)).
- **The day before:** crème pâtissière cooked, spread 2 cm thick under film in contact and chilled to ≤ 5 °C ([R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md)). Shell lined, frozen, blind baked to **full bake**, sealed, cooled and stored airtight at room temperature.
- **On the day:** tools out; glaze ingredients weighed; fruit bought ripe but firm; plate or board for the tart chilled.

## Procedure

### Shell (day before, or the morning of service)

1. **Line and bake** the shell as in [R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md) steps 6–11: roll 2.5–3 mm, line, dock, freeze 20–30 min, bake at **165–170 °C (330–340 °F)** with weights 15–18 min, then without weights **8–12 min** to a **full bake**. **Endpoint:** evenly golden, including the centre of the base (lift the shell with a palette knife and look underneath); matt and dry inside; a hollow sound when tapped.
2. **Seal with egg wash.** Mix the yolk with the cream. While the shell is still hot, brush the inside (base, corner and walls) thinly. Return to the oven **2–3 min**. **Endpoint:** the wash is set and glossy, with no wet patches. Brush a second thin coat on any pale spots and bake 1 min more.
3. **Cool** on a rack to room temperature (at least 30 min). Remove the ring. Store airtight if not using within the hour.

### Barrier (optional, up to 4 h before filling)

4. Melt the cocoa butter to **35–40 °C** (or temper chocolate). Brush a thin, complete film over the base and walls, **starting with the base–wall corner**. Hold the shell up to the light: no bare spots. Let set 5–10 min at room temperature (≤ 22 °C).

### Glaze

5. Bring the jam and water to a **full boil**, stirring, and boil 30 s. Pass through a fine sieve. Keep warm; reheat to just below boiling before use. **Endpoint:** a spoonful dropped on a cold plate sets to a soft gel within 1 min.

### Fruit (within 1 h of assembly)

6. Wash only fruit that needs it; drain on paper towels and let the surface air-dry 15–20 min. Hull and halve strawberries lengthways; cut other fruit into even slices (4–6 mm) or halves. Keep cut fruit on paper, cut side down, in the refrigerator. **Endpoint:** no visible water on any surface.

### Assembly

7. **Re-texture the cream.** Turn the cold pastry cream (≤ 5 °C) into a bowl and whisk **20–40 s** until smooth and glossy. Stop there. Fill the piping bag.
8. **Fill.** Weigh the shell on its board and tare. Pipe the cream in a spiral from the centre outward, then level with the offset spatula. **Target: 350 g**, surface **5–7 mm below the rim**, level, with a clean shell rim.
9. **Arrange the fruit.** Start at the rim: a ring of strawberry halves, cut face outward and all leaning the same way, pressed 2–3 mm into the cream. Work inward in tight concentric rings, alternating fruit colours; finish with raspberries and blueberries in the centre and gaps. **Target: 450 g** (check on the scale). No cream visible between rings.
10. **Glaze.** At **80–90 °C** (just off the boil), dab glaze onto the fruit with a soft brush, starting with the outer ring, keeping the coat thin and even; keep glaze off the shell rim. **Target: 60–80 g.** If the glaze thickens in the pan or goes on streaky, reheat it.
11. **Chill** at **≤ 5 °C**, uncovered or in a tall box, at least 20–30 min for the glaze and cream to set. Serve within about **4–6 h** for best texture; within **24 h** at the latest (a complete cocoa butter barrier is needed to keep the shell crisp that long).

### Slicing

12. Take the tart out 10–15 min before cutting. Cut 8 portions with a guide: first through the fruit with a serrated knife, then press a long, thin knife (hot, dry) straight down through cream and shell. Wipe the knife between cuts. Lift each slice with a palette knife slid under the base.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shell bake | Full bake: golden centre of the base, matt, hollow sound | A pale base is already high in moisture and softens within an hour of filling |
| Seal | Egg wash set and glossy; barrier film complete, especially in the base–wall corner | Slows water migration from the cream (aw ~0.97) into the shell (aw ~0.3) ([X1-43](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md)) |
| Pastry cream | Boiled 1–2 min when made; ≤ 5 °C; re-textured only 20–40 s | Under-boiled or over-whisked cream thins, weeps and will not hold a cut |
| Shell temperature at filling | Room temperature | Warm shell melts the barrier and warms the cream |
| Fruit surface | Dry; cut within 1 h | Free water and juice run into the cream and make glaze slide |
| Glaze temperature | 80–90 °C at application; thin coat | Cooler glaze has started to gel: streaky, cloudy, thick |
| Holding | ≤ 5 °C; serve within 4–6 h (24 h max) | Crispness and food safety for a high-aw cream filling |
| Protease fruit with diplomate | Raw kiwi, pineapple, papaya, mango, fig only if served within 2–3 h | Their proteases liquefy gelatin ([SC-09](stage-1/science/sc-09-enzymes.md)) |

## Scientific explanation

The fruit tart combines three components at very different water activities: a baked shell (aw about 0.3), pastry cream (about 0.97) and fresh fruit (about 0.98–0.99). Water moves from high aw to low aw until they are closer to equal, and the shell, being a glassy starch–sugar–protein solid, loses its snap after only a small moisture gain ([SC-01](stage-1/science/sc-01-water.md), [17.1](stage-1/module-17/lesson-01.md)). Every control in this recipe acts on that migration: a full bake starts the shell as dry as possible; the egg-wash seal adds a coagulated protein film that slows water entry; a cocoa butter film adds a hydrophobic layer that water crosses very slowly; drying the fruit removes free surface water; the glaze holds fruit juices at the surface; and cold holding slows every step.

The cream holds its cut because it is a starch gel: gelatinized cornstarch with an amylose network formed on cooling, stabilized by the butterfat ([12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md)). Whisking for 20–40 s breaks the set gel into a smooth paste that re-forms a weaker but sufficient structure in the shell; prolonged whisking breaks swollen granules and thins it permanently. The apricot glaze is a pectin–sugar gel: boiling dissolves the pectin network, and it gels again as it cools on the fruit, forming a thin film that limits evaporation (fruit stays plump) and oxidation (cut surfaces stay bright). Sugar in the glaze also draws juice out of cut fruit by osmosis, which is why cut fruit is prepared late and glazed thinly.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Seal | None → egg wash → egg wash + cocoa butter | Crisp shell life under cream from about 1–2 h → 4–6 h → about 24 h |
| Filling | Pâtissière → diplomate | Lighter, less sweet-tasting, softer; must set 1–2 h; 24 h life |
| Almond layer | Add 150 g [R1-23](recipes/R1-23-creme-damande.md) baked in a par-baked shell, reduce cream to 200 g | Richer, more forgiving to hold; shell protected by a baked cake layer |
| Jam layer | 30–40 g raspberry or apricot jam spread on the shell before the cream | Extra barrier and acid accent |
| Cream depth | 250 → 450 g | Less → more creamy; above ~400 g in 22 cm the cream dominates and slices slump |
| Glaze | Apricot → neutral → none | Amber and fruity → clear → fruit dries and dulls within 1–2 h |
| Fruit | Soft berries → firm stone fruit | Less → more weeping when cut; firm fruit needs thinner slices |

## Experiment suggestions

- The seal comparison is [X1-43 Moisture migration: sealed vs unsealed tart shells](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md).
- Glaze test: glaze one half of the tart, leave the other half bare; photograph at 0, 2 and 6 h and note gloss, colour of cut fruit, and shrinkage of berries.
- Hold test: fill a tart, cut one slice at 1 h, 6 h and 24 h; score shell crispness (1–5) and cream set; compare with and without a cocoa butter barrier.
