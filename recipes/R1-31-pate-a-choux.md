# R1-31 · Pâte à choux

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a half-milk, half-water choux paste at classic professional proportions, written for this course with measured endpoints for each stage.

## Objective

Make a smooth, glossy choux paste that pipes cleanly and bakes into light, hollow, crisp shells, by controlling three things: the panade (full boil, flour gelatinized, dried to a film and a measured weight loss), the paste temperature when the eggs go in (≤ 60 °C), and the egg quantity (stopped at the V test, weighed and recorded).

## Expected result

- **Paste:** about 730–750 g of smooth, glossy, pale yellow paste at 30–40 °C. Lifted on a spatula it falls slowly and leaves a hanging V-shaped tail of 3–5 cm that holds. A trench drawn through it closes slowly.
- **Baked test pieces (15 g balls):** about 2.5× their piped height, round, 5–5.5 cm across, even golden-brown including in the cracks, rigid, light (bake loss about 35–45 %), with a large cavity and thin (2–4 mm) walls. They stay up after cooling.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Water | 89.3 | 125 |
| Whole milk | 89.3 | 125 |
| Unsalted butter, cubed 1–2 cm | 78.6 | 110 |
| Fine salt | 3.6 | 5 |
| Caster sugar | 3.6 | 5 |
| Bread flour (12–13 % protein), sifted | 100 | 140 |
| Whole eggs, beaten and strained | 178.6 (range 157–193) | 250 (range 220–270) |
| **Total** | **≈ 543** | **760** |

Percentages are rounded to 0.1; the exact total is 542.9 % (760 ÷ 140).

**Eggs are a range.** Beat about **5–6 large eggs** (you need up to ~270 g), weigh them, and use what the paste needs: usually 220–270 g. Reserve 10–20 % (40–50 g) for the final additions. Leftover egg is your egg wash.

**Yield:** about 730–750 g of paste (15–35 g of water evaporates while drying the panade). That is about **16 éclairs** at 45 g, **48 balls** at 15 g, or 8 éclairs plus 24 profiteroles as in [R1-32](recipes/R1-32-eclairs-profiteroles.md), allowing ~30–40 g left in the bag.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 62.5 g water, 62.5 g milk, 55 g butter, 2.5 g salt, 2.5 g sugar, 70 g flour, ~125 g egg (110–135 g) (380 g). A half batch loses proportionally more water in drying and is easy to over-dry: stir over low-medium heat and stop at 1–1.5 min. ×2 = 250 g water, 250 g milk, 220 g butter, 10 g salt, 10 g sugar, 280 g flour, ~500 g egg (440–540 g) (1,520 g); use a 24 cm pan and a stand mixer for the eggs.

**Variations:** all water (250 g) for crisper, paler shells; all milk for richer, more tender, faster-browning shells (bake 10 °C cooler). All-purpose flour works, with slightly less egg absorbed and slightly smaller shells. See [14.1](stage-1/module-14/lesson-01.md).

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g; probe thermometer.
- Heavy-based stainless or aluminium saucepan, 18–20 cm (a non-stick pan works but does not show the film).
- Wooden spoon or stiff heatproof spatula; sieve.
- Stand mixer with paddle (or a large bowl and a wooden spoon and a strong arm).
- Piping bag, 12–14 mm plain tip for test pieces; tray; parchment or perforated silicone mat.
- Oven thermometer.

## Mise en place

- Weigh the pan empty and write the weight down.
- Sift the flour onto a sheet of parchment so it can be tipped in at once.
- Cube the butter. Beat and strain the eggs; weigh the total; pour 40–50 g into a separate cup as the reserve.
- Line the tray. Draw 3.5 cm circles on the underside of the parchment for test pieces.
- Preheat the oven to **200 °C (390 °F)** conventional (no fan) or **180 °C (355 °F)** fan, shelf in the middle, oven thermometer in place, at least 30 min.

## Procedure

### Panade

1. **Heat liquid and fat.** Put water, milk, butter, salt and sugar in the pan over **medium** heat. Stir occasionally. Aim for the butter to be **completely melted just as the liquid reaches a full rolling boil** (about 4–6 min). If the liquid is about to boil with butter still solid, lower the heat until it melts.
2. **Add the flour.** At a full rolling boil, take the pan off the heat and tip in **all** the flour at once. Stir hard until no dry flour remains and the paste comes together into a lumpy, then smooth, mass (20–40 s).
3. **Dry the paste (dessèchement).** Return to medium heat. Stir constantly, pressing the paste against the base and folding it over, for **1–3 min**. Stop when: the paste forms a smooth ball that leaves the sides; a thin film of paste coats the base of the pan; the paste reads **75–80 °C** in the centre; it looks slightly glossy but no fat is beading or pooling.
4. **Weigh.** Weigh the pan with the paste and subtract the pan weight and the 510 g of ingredients (water, milk, butter, salt, sugar and flour). The difference is the water lost: typically **15–35 g**. Record it.

### Eggs

5. **Cool.** Transfer the paste to the mixer bowl. Beat with the paddle on **low speed** for **1–3 min**, until steam has mostly stopped and the paste is **≤ 60 °C** (50–60 °C ideal).
6. **Add the eggs in 4–6 additions** on low-medium speed. After each addition the paste breaks into slippery pieces, then comes back together, smooth, in 20–40 s. Add the next portion only when it has. Scrape down the bowl and paddle once halfway.
7. **Test before the reserve.** When you have added all but the reserve, stop and do the **V test**: lift some paste on the paddle or a spatula and hold it so it hangs. **Target:** it falls slowly and leaves a V-shaped tail, 3–5 cm long, that holds and does not drip. Also the trench test: a trench drawn through the paste closes slowly over several seconds.
8. **Adjust.** If the paste is too stiff (falls in a lump, no tail, trench stays open, surface dull), add the reserve **5–10 g at a time**, mixing 15 s and re-testing after each. Stop as soon as it passes. If it is already too soft, stop and see Common failures below.
9. **Record** the egg weight used (total minus what is left) and the paste temperature (usually **30–40 °C**).
10. **Hold** covered with film in contact or a damp cloth. Pipe within **1 h** at room temperature.

### Test bake

11. **Pipe** 6 test balls at **15 g** each with the 12–14 mm plain tip (weigh the first two on the scale), 4 cm apart. Tap down any points with a wet fingertip. Brush thinly with egg wash (reserved egg with a pinch of salt) without letting it run onto the tray.
12. **Bake, conventional:** 200 °C for **15 min**, then reduce to **170 °C (340 °F)** for another **15–20 min**. **Fan:** 180 °C constant, **25–30 min**. **Do not open the door for the first 20 min.** Optionally prop the door 1–2 cm open for the last 5 min.
13. **Doneness:** even deep golden-brown including in the cracks; light in the hand; sides rigid when pressed; bake loss **35–45 %** (weigh the tray of pieces before and after, minus the tray). Cut one open: large cavity, walls 2–4 mm, only a thin moist membrane inside.
14. **Vent and cool.** Pierce the base of each piece with a skewer. Cool on a rack. Measure height and diameter of three pieces straight away and again after 30 min.

### Storing and using

15. **Raw paste:** film in contact, ≤ 5 °C, 24 h; beat briefly before piping. **Piped raw pieces:** freeze on the tray until hard (about 2 h), then bag; keep about 1 month; bake from frozen on a lined tray, adding **3–5 min**. **Baked shells:** airtight at room temperature 1–2 days, or frozen about 1 month; re-crisp at 160 °C for 5–8 min and cool before filling.

**Safety:** The panade is at 75–100 °C and sticks to skin; stir with a long-handled spoon. Steam escapes from pierced or cut shells. Raw egg: do not taste the paste ([FDA-eggs]). Allergens: gluten, egg, milk.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Boil | Butter fully melted at a full rolling boil | Uncontrolled evaporation changes the egg needed; flour must meet ~100 °C liquid to gelatinize |
| Flour addition | All at once, off the heat, stirred hard | Gradual addition makes lumps |
| Panade drying | 1–3 min medium heat; film; 75–80 °C; weight loss 15–35 g | Completes gelatinization and makes room for egg; too long splits the fat out |
| Paste temperature before eggs | ≤ 60 °C (50–60 °C) | Hotter cooks the egg on contact; much colder stiffens the paste |
| Egg additions | 4–6, each absorbed before the next | Prevents a separated paste and overshooting |
| Endpoint | V test: 3–5 cm tail that holds; trench closes slowly | Sets the balance between stretch and shape; the single most important control |
| Piece size | ±2 g of target | Pieces on one tray must finish together |
| Oven | 200 → 170 °C conventional or 180 °C fan; door closed ≥ 20 min | Expansion needs early heat; unset walls collapse if the door is opened |
| Doneness | Colour in the cracks, rigid sides, bake loss 35–45 % | Wet walls collapse on cooling |

## Common failures

- **Paste too runny.** See [Paste too runny](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=paste-too-runny). Rescue: make a mini panade (for example 25 g water, 25 g milk, 22 g butter, 1 g salt, 28 g flour; one fifth of the formula), dry it, cool it to 60 °C and beat the runny paste into it gradually.
- **Paste split or greasy.** See [Paste split or greasy](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=paste-split-or-greasy).
- **Did not rise.** See [Choux did not rise or baked flat](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=choux-did-not-rise-or-baked-flat).
- **Collapsed after baking.** See [Choux collapsed after baking](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=choux-collapsed-after-baking).
- **Small hollow, wet inside.** See [Small hollow or dense wet interior](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=small-hollow-or-dense-wet-interior).
- **Pale.** See [Pale shells](troubleshooting/choux.md?id=pale-shells).

## Scientific explanation

Boiling water, milk and butter with the flour gelatinizes most of the wheat starch: the granules swell with water and leak amylose, and the mixture sets into a stiff gel ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md)). Stirring the hot paste works the gluten proteins, which give the paste its elastic stretch. Drying removes water (15–35 g here), so the gel can take up more egg. Egg is added at ≤ 60 °C so its proteins stay raw; it dilutes the gel to a pipeable softness, emulsifies the butterfat with yolk lipoproteins and lecithin, and brings about 190 g of water and 30 g of protein. The finished paste holds roughly 410–425 g of water, almost three times the flour weight, which only a gelatinized starch system can do while still holding a piped shape ([14.1](stage-1/module-14/lesson-01.md)).

In the oven the interior reaches 100 °C and the water vaporizes. Steam at 100 °C occupies roughly 1,600–1,700 times the volume of the liquid water ([SC-11](stage-1/science/sc-11-phase-changes-gases.md)), and the soft paste inflates into one large cavity. The egg proteins coagulate and the starch gel dries, setting the expanded walls; afterwards the shell dries and browns. The V test is a proxy for a balance: enough egg to make the paste extensible and to supply steam and protein, not so much that it cannot hold its shape before it sets.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Egg quantity | −20 % → +20 % of the V-test amount | Stiff, rough, burst tops, thick walls → correct → spread, flat, smooth, collapse-prone ([X1-37](experiments/X1-37-choux-eggs.md)) |
| Panade drying | 30 s → 3 min → 6 min | Less egg absorbed, smaller shells → more egg, larger lighter shells → split, greasy paste |
| Liquid | All water → half/half → all milk | Paler, crisper → balanced → browner, softer, more tender |
| Flour protein | 8 % → 11 % → 13 % | Weak, low → good → strongest, tallest, most upright |
| Butter | 50 % → 80 % → 100 % of flour | Crisper, leaner → balanced → richer, more tender, weaker walls |
| Sugar | 0 → 4 → 10 % | Paler → golden → browns early; lower the oven |
| Oven | 170 constant → 200/170 → 200 constant; door opened early | See [X1-38](experiments/X1-38-choux-oven.md) |
