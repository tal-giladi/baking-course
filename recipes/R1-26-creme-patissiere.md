# R1-26 · Crème pâtissière and crème diplomate

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

An educational formulation built on established technique: a cornstarch-thickened pastry cream at standard professional proportions, and its lightened derivative, written for this course.

## Objective

Make a pastry cream that is smooth, cuts cleanly when cold and is still firm after 2–3 days, by controlling two things: full gelatinization of the starch, and inactivation of the yolk amylase by a real boil. Then lighten it into crème diplomate for filling and layering.

## Expected result

- **Crème pâtissière:** about 750 g of glossy, pale yellow cream. Cold from the tray it is a firm gel that holds a clean cut; after brief whisking it is smooth, creamy and pipeable, holding a ridged shape from a star tip. Flavour: milk, butter and vanilla, no raw-starch taste. It is still as firm on day 3 as on day 1.
- **Crème diplomate:** about 600 g of lighter, softer cream that pipes and holds its shape for layering; with gelatin it sets firm enough to slice in a layered tart or cake after 1–2 h in the refrigerator.

## Formula

### Crème pâtissière

Percentages are of the milk.

| Ingredient | % of milk | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Whole milk (3.5 % fat) | 100 | 500 |
| Egg yolks (5–6 large) | 20 | 100 |
| Caster sugar | 22 | 110 |
| Cornstarch | 9 | 45 |
| Unsalted butter, cold, cubed | 7 | 35 |
| Vanilla extract (or 1 pod) | 1 | 5 |
| Fine salt | 0.1 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **159.1** | **795.5** |

**Yield:** about 740–760 g (about 5 % evaporates during the boil). Enough for a 24 cm tart, or 18–20 éclairs.

**Scaling:** ×0.5 = 250 g milk, 50 g yolks, 55 g sugar, 22.5 g cornstarch, 17.5 g butter, 2.5 g vanilla, 0.25 g salt (397.75 g). ×2 = 1,000 g milk, 200 g yolks, 220 g sugar, 90 g cornstarch, 70 g butter, 10 g vanilla, 1 g salt (1,591 g); use a 24 cm pan and allow a longer time to boil.

**Starch options:** cornstarch alone (above) gives the firmest, cleanest cut. A traditional French mix is **22 g cornstarch + 45 g flour** (the flour counted at half the thickening power of cornstarch), for a creamier, softer body; boil at least 2 min. See [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md).

### Crème diplomate

Percentages are of the pastry cream.

| Ingredient | % of pastry cream | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Crème pâtissière (above), cold | 100 | 400 |
| Cream (35 % fat), whipped to soft peak | 50 | 200 |
| Gelatin, powdered (200 bloom) (optional) | 1 | 4 |
| Cold water to bloom the gelatin | 5 | 20 |
| **Total** | **156** | **624** |

Gelatin sheets instead of powder: two gold-grade sheets (about 2 g each), soaked in plenty of cold water 5–10 min and squeezed; omit the 20 g water. Without gelatin, diplomate is softer, must be used within a few hours, and is best for piping into choux rather than for layered cakes.

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g (0.1 g for gelatin and salt); instant-read probe thermometer.
- Heavy-based saucepan, 18–20 cm, with high sides.
- Balloon whisk (sturdy); heatproof spatula; mixing bowl.
- Shallow tray or dish (about 20 × 30 cm), lined with food film.
- Food film; small bowl for gelatin; microwave or small pan to melt it.
- Stand mixer with paddle or a sturdy whisk for re-texturing; hand whisk for the whipped cream.

## Mise en place

- Line the tray with film, leaving an overhang to wrap over the top. Clear a flat, level space in the refrigerator.
- Weigh the sugar and split: **35 g** for the milk, **75 g** for the yolks.
- Sift the cornstarch if it has lumps. Cube the butter and keep it cold.
- For diplomate: bloom the gelatin in the 20 g cold water for 10 min; chill the cream, bowl and whisk.

## Procedure

### Crème pâtissière

1. **Heat the milk** with 35 g sugar, the salt and the vanilla pod (if using) to a **boil**. The sugar in the milk helps prevent it catching on the base.
2. **Mix the base.** In the bowl, whisk the yolks with 75 g sugar until combined (about 30 s), then whisk in the cornstarch until completely smooth with no dry lumps. This mixture should be thick and paste-like.
3. **Temper.** Pour about **one third** of the boiling milk onto the yolk mixture in a thin stream, whisking constantly. Remove the vanilla pod if using.
4. **Return to the pan.** Pour the tempered mix back into the remaining milk in the pan, whisking.
5. **Cook to the boil, whisking constantly** over medium heat, reaching into the corners. At about **80–85 °C** the cream thickens suddenly and may look lumpy. Keep whisking vigorously: it smooths out.
6. **Boil for 1–2 min.** Wait until large, slow bubbles break through the surface in the centre (the cream is at about 100 °C at the base). Keep whisking and hold it at this boil for **1 min minimum, 2 min preferred** (2 min minimum with a flour blend). The cream goes from its thickest point to slightly **glossier and a little looser**: the sign that the starch is fully gelatinized. The boil also inactivates the yolk amylase.
7. **Enrich.** Off the heat, whisk in the cold butter (the cream will be about **85–95 °C**, and the butter melts in within 30 s), then the vanilla extract.
8. **Spread and seal.** Immediately pour the cream onto the lined tray and spread to about **2 cm** thick. Press film directly onto the whole surface. Fold the overhang over.
9. **Chill fast.** Refrigerate at once on a level shelf, with air around it. **Targets:** below **21 °C within 2 h**, and below **5 °C** within a further few hours at the latest; a 2 cm layer typically reaches 10 °C in about 1–1.5 h in a domestic refrigerator. Check with the probe in the centre.
10. **Store** at **≤ 5 °C**, sealed, **2–3 days**. Do not freeze.
11. **Before use,** turn the cold cream into a bowl and whisk (or beat with a paddle at low-medium speed) for **20–40 s**, until smooth, creamy and glossy. Stop there; long beating makes it runny.

### Crème diplomate

12. **Re-texture** 400 g cold pastry cream as in step 11 and let it stand until it reaches about **18–20 °C** (20–30 min at room temperature).
13. **Whip the cream** to **soft peak** ([R1-27](recipes/R1-27-creme-chantilly.md), [12.3](stage-1/module-12/lesson-03.md)). Keep it cold.
14. **Melt the gelatin** (bloomed) to **40–50 °C** (10 s bursts in a microwave, or over a warm water bath). Whisk about **40 g** of the pastry cream into the melted gelatin, then whisk this quickly into the rest of the pastry cream.
15. **Fold** the whipped cream into the pastry cream in two additions with a spatula, until no streaks remain. Do not overwork.
16. **Use immediately** to pipe or fill: with gelatin it begins to set within 30–60 min and is firm in 1–2 h at ≤ 5 °C. Use within **24 h**.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Boiling pastry cream spits hot bursts of thick cream: use a high-sided pan, whisk continuously, wear long sleeves. Contains egg and milk. Gelatin is animal-derived (pork or beef); for halal, kosher or vegetarian needs, use a certified gelatin or omit it.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Starch dispersion | Cornstarch whisked into sugar and yolks until lump-free | Dry starch meeting hot liquid forms lumps |
| Tempering | ~1/3 of boiling milk, whisking | Prevents local scrambling and lumps |
| Whisking through the thickening | Continuous, reaching corners | Base heats fastest; lumps form and scorch there |
| **Boil** | **Full boil, 1–2 min, whisking** (2 min with flour) | Completes starch gelatinization; inactivates yolk α-amylase |
| Butter | Added off the heat while cream is ≥ 85 °C | Melts and emulsifies smoothly |
| Cooling | 2 cm layer, film in contact, < 21 °C in 2 h | Food safety; prevents skin and condensation |
| Storage | ≤ 5 °C, 2–3 days, not frozen | Food safety; freezing makes it weep |
| Re-texturing | 20–40 s whisking only | Over-beating breaks the gel |
| Diplomate gelatin | Pastry cream at 18–20 °C, gelatin 40–50 °C, tempered | Avoids rubbery strings |

## Scientific explanation

Pastry cream is a starch gel with coagulated egg proteins and emulsified fat dispersed through it. Cornstarch granules, dispersed in sugar and yolk, swell as the mix heats: thickening starts at about 75–80 °C in this sweetened mix, and full gelatinization and peak viscosity need the 90–95 °C+ reached at the boil, because the sugar competes for water and raises the gelatinization temperature ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md)). Swollen granules and leaked amylose hold the egg proteins apart, so they coagulate into fine particles instead of curds; this is why the cream can boil when an anglaise cannot ([12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md)).

Egg yolk contains α-amylase, which survives heating to 80–85 °C. If it survives, it cuts the gelatinized starch during storage and the cream thins overnight. A full boil for 1–2 min inactivates it ([SC-09](stage-1/science/sc-09-enzymes.md)). On cooling, leaked amylose forms a gel network (setback), which gives the firm cold set, and the butterfat solidifies, adding firmness. Whisking the cold gel breaks it into a smooth, creamy paste; too much work breaks the swollen granules and thins it.

In crème diplomate, the whipped cream adds a fat-stabilized foam ([12.3](stage-1/module-12/lesson-03.md)) and the gelatin gels the water phase as it cools, so the air stays in and the cream can be sliced.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cornstarch | 7 → 10 % of milk | Softer, creamier → firmer, more cuttable; above 10 % pasty |
| Starch type | Cornstarch / flour / mix / potato | See [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md) and [X1-34](experiments/X1-34-starch-thickeners.md) |
| Yolks | 16 → 24 % | Leaner, whiter → richer, yellower |
| Sugar | 20 → 25 % | Sweeter and slightly softer set |
| Butter | 0 → 10 % | Richer, firmer when cold; very high levels move towards mousseline ([13.3](stage-1/module-13/lesson-03.md)) |
| Part cream for milk | 0 → 30 % | Richer, softer |
| Flavour | Chocolate (10–15 % of milk, added hot), coffee, praline paste, citrus (acid after cooking) | Chocolate firms the cream; acid softens it |
| Diplomate cream ratio | 30 → 100 % of pastry cream | Firmer, richer → lighter, softer, more fragile |
