# R1-01 · Lean straight-dough loaf (boule and bâtard)

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on established straight-dough technique (the standard four-ingredient lean dough found in any professional bread text). The numbers are chosen to be forgiving and to make every stage easy to observe. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make one lean, crusty loaf by the straight-dough method. Hit a DDT of 24–25 °C, judge the end of bulk fermentation by volume, and bake to an internal temperature of ≥96 °C. Use this loaf as your **reference product**: M04, M05 and every experiment in M03 are measured against it.

## Expected result

- One round (boule) or oval (bâtard) loaf, ~740–780 g after cooling, about 18–20 cm across (boule) and 9–11 cm tall.
- Deep golden-brown to chestnut crust, crisp when cool, with one open score and a raised ear on a bâtard.
- Moderately open, irregular crumb with cells from 1 to 5 mm and a few larger ones. It should be glossy, moist and springy, with no dense streak at the base.
- Clean wheat aroma with a mild fermented note. No alcohol or sourness.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (11.5–13 % protein) | 100.0 | 500 |
| Water | 70.0 | 350 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 10 |
| Instant yeast | 0.8 | 4 |
| **Total** | **172.8** | **864** |

**Yield:** one loaf, ~864 g dough → ~740–780 g baked and cooled (bake and cooling loss ~10–14 %).
**Hydration:** 70 %. With lower-protein flour, or on your first attempt, use 68 % (340 g water; total 854 g).
**Yeast alternatives:** fresh yeast 12 g (2.4 %) or active dry yeast 5 g (1.0 %). Conversions depend on the brand; see [03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md).
**Scaling:** ×0.5 is not recommended because a 430 g loaf behaves differently in the oven. For two loaves, use ×2: 1000 / 700 / 20 / 8 g = 1728 g, then divide into 2 × 864 g.

## Equipment

- Digital scale (1 g) and a 0.1 g scale for the yeast, or measure 4 g carefully on a 1 g scale.
- Probe thermometer.
- Straight-sided clear container, 2–3 L, with a lid. Mark the starting level with tape.
- Bowl scraper and bench scraper.
- Banneton (proofing basket, ~20 cm round or ~25 cm oval), or a bowl/colander lined with a well-floured cloth. Alternatively a floured linen couche for a bâtard.
- Cast-iron or enamelled pot with lid (~24–26 cm, 4–6 L). Or a baking stone/steel plus a steam tray.
- Lame or new razor blade, parchment, oven gloves, cooling rack.
- Optional: stand mixer with dough hook.

## Mise en place

1. Measure the flour temperature and the room temperature. Calculate the water temperature for a DDT of **24 °C** using your friction factor from [01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md):
   water = 3 × DDT − flour − room − friction.
   Example: flour 21 °C, room 22 °C, hand mixing with friction 2 °C → water = 72 − 21 − 22 − 2 = **27 °C**. If you don't know your friction factor, measure it on this bake. As a first guess, hand-mixed doughs in this batch size usually gain only a couple of degrees, and a stand mixer running 6–8 min adds more.
2. Weigh the flour and yeast into the mixing bowl and whisk them together. Weigh the salt separately. Weigh the water at the calculated temperature and hold back 20 g.
3. Rice flour or a 50:50 mix of rice flour and wheat flour for the banneton (rice flour sticks less).

## Procedure

**Step 1 — Mix (t = 0).** Add 330 g of water to the flour and yeast. Mix by hand or with the dough hook on low for 1–2 min, until no dry flour remains. The dough will be shaggy.

**Step 2 — Rest (t = 0 to 20 min).** Cover and rest for 20 min. The flour hydrates and gluten begins forming on its own (see [02.4](stage-1/module-02/lesson-04.md)).

**Step 3 — Add salt and finish mixing (t = 20 min).** Sprinkle on the salt, add the reserved 20 g of water, and work them in.
- *By hand:* pinch the dough through with a wet hand for 2 min, then squeeze-and-fold in the bowl (or slap-and-fold on the bench) for 4–6 min until the dough is cohesive and smooth-ish. It should stretch into a thick, patchy membrane that tears before it becomes translucent. That is moderate development; the folds finish the job.
- *Stand mixer:* speed 1 for 2 min, then speed 2 for 3–4 min, to the same endpoint.

**Step 4 — Check the temperature.** Target **24–25 °C** (acceptable 23–26 °C). Record it. If you are outside 23–26 °C, adjust the bulk time using [03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md) rather than trying to correct the dough.

**Step 5 — Bulk fermentation (about 1.5–2.5 h at 24–25 °C).** Move the dough into the lightly oiled straight-sided container, press it flat, and mark the level. Cover. Keep it somewhere at 24–26 °C (see the warm-spot options in [X1-07](experiments/X1-07-fermentation-temperature.md)).

**Step 6 — Folds.** At 30, 60 and 90 min, do one set of stretch-and-folds. With a wet hand, lift one side of the dough to about 20 cm, fold it over the middle, rotate the container 90°, and repeat 4 times. Each set takes about 30 s. The dough should feel noticeably tighter and smoother after each set. If it is already holding a tall shape after the second set, skip the third.

**Step 7 — End of bulk.** Stop when **all** of these hold:
- volume up **50–75 %** from the mark (typically 1.75–2.25 h from the end of mixing at 24–25 °C);
- domed top, with a few bubbles at the sides and surface;
- the dough jiggles as one mass when you shake the container, and releases cleanly from the walls;
- a scraper pulled through the dough shows an aerated, web-like structure.

**Step 8 — Pre-shape (the dividing step is skipped for one loaf).** Tip the dough onto an unfloured bench. Using the bench scraper, fold it into a loose round with light tension, seam down. Details are in [03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md).

**Step 9 — Bench rest, 20–30 min**, uncovered or loosely covered. It is ready when the round has relaxed into a flatter disc and its edges have softened.

**Step 10 — Shape.** Lightly flour the top, flip it, and shape a **boule** or a **bâtard** with a tight skin ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)). Place it seam-up in the floured banneton. For a couche, place it seam-up in a floured pleat.

**Step 11 — Final proof, about 45–75 min at 24–25 °C.** Cover. Start the oven 45 min before you expect to bake (see Step 12). Judge the proof by the **poke test**: a floured fingertip pressed 1 cm in should leave a dent that springs back slowly and only partly. Also look at the volume: about 50–75 % larger than just after shaping. *Alternative:* after 15 min at room temperature, retard in the fridge (3–5 °C) for 8–16 h and bake straight from the fridge.

**Step 12 — Preheat.** Put the pot, with its lid, in the oven and preheat to **250 °C (480 °F)** for at least 45 min. If you are using a stone, preheat for 60 min with the steam tray on the lowest rack.

**Step 13 — Score and load.** Turn the loaf onto parchment. Score it:
- boule: a square or a cross, 5–8 mm deep, blade near vertical;
- bâtard: one long cut, blade at 30–45°, ~6–8 mm deep, running from end to end slightly off-centre.

Lower the loaf into the pot on the parchment and cover it. For a stone: slide the loaf onto the stone, pour ~150 g of boiling water into the steam tray, and close the door.

**Step 14 — Bake.**
- Pot: 20 min covered at 250 °C (480 °F). Then remove the lid, lower the oven to **230 °C (445 °F)**, and bake another 18–25 min.
- Stone: 15 min at 240–250 °C (465–480 °F) with steam. Vent the steam by removing the tray, drop the oven to 230 °C (445 °F), and bake another 20–25 min.
- **Done when:** the internal temperature is **≥96 °C** (probe inserted from the base into the centre), the crust is deep golden-brown with darker edges on the score, and the base sounds hollow when tapped. Colour is part of flavour, so bake to a deep colour rather than pulling the loaf pale.

**Step 15 — Cool.** On a rack, at least **1 h** before cutting and ideally 2 h. The crumb is still setting as starch cools and moisture redistributes (see [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md)). Store it cut-side down on a board for the first day, then in a paper bag, or sliced and frozen.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** The preheated pot is at 250 °C. Use dry, thick oven gloves (wet cloth conducts heat instantly) and lower the loaf on parchment rather than dropping it in by hand. When you lift the lid or pour water into a steam tray, steam will burst out, so keep your face back and your forearms covered.

</div>

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Yeast weight | 4.0 g ± 0.2 g instant | 0.2 g on 4 g is 5 %. A 1 g error is 25 % and shifts bulk time accordingly |
| Dough temperature after mix | 24–25 °C | Rate roughly doubles per 8–10 °C. This one number predicts your timeline |
| End of bulk | +50–75 % volume, domed, aerated | Under: tight, dense crumb. Over: slack dough, weak spring, pale crust |
| Surface tension at shaping | Taut skin, no tears | Controls oven spring direction and loaf height |
| Final proof | Slow, partial spring-back on the poke test | Under-proofed dough bursts; over-proofed dough spreads and collapses at the score |
| Oven and vessel temperature | 250 °C, preheated ≥45 min | A cold pot or stone costs you oven spring |
| Internal temperature | ≥96 °C | Starch fully set. Below this the crumb is gummy near the base |
| Cooling | ≥1 h before cutting | Cutting hot gives a gummy crumb and lets moisture escape |

## Common failures

- Dense, tight crumb → [bread-lean: dense tight crumb](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=dense-tight-crumb)
- Flat loaf that spreads → [loaf spreads sideways](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=loaf-spreads-sideways), [collapsed or flat loaf](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=collapsed-or-flat-loaf)
- Burst side instead of opening at the score → [blowouts on the side](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=blowouts-on-the-side)
- Weak spring, no ear → [poor oven spring](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=poor-oven-spring)
- Pale or burnt crust → [pale crust](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=pale-crust), [burnt crust](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=burnt-crust), and [bread-baking](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md)

## Scientific explanation

Mixing hydrates the flour proteins and develops a gluten network ([02.2](stage-1/module-02/lesson-02.md)), and it traps small air cells. Instant yeast hydrates within minutes. It first ferments the flour's small stock of free sugars and invertase-split sucrose, then moves on to maltose that flour amylases produce from damaged starch ([03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md)). CO₂ saturates the dough water and then diffuses into the existing air cells and inflates them. The folds stretch and realign the gluten, subdivide large cells, and even out the temperature, so the dough gains strength during bulk without intensive mixing. At the end of bulk the dough is strong but extensible, and there is still fermentable sugar left. Shaping builds surface tension that holds gas pressure as a tall form. Final proof inflates the cells to the point where the network is stretched but not yet torn. In the oven, heat speeds up yeast until it dies at ~55–60 °C. Meanwhile gas expands, water and ethanol vaporize into the cells, and the loaf springs. The score lets this expansion happen at a planned weak point. Starch gelatinizes and proteins set from about 60–80 °C onward, fixing the structure. Above 96 °C at the centre, the crumb is set throughout. The dry crust browns through Maillard reactions and caramelization using the residual sugars ([04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md)).

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hydration | 68 → 75 % | More open, glossier crumb. Slacker dough that is harder to shape. Spreads more if under-shaped |
| Instant yeast | 0.8 → 0.4 % | Bulk about 1.5–2× longer. More flavour. Slightly more acidity |
| Instant yeast | 0.8 → 1.5 % | Faster bulk (~1–1.25 h), blander, yeasty smell. Easier to over-proof |
| DDT | 24 → 20 °C | Bulk ~1.5× longer. More complex flavour |
| Salt | 2 → 1.5 % | Faster fermentation, slightly slacker dough, flatter taste |
| Retard final proof | 12 h at 4 °C | More flavour, thin blistered crust, easier scoring. Schedule becomes flexible |
| Flour | Bread → all-purpose (~10–11 % protein) | Weaker dough. Drop hydration to ~65–67 % and expect a lower loaf |
