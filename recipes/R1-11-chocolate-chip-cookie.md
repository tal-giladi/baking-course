# R1-11 · Baseline chocolate chip cookie

<span class="tag educational">Educational formulation</span>

This is an educational formulation built on the established creaming method for drop cookies. The percentages were chosen to sit in the middle of the normal professional range for each ingredient, so that every variable in [07.3](stage-1/module-07/lesson-03.md) can move the result in both directions. It is the reference against which [X1-18](experiments/X1-18-cookie-sugar-level.md), [X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md) and [X1-20](experiments/X1-20-cookie-spread.md) are measured. It is not a reproduction of any published recipe.

## Objective

Make a consistent, measured batch of chocolate chip cookies with crisp golden edges and a soft, chewy centre, using controlled butter temperature, creaming time, portion weight, dough rest and bake. Record the numbers so this batch becomes your baseline.

## Expected result

- 21 cookies from 48 g dough portions; baked weight ~43–44 g each (bake loss ~8–11 %).
- Diameter **8.5–9.5 cm**, centre thickness **12–15 mm**, spread ratio (diameter ÷ thickness) **~6–8**.
- Edges golden brown for 5–10 mm, crisp; centre pale gold, soft and chewy, slightly glossy; crackled top with chocolate pools.
- Flavour: butterscotch and toffee from brown sugar and browning, balanced salt, no soapy or metallic note from soda.
- On day 2, stored airtight: centre still bends before it breaks; edges still crisp.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour (10–11.5 % protein) | 100.0 | 250 |
| Unsalted butter, 18–20 °C | 76.0 | 190 |
| Light brown sugar | 56.0 | 140 |
| Granulated sugar | 44.0 | 110 |
| Whole egg, beaten (~2 medium eggs) | 38.0 | 95 |
| Baking soda | 0.8 | 2 |
| Fine salt | 1.6 | 4 |
| Vanilla extract | 1.2 | 3 |
| Dark chocolate (55–70 %), chopped into 0.5–1 cm pieces, or chips | 100.0 | 250 |
| **Total** | **417.6** | **1044** |

**Yield:** 1044 g dough, minus ~10 g lost to bowl and scoop → **21 portions of 48 g** (about 25 g left over: bake it as a tester, and do not count it in measurements). Four trays: 6 + 6 + 6 + 3.
**Sugar:** 100 % of flour in total (56 % brown, 44 % white).
**Water in the formula:** about 105 g (butter ~30 g, egg ~71 g, brown sugar ~4 g) against 250 g sugar; most of the sugar stays crystalline until baking ([07.1](stage-1/module-07/lesson-01.md)).
**Scaling:** ×0.5 (125 g flour, 1 egg of ~48 g) gives 10 cookies and creams well with a hand mixer. ×2 (500 g flour) gives 43 cookies; use a stand mixer. Weigh soda to 0.1 g at any scale: 0.5 g too much is enough to taste.
**Salted butter:** use 190 g salted butter (≈ 1.5–2 % salt) and reduce the added salt to 1 g.

## Equipment

- Scale reading to 1 g, and a 0.1 g scale for soda and salt (or weigh them together into a small dish).
- Probe thermometer (butter, dough, and to check the oven with an oven thermometer).
- Stand mixer with paddle, or hand mixer, or a sturdy spatula and bowl.
- Sieve or whisk for the dry ingredients.
- Cookie scoop (about 50 ml) or spoon; a lidded container or tray that fits in the refrigerator.
- Light-coloured aluminium baking trays, ~30 × 40 cm, with parchment. Not dark non-stick, not silicone mats, for the baseline.
- Ruler or calliper; cooling rack; oven thermometer.

## Mise en place

1. Take the butter out 1–2 h before (depending on the room) so it reaches **18–20 °C**: it bends without cracking and a finger leaves a dent, but it is not shiny or greasy. Probe the centre of the block.
2. Eggs at 18–22 °C (30 min out of the refrigerator, or 5 min in warm tap water). Beat and weigh 95 g.
3. Whisk flour, soda and salt together until uniform; sieve if the soda has any lumps. Soda lumps give bitter brown spots.
4. Chop the chocolate and sift off the fine dust if you want cleaner dough (optional; the dust gives a streaky, darker dough).

**Safety:** Raw cookie dough contains raw flour and raw egg; do not taste it ([FDA-flour], [FDA-eggs]). Wash hands, bowls and tools after handling it.

## Procedure

1. **Cream, 2–3 min.** In the mixer bowl, beat the butter, both sugars and the vanilla with the paddle on **medium speed** for **2–3 min**, scraping down once. Endpoint: uniform, slightly paler, and softer; the brown sugar is fully worked in with no streaks. You are aerating moderately, not creaming to the white, fluffy stage of a cake (that takes 5–8 min and gives cakey cookies).
2. **Add egg, 1 min.** On medium-low, add the egg in two parts, mixing about 30 s after each until the mixture is smooth and glossy with no free liquid. Scrape the bowl and paddle.
3. **Add the dry ingredients, 20–30 s.** On low speed, add the flour mixture and mix only until no dry flour is visible. Overmixing develops gluten and makes a tougher, more domed cookie.
4. **Add the chocolate, 10–15 s.** Mix on low or fold in by hand until evenly distributed.
5. **Check the dough temperature:** target **20–23 °C**. Record it. Above 24 °C the butter is starting to melt; chill the dough 15 min before portioning.
6. **Portion.** Scoop and weigh **48 g ± 1 g** portions. Roll each lightly into a ball, then press it into a slightly taller-than-wide shape (about 4 cm tall); tall balls spread more evenly than flat ones. Put them close together in a covered container or on a tray wrapped in film.
7. **Rest 24 h at 3–5 °C** (acceptable range 12–72 h). This is part of the baseline: the flour hydrates, the dough firms, and colour and flavour improve. *Same-day option:* at least 1 h at 3–5 °C; expect slightly more spread and paler colour, and note it. *Freezing:* freeze the portioned balls on a tray, then bag them; keep up to 1 month; bake from frozen with 1–2 min extra.
8. **Preheat** the oven to **180 °C (355 °F)**, conventional (no fan), rack in the middle, for at least 30 min. With a fan oven use 165–170 °C (330–340 °F). Check with an oven thermometer.
9. **Load.** Place **6 cold dough balls** per parchment-lined tray, at least 7–8 cm apart (3 rows of 2 across a 30 × 40 cm tray). Bake straight from the refrigerator: dough temperature at loading 4–8 °C.
10. **Bake 10–12 min**, one tray at a time. If your oven browns unevenly, rotate the tray 180° at 6 min. **Endpoint:** edges set and golden brown for 5–10 mm; the top has lost its gloss at the edges; the centre is puffed, pale and looks slightly underdone and glossy. Do not wait for the centre to look done.
11. **Carry-over, 5 min on the tray.** The centre sinks slightly and sets from the heat of the tray. Then move the cookies to a rack with a thin spatula.
12. **Measure** (first tray): weigh the tray of 6 before and after baking (bake loss %); after 20 min cooling, measure diameter (two readings at right angles) and centre thickness for each cookie; calculate the spread ratio. Photograph the tray next to a ruler and white card.
13. **Store** fully cooled cookies airtight at room temperature: best within 3 days. Freeze baked cookies for up to 1 month.

## Critical control points

| CCP | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter temperature | 18–20 °C | Warmer = more spread and greasy; colder = poor creaming, uneven dough |
| Creaming time | 2–3 min at medium | Longer = more air = cakier, puffier cookies |
| Mixing after flour | Only until no dry flour (20–30 s) | Gluten development makes tough, domed cookies |
| Dough temperature at end of mixing | 20–23 °C | Sets how much fat is liquid and sugar dissolved |
| Portion weight | 48 g ± 1 g | Size changes spread, bake time and texture |
| Rest | 24 h at 3–5 °C (min 1 h) | Hydration, less spread, colour and flavour |
| Oven | 180 °C measured, middle rack, light tray + parchment | Temperature and tray set the balance between flow and set |
| Bake endpoint | Golden edges, pale soft centre | Overbaking removes the chewy centre |

## Common failures

- Thin, greasy, lacy cookies → [excessive spreading](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=excessive-spreading), [greasy cookies](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=greasy-cookies)
- Thick domes that never spread → [cookies do not spread](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=cookies-do-not-spread)
- Hard throughout → [cookies too hard](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=cookies-too-hard)
- Raw centre → [too soft or raw centre](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=too-soft-or-raw-centre)

## Scientific explanation

Creaming at 18–20 °C traps small air cells in the plastic butter; sugar crystals cut those cells in. The egg adds water and emulsifiers, and brief mixing after the flour keeps gluten low. In the dough about 105 g of water faces 250 g of sugar and 250 g of flour, so most sugar stays as crystals ([07.1](stage-1/module-07/lesson-01.md)). A 24 h rest lets the flour absorb the free water, firms the butter, and gives flour and egg enzymes time to release sugars and amino acids that later brown.

In the oven, spread is a race ([07.3](stage-1/module-07/lesson-03.md)). The butter melts, sugar dissolves into a growing syrup, soda raises the pH (weakening proteins and delaying set) and releases CO₂ as it reacts with the acids in brown sugar and as it decomposes with heat. The dough flows until the edges dry and the egg and flour proteins set, at which point the cookie stops widening. The thin edges dry quickly, pass 140–160 °C and brown by Maillard reactions and some caramelization; the sucrose there recrystallizes on cooling, giving the crisp rim. The thick centre stays below 100 °C with more water. The invert sugar and molasses in the brown sugar hold that water and keep the sugar amorphous, so the centre stays chewy. The CO₂ bubbles collapse as the cookie cools, leaving the crackled top. Chocolate pieces interrupt the dough and reduce spread slightly.

## Variables you can change

| Variable | Direction | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| Total sugar | ±20 % | More: more spread, darker, crisper edges. Less: thicker, paler, cakier, stales faster ([X1-18](experiments/X1-18-cookie-sugar-level.md)) |
| Brown : white ratio | More brown | Chewier, moister, darker, puffier; more white: flatter, crisper ([X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md)) |
| Butter state | Melted / cold | Melted: more spread, chewier, denser. Cold: thicker, more tender ([X1-20](experiments/X1-20-cookie-spread.md)) |
| Rest | 0 h → 72 h | Less spread, more even colour, deeper toffee flavour ([X1-20](experiments/X1-20-cookie-spread.md)) |
| Flour | Bread flour | Thicker, chewier, darker |
| Egg | Replace 20 g whole egg with 20 g yolk | Richer, fudgier, slightly more spread |
| Soda | 0.8 → 1.2 % | More spread, darker, risk of soapy taste; baking powder instead: puffier, paler, cakier |
| Portion | 48 → 70 g | Thicker, softer centre, +1–2 min |
| Oven | 170 °C / 190 °C | Cooler: thinner, crisper, more even. Hotter: thicker, softer centre |
| Tray | Dark non-stick, silicone mat | Dark: darker bottoms, less spread. Silicone: paler, less spread |
