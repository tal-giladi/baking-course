# Short pastry, pies and tarts

This category covers short doughs (pâte brisée, pâte sucrée, sablée), flaky pie dough, tart shells and their baked fillings (fruit pie fillings, crème d'amande). Faults in unbaked fillings set in a shell (pastry cream, curd, ganache) are in [Custards and creams](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md) and [Assembled pastries](troubleshooting/assembled-pastries.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose, check three numbers.** Almost every short-pastry fault traces back to (1) **how much water** went in (including the water in butter and eggs), (2) **how warm the butter got** (dough temperature at mixing, rolling and loading), and (3) **how long the dough rested** and whether the lined shell went into the oven cold. Record all three every time; most entries below start there. The mechanisms are in [11.1](stage-1/module-11/lesson-01.md) (gluten, fat, water), [11.2](stage-1/module-11/lesson-02.md) (resting, lining, blind baking) and [11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md) (flaky dough, starch-thickened fillings, crème d'amande).

### Tough pastry

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much water | Compare added water with the formula (brisée 18–24 %, pie 25–30 %); dough felt soft and elastic, a cut strip sprang back | Hold back the last 10–15 % of the water and add only if the squeeze test fails |
| Overmixing after the liquid (or flour, in crémage) went in | Mixing time log; dough smooth and springy rather than just cohesive | Stop as soon as no dry flour remains; homogenize with one fraisage pass instead of kneading |
| Butter warmed and released its water | Dough above 18–20 °C at the end of mixing; shiny, smeared butter | Start with butter at 4–8 °C (sablage, pie) or 16–18 °C (crémage); chill flour and bowl in a warm kitchen |
| Strong flour | Flour protein on the label above ~12 % | Use plain/all-purpose (9.5–11.5 %) or pastry flour |
| Heavy re-rolling of scraps | Tough pieces came from second or third rolls | Stack scraps flat, press (do not knead), rest 30 min; use them for small items |

Toughness means gluten formed. Find which of water, work or flour strength supplied it. See [11.1](stage-1/module-11/lesson-01.md) and [X1-30](experiments/X1-30-sablage-cremage.md).

### Crumbly pastry that falls apart

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too little water or egg | Squeeze test crumbled; dough cracked all over when rolled; baked pastry sandy and falls apart when cut | Add water 5 g at a time until a squeezed handful holds; record the new amount |
| Butter rubbed in too warm or too long | Mixture looked greasy/pasty before the liquid went in | Keep the rubbed-in mixture ≤ 16–18 °C; stop at sand/breadcrumb stage |
| Too much fat or sugar for the flour | Formula check: sucrée butter > 70 %, sugar > 45 % | Return to the standard ranges, or accept it as a sablée and press it into a ring rather than rolling |
| Dough not rested, uneven hydration | Dry patches and cracks at the edges | Rest ≥ 2 h (overnight is better); fraisage once |
| Underbaked (still soft and fragile when warm) | Pale base; breaks when lifted warm | Bake fully; let shells cool 10 min before unmoulding |

A short dough should be tender but still cohesive. If it will not hold at the bench, it lacks binder (water, egg) relative to tenderizers (fat, sugar).

### Shrinking tart shell

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough not rested before rolling | Rest < 1 h; dough springs back when rolled | Rest ≥ 1–2 h, ideally overnight ([X1-32](experiments/X1-32-tart-shrinkage.md)) |
| Dough stretched while lining | Shrinkage worst at the walls, dough thinner at the top of the wall | Lift and let the dough fall into the corner; never push or pull it down the wall |
| Shell baked warm | Lined shell went straight into the oven | Freeze 15–30 min or refrigerate 30 min after lining |
| Too much water or overmixing | See Tough pastry | Reduce water; mix less |
| No weights, or weights removed too early | Walls dropped mainly in the first 10–15 min | Fill weights to the rim; remove only when the rim is set and the paper lifts cleanly |
| Oven too cool | Measured oven below 165 °C; long bake to set | Check with an oven thermometer; bake at 170–180 °C (sucrée 165–170 °C) |

Shrinkage is elastic recovery of stretched gluten during the window when the butter has melted but the structure has not set. Reduce the strain (rest, no stretching) and shorten the window (bake cold). For sucrée, leave 2 mm above the rim and rasp level after baking. See [11.2](stage-1/module-11/lesson-02.md).

### Slumped sides in blind baking

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Weights only covering the base | Walls slid down but the base is flat | Fill weights to the rim; use crumpled parchment that fits the corner |
| Shell warm at loading | Butter soft when lined; no chill | Freeze the lined shell 15–30 min |
| High-fat dough | Sucrée butter > 65 %, sablée | Freeze before baking; bake in a ring on a perforated mat; accept slightly thicker walls |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer; walls took > 20 min to set | Bake at 170–180 °C; preheat the tray |
| Walls too thin or rounded corner | Thin wall at the top; thick rounded base corner | Press a sharp 90° corner; keep walls 3 mm |

Slumping happens when melted butter lubricates a dough that has not yet set and the wall slides under its own weight. Support it (weights) and keep the butter solid longer (cold shell).

### Puffed or bubbled bottom

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Base not docked | No holes; dome in the centre | Dock every 1–2 cm with a fork or docker |
| Weights removed too early | Base domed during the unweighted phase | Leave weights until the rim is set; press any dome down gently with a cloth while soft |
| Air trapped between dough and pan when lining | Localized bubble, often at the corner | Ease dough into the corner; prick any trapped bubble before baking |
| Creamed dough over-aerated (sucrée) | Small blisters over the whole surface | Mix butter and sugar on low only until smooth |
| Pie base under a heavy filling lifted | Gap between filling and crust after baking | Chill the assembled pie; start hot on a preheated tray |

Steam and air under or inside the base expand in the oven. Give them a way out (docking, perforated mats) and hold the base down until it sets.

### Soggy bottom

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Shell underbaked before a wet filling | Base pale and soft at the centre when cut | Par-bake until dry and matt; fully bake for unbaked fillings |
| Shell not sealed | No egg wash or cocoa butter layer | Brush with egg wash and bake 2–3 min; for cold fillings, a thin cocoa butter or chocolate layer ([17.2](stage-1/module-17/lesson-02.md), [X1-43](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md)) |
| Too little bottom heat | Pie or tart baked high in the oven; ceramic dish | Lowest rack on a preheated tray; glass or metal plate for pies |
| Filling too wet or not thickened enough | Juice pooled under the fruit; pears not drained | Drain and pat fruit dry; use enough starch; pre-cook juicy fillings |
| Flaky dough used under a wet filling | Layers visibly saturated | Use a mealy dough (fat rubbed in fine) for bottom crusts under custards and very wet fruit |
| Filled too long before serving | Crisp when filled, soggy hours later | Fill unbaked-filling tarts close to service; seal the shell |

Water moves from a wet filling into a dry crust. Bake the crust drier, put a barrier between them, or reduce the free water in the filling.

### Greasy pastry, butter leaking

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter softened or melted during mixing | Dough shiny, soft, greasy to the touch; dough temperature > 20 °C | Cold butter; work fast; chill 10–20 min whenever the butter looks shiny |
| Pastry warm at loading | Butter pooled on the tray in the first minutes | Chill or freeze before baking |
| Oven too cool | Butter melted out before the dough set | Preheat fully; start pie dough at 200–220 °C |
| Butter pieces too large (pie dough) | Large grease ring; pockets of melted butter | Keep pieces pea- to hazelnut-sized ([X1-31](experiments/X1-31-pie-fat-size.md)) |
| Crème d'amande split or butter too warm | Cream grainy or oily before baking | Butter 18–20 °C; eggs at room temperature; add eggs gradually |

Butter must stay solid until the structure around it sets. Greasiness means it melted out first.

### Cracks when rolling

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough too cold | Core below ~6 °C; cracks at every stroke | Temper 5–10 min to 8–12 °C, or beat with the pin to soften evenly |
| Too little water | Cracks persist at 10–12 °C; crumbly squeeze test | Next time add 3–5 % more water; now, patch cracks by pressing |
| Uneven hydration (no rest) | Dry, cracked patches beside smooth areas | Rest ≥ 2 h |
| Rolling over the edge | Cracks start at the rim | Roll from the centre outward; stop short of the edge; turn often |
| Very short dough (sablage, sablée) | Expected fragility | Roll between parchment; patch with scraps; press rather than roll for sablée |

Small edge cracks can be pressed together; large cracks in the sheet can be patched with a thin piece of dough pressed on (not stretched). Always patch the lined shell before baking.

### Dough sticks when rolling

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough too warm | Core above ~14 °C; surface shiny | Return to the refrigerator for 10–15 min on a tray |
| Warm bench or hands | Kitchen above 24 °C | Chill the bench with a tray of ice; roll between parchment |
| Too much water or egg | Sticky from the start, even cold | Correct the formula; flour the bench lightly |
| Not turned often enough | Stuck to the bench after a few strokes | Quarter-turn every few strokes; slide a scraper underneath; peel and replace parchment |

Use as little flour as possible: excess flour dries the surface and bakes into a raw, dusty layer. Parchment is the cleaner fix for sweet doughs.

### Pale, underbaked base

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too little bottom heat | Walls golden, base pale | Lower rack; preheated heavy tray under the ring or pan; perforated mat |
| Second (unweighted) bake too short | Base still matt-grey at the centre | Bake until the base centre is dry and golden (full bake) |
| Fluted pan with metal base, or glass/ceramic dish | Slower conduction to the base | Preheat the tray; extend the bake; use a ring on a mat |
| Oven cooler than set | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |
| Sheet too thick at the centre | Measure a cut section: base > 4 mm | Roll evenly with guides |

The base is the last part to bake: it is shielded by the filling or weights and touches the tray only through a pan. Give it direct heat, and judge doneness by the centre of the base, not the rim.

### Overbrowned edges

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot for a sugared dough | Sucrée baked at brisée temperatures | Sucrée at 165–170 °C still |
| Long bake with a filling | Rim dark before filling or base finished | Shield the rim with a foil ring after it colours |
| Rim thinner than the rest | Rolled over the edge; rim thin | Roll evenly; keep walls 3 mm |
| Egg wash pooled on the crimp (pie) | Dark spots in the crimp | Brush thinly; wipe the crimp |
| Top heat (element, rack too high) | Top edges darker than the lower wall | Lower the rack; tray on the rack above as a shield |

Rims brown first because they are thin, exposed on both faces and furthest from the moist filling.

### Runny fruit pie filling

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough thickener for that fruit | Compare dose with the table in [11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md); frozen or very juicy fruit | Increase by 0.5–1 % of fruit weight; frozen fruit needs about 1 % more |
| Pulled before the centre boiled | Bubbling at the edge only; centre < 95 °C | Bake until thick, slow bubbles in the centre vents; tent the crust if needed |
| Cut while warm | Cut before 3–4 h | Cool to near room temperature |
| Starch not dispersed (lumps) | White lumps in the cooled filling | Whisk starch with the sugar before tossing with the fruit |
| Acid weakened the starch | Very acidic fruit; lots of added lemon; long bake | Top of the range; add less lemon; or pre-cook the juice with the starch |
| Coarse tapioca not soaked | Gelatinous beads, thin juice | Soak 15 min in the juice before filling, or use ground/instant tapioca |

A fruit filling sets only if there is enough starch **and** it reaches near boiling in the centre **and** it is allowed to cool. Find which of the three was missing. The opposite fault, a gluey, cloudy, pasty filling, means too much starch (especially flour).

### Frangipane sinks or overflows

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overcreamed (too much air) | Cream was light and fluffy; rose high in the oven, then collapsed and cracked | Mix butter and sugar on low only until smooth; ≤ 2 min after the eggs |
| Overfilled | Cream above 2/3 of the wall before baking | Fill ≤ 1/2–2/3 of the wall height |
| Underbaked centre | Centre wet, below 85 °C; sank on cooling | Bake until the centre springs back and reaches ≥ 85 °C |
| Too much free water (fruit juice, no flour) | Pears wet; wet layer under the fruit | Drain and pat fruit dry; include flour at ~10 % of the almond weight |
| Oven too hot | Fast rise, dark surface, raw middle | 165–175 °C; shield the rim |
| Cream split (butter or eggs too cold, or butter too warm) | Grainy, oily cream; fat leaks | Temper butter to 18–20 °C and eggs to room temperature |

Crème d'amande is set by egg protein alone (plus a little starch), so it cannot hold much more air than it needs. Rise it less and bake it through. See [R1-23](recipes/R1-23-creme-damande.md).
