# Troubleshooting: sourdough and preferments

This page covers faults in starters, levains and yeasted preferments, and faults specific to preferment and sourdough breads. It is owned by [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md). General lean-dough faults (mixing, shaping, proof) are in [lean bread: dough and fermentation](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md), and baking, crust and staling faults are in [bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md). Use all three together; many sourdough faults are fermentation faults that look different because the process is slower and more acidic.

**How to use it.** Start from the symptom. Read the likely causes in order: they are listed from most to least common for a home baker. Then use your bake record (temperatures, times, % rise) to eliminate causes before changing anything. Change **one** variable in the next bake. The method is explained in the [troubleshooting README](troubleshooting/README.md) and practised in the bread clinic in [05.4](stage-1/module-05/lesson-04.md).

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Starters and preferments are raw flour ([FDA-flour]). Smell them freely, but don't taste them raw. Any fuzzy growth, or pink, orange or black colour, means the whole culture is discarded; see [mould on the starter](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=mould-on-the-starter).

</div>

## Starter and levain

### Starter not rising

No meaningful rise (less than +50 %) between feeds after more than 7 days, or a formerly active starter that has gone sluggish.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too cold (below ~22 °C) | Thermometer next to the jar for 24 h | Keep at 24–27 °C: oven with light on, top of fridge, cooler with warm water. Establishment at 20 °C can take 2–3 weeks |
| Still in the quiet phase after the false rise (days 3–7) | Check your log: was there a strong rise on day 2–3? | Keep feeding on schedule. See [starter rose strongly on day 2 then stopped](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=starter-rose-strongly-on-day-2-then-stopped) |
| Feeding too diluted too early (1:5:5 or more before it is established) | Log of ratios | Use 1:1:1 every 24 h until it rises, then 1:2:2 every 12 h |
| Only white or bleached flour | Label | Use 10–30 % wholemeal rye or wheat in each feed; use unbleached flour |
| Too wet (125 %+) so gas escapes without visible rise | Look for bubbles throughout, but no height gain | Feed at 80–100 % hydration; bubbles without rise can still mean an active culture |
| Revived from the fridge with only one feed | Log | 2–3 feeds at 24–26 °C, 12 h apart |
| Heavily chlorinated water | Smell of the tap water | Use filtered water, or leave it open overnight |

A starter that bubbles throughout but doesn't rise is usually alive but too wet or too weak in gluten. A starter with no bubbles at all after 10 warm days needs a restart with wholemeal rye. See [05.3](stage-1/module-05/lesson-03.md) and [R1-05](recipes/R1-05-levain.md).

### Starter rose strongly on day 2 then stopped

A new starter doubled or tripled on day 2–3, often with a cheesy or unpleasant smell, then barely moved for several days.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| The normal **false rise**: early non-sourdough bacteria (enterobacteria, *Leuconostoc* and similar) bloom, then acid suppresses them | Timing: vigorous on day 2–3, quiet on days 3–6 | Nothing is wrong. Keep feeding every 24 h at 24–27 °C; activity returns around day 5–10 |
| Too cold during the quiet phase, prolonging it | Thermometer | Warm to 24–27 °C |
| Feeding too often or too diluted during the quiet phase | Log | Feed only every 24 h at 1:1:1 until regular rises return |

Don't add commercial yeast and don't throw the starter away. The quiet phase is when the sourdough community takes over ([05.3](stage-1/module-05/lesson-03.md)).

### Starter peaks too fast and collapses

The starter peaks in 2–3 h after a 1:2:2 feed and is sunken, runny and sharp-smelling long before the next feed.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too warm (28 °C+) | Thermometer | Move to 21–24 °C, or use cooler water in the feed |
| Feeding ratio too low for the interval | Peak time vs feeding interval | Feed at a higher dilution (1:5:5 or 1:10:10) so the peak lands near the next feed |
| High wholemeal content | Flour log | Reduce wholemeal to 10 %; it ferments faster |
| Very liquid culture | Hydration | Lower to 80–100 %, or keep a stiff starter (55 %) |

A starter that peaks and then starves for hours every cycle gets progressively more acidic and sluggish. Match the ratio to your schedule using [X1-14](experiments/X1-14-levain.md).

### Starter smells of acetone or nail polish

A solvent-like smell, usually just before feeding or after time in the fridge.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Starving culture: long past peak, sugar exhausted | Smell right after the peak (fine) vs 8 h later (solvent): hunger | Feed more often, or feed at a higher dilution timed to peak nearer feeding time |
| Warm and fed too rarely | Temperature and feeding log | Cooler spot or more frequent feeds |
| Long storage in the fridge | Days since last feed | Two or three refreshes at 24–26 °C; the smell disappears within a day |

The smell comes from volatile compounds that stressed yeasts and bacteria produce (commonly described as ethyl acetate-like). It is not dangerous, but a starter that smells like this every cycle is being mismanaged. Levain used in this state makes a sour, slow, dense loaf.

### Hooch on the starter

A layer of grey, brown or clear liquid on top, smelling of alcohol.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hungry starter: yeast has produced ethanol and the structure has broken down | Time since feed; smell | Stir in (more sour) or pour off (milder), then feed. Feed more often or at a higher dilution |
| Very liquid culture | Hydration | Lower hydration to 80–100 %, or keep a stiff starter for fridge storage |
| Long storage in the fridge | Days since last feed | Normal after 1–2 weeks. Pour off, take 10 g from the middle, feed 1:5:5 twice at 24–27 °C |

Hooch is not mould and not a safety problem. It is a clock: it tells you the starter has been hungry for some time.

### Mould on the starter

Fuzzy growth, or pink, orange, red or black spots or streaks, on the surface or on the jar rim.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dried culture on the jar rim and lid, left for days | Look at the rim | **Discard the entire starter.** Wash and scald the jar. Restart from a dried backup or from scratch. From now on, move the culture to a clean jar every few days or wipe the rim after each feed |
| Starter neglected for weeks at room temperature | Log | As above |
| Starter not yet acidic enough (early days) with contamination | Days since start | As above; restart with cleaner handling |

Do not scrape mould off and continue: hyphae and toxins can extend beyond what you see, and pink or orange colour can indicate bacteria that should not be eaten ([FDA-flour], [SC-08](stage-1/science/sc-08-microbiology.md)). Keep a dried backup ([R1-05](recipes/R1-05-levain.md) step 12) so a discard is not a disaster.

## Yeasted preferments

### Poolish or biga over-ripe

At use the preferment has collapsed well below its high-water mark, looks thin, soupy (poolish) or wet and sticky (biga), and smells sharply of alcohol or acid. The final dough ferments fast, feels slack, and bakes paler.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Room warmer than planned overnight | Min/max thermometer | Use ~0.05 % yeast at 24–25 °C, or a cooler spot. See the table in [05.2](stage-1/module-05/lesson-02.md) |
| Too much yeast (weighing error) | Did you use a fresh 1 % solution? | Always use a yeast solution for sub-gram doses |
| Left too long (plans changed) | Hours since mixing | Next time, retard the preferment at 3–5 °C once ripe; it holds for several hours |
| High wholemeal content | Flour used | Reduce yeast further or shorten time |

**Using it today:** lower DDT to 22–23 °C, end bulk at the low end (+50 %), shorten proof, handle gently. If it is badly collapsed, use half of it and make up the flour and water in the final dough. Expect a paler crust: the preferment has used sugar that would have browned it ([05.1](stage-1/module-05/lesson-01.md)).

### Poolish or biga under-ripe

At use the preferment is flat or only slightly risen, with few bubbles, and smells of raw flour. The final dough is slow, and the bread tastes flat.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Room colder than planned | Min/max thermometer | Raise yeast to 0.15–0.2 % at 18 °C, or use a warmer spot |
| Yeast solution made with too-hot water, or old yeast | Test the yeast: 1 g in 50 g warm water with a pinch of sugar should foam within 10 min | Solution water at ~25 °C; fresh yeast |
| Not enough time | Hours since mixing | Give it 1–2 h more at 24–25 °C until domed and bubbly |
| Biga mixed too stiff (<50 %) | Recheck the water | Keep biga at 55–60 % |

**Using it today:** increase final-dough yeast by ~25 % and raise DDT to 25–26 °C; expect less flavour and extensibility.

## Sourdough dough and loaf

### Sourdough bulk far slower than expected

Bulk has run 6–8 h and the dough has risen less than +50 %, with flat edges and few bubbles.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough too cold | Dough temperature after mixing and during bulk | DDT 25–26 °C and a warm spot; bulk at 22 °C takes ~1.4× longer |
| Weak or immature levain | Did the levain at least double? Is the starter passing the readiness test? | Refresh the starter 2–3 times; build the levain from a starter peaking on time |
| Levain used too early or too late | Levain rise at use | Use at or just before peak |
| Too little levain (arithmetic) | Recheck PFF | 10–20 % PFF; recalculate the formula |
| Salt added twice or too much | Recheck weights | 2 % of total flour |

The fix is not to shape anyway. An under-fermented sourdough is the commonest cause of dense loaves and tunnel holes. Extend bulk and check every 30 min ([05.4](stage-1/module-05/lesson-04.md)).

### Dough turns slack and sticky late in bulk

A dough that was strong after folding becomes loose, sticky and shiny, and won't hold a pre-shape.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-fermentation: acid and proteases have weakened gluten | Rise well over +75 %, large surface bubbles, sharp smell | End bulk earlier (+50–75 %); lower DDT or PFF |
| Too much levain, or over-ripe levain | PFF and levain state at use | 10–15 % PFF; levain at peak |
| Hydration too high for the flour | Compare with 70 % | Drop to 70–72 % and build up |
| High wholemeal share (more enzymes, faster) | Flour log | Reduce, or shorten bulk |

If it happens now: pre-shape firmly, give an extra set of folds on the bench (a letter fold), shorten the bench rest, shape tightly, and retard immediately.

### Dense or gummy sourdough crumb

A low loaf with tight, damp crumb, often with a few large holes near the top, or a sticky crumb that compresses into a paste.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-fermented bulk (weak levain, cool dough, ended by the clock) | Bulk log: rise under +50 %? DDT below 24 °C? Levain less than doubled? | Mature the starter; DDT 25–26 °C; end bulk by cues |
| Under-proofed after a cold retard | Loaf small and tight on removal from the fridge | Leave at room temperature 30–90 min until it has visibly grown |
| Under-baked | Internal temperature below 96 °C; pale crust | Bake to ≥96 °C and to a deep colour |
| Cut before cooled | Time from oven to knife | Cool ≥2 h |
| Over-fermented, acid-degraded crumb (gummy, collapsed rather than tight) | Flat loaf, pale crust, very sour | See [flat spreading sourdough loaf](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=flat-spreading-sourdough-loaf) |
| High wholemeal share | Flour log | Expect a denser crumb; add water, reduce bulk slightly |

Distinguish **tight and dense** (under-fermented) from **gummy and collapsed** (over-fermented or under-baked). See also [dense, tight crumb](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=dense-tight-crumb) and [gummy crumb](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md?id=gummy-crumb).

### Flat spreading sourdough loaf

The loaf slumps when turned out of the banneton and bakes wide and low, with a score that barely opens.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-fermented in bulk | Rise over +75–100 %, slack at pre-shape | End bulk earlier; lower DDT |
| Over-proofed in the retard | Fridge temperature at the shelf (5 °C+?); retard hours | Coldest shelf, 3–5 °C; shorter retard, or reduce PFF for long retards |
| Weak shaping tension | Did the shaped loaf hold a tall form in the banneton? | Tighter shaping, stitched bâtard, extra bench fold for wet doughs |
| Hydration too high for the flour | Compare with 70–74 % | Reduce hydration by 2–4 % |
| Insufficient folds | Fold log | 3–4 sets in the first 2 h |
| Weak flour | Protein % | Bread flour at 12–13 %, or reduce hydration |

See also [loaf spreads sideways](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=loaf-spreads-sideways) and [collapsed or flat loaf](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=collapsed-or-flat-loaf).

### Large tunnel holes or flying crust

One or several big holes, often just under the top crust (which is separated from the crumb), with tighter crumb elsewhere.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-fermented bulk | Rise under +50 %, dense crumb between holes | Extend bulk to +50–75 % |
| Under-proofed loaf | Poke test springs back fast; side blowouts | Proof longer |
| Flour trapped in the shaping (heavy bench flour folded in) | Holes follow a seam or a spiral | Flour only the surface that will face the banneton |
| Large gas pockets not degassed at shaping | Big bubbles on the surface at pre-shape | Pop large surface bubbles; firmer pre-shape |
| Skin dried during proof | Leathery surface | Cover in the retard |
| Oven too hot on top relative to spring | Top crust very dark early | Lower rack position or temperature slightly |

A "flying crust" means strong oven spring hit a skin that set early over a tight crumb. It is mainly a fermentation fault, not an oven fault. See [blowouts on the side](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=blowouts-on-the-side).

### Pale crust on sourdough

The crust stays pale gold or matt tan despite a full bake time and a verified oven temperature.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar depletion from over-fermentation (long bulk, warm retard, over-ripe levain or poolish) | Other signs of over-fermentation: slack dough, flat loaf, sharp sourness | Shorten bulk or retard; use levain at peak |
| Low pH slows Maillard browning | Very sour loaf | Milder process (see [loaf too sour](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=loaf-too-sour)) |
| Bake too short (sourdough colours more slowly) | Bake log | Bake longer uncovered until deep reddish-brown; ≥96 °C internal |
| Lid left on too long, or too much steam | Bake log | Lid off at 20 min |
| Oven cooler than set | Oven thermometer | Calibrate ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |

Sourdough runs close to the sugar limit because fermentation lasts 12–24 h ([05.1](stage-1/module-05/lesson-01.md), [05.4](stage-1/module-05/lesson-04.md)). A pale sourdough after a full, verified bake is evidence of over-fermentation. See also [pale crust](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md?id=pale-crust).

### Loaf too sour

Sharp, vinegary or harsh acidity that dominates the wheat flavour.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, old starter used after one feed | Refresh log | 2–3 feeds at 24–26 °C before building the levain |
| Levain used long past peak | Levain state at use | Use at or just before peak |
| Too much levain | PFF | Reduce to 10–15 % |
| Stiff levain, cool bulk, long retard (more acetic, practitioner guidance) | Process log | Liquid levain, bulk at 25–26 °C, retard 8–12 h |
| High wholemeal or rye share | Flour log | Reduce wholemeal; higher buffering holds more acid |
| Over-fermentation in general | Flat, pale loaf too | See [flat spreading sourdough loaf](troubleshooting/sourdough.md?id=flat-spreading-sourdough-loaf) |

Change one lever at a time; several of them also change the loaf's timing and strength ([05.4](stage-1/module-05/lesson-04.md)).

### Loaf not sour enough

A pleasant loaf that tastes like a yeasted bread, with little tang.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Young levain, low PFF, warm fast process | Process log | Raise PFF to 15–20 %, or use the levain slightly past peak |
| No retard, or a short one | Process log | Retard 12–16 h at 3–5 °C |
| Liquid levain, warm starter (more lactic, milder) | Levain hydration and temperature | Try a stiff levain (55 %) or a cooler levain build (21 °C) |
| All white flour | Flour log | Add 10–20 % wholemeal wheat or rye |

Sourness is a design choice, not a quality mark. Decide your target before changing the process.
