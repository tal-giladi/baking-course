# Laminated doughs

This category covers puff pastry (classic and blitz), croissant, pain au chocolat and Danish: doughs in which layers of plastic fat separate layers of dough. Faults in the fillings themselves (pastry cream texture, curdling) are in [Custards and creams](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md); faults of fermentation that are not specific to lamination are in [Lean bread](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md) and [Enriched doughs](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose, collect three pieces of evidence.** (1) **The tray:** where the butter went and when (a sheen before baking, pools after). (2) **The cross-section:** cut lengthwise with a serrated knife after 30 minutes and photograph it with a ruler; it shows whether the layers were continuous (lamination), inflated (proof) and dry (bake). (3) **The log:** butter and dough temperatures at lock-in, block temperature before and after every turn, proof temperature and time, measured oven temperature. Most laminated faults are temperature faults that happened hours before they became visible; the log tells you where. The mechanisms are in [15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md) (layers, plasticity, temperature), [15.2](stage-1/module-15/lesson-02.md) (puff), [15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md) (croissant, proof) and [15.4](stage-1/module-15/lesson-04.md) (Danish, fillings and the clinic method).

### Butter leaking during baking

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Proof too warm (above ~28 °C) | Butter sheen or droplets on the tray *before* baking; proof log; bready cross-section | Proof at 24–27 °C with a thermometer in the box; never in a warm oven without monitoring ([X1-40](experiments/X1-40-croissant-temperature.md)) |
| Underproofed | Pieces small, tight, heavy; sides split in the oven; dense core | Proof to 2–2.5× with jiggle (croissant), 1.5–2× (Danish); judge by the dough, not the clock |
| Butter layers broken at lamination (too cold) | Log: plaque below ~10 °C; shards seen while rolling; irregular holes in cross-section | Plaque at 12–15 °C, bent without cracking; see [Butter breaks into shards](troubleshooting/laminated.md?id=butter-breaks-into-shards-during-lamination) |
| Butter worked into the dough (too warm) | Log: block above ~16 °C during turns; bready crumb | Rest 20–30 min between turns; see [Butter smears or melts](troubleshooting/laminated.md?id=butter-smears-or-melts-during-lamination) |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer below setting; slow colour | Calibrate; puff 210–220 °C start, croissant 190 °C fan / 200 °C conventional |
| Too few turns (puff) | Thick butter layers; big blisters | 6 singles or 4 doubles ([X1-39](experiments/X1-39-puff-folds.md)) |
| Pieces loaded warm | Shaped or cut pieces left on a warm bench | Puff: rest cut pieces 30 min refrigerated; croissant: proof then bake straight away |

Leakage is the least specific laminated symptom: at least five causes produce it. A leak that starts before the oven is a proof problem; one that comes with a bready crumb is a lamination-temperature problem; one that comes with split sides and a tight core is underproof. See [15.4](stage-1/module-15/lesson-04.md).

### Bready, dense interior

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter smeared during lamination | Block log above 15–16 °C; butter visible as shiny patches while rolling; raw block cut face shows blurred lines | Keep the block at 10–15 °C; rest after each turn in a warm kitchen |
| Too many turns | Count: croissant > 36 fat layers, puff > 729 | Croissant 1D + 1S or 3S; puff 6S or 4D |
| Proof too warm | Proof log above 28 °C; butter on tray before baking | 24–27 °C |
| Lamination took too long (croissant) | Turns and sheeting over 2 h at room temperature; block gassy and springy | Finish lamination within about 1–1.5 h including rests in the refrigerator |
| Pressed during shaping (core only) | Honeycomb outside, dense spiral inside | Roll triangles lightly, without pressing down |
| Underproofed (core only) | Tight, heavy pieces | Longer proof to the full endpoint |

A bready crumb means dough layers fused into one dough. Find the step at which the fat stopped being a separate, continuous layer. See [15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md?id=reading-a-cross-section).

### Layers not distinct

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too many turns | Turn log | Reduce by one single turn |
| Butter smeared or melted | See Bready, dense interior | Temperature control |
| Edges sealed by cutting | Dragged or dull knife; layers pressed down at the cut face | Sharp blade, straight down |
| Egg wash on the edges | Low rise on washed sides | Wash the top only |
| Flour trapped between layers | Dry, white streaks in the cross-section | Brush off all flour before every fold |
| Scraps re-rolled as a ball | Product made from rognures | Stack scraps flat, same orientation; use for straws and bases |

### Butter breaks into shards during lamination

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Plaque too cold | Probe below 10–12 °C; butter snaps when bent; audible cracking under the pin | Temper the plaque to 12–15 °C; beat it with the pin to plasticize it |
| Block over-chilled between turns | Rest over 1 h in the refrigerator, or any time in the freezer | Rest 20–30 min; give 5–10 min on the bench after long rests; bend a corner to check |
| Butter firmer than the dough | Finger test: dough dents easily, butter does not | Match firmness: warm the butter slightly or chill the dough |
| Brittle (hard, winter) butter | Same temperature, still cracks | Beat longer; try another brand; use laminating butter if available |
| Rolling too fast and hard at the start | Cracks appear on the first strokes | Press the block with the pin in parallel ridges first, then roll gently |

Shards leave some dough layers without a fat barrier (they fuse) and others with thick fat pockets (they leak). See [15.1](stage-1/module-15/lesson-01.md?id=fat-plasticity-the-temperature-window) and Part B of [X1-40](experiments/X1-40-croissant-temperature.md).

### Butter smears or melts during lamination

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Plaque too warm at lock-in | Probe above 16 °C; butter shiny and sticky; squeezes out of the ends | Plaque at 12–15 °C |
| Block warmed during turns | Block above 16 °C after rolling | Rest 20–30 min after each turn (or each pair in a cool kitchen) |
| Warm kitchen, bench or hands | Room above 24 °C | Chill bench and pin; work faster; roll with the pin, not the hands |
| Dough torn, butter exposed | Butter showing through holes; sticks to the pin | Patch with flour, chill 15 min; see Dough tears during rolling |
| Too slow | Many small strokes, block handled a long time | Fewer, even, decisive strokes; plan dimensions in advance |

### Dough tears during rolling

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter much firmer than dough | Tears over hard butter patches | Match firmness |
| Détrempe underdeveloped or too soft | Sticky, weak dough; tears easily when stretched | Mix to moderate development (croissant) or a cohesive dough (puff); check hydration |
| Gluten tight, forced | Dough springs back; you pushed harder | Rest 20–30 min instead of forcing |
| Sheet rolled too thin locally | Thin spots near edges | Roll from the centre; guides; don't roll over the ends |

Patch a tear with a dusting of flour, brush off the excess, chill 15 minutes and continue.

### Dough shrinks after rolling or cutting

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Unrelaxed gluten after sheeting | Pieces shrink in the last rolling direction (squares become rectangles, circles ovals) | Roll in both directions; lift the sheet to let it shrink before cutting; rest cut pieces 30 min refrigerated |
| Détrempe overmixed | Mixing log (croissant beyond moderate development; puff beyond "just combined") | Short mix |
| Too few rests during turns | Dough fought every turn | Rest after every pair (puff) or each turn (Danish) |
| Strong flour | Protein above ~13 % | Blend with all-purpose flour |
| Sheeted too warm, then chilled | Triangles cut from a soft sheet shorten in the refrigerator | Chill the sheet before cutting |

Shrinkage is elastic recovery of stretched gluten ([SC-12](stage-1/science/sc-12-rheology.md)). Remove the strain (rest) before you cut, not after.

### Uneven rise, lopsided puff

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Egg wash on one edge | Low side has a glossy, stuck edge | Wash the top only |
| Dragged or dull cut on one side | Pressed-down layers at the low edge | Sharp blade, straight down; flip rounds |
| Uneven sheet thickness | Measure the sheet at several points; high side was thicker | Guides; roll from the centre |
| Folded edge not trimmed | One side of a square is a continuous fold | Trim all edges of the final sheet |
| Uneven plaque or lock-in | Cross-section of the block shows thick and thin butter zones | Plaque in a parchment packet of exact size; square corners |
| Unrested pieces | Shrinkage in one direction plus lopsided rise | 30 min rest after cutting |
| Oven hot spot | Pieces on one side of the tray always higher | Rotate the tray; map the oven ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |

### Tough, hard puff pastry

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Détrempe overmixed or too little butter in it | Mixing log; détrempe butter 0 % | Short mix; 10–15 % butter in the détrempe |
| Too many re-rolls of scraps | Tough pieces came from rognures | Use scraps for straws and bases, stacked flat |
| Too much flour on the bench | Dry, white streaks; dusty surface | Brush off flour before every fold and after sheeting |
| Underbaked, then softened | Chewy rather than crisp; pale inner leaves | Full drying phase at 180–190 °C |
| Butter smeared (fewer layers) | Dense, hard leaves | Temperature control |

### Croissants collapse

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overproofed | Volume above 3× before baking; spreading; dough fragile, dent does not spring back | Proof to 2–2.5×; check earlier; lower yeast or proof temperature if it always overshoots |
| Underbaked | Pale creases between the steps; collapse and wrinkling as they cool; soft within the hour | Bake until the creases are deep brown; tent if tops darken |
| Proof too warm | Butter lost during proof; weak structure | 24–27 °C |
| Too-soft or weak détrempe | Dough slack and sticky at shaping | Check hydration and mixing (moderate development) |
| Danish shapes open up | Points not pressed firmly; overproofed | Press points with a dab of egg wash; proof Danish to 1.5–2× |

Look at the creases to separate the causes: pale creases point to the bake, brown creases with a flat, spread shape point to the proof.

### Underproofed croissants, tight and leaking

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Proof judged by the clock | Proof time short for dough straight from the refrigerator | Judge by volume (2–2.5×), jiggle and visible layers; allow 45–60 min just to warm up |
| Proof environment too cool | Box below 22 °C | 24–27 °C |
| Yeast slowed by sugar | Standard instant yeast in 11–12 % sugar dough | Osmotolerant yeast, or raise standard instant yeast to about 1.5 % |
| Dough over-chilled | Very long retard of shaped croissants, very cold refrigerator | Longer proof; note the actual time |

Signature: small, heavy croissants, split sides and burst steps, butter pooled on the tray, a dense and slightly gummy centre. It is often mistaken for a lamination fault. See [15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md?id=reading-the-proof).

### Pale pastry

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer | Calibrate; bake at the specified temperature |
| Pulled too early | Bake time log; pale creases and base | Bake to deep golden-brown, creases included |
| No or thin egg wash | Matt, uneven colour | Wash twice (croissant, Danish), top only |
| Egg wash too diluted | Wash with a lot of water or milk | Egg + yolk + pinch of salt |
| Low sugar or no milk in the dough | Puff pastry: normal to be lighter without wash | Accept, or wash; bake longer at 180–190 °C |
| Overproofed | Stretched surface browns less | Proof to the correct endpoint |

### Too dark outside

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot | Oven thermometer above setting | Calibrate; lower 10 °C and extend the time |
| High sugar or thick wash pooled | Dark spots where the wash pooled; Danish darker than croissant at the same setting | Thin, even wash; lower the temperature for sugary products |
| Dark tray or top rack | Dark bases, or dark tops only | Light aluminium tray; middle rack; double-tray the base |
| Fan oven set as conventional | Fan on at conventional temperature | Reduce by 10–20 °C for fan |

If the outside is dark and the inside is still wet, see also Raw or doughy centre.

### Raw or doughy centre

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Pale creases (croissant); pale sides and base (puff) | Longer bake; two-phase profile for puff (210–220 °C, then 180–190 °C to dry) |
| Oven too hot for the product | Dark outside before the inside set | Lower 10 °C, extend the time; tent with foil |
| Pieces too thick or heavy | Weigh: croissant above ~70 g; puff sheet above 5 mm | Sheet to 4 mm; cut to the specified weight |
| Triangle rolled too tightly or pressed | Dense spiral in the core | Roll lightly |
| Underproofed (croissant) | Tight, gummy core | Full proof |

### Danish filling boils over

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Pastry cream too soft | Starch below ~10 % of milk; cream spreads when piped | Raise cornstarch to about 11 % of milk; boil 2 min; use cold and thick ([R1-35](recipes/R1-35-danish.md)) |
| Too much filling | Weigh: over ~25 g on an 11 cm square | 20–25 g |
| Filled before the proof | Cream slid as the dough grew | Fill open shapes after the proof, into a re-pressed well |
| Overproofed | Shapes opened and spread | Proof to 1.5–2× |
| Jam filling with low solids | Ordinary jam runs and boils | Bake-stable jam, or cook the jam down; small amounts |
| Oven too cool | Long bake; filling heats for longer before the pastry sets | Correct temperature |

### Soggy base under the filling

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Wet fruit | Juice around the fruit after baking | Drain and dry canned fruit; toss fresh juicy fruit with sugar and 5 % cornstarch, or pre-cook it |
| Underbaked base | Lift one with a spatula: base pale | Bake until the base is golden; lower rack or preheated tray for the first half |
| Too much filling | Weigh | 20–25 g per 11 cm square |
| Held too long, or refrigerated uncovered | Soft after several hours | Serve within 8 h; glaze fruit with hot nappage to seal it |

Moisture always moves from the wet filling into the drier pastry; you can only slow it and bake the base dry first. Moisture migration is measured in [X1-43](experiments/X1-43-moisture-migration.md).
