# Chocolate and ganache

This category covers faults in the three ways M16 uses chocolate: **melted** (into a batter or before tempering), **emulsified** with cream (ganache) and **crystallized** (tempered work). Most chocolate faults come down to four measurable things: **temperature** (melting, working, setting and storage, measured with a probe), **water** (a drop of steam in melted chocolate; too little water phase in a ganache), **crystal state** (tempered or not, proven only by a test strip) and **storage conditions** (temperature swings and humidity). Measure those before changing a formula. For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md). A split ganache is an emulsion fault: the general rules are in [Eggs, foams and emulsions](troubleshooting/eggs-foams-emulsions.md?id=broken-mayonnaise).

The mechanisms are in [16.1](stage-1/module-16/lesson-01.md) (composition, melting, seizing), [16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md) (ganache) and [16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md) (tempering, bloom), with the science in [SC-13](stage-1/science/sc-13-crystallization.md) and [SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md).

### Chocolate seized

The melted chocolate suddenly turns thick, dull and grainy, and stiffens into a paste that will not flow.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Steam from the bain-marie | Water was boiling; bowl did not seal the pan; condensation on the bowl rim | Water at a bare simmer, heat off; bowl sealing the pan and not touching the water; or use the microwave |
| Wet bowl, spatula or chopping board | Tools rinsed and not dried; drops on the bowl base | Dry everything; wipe the base of the bowl when lifting it off |
| Small amount of water-based flavouring (extract, liqueur, purée) | Seized immediately after the addition | Add water-based flavours to the cream of a ganache; use oil-based flavours for pure chocolate |
| Cold liquid added in small amounts to melted chocolate | Seized when the first spoonfuls went in | Add liquid **warm** and at least a third of it at once, then blend; or pour hot cream over chopped chocolate |

A few grams of water dissolve sugar at particle surfaces into a sticky syrup that glues particles together; the fat is squeezed out. Seized chocolate cannot be tempered. Rescue it into a sauce or ganache by stirring in **hot** water, milk or cream, 10 g at a time, until smooth and glossy. See [16.1](stage-1/module-16/lesson-01.md).

### Thick, burnt or grainy melted chocolate

The chocolate is thick, matte or lumpy although no water got in; it may smell scorched, and milk or white chocolate may look yellowish and curdled.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overheated (dark above ~50–55 °C, milk/white above ~45 °C) | Probe reading; microwave used at full power or long bursts; hot spots | Dark ≤ 50 °C, milk and white ≤ 45 °C; microwave at 50 % power, 20–30 s bursts, stir between; lift off the bain-marie at two thirds melted |
| Milk proteins scorched (milk and white chocolate) | Brown specks, burnt-milk smell, grainy lumps that do not melt | Discard for tempering; slightly scorched chocolate can go into a strongly flavoured batter. Next time lower heat |
| Low-fat chocolate (chips, "baking" bars) | Label and fat content; chips hold shape when melted | Use couverture for coating and tempering; chips are formulated to hold shape |
| Chocolate partly crystallized (cooled while stirring) | Thickens gradually at 27–30 °C; reheating to 35 °C thins it | Not a fault if you are tempering; otherwise rewarm to 40–45 °C |

Overheated chocolate cannot be un-burnt. Strain out lumps; if it only thickened, a small addition of cocoa butter (5–10 %) restores fluidity for coating work. See [16.1](stage-1/module-16/lesson-01.md).

### Grey streaks or haze after setting

Fat bloom: grey-white streaks, swirls or haze, soft and greasy to the touch; a warm finger smears it; a drop of water does not change it.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not tempered, or poorly tempered | Streaks appeared within hours or 1–3 days; slow set; test strip not done or set slowly | Temper by seeding and prove it with a test strip before use ([R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)) |
| Worked above working temperature (temper lost) | Log shows dark chocolate at 33–36 °C during use | Keep dark at 31–32 °C (milk 29–30 °C, white 28–29 °C); re-seed if it drifted |
| Storage too warm or with temperature swings | Stored near the oven or a window; room 20–28 °C over the day | Store at 15–18 °C, stable, in closed boxes |
| Set too slowly in a warm room, or too cold (fridge, freezer) and then warmed | Room above ~24 °C during setting; or frozen/refrigerated for hours | Set at 15–18 °C; fridge only 10–15 min for moulds |
| Fat migration from a filling (nut pastes, praline, butter-rich ganache) | Bloom starts at the join between filling and shell, after weeks | Stage 2: barrier layers, filling formulation; for Stage 1, eat within days |

Unstable crystal forms melt and recrystallize over time and fat moves to the surface as large, light-scattering crystals ([16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md), [X1-41](experiments/X1-41-tempering.md)). Bloomed chocolate is safe: melt and re-temper it.

### White dusty surface

Sugar bloom: a dry, white, dusty or rough surface that does not smear; a drop of water dissolves it.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Condensation after refrigeration | Chocolate was taken from the fridge and unwrapped in a warm room | Store at 15–18 °C; if refrigerated, let it warm in a sealed container before opening |
| Damp storage | Humid pantry, unsealed packaging, near a dishwasher or kettle | Store dry (below ~50–60 % RH), sealed |
| Water on the surface during work | Wiped with a damp cloth; steam in the room | Keep water and steam away from set chocolate |

Water on the surface dissolves sugar; as it evaporates, the sugar recrystallizes as fine dry crystals. It is cosmetic; for decorative work, re-melt and re-temper. See [16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md).

### Chocolate will not set or stays soft

The test strip or the product is still soft or tacky after 10 minutes at ~20 °C; it may set later, dull and streaky.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not in temper (too few form V crystals) | Test strip takes more than 8–10 min | Add 5–10 % more seed, stir 2 min, re-test |
| Working temperature too high | Probe reads above 32 °C (dark), 30 °C (milk), 29 °C (white) | Cool with more seed to working temperature; re-test |
| Room too warm | Room above ~24 °C | Work and set in a room at 18–22 °C; set at 15–18 °C |
| Compound coating mixed with couverture, or butter/oil added | Recipe added fat "for shine"; mixed products | Use couverture alone; never add butter or oil to chocolate for tempered work |
| Milk or white chocolate treated like dark | Worked at 31–32 °C | Use their lower curves or the pack's curve |

Only form V crystals set quickly and firmly at room temperature. See [16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md) and [R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md).

### Dull chocolate with no snap

The set chocolate is matte or satin, bends before it breaks, marks with fingerprints.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not tempered or lost temper | Test strip slow or streaky; see above | Temper and test |
| Over-crystallized when deposited | Chocolate thick and heavy in the bowl; very fast set; thick coats | Warm by 1 °C or add warm chocolate before depositing |
| Dirty or scratched mould, or unpolished | Dull patches match the mould's marks | Clean in warm water without abrasives, dry, polish with cotton wool |
| Set in the fridge or freezer from the start | Fast, cold setting | Set at 15–18 °C |
| Too thick a piece tested cold, or too warm when tested | Snap tested at 25 °C, or on a thick piece | Test snap at 18–20 °C on a 3–5 mm piece |

Gloss and snap come from a dense network of small form V crystals ([16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md)).

### Chocolate sticks in the mould

The bar or shells will not drop out; they come out with broken edges or dull patches.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not tempered | No visible gap between chocolate and mould edge after setting | Temper and test; tempered chocolate contracts and releases |
| Not set long enough | Unmoulded after 10–20 min at room temperature | Set 30–60 min at 15–18 °C, or 10–15 min in the fridge after the surface has set, then out |
| Silicone mould | Silicone flexes and does not show contraction | Use polycarbonate for glossy release; silicone for practice only |
| Mould too cold or too warm when filled | Mould straight from the fridge, or warm from washing | Moulds at room temperature (~20 °C) |

Form V is denser than the liquid and the unstable forms, so tempered chocolate shrinks slightly as it sets and pulls away from the mould ([X1-41](experiments/X1-41-tempering.md)).

### Tempered chocolate thickens while working

The chocolate in the bowl becomes thick and heavy, coats too thickly, sets in under 2 minutes on the strip.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-crystallized: form V crystals keep growing at working temperature | Temperature still in range, but viscosity rising over 15–30 min | Warm by 1 °C (3–5 s heat, stir) or stir in 10–20 % warm (35–40 °C) melted chocolate of the same kind; re-test |
| Chocolate cooled below working temperature | Probe reads below 30 °C (dark) | Warm back to 31–32 °C in short bursts, stirring |
| Too small a batch | Under ~500 g loses heat quickly | Work with 500 g or more; keep it on a heating pad set low |

A little thickening over time is normal. Stir every 1–2 minutes and check temperature every 5 ([R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md), step 8).

### Ganache split or greasy

Oily sheen, fat beading or pooling at the edge, curdled or grainy body, greasy mouthfeel.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too hot | Cream boiled hard; mixture above ~50 °C; reheated at full power | Cool to 32–35 °C, add 5–10 % warm milk or cream, blend with the head submerged |
| Too much fat for the water phase | High-% or high-fat chocolate at a firm ratio; calculated water below ~17–18 % | Same rescue; reformulate: less chocolate, or 5–10 % glucose/invert, or a little milk in place of some cream |
| Cream reduced during infusion | Cream weight after straining below the formula weight | Re-weigh after infusing and top up with fresh cream |
| Butter or liqueur added too hot or too cold | Butter added above 40 °C (melted to oil) or straight from the fridge | Add soft butter at 35–40 °C; liquids at room temperature |
| Over-agitation while cooling (whisking) | Whisked while cooling or setting; air bubbles | Emulsify once at 35–45 °C, then leave it still |

A ganache splits when its water phase cannot hold its fat droplets. Warm liquid gives the fat somewhere to disperse; if it will not come back, blend 20–30 g warm milk in a clean jug and add the broken ganache gradually, as for a broken mayonnaise ([16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md), [X1-42](experiments/X1-42-ganache-ratio.md)).

### Grainy ganache

Sandy, gritty or dull texture after setting, without free oil.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Coarse cocoa butter crystals from fast cooling (fridge) | Set in the fridge; dull surface, grainy in the mouth | Warm to 34–35 °C, blend, set undisturbed at 16–18 °C for 12 h |
| Stirred or piped while crystallizing | Worked at 24–28 °C for a long time | Pipe when just thick (26–28 °C) or after full crystallization; don't stir during setting |
| Sugar recrystallizing over days | Grain develops after 3–7 days; firm ratio; no glucose or invert | Add 5–10 % glucose syrup or invert sugar (on chocolate); keep ratio suited to the chocolate |
| Not fully emulsified (seizing zone) | Very firm ratio; too little hot liquid to melt the chocolate | Pre-melt chocolate to 45 °C for firm ratios; blend at 35–45 °C |
| Chocolate overheated before use | See [Thick, burnt or grainy melted chocolate](troubleshooting/chocolate.md?id=thick-burnt-or-grainy-melted-chocolate) | Replace chocolate |

Fat graininess melts out with gentle warming and blending; sugar graininess must be prevented by formulation ([16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md)).

### Ganache too soft or too firm

Too soft to hold a piped shape or roll; or too hard to pipe, cracking when cut.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong ratio for the job | Compare with the ratio tables in [16.2](stage-1/module-16/lesson-02.md) | Glaze 1 : 1, piping 1.5 : 1, truffle 2 : 1 (dark); milk and white need more chocolate |
| Chocolate changed (%, brand, fat, milk vs dark) | Formula written for a different chocolate | Re-run a small ratio test ([X1-42](experiments/X1-42-ganache-ratio.md)); adjust by 10–15 % |
| Not crystallized long enough, or too warm | Used after 1–2 h, or set at 22–25 °C | 12 h at 16–18 °C |
| Too cold (used straight from the fridge) | Hard, then soft at room temperature | Bring to 18–20 °C before piping or rolling |
| Too much butter or liquid added | Formula additions above ~15 % | Reduce; add melted chocolate to a soft ganache at 35 °C and re-blend |

A soft ganache can be firmed by blending in melted chocolate at 35 °C; a firm one softened with warm cream. Both change the ratio: record the new one.

### Brownies dry or cakey

Crumb is dry, light and cake-like; edges hard; little fudgy centre.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overbaked | Pulled after the centre had no wobble; toothpick midway came out clean | Pull when edges are set, top fully matte-crackled and the centre still wobbles slightly; start checking at 22 min |
| Oven hot | Oven thermometer reads above 175 °C | Calibrate ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)); set by the thermometer |
| Too much flour or cocoa | Flour measured by volume; formula changed | Weigh; keep flour ~75 g per 20 × 20 cm pan ([R1-38](recipes/R1-38-brownies.md)) |
| Eggs whipped too long (full ribbon) or leavening added | Batter very light and voluminous; baking powder in the formula | Whip eggs and sugar 3–4 min, short of a ribbon; omit leavening for fudgy |
| Less chocolate or butter, or low-fat chocolate | Formula changed; chips used | Keep the chocolate and butter weights; use a 60–70 % bar or couverture |
| Dark or glass pan | Edges overbaked before the centre set | Light metal pan; for glass, 160–165 °C and longer |

Fudgy vs cakey is the balance of tenderizers (fat, chocolate, sugar) against builders and air (flour, whipped egg, leavening), plus bake time ([R1-38](recipes/R1-38-brownies.md), [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)).

### Brownies raw in the centre

A wet, glossy band of raw batter in the middle; centre sinks deeply after cooling; edges fine or overdone.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Centre still rippled when shaken; shiny wet batter visible in the middle of the top | Bake 2–4 min more; use all the doneness cues |
| Oven cool, or pan too small/deep | Oven thermometer below target; batter deeper than ~2.5 cm | Calibrate the oven; scale batter to pan area |
| Glass or ceramic pan | Slow heat transfer to the centre | Metal pan, or lower temperature and longer time |
| Cut while warm | Cut before 1 h of cooling | Cool at least 1 h, chill 1 h for clean cuts; the centre finishes setting on cooling |
| Cold eggs | Batter thick and lumpy; chocolate set in streaks | Eggs at ~20 °C |

A fudgy brownie should be moist and dense in the centre, not liquid. Distinguish by cutting after full cooling: fudgy holds its cut face; raw flows or smears wet.

### Brownies without a shiny top

The top is dull, matte or cakey, with no thin crackled crust.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar not dissolved | Eggs and sugar only stirred; batter gritty between the fingers | Whip eggs and sugar 3–4 min until paler and thickened, sugar mostly dissolved |
| Chocolate mixture too cold when added | Melted chocolate cooled below ~35 °C | Add at 40–45 °C: the warmth helps dissolve sugar |
| Brown sugar or a high proportion of liquid sugars | Formula used brown sugar or honey | Use caster sugar for a shiny top; accept duller tops with brown sugar |
| Batter overmixed after flour | Beaten hard after adding flour | Fold just until no dry streaks |

The crust is a thin film of egg and dissolved sugar that rises to the surface and dries like a meringue ([R1-38](recipes/R1-38-brownies.md)).
