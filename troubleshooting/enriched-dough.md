# Troubleshooting: enriched doughs

This database covers yeast doughs enriched with sugar, fat, eggs and milk: soft milk bread and rolls ([R1-07](recipes/R1-07-milk-bread.md)), challah ([R1-08](recipes/R1-08-challah.md)), brioche ([R1-09](recipes/R1-09-brioche.md)) and sweet doughs. It is owned by [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md).

Enriched doughs fail in different ways from lean doughs, for three reasons that run through almost every entry below:

1. **Sugar and fat slow fermentation.** Timelines from lean bread do not transfer ([06.1](stage-1/module-06/lesson-01.md)).
2. **Butter is temperature-sensitive.** Above about 26–27 °C it starts to leave the dough as free fat ([06.4](stage-1/module-06/lesson-04.md)).
3. **Sugar, milk and eggs brown fast.** Colour arrives long before the centre is done, so ovens run at 175–190 °C and doneness is judged by internal temperature, 88–93 °C ([06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md)).

Use each entry as a diagnostic procedure: read the symptom, test the most likely causes in order, change **one** variable, and record the result in your lab notebook. Faults common to all breads (for example over-fermentation smell or gummy crumb in lean bread) are in [Lean bread](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md) and [Bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md). The general method is in the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

### Slow or no rise in sweet dough

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar too high for the yeast type (osmotic stress) | Compute sugar ÷ (sugar + dough water). Above ~15 % with standard instant yeast, expect a slow rise. Compare with your [X1-15](experiments/X1-15-sugar-in-dough.md) curve | Use osmotolerant (gold) instant yeast; raise the dose to 1.5–2.5 % |
| Yeast dose too low for an enriched dough | Compare the yeast % with 06.1's table (sweet doughs 1.5–2.5 % instant) | Raise yeast; or accept a longer time and plan for it |
| Dough too cold | Probe the dough after mixing and during bulk. Cold eggs and milk often give 20–22 °C | Warm the milk slightly, target 24–27 °C, and ferment somewhere warm (brioche ≤27 °C) |
| Retarded dough not yet warmed up | Probe the core of the shaped dough. Below ~15 °C, it is barely fermenting | Allow the first hour of proof for warming; judge proof by volume, not the clock |
| Salt and sugar together, or salt doubled | Recheck the formula sheet and the weighing | Salt 1.6–2 % in sweet doughs |
| Hot liquid killed part of the yeast | Was scalded milk above ~45 °C when it met the yeast? | Cool scalded milk to ≤40 °C; probe it |
| Old or mis-stored yeast | Proof 2 g yeast + 2 g sugar in 50 g water at 32 °C: it should foam within 10 min | Replace the yeast; store it sealed in the fridge or freezer |

In sweet doughs, what matters to the yeast is sugar concentration in the dough water, not sugar % on flour. That is why a low-water brioche with 12 % sugar is slower than a milk bread with 10 %. See [06.1](stage-1/module-06/lesson-01.md) and [X1-15](experiments/X1-15-sugar-in-dough.md).

### Greasy, broken brioche dough

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough too warm when the butter went in | Probe: base dough above 24 °C, final dough above 26 °C | Cold eggs and milk (4–5 °C); chill the base 10–15 min before the butter; chill the bowl and remix if above 26 °C |
| Butter too soft | Butter above ~18 °C, shiny, smearing on the bowl walls instead of disappearing | Beat cold butter between parchment to a plastic 13–16 °C |
| Butter added too fast | Were large amounts added at once, before the previous addition was absorbed? | Add in 4–5 additions, each fully absorbed before the next |
| Base dough under-developed before the butter | The base did not windowpane before the butter went in | Develop the base to a good windowpane first (10–15 min intensive mixing) |
| Over-mixing after the butter in a warm kitchen | The dough temperature kept rising after it came together | Stop once the dough is smooth and windowpanes; chill if needed |
| Proofed too warm (grease appears later) | Fat pooling under moulds; proof spot above 27 °C | Proof at 24–27 °C with a thermometer |

A broken dough is butterfat melting out of the dough as liquid oil that can no longer be held as a dispersion ([06.4](stage-1/module-06/lesson-04.md), [SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md)). Chilling for 15–20 min and remixing briefly on low often rescues it if the base was well developed. If not, bake it as a dense brioche for bread pudding and start again colder.

### Dense brioche

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Base dough under-developed | Check your notes: did the base windowpane before the butter? A dense, cakey, close crumb with a tight base | Mix the base longer before the butter; butter cannot be added until development is complete |
| Dough broke or turned greasy during mixing | Final dough temperature above 26 °C; shiny, curdled dough | See [greasy, broken brioche dough](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=greasy-broken-brioche-dough) |
| Under-proofed | Shaped dough less than 2× before baking; retarded dough baked while still cold | Proof to 2–2.5× at 24–27 °C; expect 2–3 h from the fridge |
| Proofed too warm | Grease in the moulds after proof, coarse, greasy crumb | Proof at ≤27 °C |
| Too much butter for the flour or development | Butter above ~70 % with all-purpose flour or hand mixing | Use bread flour; reduce butter to 40–50 % until your technique is reliable ([X1-17](experiments/X1-17-brioche-butter.md)) |
| Under-baked centre that sank | Internal below 88 °C; compressed, damp band near the base | Bake to 88–93 °C; tent to protect the colour |

Brioche height comes from the gluten network, not the butter. Every dense brioche should first be checked against the base-dough windowpane in your notes.

### Crust too dark before the inside is done

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot | Oven thermometer at the rack position | 175–180 °C (350–355 °F) for loaves, 180–190 °C (355–375 °F) for small pieces and challah |
| Rack too high, or top element cycling | Top dark, sides and base pale | Lower third of the oven; a sheet of foil or a tray on the rack above as a shield |
| Too much sugar, honey or milk in the formula | Compare the formula with the family ranges in 06.1 | Lower the oven further for sweeter doughs; honey and fructose brown fastest |
| Egg wash too thick, or with sugar or honey added | Heavy, dark, lacquered patches | A thin, strained coat; add sugar to the wash only for short bakes |
| Not tented | The crust reached deep golden before half the bake time | Tent loosely with foil once colour is right, often at 15–20 min |
| Loaf too large for the temperature | 900 g challah or a loaf baked at the setting for rolls | Larger pieces: lower temperature, longer time |

Always judge enriched bread by internal temperature (88–93 °C), not crust colour. Colour arrives early because residual sugar, lactose and egg protein all feed the Maillard reaction ([04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md), [SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)). See also [Bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md).

### Dry crumb

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-baked | Internal well above 93 °C, or bake time far longer than the recipe | Pull at 88–93 °C; use a probe |
| True hydration too low | Compute it, counting milk at 87 %, egg at 75 %, butter at 16 % water | Raise water or milk by 2–4 points; or use a tangzhong ([06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md)) |
| Too much egg white | Dry, slightly rubbery crumb; formula with high whole-egg and no extra yolk | Replace part of the whole egg with yolks plus water |
| Too little fat for the product | Compare fat % with the family ranges | Raise fat by 2–5 points |
| Tangzhong lost water, or lumpy | Weigh the tangzhong after cooking; lumps visible in the crumb | Top up to the original mass; whisk while cold before heating |
| Stored unsealed or refrigerated | Storage notes | Seal once fully cool, at room temperature; freeze for longer than 2–3 days |
| Too much flour on the bench | Flour streaks or seams in the crumb | Shape on a lightly oiled or unfloured bench |

Firming during storage and "dryness" feel similar, but they are different: weigh the loaf. Little weight loss plus firming is staling (starch retrogradation), which slows with more water, fat and tangzhong. See [X1-16](experiments/X1-16-tangzhong.md) and [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md).

### Braids splitting or tearing

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-proofed | Deep, pale cracks at the joins; strands still distinct with deep gaps at baking | Proof to ~+75 %, until the gaps between strands are closing |
| Braided too tight (strands stretched) | Tears on the tighter side or along one strand | Lay strands snugly without pulling |
| Strands not rested before rolling | Strands shrank back; rough, torn surface | Rest pre-shaped logs 15–20 min, covered |
| Dough under-developed | Crumb dense and cakey, surface tears easily | Mix to a membrane you can see light through |
| Dough too stiff or dried surface | Surface cracks before baking; true hydration below ~50 %; uncovered proof in a dry room | Keep true hydration 50–56 %; first egg wash right after braiding; cover during proof |
| Oven too hot | Crust set before oven spring ended, so the joins ripped | 180–190 °C; the right temperature for the loaf size |

The joins are the weakest points of a braid. Anything that makes oven spring larger than the dough can stretch (under-proof, over-tight braiding) opens them. See [06.3](stage-1/module-06/lesson-03.md).

### Egg wash streaks or blotches

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Wash not strained, or poorly beaten | Glossy blobs and patches of cooked white | Beat thoroughly with a pinch of salt; strain through a fine sieve |
| Wash too thick, or pooled | Dark tide-marks at the base or in joins; drips down the sides | Thin coats; brush out excess; wipe pools with a dry brush |
| Single coat on an expanded surface | Glossy strands with dull, pale stretched areas between them | Double wash: after shaping and again before baking |
| Hard or dry brush | Visible brush marks, streaks | Soft brush, light strokes, lightly loaded |
| Wash on a wet or floury surface | Blotches where flour or condensation was | Brush off flour before washing; let condensation dry |
| Wash applied late, onto a skinned surface | Uneven gloss, cracking | Wash promptly after shaping; don't leave dough uncovered to skin |

Salt breaks down egg proteins slightly so the wash flows evenly; straining removes the chalazae and gelled white that bake as blotches. See [06.3](stage-1/module-06/lesson-03.md).

### Loaf collapses or caves in

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-baked | Internal below 88 °C; sides soft and pale; damp band at the base | Bake to 88–93 °C; bake out of the pan for 3–5 min to firm the sides |
| Over-proofed | Top was above the rim or very soft before baking; wrinkled, sunken top | Proof milk bread to ~1 cm below the rim; brioche to 2–2.5× |
| Left in the pan | Sides wrinkled and concave, pan steamy | Demould within 5–10 min |
| Dough too soft or weak for its height | True hydration high, weak flour, under-developed | Bread flour; mix to a thin windowpane; reduce water 2–3 points |
| Too much sugar or fat for the structure | Formula outside the family ranges | Reduce, or add yolk or develop more |
| Cut too soon | Crumb compressed under the knife, gummy | Cool at least 1–1.5 h before slicing |

In enriched pan loaves the crust is thin and soft, so it gives little support. The sides cave in whenever the crumb is still soft (under-baked, hot) or too weak. See [06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md).

### Uneven colour on challah

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Egg wash missed areas | Pale joins, lower sides or ends; photograph before baking | Wash thoroughly into joins and down to the base, twice |
| Joins opened late in the bake (under-proof) | Pale, fresh-looking dough in the cracks | Proof longer; see [braids splitting or tearing](troubleshooting/enriched-dough.md?id=braids-splitting-or-tearing) |
| Oven hot spots | One side darker; consistent with your oven map | Rotate at halfway; see your oven map ([00.2](stage-1/module-00/lesson-02.md)) |
| Rack too high or low | Top dark and base pale, or the reverse | Centre rack; double tray for the base |
| Pooled wash | Dark patches in the joins and at the base | Thin coats; brush out pools |
| Tent put on too late or unevenly | Dark top, lighter sides | Tent loosely over the whole loaf when the top reaches deep golden |

Colour is part of the specification for challah. Record where the loaf was on the tray and photograph it, so that colour faults can be separated into formula, wash and oven causes. See [06.3](stage-1/module-06/lesson-03.md).

### Torn or burst sides

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-proofed | Ragged tear along the side or at the top of the pan wall, with a dense band below; loaf below the proof mark when baked | Proof milk bread to ~1 cm below the rim; brioche to 2–2.5× |
| Pieces shaped too tight or rolled too tightly | Tears along the spiral or seam; surface skin torn during shaping | Firm but not tearing tension; rest before final shaping |
| Seam not sealed or not facing down | Split along a seam | Pinch seams; place seam-down |
| Crust set too early (oven too hot, skinned surface) | Tear at the shoulder with a dark top | Correct oven temperature; cover during proof; egg wash |
| Brioche à tête: shallow well | Head tilted or fallen off, split at the neck | Push the fingertips to the bottom of the mould; or the two-piece method |

A tear shows where the loaf was still expanding after the crust had set. It almost always points to proof level or shaping tension. See [06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md) and [06.4](stage-1/module-06/lesson-04.md), and for the lean-bread version, [blowouts on the side](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=blowouts-on-the-side).
