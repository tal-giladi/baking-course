# Lean bread: dough and fermentation

This database covers failures in lean yeasted bread (flour, water, salt, yeast) that start in the dough: mixing, fermentation, shaping, proofing and scoring. Each entry runs from **symptom** to **likely causes**, then **how to test**, then **correction**. The causes are ordered from most to least common for a learner baking [R1-01](recipes/R1-01-lean-loaf.md) or [R1-02](recipes/R1-02-rolls-pan-loaf.md). Before you change anything, check your bake sheet ([03.2](stage-1/module-03/lesson-02.md) lab notebook). Most failures are explained by one of three numbers: dough temperature after mixing, % rise at the end of bulk, or internal temperature at bake. Change **one** variable per bake. Faults that start in the oven (crust colour, steam, bake time, staling) belong to [Bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md). Sourdough-specific faults are in [sourdough](troubleshooting/sourdough.md).

### Dense, tight crumb

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-fermented bulk (ended at +20–40 %) | Check the % rise on the container mark at the end of bulk in your log | Extend bulk to +50–75 %. Judge by volume, not by the clock ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)) |
| Dough too cold, so fermentation slower than expected | Dough temperature after mixing was below 23 °C? | Recalculate the water temperature from DDT ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)). Extend time or find a warmer spot |
| Under-proofed | The poke test sprang back fast. Look for a burst side and a dense base | Proof longer, to a slow partial spring-back ([X1-09](experiments/X1-09-proof-level.md)) |
| Too little yeast, or dead or old yeast | Re-weigh the dose. Test 1 g yeast in 20 g water at 35 °C with a pinch of sugar: it should foam in 10 min | Weigh on a 0.1 g scale. Replace the yeast. Store opened instant yeast airtight and cold |
| Hydration too low for the flour | Dough stiff and hard to fold. Compare with your notes | Raise water 2–3 % |
| Underbaked or cut hot | Internal temperature below 96 °C? Cut within 1 h? | Bake to ≥96 °C and cool for 1–2 h ([04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md)) |
| Weak flour (low protein) | Check the label (<11 %). Dough doesn't gain strength with folds | Use bread flour (11.5–13 %) or add a fold ([02.1](stage-1/module-02/lesson-01.md)) |

A dense crumb nearly always means the network didn't hold enough gas at the moment it set. Either too little gas was produced (fermentation) or the gas escaped (a weak network or cutting hot). Look at the bottom third of the loaf first: a dense base with a burst side points to under-proof.

### Collapsed or flat loaf

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-proofed | The poke test left a dent that stayed. The loaf deflated when scored. Pale crust | Shorten proof. Check 15 min earlier. Score shallowly when in doubt ([X1-09](experiments/X1-09-proof-level.md)) |
| Over-fermented bulk (+100 % or more, flat top) | Container mark. Boozy smell | End bulk at +50–75 %. Reduce yeast or temperature ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)) |
| Too little shaping tension | The loaf spreads within minutes of leaving the banneton | Tighter shape. Pre-shape plus a 20 min rest first ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Dough too wet for the flour | Slack at every stage. Won't hold a fold | Drop hydration 2–5 %. Use stronger flour |
| No yeast activity at all (flat *and* dense) | Did the dough rise in bulk? Yeast test as above | Replace the yeast. Check the water was not above ~50 °C |
| Retarded loaf warmed too long before baking | Log: how long out of the fridge? | Bake straight from the fridge, or within 30 min |

A flat loaf with an open crumb means too much fermentation or too little tension. A flat loaf with a dense crumb means too little fermentation or dead yeast. Cut the loaf before you decide which.

### Weak, slack dough

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hydration too high for the flour | Compare with X1-04 results for your flour ([X1-04](experiments/X1-04-hydration-series.md)) | Reduce water 2–5 %, or hold back 5 % and add it only if the dough can take it |
| Under-developed gluten | No membrane at the end of mixing. Dough doesn't tighten after folds | Mix 2–3 min longer, or add a fold set ([X1-05](experiments/X1-05-mixing-time.md)) |
| Over-fermented (warm, high yeast, long) | Dough temperature >27 °C, rise >100 %, sticky, smells sour or boozy | Lower DDT or yeast. End bulk earlier |
| Salt forgotten or low | Taste a crumb of raw dough (then spit it out), or check the log | Add salt at 2 % ([X1-06](experiments/X1-06-salt.md)) |
| Low-protein or weak flour | Label protein; gluten wash ([X1-03](experiments/X1-03-gluten-wash.md)) | Switch to bread flour. Lower hydration |
| Cold dried yeast rehydration (glutathione release) | Was ADY rehydrated in cold water? | Rehydrate ADY at ~38–40 °C, or use instant yeast mixed into the flour |

Slackness appears at shaping as a dough that won't hold tension and spreads within minutes. Decide whether the dough was *always* slack (formula or flour) or *became* slack during bulk (over-fermentation).

### Dough tears or feels too tight

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| No or too short bench rest | The dough fights back and snaps when stretched | Rest 20–30 min after pre-shaping ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Too much tension in pre-shape or shape | The surface shows strain lines, then tears | Shape until just taut. Stop at the first sign of strain |
| Over-mixed plus many folds | Very elastic, bouncy dough | Mix less, or drop the last fold set |
| Hydration too low | Stiff dough, hard to stretch | Raise water 2–3 % |
| Under-fermented bulk | Dense, rubbery, little gas | Extend bulk |
| Flour on the bench under the dough | The dough slides and won't grip, so the skin has to be forced | Shape on unfloured bench. Flour only the top |

A tear lets gas escape and leaves a rough spot where the loaf will blow out. If the dough tears, stop, cover it, rest it 10 min, and finish shaping.

### Bulk fermentation too slow or too fast

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dough temperature off target | Dough temperature after mixing vs 24–25 °C | Fix the water temperature. At ±3 °C, expect ~±25–30 % in time ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md)) |
| Yeast weighed wrong (for example, fresh-yeast grams used for instant) | Re-read the formula and the conversion ([03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md)) | Weigh on a 0.1 g scale. Instant ≈ ⅓ of fresh |
| Room much colder or warmer than the dough | Thermometer beside the container | Move to a controlled spot (see [X1-07](experiments/X1-07-fermentation-temperature.md)) |
| Old or poorly stored yeast (slow) | Yeast foam test | Replace the yeast |
| Salt doubled (slow) | Log. Very salty taste | Weigh salt separately. Use 2 % |
| Container wider than usual (looks slow) | Straight-sided or not? | Always use the same straight-sided, marked container |

Don't correct timing by changing the formula mid-bake. Adjust the *time*, and fix the cause next bake.

### Poor oven spring

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-proofed | The poke test left a lasting dent. Pale crust. Score barely opened | Shorten proof ([X1-09](experiments/X1-09-proof-level.md)) |
| Oven, pot or stone not fully preheated | Preheat was under 45 min? Measure the stone with an IR thermometer | Preheat the pot 45 min, the stone 60 min, at 250 °C (480 °F) |
| No steam, or the lid came off too early | Dull crust, score sealed over | Covered pot for 20 min, or steam tray ([X1-10](experiments/X1-10-steam.md)) |
| Score too shallow, too vertical or dragged | Inspect the cut. Is the blade old? | New blade, 30–45°, 5–8 mm, one stroke ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Weak shaping tension | The loaf spread before scoring | Tighter shape |
| Skin dried during proof | Leathery surface, drag when scoring | Cover during proof |
| Weak or slack dough | See [weak, slack dough](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md?id=weak-slack-dough) | — |

Oven spring needs three things together: gas and fermentation potential left in the dough, a skin that holds it, and a hot, humid oven that keeps the surface extensible for the first 10–15 min.

### Large holes under the crust (flying crust)

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-proofed, so cells merge under the skin | Pale top, lasting poke dent | Shorten proof |
| Under-proofed plus a high oven, so the crust sets and the crumb springs away | Dense crumb under the cavity, burst side | Proof longer |
| Gas pocket trapped at shaping | One large, smooth-walled void | Degas gently at pre-shape. Pop large surface bubbles before shaping |
| Pan loaf: top crust set before the crumb finished rising | Pan loaf only. Cavity along the whole top | Proof to 1–2 cm above the rim, not higher. Lower the oven by 10 °C ([R1-02](recipes/R1-02-rolls-pan-loaf.md)) |
| Dry skin during proof | Top crust separates cleanly from the crumb | Cover the dough |

### Uneven or large irregular holes

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Uneven shaping (air pockets, seams, flour trapped inside) | Holes run along the spiral of the roll-up | Degas lightly. Flour only the outside. Seal each roll-up turn |
| Under-developed gluten plus high hydration | Holes are big and random, with dense areas between | More mixing or one more fold ([X1-05](experiments/X1-05-mixing-time.md)) |
| Under-proofed | Tunnels near the score, dense base | Proof longer |
| Too few folds, so large cells were never subdivided | Very irregular crumb in a well-fermented dough | 3 fold sets during bulk |
| Expectation | An open, irregular crumb is *correct* for a rustic hearth loaf | Decide your spec first. For an even crumb, use lower hydration and more mixing, as in R1-02 |

### Sour or alcoholic off-flavour

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-fermented (bulk or proof too long or too warm) | Rise well past +100 %. Boozy smell before baking | End bulk at +50–75 %. Shorten proof |
| Too much yeast (conversion error) | Formula check. Yeasty, bitter taste | Use the correct dose. Instant ≈ ⅓ of fresh |
| Dough too warm (>28 °C) | Dough temperature log | Lower DDT |
| Very long retard with normal yeast | Retard over 16 h at 0.8–1 % yeast | Drop yeast to 0.4–0.5 % for long retards |
| Fridge too warm (7–8 °C) | Thermometer in the fridge where the dough sits | Use the back of the fridge, or set it to 3–5 °C |
| Contaminated or old container or dough scraps | Sour smell even on short ferments | Clean containers. Don't add old dough unless it is deliberate ([05.1](stage-1/module-05/lesson-01.md)) |

A little acidity after a long, cool ferment is desirable. It becomes a fault when it dominates or tastes of solvent.

### Loaf spreads sideways

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too little shaping tension | The surface looks slack. No resistance at shaping | Shape tighter. Make sure the pre-shape and rest are right ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Over-proofed | Lasting poke dent | Shorten proof |
| Hydration too high or flour too weak | Always slack | Drop water 2–5 %. Use stronger flour |
| Proofed without support | Free-standing on a tray | Use a banneton or couche |
| Over-fermented bulk | Slack, sticky, sour at shaping | End bulk earlier |
| Oven not hot enough at loading | Slow spring, spreads before setting | Preheat fully. Use a stone or pot |

### Blowouts on the side

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-proofed | Fast poke spring-back. Dense base | Proof longer |
| Score too shallow or too short | The score barely opened while the side ruptured | Cut 5–8 mm deep, full length, confidently |
| Seam not sealed, or seam placed on the side | The split runs along the seam | Pinch the seam closed. Proof seam-up so the seam ends up on the base |
| Uneven shaping, so a thin spot in the skin | The blowout is at a torn or thin area | Even tension. Avoid tears |
| Skin dried out, so the score couldn't open | Leathery crust at scoring | Cover during proof. Steam or lid |
| Pan loaf: under-proofed or overfilled | Split along one side just above the pan rim | Proof to 1–2 cm above the rim. Check dough weight vs pan |

### Pale crust

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Over-fermented or over-proofed, so sugar is depleted | Long, warm ferment. Lasting poke dent | Shorten bulk or proof. Use less yeast ([03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md)) |
| Oven too cool, or pulled too early | Oven thermometer. Internal temperature | See [bread-baking](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md) (M04) |
| Lid left on too long / not enough dry baking | Bake log | Remove the lid at 20 min and bake 18–25 min more |

Dough-side causes are covered here. Oven-side causes are in [Bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md).

### Burnt crust

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven hotter than the dial, or the bottom element too close | Oven map from [X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md) | Calibrate. Put a baking sheet under the pot for the uncovered stage |
| Burnt base in the pot | Only the base is burnt | Raise the rack one level. Put a tray or a sprinkling of semolina under the parchment |
| Baked too long for the loaf size | Internal temperature far above 99 °C | Bake to temperature, not by the clock |
| Added sugar or malt in the dough | Formula check | Lower the finish temperature by 10–15 °C |

Full oven diagnostics are in [Bread: baking, crust and staling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md).
