# Cookies and caramel

This category covers drop cookies (the R1-11 family) and cooked sugar (caramel and caramel sauce, R1-10). Short-dough cookies such as sablés and shortbread share some causes with short pastry; see [Short pastry, pies, tarts](troubleshooting/short-pastry.md). Sugar syrups cooked to a stage (soft ball to hard crack) and their crystallization are in [Syrups, meringues, buttercreams](troubleshooting/meringues-buttercreams.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose a cookie fault, check the four measurements that explain most of them:** butter temperature at mixing (18–20 °C for creamed dough), dough temperature at oven entry, portion weight, and the real oven temperature (not the dial; see [X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)). Then use the race model from [07.3](stage-1/module-07/lesson-03.md): a cookie spreads until its structure sets, so every spread fault is either too much flow, too little set, or the reverse. Caramel faults come down to three things: seeds for crystallization, carry-over heat, and temperature shock ([07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md)).

### Excessive spreading

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter too warm or melted at mixing; dough warm at oven entry | Probe: butter above 21 °C, dough above 24 °C; dough shiny and soft | Butter at 18–20 °C; chill dough; bake from the refrigerator at 4–8 °C |
| Dough not rested | Compare with a ball from the same batch rested 24 h ([X1-20](experiments/X1-20-cookie-spread.md)) | Rest 12–72 h at 3–5 °C |
| Too much sugar, or a syrup (honey, invert, golden syrup) swapped in by weight | Check the formula: sugar above ~110 % of flour; syrup present | Return to baseline sugar; substitute syrups on solids ([X1-18](experiments/X1-18-cookie-sugar-level.md), [X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md)) |
| Too little flour (volume measuring, or a low-absorption cake flour) | Weigh the flour; compare with a batch made with all-purpose flour | Weigh flour; use 10–11.5 % protein flour |
| Oven cooler than set | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting; preheat 30 min |
| Greased tray, or a hot tray reused | Tray still warm to the touch; spread worse on later trays | Parchment on a cool tray every time |
| Too much baking soda | Weigh: above ~1 % of flour; cookies dark, coarse, soapy taste | Weigh soda to 0.1 g; 0.5–0.8 % of flour |
| Creamed too long, especially warm | Creaming time above 4–5 min; dough very pale and fluffy | Cream 2–3 min at medium |

Spread is too much flow before the edges set. Find which ingredient or temperature made the dough fluid too early. See [07.3](stage-1/module-07/lesson-03.md).

### Cookies do not spread

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much flour (scooped by volume) | Weigh a "cup" of your flour ([X1-01](experiments/X1-01-volume-vs-weight.md)); dough stiff and dry | Weigh the flour |
| Dough very cold, rested long, or frozen, and oven hot | Ball still hard at loading; cookies thick with raw centres | Let balls stand 10–20 min before baking, or bake at 170 °C |
| Too little sugar, or sugar reduced | Formula check | Restore sugar or compensate (fat state, soda) ([07.1](stage-1/module-07/lesson-01.md)) |
| Baking powder instead of soda, or too much egg | Formula check; cookies puffy and cakey | Use soda at 0.5–1 %; egg ~35–40 % |
| High-protein flour | Label; cookies thick, chewy, dark | Use all-purpose or blend with pastry flour |
| Silicone mat or dark insulated tray | Compare with light aluminium + parchment | Use a light tray with parchment |
| Butter too cold or not creamed; shortening instead of butter | Visible butter lumps; formula | Butter at 18–20 °C, creamed 2–3 min |

If the dough balls keep their shape, the structure set before the dough could flow: either the dough was too stiff (flour, cold) or the set came too early (hot oven, dark tray, low pH).

### Cookies too hard

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overbaked | Centre fully coloured when pulled; bake loss above ~12 % | Pull when the centre is still pale and puffed; weigh the tray before and after |
| All or mostly white sugar | Formula; cookies snap | More brown sugar, or 10–15 % invert/honey on solids ([X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md)) |
| Overmixed after the flour | Mixing time above 1 min; tough, dense bite | Mix only until the flour disappears |
| Too thin (excessive spread) | Spread ratio above ~10 | See [excessive spreading](troubleshooting/cookies.md?id=excessive-spreading) |
| Stored uncovered | Weight loss in storage | Airtight storage |

Hard means dry and crystalline or glassy. Keep more water in the centre and more sugar amorphous.

### Too soft or raw centre

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Centre wet, collapses and stays gummy after 20 min cooling | Bake 1–2 min longer; pull when the edges are golden brown, not pale |
| Oven too hot: edges done before the centre | Oven thermometer; dark edges with raw middle | Lower the oven 10 °C, bake longer |
| Portion too large or dough frozen | Portion weight; ball temperature | Weigh portions; add 1–2 min from frozen |
| Too little flour, or too much egg or syrup | Formula check; weigh flour | Correct the formula |
| Moved off the tray too soon | Cookie tears when lifted | 5 min carry-over on the tray |

A soft centre is the goal; a raw one is not. The test is a centre that holds together and is no longer glossy-wet after 20 minutes.

### Burnt bottoms

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dark or thin tray | Compare with a light, heavy aluminium tray | Light tray; or double-tray (one tray under another) |
| Rack too low, or bottom element exposed | Bottoms dark, tops pale | Middle rack |
| Oven hotter than set, or hot spots | Oven thermometer; oven map ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) | Correct the setting; rotate the tray at half time |
| Sugar high in reducing sugars (honey, invert, brown) | Formula; bases dark on these variants only ([X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md)) | Lower the oven 10 °C; use parchment |
| Baked on a hot tray | Tray reused without cooling | Cool trays between batches |

### Pale cookies

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too cool or bake too short | Oven thermometer; timer | Correct oven; bake to golden edges |
| All white sugar, low sugar | Formula | Part brown sugar; restore sugar ([X1-18](experiments/X1-18-cookie-sugar-level.md)) |
| Baking powder instead of soda, or acid in the formula | Formula; cookies puffy and pale | Soda at 0.5–1 % for colour ([07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md)) |
| Dough not rested | Compare with a 24 h rested ball | Rest 24–72 h |
| Silicone mat or very light, insulated tray | Pale bases | Parchment on aluminium |
| Shortening instead of butter | Formula | Butter (milk solids brown), or add 2–3 % milk powder |

Colour needs reducing sugars, protein, a dry surface above ~140 °C and, for speed, a higher pH. Find which was missing.

### Dry, crumbly cookies

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much flour | Weigh flour; dough stiff and cracks when rolled | Weigh flour |
| Too little fat or egg | Formula check | Butter 70–80 %, egg 35–40 % of flour |
| Sugar reduced, or all white sugar | Formula; cookies stale fast | Restore sugar; part brown sugar |
| Overbaked | Bake loss above ~12 % | Shorter bake |
| Dough rested too long uncovered | Dough surface dry and cracked | Cover dough airtight while resting |

### Greasy cookies

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter too warm or partly melted when creamed | Butter temperature above 21 °C; creamed mass shiny and slack | Butter at 18–20 °C |
| Emulsion broke when the egg went in (cold eggs, egg added all at once) | Curdled, wet-looking mixture before the flour | Eggs at 18–22 °C, added in two or three parts |
| Too much fat for the flour | Formula check; weigh flour | Weigh flour; fat ≤ ~80 % of flour for this style |
| Dough warm at oven entry and oven cool | Probe dough; oven thermometer | Chill dough; correct the oven |

Greasiness is fat that left the dough before the structure set around it.

### Cookies harden overnight

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Stored uncovered or in a leaky container | Weigh a cookie on day 0 and day 1; loss above ~1 % | Airtight container once fully cooled |
| Mostly white sugar: sucrose recrystallizes | Formula; compare with a brown-sugar batch ([X1-19](experiments/X1-19-sugar-type.md)) | More brown sugar; 10–15 % invert/honey on solids |
| Overbaked | Centre fully set at removal | Pull earlier |
| Stored with crisp items or in dry air | Storage conditions | Store soft cookies apart; a slice of bread in the tin keeps them soft (replace daily) |

Soft cookies harden because they lose water and their sugar crystallizes. Keep the water in and the sugar amorphous ([07.3](stage-1/module-07/lesson-03.md)).

### Crisp cookies go soft

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Stored with soft cookies or cake | Storage conditions | Separate containers |
| Humid air; container not airtight | Weight gain in storage | Airtight tin; a food-safe desiccant sachet |
| Packed before fully cool | Condensation in the container | Cool completely (30–60 min) before storing |
| Hygroscopic sugars in the formula (honey, invert, brown) | Formula | Use white sugar for crisp cookies |

Re-crisp at 150 °C for 3–5 minutes and cool on a rack. Water moves from high to low water activity ([SC-01](stage-1/science/sc-01-water.md)).

### Caramel crystallized or grainy

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar not fully dissolved before boiling | Crystals visible on the spoon or pan base at the boil | Stir until clear before it boils; then stop stirring |
| Crystals on the pan walls fell back in | Crust of sugar above the syrup line | Wash down the walls with a wet brush, or cover 1–2 min at the boil |
| Stirred after boiling | Your own notes | Swirl the pan; do not stir |
| No interfering agent | Formula | Add glucose syrup at 10–20 % of sugar, or a few drops of lemon juice ([R1-10](recipes/R1-10-caramel.md)) |
| Dirty pan, spoon or thermometer | Residue, crumbs, fat | Clean tools; keep the thermometer in hot water between uses |

**Rescue:** add water (about 50 % of the sugar weight), stir over heat until every crystal dissolves, wash down the walls, and start the boil again. In a dry caramel, lumps are normal partway through; keep stirring gently on low heat and they melt. See [07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md) and [X1-36](experiments/X1-36-sugar-crystallization.md).

### Caramel burnt or bitter

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Carry-over after removing from heat | Colour darkened after you took the pan off | Remove one shade lighter than the target; deglaze or dip the pan base at once |
| Heat too high; hot spots in a thin pan | Dark patches while other sugar is unmelted | Medium heat; heavy pan; swirl to even out |
| Thermometer read low (thin layer, not calibrated) | Colour much darker than the reading suggests | Judge by colour on white paper; calibrate in boiling water |
| Dark pan hides the colour | Colour looks lighter in the pan than on paper | Light stainless pan; drip-test on paper |

Past ~190 °C (375 °F) the bitterness cannot be removed. Discard and start again; do not try to mask it with more cream or sugar.

### Caramel sauce seized when cream added

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cold cream | Cream below ~50 °C; hard lump of caramel in the pan | Warm cream to 60–80 °C before adding |
| Cream added all at once | Large lump, violent boil | Add in three parts, whisking between |
| Pan off the heat too long before deglazing | Caramel already stiffening at the edges | Deglaze within 10–20 s of taking it off the heat |

**Rescue:** return the pan to low heat and stir; the lump dissolves in the hot cream in a few minutes. If the sauce is grainy after that, strain it. See [R1-10](recipes/R1-10-caramel.md).

### Caramel sauce too thick or too thin

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too thin: not simmered after deglazing | Final temperature below ~103 °C | Simmer to 105–108 °C |
| Too thick: simmered too long | Final temperature above ~110 °C; stiff when cold | Whisk in 10–20 g warm cream per 100 g sauce |
| Cream-to-sugar ratio different from the formula | Weighing record | Weigh the cream; 1 : 1 for a pourable sauce |
| Very dark caramel (less sugar left to thicken) | Colour record | Cook a little further after deglazing |

Thickness follows the final temperature, which measures how concentrated the sugar is ([07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md)).
