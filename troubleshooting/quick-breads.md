# Muffins, quick breads and scones

This category covers chemically leavened products made by the three methods of M08: the muffin method (melted fat or oil, [08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)), the creaming method for quick loaves ([08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)) and the rubbing-in method for scones and biscuits ([08.4](stage-1/module-08/lesson-04.md)). Creamed cakes share many faults with quick loaves and are covered further in [Cakes](troubleshooting/cakes.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose, check the four numbers that cause most faults here:**

1. **Leavener:** weighed to 0.1 g, sifted, fresh (fizz test in [08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)), and soda matched to the acid in the formula.
2. **Fat temperature at use:** probe it. Liquid for the muffin method, 18–20 °C for creaming, 4–7 °C for rubbing-in ([08.1](stage-1/module-08/lesson-01.md)).
3. **Mixing after flour meets liquid:** counted folds or seconds, not "until combined".
4. **Oven:** real temperature from an oven thermometer, and internal temperature of the product at removal.

### Tunnels in muffins

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overmixed after the wet and dry were combined | Batter was smooth and glossy, pulled into strands; fold count > ~20 for a 300 g-flour batch, or a mixer was used | 10–15 folds, stop while lumpy ([X1-22](experiments/X1-22-muffin-overmixing.md)) |
| High-protein flour | Label: bread flour, or ≥12 % protein | Use plain (all-purpose) flour at ~10–11.5 % protein, or replace 20 % with cake flour |
| Too little fat or sugar (both limit gluten) | Formula below ~20 % fat or ~35 % sugar | Raise fat or sugar within range, or mix even less |
| Oven too hot, very fast crust set | Oven thermometer; tunnels plus a dark, peaked top | Verify temperature; lower the second stage to 180–190 °C |

Tunnels are channels left by gas that merged and travelled up through an elastic, developed gluten network while the sides set. Remove the elasticity (less mixing, weaker flour) and the gas stays where it formed. See [08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md).

### Peaked or cracked muffin tops

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overmixed batter | Peak plus tunnels and a chewy bite | Fewer folds ([X1-22](experiments/X1-22-muffin-overmixing.md)) |
| Oven too hot throughout | Oven thermometer; very dark top at the end of the bake | Use the high start for only 5 min, then drop to 190 °C; or bake at a constant 200 °C |
| Too much leavener | Weigh the powder you used; rapid rise then cracked or sunken peak, coarse crumb | Return to 3–5 % powder, weighed to 0.1 g |
| Batter too stiff (too little liquid, too much flour) | Batter holds a stiff peak from the spoon; flour measured by volume | Weigh flour; check liquid percentage |

A rounded dome is the goal; a narrow peak means the centre burst up through a crust that set too early or a batter too tough to expand sideways.

### Flat muffins with no dome

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cups underfilled | Batter weight per cup; cups half full | Fill to the rim (~80–90 g in a standard tin) |
| Stale or under-dosed baking powder | Fizz test in hot water; weigh the dose | Replace powder; 3–5 % of flour |
| Soda-only batter waited before baking | Time from mixing to oven > ~15 min | Bake at once, or switch part of the soda to double-acting powder |
| Oven too cool, or no high start | Oven thermometer; pale tops | 220 °C for 5 min then 190 °C ([R1-12](recipes/R1-12-muffins.md)) |
| Batter too thin (too much liquid or melted fat too hot) | Batter pours rather than mounds | Check liquid percentage; cool melted butter to 35–45 °C; rest batter 30 min chilled |
| Excess sugar or fat | Formula above ~60 % sugar or ~40 % fat | Reduce within range |

### Soapy or metallic taste

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much baking soda for the acid present | Calculate the acid (e.g. buttermilk × 0.8 % lactic acid × 0.93 = neutralizing soda); batter pH ≥ ~7.5 | Reduce soda to the calculated amount, or add acid (buttermilk, yogurt) ([08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)) |
| Acidic ingredient replaced with a neutral one (buttermilk → milk, natural cocoa → Dutch-processed) | Compare to the original formula | Restore the acid, or replace soda with ~4× its weight of baking powder |
| Soda measured by volume, or a teaspoon confused with a tablespoon | Re-weigh your spoon: 1 tsp soda ≈ 5–6 g | Weigh to 0.1 g |
| Very high baking powder dose | Powder above ~6 % of flour | Reduce; some acids (SAPP, SAS) leave an aftertaste at high levels |
| Undispersed soda lumps | Bitter spots rather than an overall taste; see specks below | Sift leaveners with part of the flour |

A metallic or soapy aftertaste is alkaline sodium carbonate from soda that found no acid. It usually comes with a yellowish crumb and extra browning ([X1-21](experiments/X1-21-leaveners.md)).

### Discoloured crumb or coloured specks

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow or yellow-green crumb: excess soda raising pH | Batter pH ≥ ~7.5; soapy taste | Correct the soda–acid balance ([08.2](stage-1/module-08/lesson-02.md)) |
| Green-grey streaks around blueberries or green walnuts or sunflower seeds | Colour only around fruit or nuts; batter alkaline | Less soda or more acid; add 1–2 % lemon juice; toss berries in flour and fold in last |
| Brown, orange or dark specks with a bitter taste | Specks are local; taste one | Undissolved soda or powder lumps: sift leaveners with part of the flour, whisk 30 s |
| White flour pockets in the crumb | Dry flour visible in the cut face | Undermixed: a few more folds until no dry pockets remain (stay lumpy) |
| Very dark brown crumb and crust | pH high (soda) or brown sugar and banana | Expected in banana bread; if too dark, reduce soda |

### Sunken centre in a quick loaf

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much leavener: cells over-expand and collapse | Weigh your dose; crumb coarse with large holes near the top | Reduce powder to 2–3 %, soda to the acid present |
| Underbaked centre | Internal temperature at removal < ~93 °C; wet band below the sink | Bake to 94–98 °C; tent with foil if the top darkens |
| Oven door opened early | Sank during the first 20 min | Keep the door closed until the structure sets (first 60 % of the bake) |
| Too much sugar, fat or fruit for the flour (weak structure) | Formula outside typical ranges; banana > ~160 % | Rebalance: less banana or more flour ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)) |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer | Verify and correct; the structure set too slowly |

A loaf that rises well then sinks has made more gas than its structure could hold, or was taken out before the centre set. Check leavening first, then internal temperature.

### Gummy streak in banana bread

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Internal temperature < ~93 °C; streak in the lower middle | Bake to 94–98 °C, not to a time |
| Cut while hot | Streak appears only on slices cut within ~1 h | Cool ≥ 2 h; better, cut the next day |
| Too much banana or liquid | Banana > ~160 % of flour, or bananas very large; batter very loose | Weigh peeled banana; reduce to 140 % |
| Overmixed after flour | Tunnels above the streak, chewy crumb | Mix dry ingredients ≤ 20 s on low |
| Too much leavener; the centre rose and collapsed into a dense band | Coarse crumb above a dense band, sunken top | Reduce leavener |
| Oven too hot: crust set, centre never caught up | Dark crust, gummy centre, high internal gradient | Lower to 160–165 °C and bake longer |

A gummy streak is crumb whose starch did not fully set or that collapsed after baking. Most cases are underbaking; confirm with a probe before changing the formula. See [R1-13](recipes/R1-13-banana-bread.md).

### Dense heavy banana bread

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter too warm for creaming | Butter > 21 °C; creamed mix shiny and slack; SG > ~1.0 | Butter at 18–20 °C; chill the bowl in a warm kitchen |
| Butter too cold | Lumps of butter; gritty; SG > ~1.05 | Warm to 18–20 °C before creaming, do not just beat longer |
| Undercreamed | Creaming < 2 min; mixture not paler; SG close to 1.2 | Cream 3–5 min on medium; measure SG ([08.3](stage-1/module-08/lesson-03.md)) |
| Curdled egg addition | Grainy mix at the egg stage | See [creamed mixture curdled](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md?id=creamed-mixture-curdled) |
| Stale leavener or missing powder | Fizz test; recipe log | Replace; weigh |
| Too much banana | Heavy, moist, sticky crumb throughout | Weigh peeled banana; 120–150 % |

### Creamed mixture curdled

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Eggs cold | Egg temperature < 15 °C | Eggs at 18–22 °C (5 min in warm water) |
| Eggs added too fast | Egg added in 1–2 additions | 3–4 additions, beat 20–30 s each until smooth |
| Butter too warm (fat too soft to hold the water phase) | Mix shiny and slack before eggs | Start with butter at 18–20 °C |
| High liquid formula (banana, yogurt, milk) | Separation appears when wet ingredients are added | Expected briefly; it comes together with the flour. Add a spoonful of the measured flour with the eggs |

Curdling means the fat phase could not hold the added water as small droplets and the emulsion broke; some air cells are lost. A slightly curdled mix still bakes acceptably but denser. Emulsions are explained in [09.3](stage-1/module-09/lesson-03.md).

### Dry crumbly scones

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too little liquid | Dough was dry and cracked, not shaggy and slightly sticky; flour measured by volume | Weigh flour; 55–60 % liquid; add the reserved milk if dry patches remain |
| Overbaked | Internal temperature ≫ 97 °C; dark bases; bake loss high | Pull at 93–97 °C, golden not brown |
| Too much bench flour worked in | Heavy flour dusting on the bench and in the folds | Use the minimum; brush off excess |
| Too little fat, or fat rubbed in too finely | Formula < 20 % butter; mixture fully sandy | 20–25 % butter; stop rubbing with pea-size pieces left |
| Stored uncovered or eaten the next day | Fine on day 0, dry on day 1 | Scones stale fast: serve within hours; freeze baked scones and warm them, or freeze unbaked and bake to order |

### Scones spread or did not rise

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Butter or dough too warm | Butter > 7 °C at rubbing; dough > 15 °C after mixing; greasy dough | Butter 4–7 °C or frozen and grated; chill dough 20 min before cutting ([X1-23](experiments/X1-23-scone-fat.md)) |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer below 210 °C; pale, greasy scones | 220 °C verified, ≥ 25 min preheat |
| Cutter twisted, or blunt cutter | Low or leaning scones with sealed, smooth sides | Press straight down and lift; use a sharp cutter or a knife |
| Egg wash ran down the sides | Glossy glued sides | Brush tops only |
| Dough too wet | Dough could not hold its cut shape | 55 % liquid; hold back 10 g and add only if needed |
| Stale or low baking powder | Fizz test; dose < 4 % | 5–6 % fresh powder |
| Patted too thin | Raw height < 2 cm | Pat to 2.5–3 cm |

### Tough scones

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overmixed after adding liquid | Mixing > 30 s; smooth, elastic dough | Mix 15–30 s to a shaggy dough |
| Too many folds, or kneaded | > 3 folds; dough springs back | 2–3 folds, total handling ~1 min |
| Rerolled scraps | Tough scones are the reroll batch | Reroll once only, or cut squares to avoid scraps |
| High-protein flour | Bread flour used | Plain flour, ~10–11.5 % protein |
| Too little fat, or fat left almost entirely in large lumps (little flour coated) | Formula < 20 % butter; big lumps, little fine crumb | 20–25 % butter; rub to mostly crumbs with some pea-size pieces |

### Burnt bottoms

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dark or thin tray | Dark metal tray; base much darker than top | Light-coloured heavy tray, or double-tray (a second tray underneath) |
| Rack too low, or bottom element exposed | Oven map ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)); rack position | Middle rack |
| Oven hotter than set | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting |
| Sugar-rich base (muffins, banana bread) in a dark pan | Dark pan; base bitter | Lower temperature 10–15 °C for dark pans; parchment liner; muffin liners |
| Stone or steel left in the oven below the tray | Stone preheated under the rack | Remove the stone or put the tray on a rack above it |

Chemically leavened products are sugar-rich and sit on metal: bottom heat decides their base colour. Rule out the tray and rack before changing the formula.
