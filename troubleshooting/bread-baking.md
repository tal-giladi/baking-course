# Bread: baking, crust and staling

This category covers faults that are decided in the oven and after it: crust colour and thickness, bottom and top heat, underbaking, cooling and storage. Faults that start in the dough (weak gluten, under- or over-fermentation, poor shaping, poor oven spring from proof) are in [Lean bread: dough and fermentation](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md); many symptoms have causes in both places, so check that page too. For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose an oven fault, rule out the oven.** Most baking faults at home trace back to three measurable things: the real oven temperature (not the dial), the temperature of the surface the bread sits on, and whether steam was present and then removed. If you have not mapped your oven in [X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md), do that first. The mechanisms behind every entry are in [04.1](stage-1/module-04/lesson-01.md) (heat and steam), [04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md) (spring, set, crust, colour) and [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md) (cooling and staling).

### Burnt crust or burnt bottom

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven runs hotter than the dial | Oven thermometer in the centre, readings over 15 min | Set the dial by the measured temperature; recalibrate ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |
| Too much bottom heat: steel, stone on lowest rack, pot on oven floor, bottom element exposed | Base much darker than top on the same loaf; IR reading of stone well above air temperature | Move the stone/pot up one rack; put a tray on the rack below as a heat shield; use a stone instead of a steel for bread; sprinkle semolina or put parchment under the loaf |
| Too much top heat: loaf too close to roof, grill element cycling during preheat or recovery | Top dark, sides and base pale; watch whether the top element glows after loading | Lower the rack (≥ 10 cm clearance above the loaf); tent with foil after colour is reached; load after the oven has stabilized |
| Too much sugar or milk in the dough or wash (enriched doughs) | Formula contains sugar, milk, egg wash | Lower oven 15–25 °C for enriched doughs; tent with foil |
| Bake too long for the target | Internal temperature was reached well before removal | Pull at 96–99 °C with the target colour; for the next bake, reduce time or temperature |

Burning is a heat-balance problem more often than a time problem: identify whether the excess comes from below, above or everywhere before changing the dial. See [04.1](stage-1/module-04/lesson-01.md).

### Pale crust

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overproofed dough: yeast consumed the free sugars | Loaf also flat, weak score, large irregular holes under crust; finger-dent test did not spring back | Shorten proof or lower proof temperature ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)) |
| Oven cooler than set, or long recovery after loading | Oven thermometer; air temperature after loading | Preheat 20–30 °C higher and turn down after loading; allow full preheat |
| Steam left in too long | Steam tray still wet at the end; crust glossy but pale and soft | Remove steam after 10–20 min; vent the door briefly |
| Low amylase activity in flour (few sugars made) | Pale crust even with correct proof; flour label (no malted barley flour) | Use flour with added malted barley flour, or add 0.2–0.5 % diastatic malt; consider a cold retard |
| Underbaked | Internal temperature below 96 °C; bake loss below ~10 % | Bake longer; check internal temperature |
| Flour dusting too thick | Pale patches match floured areas | Brush off excess flour before loading |

Colour needs reducing sugars, a dry surface and temperature above about 140 °C. Find which one was missing. See [04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md) and [X1-11](experiments/X1-11-bake-time.md).

### Uneven browning

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven hot spots (back, one side, near the element) | Oven map from [X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md); toast test (slices of white bread across a tray) | Rotate the loaf 180° when you remove the steam; avoid the hottest zone |
| Loaves too close together or to the walls | Pale sides facing each other | Space loaves ≥ 5 cm apart and ≥ 5 cm from the walls |
| Uneven steam or a dried flour coat | Glossy and dull areas on the same crust | Load quickly, spread steam; dust flour evenly and lightly |
| Fan oven with blocked airflow, or fan on one side | Leeward side paler | Rotate; do not crowd trays |
| Steel or stone smaller than the loaf, or edge overhanging | Pale band on the base | Use a surface larger than the loaf |

### Thick, hard crust

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| No steam or steam lost early | Dull, matt crust; score torn rather than opened; compare with [X1-10](experiments/X1-10-steam.md) | Add steam or use a covered pot for the first 15–20 min |
| Oven too cool, bake too long | Long bake to reach colour; high bake loss (> 20 % for a large loaf) | Raise temperature 10–20 °C and shorten time |
| Fan on from the start | Fan oven, early drying | Fan off for the steam phase |
| Dough skinned during proof | Dry surface before baking | Cover during proof; proof in a humid container |
| Low sugar availability (overproofed or very lean flour) | Crust pale as well as thick | See Pale crust |

A thick crust means the drying front moved inward for a long time before the crust was browned. Get colour sooner (hotter, sugars available) or delay drying at the start (steam).

### Soft, leathery crust

This entry covers a crust that is soft or leathery **straight from the oven or within the first hour**. If it was crisp at first and softened later, see [Crust goes soft after cooling](troubleshooting/bread-baking.md?id=crust-goes-soft-after-cooling).

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Steam never removed, or pot lid left on too long | Pale, glossy, soft crust | Vent steam / remove lid after 15–20 min |
| Underbaked | Internal below 96 °C, low bake loss, pale | Bake longer; target colour and 96–99 °C |
| Cooled on a solid surface or in the pan | Base soft and wet | Demould immediately; cool on a rack |
| Oven too cool for the formula | Long bake, little colour | Raise temperature |
| Enriched formula (fat, sugar, milk) | Formula check | Expected: enriched breads have soft crusts by design |

### Gummy crumb

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cut while hot | Gummy mainly near the knife path, better in slices cut later | Cool 1–2 h (lean loaf) to below ~35 °C before cutting ([04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md)) |
| Underbaked | Internal temperature below 96 °C at removal; low bake loss; gummy band near the base or centre | Bake to 96–99 °C and adequate bake loss ([X1-11](experiments/X1-11-bake-time.md)) |
| Oven too hot for the loaf size: crust coloured before centre baked | Dark crust, gummy centre | See Raw centre with dark crust |
| Too much water for the flour, or very high hydration dough baked too briefly | Formula hydration high; crumb wet and shiny | Extend bake at slightly lower temperature; reduce hydration 2–3 points |
| Excess amylase (sprouted wheat, overdose of diastatic malt) | Sticky, wet crumb even when fully baked and cooled; very dark crust | Reduce or remove diastatic malt; change flour |
| Underproofed dough with dense zones | Dense, tight streaks with gummy texture | Correct proof ([bread-lean](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md)) |

### Flying crust

The top crust separates from the crumb, leaving a large void just under the surface.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Surface skinned (dried) during proof, forming a separate layer | Crust feels leathery before baking; loaf uncovered in a dry room | Cover during proof; avoid drafts |
| Underproofed dough plus top heat: the crust set while the interior was still expanding, and gas collected under it | Tight crumb, torn sides, large gap under top crust | Proof longer; add steam; lower the top heat or the rack |
| Excessive flour dusting or a wash that dried before baking | Flour layer visible at the separation line | Brush off flour; apply wash just before baking |
| Degassed or folded-in air pocket during shaping | Large void at the seam or under the top | Shape more tightly; pop large bubbles before shaping |

Flying crust usually starts in the dough stage; see [bread-lean](troubleshooting/bread-lean.md) for shaping and proof faults, and [03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md).

### Loaf cracks in the wrong places

The loaf bursts at the side or base (or splits irregularly) instead of opening along the score.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| No or too little steam: crust set before spring ended | Dull crust; compare with [X1-10](experiments/X1-10-steam.md) | Steam or covered pot |
| Underproofed: too much expansion left for the oven | Dense crumb, big oven spring, burst side | Proof longer; finger-dent test |
| Score too shallow, too short or too vertical | Score barely opened | Score 0.5–1 cm deep at about 30° for an ear; longer cuts |
| Seam not sealed or placed up by mistake | Burst follows the seam | Seal the seam; bake seam down (or seam up deliberately, unscored) |
| Bottom heat too strong at loading | Burst at the base | Raise the stone one rack; check stone temperature |

### Raw centre with dark crust

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot for the loaf size | Crust reaches colour in far less time than the internal temperature target | Lower the oven 15–25 °C and bake longer; for large loaves, bake at a falling temperature |
| Judged by colour, not temperature | Internal below 96 °C (lean) or 88 °C (enriched) at removal | Always probe on a new formula; use colour as a secondary cue |
| Sugar, milk, egg or malt in the formula colour the crust early | Formula check | Lower temperature 15–25 °C; tent with foil once coloured |
| Dough loaded cold and very large | Retarded 1 kg+ loaf | Allow longer bake; tent; or warm 30–60 min before baking |
| Pan loaf: dark metal pan, loaf too big for the pan | Base and sides dark, centre gummy | Use a lighter pan, or reduce dough weight to fit |

### Crust goes soft after cooling

A crisp crust at 30–60 min turns soft or leathery within hours or overnight.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture migration from crumb to crust (normal, but fast) | Happens even in open air, within hours | Bake a little longer for a thicker, drier crust; re-crisp at 200 °C for 5–8 min before serving |
| Bagged in plastic or while still warm | Condensation in the bag | Cool fully; use paper or linen for crusty bread |
| Humid kitchen or storage | Crust softens faster on humid days | Store in paper; re-crisp before serving |
| Underbaked crust (thin, pale) | Pale colour, low bake loss | Bake to full colour; steam out on time |

See [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md) for moisture migration.

### Bread stales fast

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Stored in the refrigerator | Storage record; firm, crumbly crumb with little weight loss | Room temperature for 1–2 days; freeze the rest ([X1-12](experiments/X1-12-staling.md)) |
| Stored unwrapped with cut face exposed | Weight loss high; hard cut face | Cut face down on a board, or paper bag |
| Lean formula (no fat, sugar or pre-gelatinized starch) | Formula check | Expected: lean bread stales in 1–2 days. For longer shelf life use enrichment or tangzhong ([06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md)) |
| Underbaked or overbaked | Bake loss and internal temperature; overbaked crumb is dry from the start | Bake to spec |
| Slow freezing or slow thawing without reheating | Frozen bread still stale after thaw | Freeze fast, wrapped; reheat after thawing |
| Very short fermentation | Straight dough with high yeast, short time | Longer or cold fermentation and preferments (M05) improve keeping quality |

Staling is starch retrogradation, not drying, and it is fastest at refrigerator temperatures. See [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md).

### Pita does not puff

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Surface not hot enough | IR reading of stone below 250 °C; puff time over 3 min | Preheat stone/steel 45–60 min at maximum; use the upper-middle rack |
| Rolled too thin (below ~3 mm) | Measure a rolled round; blisters instead of a pocket | Roll to 4–5 mm, even thickness |
| Rolled too thick (above ~6 mm) | Partial pocket, bready middle | Roll thinner or use smaller pieces |
| Holes, tears or creases in the round | Steam escapes at a visible point; folded edge | Do not fold or re-roll scraps; keep the surface unbroken |
| Surface dried before baking | Rounds left uncovered; skin feels dry | Keep rounds covered; bake within 10–15 min of rolling |
| Dough too dry or underdeveloped | Hydration below ~58 %; dough cracks at the edges when rolled | Increase water to 62–65 %; knead to a smooth dough |
| Skillet not hot enough or not flipped | Only the bottom set | Heat to 230–260 °C; flip at 30–45 s and press gently |

See [R1-03](recipes/R1-03-pita-flatbreads.md) for the mechanism.
