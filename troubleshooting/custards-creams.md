# Custards and creams

This category covers the three systems of M12: egg-thickened custards (stirred crème anglaise and baked crème brûlée and crème caramel), starch-and-egg creams (crème pâtissière and its derivatives), and whipped dairy cream (Chantilly). Faults in egg foams and emulsions (sabayon, mayonnaise, meringue) are in [Eggs, foams, emulsions](troubleshooting/eggs-foams-emulsions.md); faults in buttercreams and crème mousseline are in [Syrups, meringues, buttercreams](troubleshooting/meringues-buttercreams.md); fillings that soak into pastry are in [Assembled pastries](troubleshooting/assembled-pastries.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose, check three measurements.** Almost every fault on this page traces to one of them:

1. **Temperature at the endpoint**, read with a calibrated probe: anglaise 82–84 °C (never above 85 °C); baked custards 78–82 °C in the centre; pastry cream at a full boil for 1–2 min.
2. **Temperature of the cream** before whipping: ≤ 5 °C, bowl chilled.
3. **What happened after cooking**: how fast it cooled, how it was covered, how long and how cold it was stored.

The mechanisms are in [12.1](stage-1/module-12/lesson-01.md) (egg coagulation, water baths), [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md) (starch, yolk amylase) and [12.3](stage-1/module-12/lesson-03.md) (partial coalescence).

### Curdled or grainy anglaise

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked above ~85 °C | Temperature log; eggy or sulfurous smell; residue in the sieve | Pull at 82–84 °C; at the first grain, pour into a cold bowl and blend 20–30 s with an immersion blender |
| Hot bottom layer: heat too high, thin pan, base not scraped | Probe read ≤ 84 °C yet grains; scorched film on the pan base | Medium-low heat, heavy pan, spatula in a figure-eight across the whole base and corners; rise ≤ 2–3 °C per 10 s near the end |
| Carryover in the hot pan | Custard was left in the pan after the pull | Strain immediately into a bowl set in ice |
| Yolks scrambled at tempering | Specks of cooked yolk before cooking started | Pour hot dairy slowly onto the yolks, whisking; or temper with dairy at 80 °C instead of boiling |
| Low sugar or high yolk formula | Formula below ~15 % sugar or above ~25 % yolks on dairy | Expect curdling earlier; pull at the nappe line (often 80–82 °C) rather than the usual temperature |
| Acid added hot (fruit, lemon) | Formula includes acidic ingredients | Add acid after cooling |

Grain means the yolk proteins over-coagulated and squeezed out water. Slight grain is recoverable by blending; visible curds in watery liquid are not. See [12.1](stage-1/module-12/lesson-01.md) and [X1-33](experiments/X1-33-custard-endpoint.md).

### Thin anglaise

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Pulled below ~80 °C | Temperature log; nappe line closed at the pull; foam still on the surface | Return to the pan and cook to 82–84 °C (it is safe to reheat if it was chilled promptly) |
| Thermometer reads high | Check in ice water (0 °C) and boiling water | Recalibrate ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |
| Too few yolks or yolks weighed by count | Yolk weight below ~18 % of dairy; small eggs | Weigh yolks: 20 % of dairy (100 g per 500 g) |
| All-milk formula judged against a cream anglaise | Formula has no cream | Accept a lighter sauce, or use part cream |
| Judged while hot | Tested at 60 °C | Judge after chilling: anglaise thickens noticeably as it cools |

A 4 °C shortfall makes a large difference in thickness; see the line-spread results of [X1-33](experiments/X1-33-custard-endpoint.md).

### Baked custard weeping, grainy or full of holes

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overbaked: centre above ~85 °C | Probe reading at pull; custard firm, not wobbling, when removed | Pull at 78–82 °C with a sheet-like wobble; it firms as it chills |
| No water bath, or bath too shallow | Edges more grainy and holey than the centre; edges puffed or browned | Water bath at half to two-thirds of the mould height, at 60–70 °C, in a 150 °C oven; or a 100–110 °C fan oven |
| Oven hotter than the dial | Oven thermometer | Set by the measured temperature ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |
| Left in the hot water bath after baking | Timeline | Lift the moulds out at the endpoint |
| Moulds on the bare tin in the bath | Base more curdled than top | Cloth or paper under the moulds |

Over-coagulated protein contracts and expels water (weeping, syneresis), and water turned to steam leaves holes. The water bath limits the edge temperature to about 80–90 °C. See [12.1](stage-1/module-12/lesson-01.md) and [R1-25](recipes/R1-25-creme-brulee.md).

### Baked custard not setting

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Centre below ~76 °C; liquid ripples in the centre when tapped | Bake until 78–82 °C in the centre; check every 5 min near the end |
| Oven cooler than set, or low-oven method in an oven that cannot hold 100–110 °C | Oven thermometer | Use the 150 °C water-bath method; or extend time |
| Moulds deeper than the recipe | Fill depth > 3 cm for a brûlée | Allow more time; use shallower dishes for brûlée |
| Cold water in the bath | Water temperature at the start | Fill the bath with 60–70 °C water |
| Not chilled long enough | Tested after less than 4 h (brûlée) or 6 h (caramel) | Chill fully; the gel strengthens cold |
| Formula too dilute or cream–yolk custard expected to unmould | Protein level; egg type | Crème caramel needs whole eggs and milk ([R1-25](recipes/R1-25-creme-brulee.md)) |
| Acidic or enzyme-rich add-in (fresh pineapple, kiwi, ginger) | Formula | Cook the fruit first or leave it out; see [SC-09](stage-1/science/sc-09-enzymes.md) |

### Bubbles, foam or tunnels in baked custard

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Mix beaten instead of stirred | Foamy layer on the jug before baking | Stir yolks and sugar with the whisk held down; do not beat |
| Foam not removed | Spongy layer on top after baking | Strain, skim, rest the mix 10–30 min, torch the surface bubbles before baking |
| Overbaking (steam holes) | Holes throughout, especially near edges and base; custard also grainy | See [Baked custard weeping, grainy or full of holes](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md?id=baked-custard-weeping-grainy-or-full-of-holes) |
| Water bath boiling hard | Bubbling water visible during the bake | Lower the oven to 140–150 °C; cloth under moulds |

Small uniform bubbles at the surface are air; irregular holes and tunnels in the body are steam from overheating.

### Lumpy pastry cream

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Starch not dispersed in sugar and yolks | Lumps with dry white centres | Whisk starch into the sugar–yolk mixture until smooth; sift the starch |
| Hot milk added too fast | Lumps formed during tempering | Add one third of the milk in a thin stream while whisking |
| Not whisked through the thickening stage | Lumps appeared when it suddenly thickened | Whisk vigorously and continuously from 75 °C to the end of the boil |
| Scorched or set layer on the pan base | Brown flecks; skin stuck to the base | Heavy pan, medium heat, whisk into the corners |
| Skin whisked in | Lumps only after chilling | Film in contact with the surface ([Skin on custard or pastry cream](troubleshooting/custards-creams.md?id=skin-on-custard-or-pastry-cream)) |

Rescue: while hot, pass the cream through a fine sieve or blend briefly with an immersion blender (a few seconds only; long blending thins it). See [12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md).

### Pastry cream runny the next day

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not boiled, or boiled too briefly: yolk α-amylase survived | Thick when made, thin after hours; process notes show a pull at first thickening | Full boil, held 1–2 min while whisking; cannot be rescued once thinned |
| Incomplete gelatinization (not boiled) | Already softer than usual at 2 h | Same correction |
| Acid added during cooking | Lemon or fruit purée added at the boil | Add acid after cooking and cooling slightly; increase starch |
| Over-beaten when re-textured | Smooth but runny immediately after beating in a mixer | Whisk 20–40 s only |
| Too little starch or wrong starch | Formula < 7 % cornstarch; potato or tapioca used | 8–10 % cornstarch on milk |
| Contamination with saliva or a tasting spoon dipped back | Thinning spots | Clean spoon for every taste |

This is the amylase trap. Test it in [X1-34](experiments/X1-34-starch-thickeners.md); mechanism in [SC-09](stage-1/science/sc-09-enzymes.md).

### Rubbery or pasty pastry cream

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much starch | Formula > 10 % cornstarch on milk | Reduce to 8–9 % |
| Too much flour, or flour-only formula | Dull, gluey texture | Use cornstarch or a cornstarch–flour blend |
| Reduced by over-long boiling | Weight loss > 8–10 % | Boil 1–2 min only; weigh before and after |
| Used straight from the refrigerator without re-texturing | Firm set slab | Whisk 20–40 s until smooth before piping |
| Tested too cold | At 2 °C | Let it stand to 8–12 °C before judging |

### Starchy or floury taste

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not boiled long enough | Process notes | Boil 1 min minimum with cornstarch, 2 min minimum with flour |
| Flour-based cream | Formula | Use cornstarch or a blend; boil longer |
| Too much starch | Formula | Reduce starch; add more yolk or butter for richness |
| Low flavouring | Tastes flat as well as starchy | Vanilla, salt (0.1 %), butter 5–10 % |

### Skin on custard or pastry cream

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Surface exposed to air while cooling | Skin only on top | Press food film directly onto the surface |
| Cooled slowly, uncovered, in a deep container | Thick skin plus condensation | Shallow tray (2 cm), film in contact, refrigerate immediately |
| Stored uncovered in the refrigerator | Dry, leathery surface | Cover in contact |

The surface loses water and the proteins and starch there gel into a film. Whisked in, it makes lumps.

### Whipped cream will not whip

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Cream too warm | Probe: above ~7 °C at the start | Chill cream to ≤ 5 °C; chill bowl and whisk 10–15 min; whip over iced water |
| Fat content too low | Label below 30 % | Use 35–40 % cream |
| UHT cream | Label | Use pasteurized cream; or add a stabilizer; expect a softer foam |
| Cream warmed and re-chilled | History (left out, then refrigerated) | Keep cream cold throughout |
| Warm kitchen, large batch in a mixer | Cream temperature rises during whipping | Whip in smaller batches, over iced water |
| Sugar or liquid (liqueur, purée) added too early or in excess | Formula and timing | Add sugar at soft trails; limit added liquid to ≤ 5 % |

Partial coalescence needs partly crystalline fat globules; see [12.3](stage-1/module-12/lesson-03.md) and [SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md).

### Overwhipped or grainy whipped cream

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Whipped past medium–firm peak | Matte, rough surface; grains | Finish at low speed or by hand; check every 10–15 s; fold in 5–10 % liquid cream to recover slight overwhipping |
| Whipped to firm peak and then folded or piped | Grainy after folding or in the bag | Whip to soft peak for folding; medium–firm for piping; minimal handling |
| Very high-fat cream (double cream 48 %) | Label | Blend with milk or 35 % cream to ~38–40 % fat |
| Cream too warm (greasy grain) | Temperature | Keep at ≤ 5–7 °C |
| Squeezed with warm hands in the piping bag | Grain develops while piping | Fill the bag half full; hold it by the top |

Beyond grains, the fat churns into butter; the emulsion has inverted and cannot be reversed ([SC-06](stage-1/science/sc-06-colloids.md)).

### Whipped cream weeping

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underwhipped | Soft, glossy, flows; liquid within 1–2 h | Whip to medium–firm peak for piping and holding |
| Held too long unstabilized | Fine at 1 h, liquid at 12–24 h | Use within 2–4 h, or stabilize: gelatin 0.5–1 %, mascarpone 10–25 %, milk powder 3–5 % |
| Stored too warm | Refrigerator temperature > 5 °C | ≤ 5 °C |
| UHT or low-fat cream | Label | 35–40 % pasteurized |
| Contact with a wet or acidic component (fruit, syrup-soaked sponge) | Liquid only at the contact surface | Seal the component (e.g. drain fruit, brush a thin chocolate or jam layer); assemble closer to service; see [17.1](stage-1/module-17/lesson-01.md) |

Liquid drains from between the bubbles over time; stabilizers thicken or gel that liquid, or add fat to the network. See [R1-27](recipes/R1-27-creme-chantilly.md).
