# Cakes

This category covers creamed (butter and pound) cakes and foam cakes (génoise, biscuit, chiffon): batter mixing, formula balance, pans, baking, cooling and storage. Egg foams that fail before they become a cake batter are in [Eggs, foams, emulsions](troubleshooting/eggs-foams-emulsions.md); quick breads and muffins are in [Muffins, quick breads, scones](troubleshooting/quick-breads.md). For the diagnostic method, see the [troubleshooting index](troubleshooting/README.md).

**Before you diagnose a cake, collect four numbers.** Most cake faults can be traced with: the **batter specific gravity** (was the air there?), the **batter temperature** (was the emulsion or foam in its window?), the **real oven temperature** (oven thermometer, not the dial), and the **internal temperature at removal** (was it set?). The model behind every entry is in [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md): a cake fails when it sets too early (domes, peaks, tunnels, toughness) or too late (sinks, coarse, gummy), or when the air never got into the batter in the first place (dense). Mixing is in [10.2](stage-1/module-10/lesson-02.md) and [10.3](stage-1/module-10/lesson-03.md); pans, doneness, cooling and storage in [10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md).

### Dense, heavy cake

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Under-creamed (creamed cakes) | Batter SG above ~0.9–0.95 for a pound or butter cake; creaming time under 3 min | Cream 4–6 min at medium until pale and fluffy; measure SG ([X1-26](experiments/X1-26-creaming-time.md)) |
| Butter too cold or too warm | Probe the butter: below ~15 °C or above ~23 °C at the start; shiny, oily butter | Start at 18–20 °C; chill bowl in a hot kitchen |
| Broken emulsion (curdled batter lost its air) | Batter looked grainy and wet at the egg stage | See [Curdled creamed batter](troubleshooting/cakes.md?id=curdled-creamed-batter) |
| Foam under-whipped or over-folded (foam cakes) | Foam SG above ~0.35 or batter SG above ~0.55; no ribbon | Whip to ribbon (3–5 s); fold in 3 additions, 10–15 strokes ([10.3](stage-1/module-10/lesson-03.md)) |
| Powdered sugar or very coarse sugar used for creaming | Recipe check | Use caster or fine granulated sugar |
| Leavener missing, expired or mis-weighed | Taste (soapy / flat), weigh on 0.1 g scale; put 2 g powder in hot water: vigorous fizz = active | Weigh on a 0.1 g scale; replace powder older than its date |
| Too much liquid or too little leavener for the formula | Balance check ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)) | Rebalance liquids ≈ sugar |
| Batter stood too long before baking | Time from mixing to oven over ~20–30 min (leavened batters, foams) | Pan and bake immediately; for foams within 3 min |

Dense means the air never got in, or got out before the cake set. SG tells you which: a high SG before baking points to mixing; a normal SG with a dense cake points to the formula or the oven.

### Dry cake

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overbaked | Internal temperature well above 99 °C at removal, or removed long after springback; high bake loss; sides pulled far from the pan | Pull at 93–99 °C with springback and a clean skewer ([10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md)) |
| Driers exceed moisteners | Balance check: low liquids vs sugar; extra flour, cocoa or milk powder | Rebalance; add liquid with cocoa |
| Flour measured by volume or over-weighed | Weigh the flour again | Weigh everything ([01.1](stage-1/module-01/lesson-01.md)) |
| Too little sugar or fat | Formula compared with a balanced reference | Restore tenderizers; sugar also holds water |
| Génoise or biscuit not soaked | These are meant to be moistened | Brush with syrup at 25–40 % of layer weight |
| Stored unwrapped, cut, or refrigerated | Dry cut edges; firm crumb after refrigeration | Wrap tightly once cool; room temperature for plain cakes; freeze for longer |
| Oven hotter than set | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting |

A cake can be dry because it lost water (overbaking, storage) or because it never had enough relative to its driers (formula). Weigh the cake before and after baking: bake loss above about 12–15 % for a butter cake points to the oven.

### Cracked or domed top

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot | Oven thermometer; crust also dark | Lower to the formula's temperature (160–170 °C pound cake, 170–180 °C butter and sponge) |
| Dark or glass pan | Compare with a light aluminium pan | Lower 10–15 °C; use baking strips |
| Too much flour or too little sugar/fat (sets too early) | Balance check; X1-29 baseline vs your formula | Rebalance ([10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md)) |
| Overmixing after flour (gluten) | Mixing time after flour; tunnels and chewy crumb present | Mix only until just combined; consider reverse creaming |
| Strong flour | Flour protein above ~11 % | Use cake or soft flour, or AP + 15–20 % cornstarch ([X1-28](experiments/X1-28-cake-flour.md)) |
| Pan too full or too deep; oven rack too high | Batter above ⅔ of pan; top close to the oven roof | Fill ½–⅔; centre rack |
| Loaf-shaped pound cake | Expected: the crust sets before the centre | Control the crack with a piped butter line or a score ([R1-17](recipes/R1-17-pound-cake.md)) |

The edges and top set first, and the still-fluid centre pushes up and through. Anything that makes the outside set much earlier than the centre gives a dome. A single controlled split is normal in a loaf cake; a peak with cracks in a layer cake is a fault.

### Sunken centre

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Internal below ~93 °C at removal; wet skewer; sank after removal; gummy band under the dip | Bake to 93–99 °C with springback and clean skewer |
| Too much sugar (sets too late) | Balance check: sugar > flour with ordinary flour; dark sugary crust | Sugar ≤ flour, or use chlorinated/heat-treated flour and emulsified fat ([X1-29](experiments/X1-29-cake-formula-balance.md)) |
| Too much leavener | Weigh leavener on 0.1 g scale; coarse crumb; soapy or bitter taste | Correct dose (typically 2–4 % of flour in butter cakes) |
| Too much liquid or fat | Balance check | Rebalance |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer; long bake, pale top | Correct the setting |
| Door opened or pan moved before the structure set | Log: door opened in the first half of the bake | Keep the door shut until at least two-thirds of the bake |
| Weak flour for a high-sugar formula | Formula sugar > flour; flour unchlorinated | Lower sugar, or change flour |

If it sank in the oven, suspect the formula or leavener. If it sank after removal, suspect underbaking first.

### Tunnels

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Overmixing after the flour was added (gluten) | Long vertical holes from base towards the top, peaked top, chewy crumb; mixing time log | Mix just until combined; alternate dry/liquid; reverse creaming for fine crumb |
| Oven too hot | Oven thermometer; dark crust, peak | Lower temperature |
| Strong flour | Flour protein | Cake or soft flour |
| Too much egg white relative to tenderizers | Balance check | Rebalance; replace some whole egg with yolk |
| Leavener not evenly dispersed | Holes clustered, yellow-brown spots | Sift leavener with flour twice |

Tunnels form when a strong batter sets early at the edges and hot gas forces its way up through the still-fluid centre, leaving channels. The same conditions give the peaked, cracked dome.

### Coarse, open crumb

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too much leavener | Weigh; SG normal but cells large; sunken or flat top | Reduce leavener |
| Too much sugar | Balance check; very tender, dark crust | Reduce sugar or rebalance |
| Under-creamed: few cells, all over-inflated by leavener | Batter SG high; C1/C3 of [X1-26](experiments/X1-26-creaming-time.md) | Cream 4–6 min at 18–20 °C |
| Butter too warm during creaming | Creamed mixture above ~23–24 °C | Start cooler; shorter creaming in warm kitchens |
| Meringue over-whipped (chiffon, biscuit) | White lumps in batter; holes in the cake | Stop at firm, glossy peaks; lighten with ⅓ first |
| Under-folded (foam cakes) | Streaks of foam or meringue; large air pockets | Fold until uniform; run a spatula through the pan to pop pockets |
| Oven too cool | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting |

Coarse crumb means the cells over-expanded and merged before the walls set, or were few and large from the start. A normal batter SG points to the formula or oven; a high SG points to mixing. See [10.1](stage-1/module-10/lesson-01.md).

### Gummy layer or streak

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Underbaked | Grey, dense, wet band just under the top or under the split; internal below ~93 °C at removal | Bake to 93–99 °C; test with skewer at the centre of the split |
| Batter separated in the pan: butter or flour sank | Dense band at the **base**; greasy (génoise butter) | Temper butter with batter; fold flour fully; check bowl bottom |
| Too much liquid | Balance check; long bake; wet crumb throughout | Rebalance liquids ≈ sugar ([X1-29](experiments/X1-29-cake-formula-balance.md)) |
| Cake sank (compression of the soft centre) | Band located under a dip | See [Sunken centre](troubleshooting/cakes.md?id=sunken-centre) |
| Cut or wrapped while hot | Cut within 30 min; condensation in the wrap | Cool fully (centre below ~30 °C) before cutting or wrapping |
| Under-creamed or curdled batter in a pound cake | Streak parallel to the base, dense | Correct creaming and egg addition ([10.2](stage-1/module-10/lesson-02.md)) |

Where the band is tells you the cause: at the top or under the split = underbaked; at the base = separation (fat, flour, undissolved sugar, or deflated foam).

### Curdled creamed batter

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Eggs cold | Egg temperature below ~15 °C | Warm the bowl briefly while mixing until smooth; next time eggs at 18–22 °C |
| Eggs added too fast | Large additions | Stop adding; beat 1–2 min; then small additions, smooth each time |
| Butter too warm | Creamed mixture above ~24 °C; greasy look | Chill the bowl 5–10 min, beat; start at 18–20 °C next time |
| Formula very high in egg relative to fat | Eggs ≫ fat; high-liquid formula | Add 1–2 tablespoons of the weighed flour with the last additions; consider reverse creaming |
| Liquid (milk) added too early or all at once | Method check | Alternate D-L-D-L-D, starting and ending with flour |

A curdled batter that becomes smooth when the flour goes in usually bakes acceptably; a badly broken one loses air and bakes dense and greasy. Fix the temperature first; flour is a patch. See [10.2](stage-1/module-10/lesson-02.md).

### Sponge cake collapsed or deflated

Génoise, biscuit and other egg-foam cakes.

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Foam under-whipped | No ribbon, or ribbon disappears at once; foam SG above ~0.35 | Warm to 40–45 °C; whip to 3–4× volume and a 3–5 s ribbon ([R1-18](recipes/R1-18-genoise.md)) |
| Over-folding | Batter SG above ~0.55; many strokes logged | Three additions, 10–15 strokes each; stop at no visible flour |
| Butter too hot or not tempered | Rubbery, greasy layer at the base; butter above ~50 °C | Butter at 40–50 °C, tempered with ~50 g of batter |
| Batter waited before baking | More than 3 min from last fold to oven | Prepare pan and oven first; bake at once |
| Underbaked | Did not spring back; sank after removal | Bake until springback and edges pull away; ~95–99 °C centre |
| Oven too cool or door opened early | Oven thermometer; log | Correct; do not open before ~18 min |
| Fat in the bowl or yolk in the whites (biscuit, chiffon) | Meringue would not reach firm peaks | Clean, grease-free bowl; separate carefully ([Eggs, foams, emulsions](troubleshooting/eggs-foams-emulsions.md)) |

Measure SG of the foam and of the finished batter: a normal foam SG with a high batter SG means the air was lost in folding or to the butter; a high foam SG means the foam never formed. See [10.3](stage-1/module-10/lesson-03.md).

### Chiffon fell out of the pan or shrank

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Pan greased, lined or nonstick | Pan type; cake slid out when inverted | Plain, ungreased aluminium tube pan ([R1-19](recipes/R1-19-chiffon-cake.md)) |
| Not inverted, or inverted late | Log; height loss of 15 % or more | Invert immediately; cool 1.5–2 h inverted |
| Underbaked | Wet, shiny cracks at removal; sticky crumb near the tube | Bake until cracks look dry, springback, ~95–99 °C |
| Meringue under-whipped | Soft peaks only; batter SG above ~0.55 | Firm, glossy peaks |
| Too much oil or liquid | Formula check | Keep oil around 40–55 % of flour |
| Released before fully cool | Released within 1 h | Wait until the pan is cold |

Some shrinkage (up to about 5–8 %) is normal. A chiffon that falls out was not able to grip the pan, or its crumb had not set.

### Cake stuck in the pan

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Pan not greased and floured, or missed spots | Stuck patches match bare metal | Grease evenly including corners; flour and tap out; or line with parchment |
| Left too long in the pan | Damp sides, sugar glued to the metal | Butter cakes 10–15 min, génoise 5 min, then unmould |
| Turned out too early | Cake broke rather than stuck | Wait the stated time for the crumb to firm |
| High-sugar batter caramelized at the wall | Dark, sticky sides | Line with parchment; lower oven temperature |
| Damaged nonstick or scratched pan | Pan surface | Line with parchment |
| Chiffon: expected to stick | — | Release with a thin knife pressed against the wall |

Release is decided by pan preparation and by timing: steam condensing on the pan wall after about 15 min turns the crust damp and sugary, and it bonds to the metal. See [10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md).

### Uneven rise or lopsided cake

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven hot spot | Oven map ([X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)); higher side faces the same direction every time | Rotate pan 180° after two-thirds of the bake; avoid the hot zone |
| Oven rack or oven not level | Spirit level on the rack | Level the oven or rack |
| Batter not levelled in the pan | Visual | Spread level; for pound cake make the centre slightly lower |
| Pan too close to a wall or another pan | Low side faces the neighbour; pale side | ≥ 2.5–5 cm between pans and walls |
| Fan blowing on a light batter | Fan oven; top tilted away from the fan | Fan off, or lower speed/temperature |
| Warped pan | Pan rocks on a flat surface | Replace |

If the high side always faces the same part of the oven, it is the oven; if it changes from bake to bake, it is levelling or pan placement. See [10.4](stage-1/module-10/lesson-04.md).

### Crust too dark

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Oven too hot | Oven thermometer | Correct the setting |
| Dark or glass pan | Pan type; sides and base darker than top | Lower 10–15 °C; light aluminium pan |
| High sugar, honey, milk or other reducing sugars | Formula check | Lower oven 10 °C; tent with foil after colour is reached |
| Rack too high or too low | Top dark (high) or base dark (low) | Centre rack |
| Overbaked | Internal well above 99 °C; high bake loss | Pull at target temperature |
| Long bake for a deep cake | Deep pan; pound cake | Lower oven, foil tent at 35–40 min; baking strips |

Cake crusts brown faster than bread crusts at the same temperature because they carry more sugar, milk and egg for Maillard browning and caramelization ([07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md)). Colour is not a doneness test: use internal temperature.

### Soggy or sticky top

| Likely cause | How to test | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Wrapped or covered while warm | Condensation in the wrap | Cool fully uncovered on a rack first |
| Underbaked | Tacky, wet top; wet skewer; low internal | Bake to target temperature and springback |
| High sugar or liquid sugars (hygroscopic) | Formula; humid kitchen | Expected in part; store in a box rather than airtight film; reduce liquid sugars |
| Too much syrup, or syrup on a warm cake | Syrup dose above ~40 % of layer weight; pooling | Dose by weight; brush in two passes on a cool cake |
| Humid storage or refrigeration with cream | Top tacky after hours in a humid place | Store in a closed container; add finish just before serving |
| Chiffon cooled upright or not fully baked | Top compressed, damp | Invert immediately; bake to dry cracks |

Sugar in the crust draws moisture from the air and from the crumb ([07.1](stage-1/module-07/lesson-01.md)); sweet cakes that are covered warm or kept humid develop a sticky top.
