## A. Knowledge check

1. Name the four dimensions of the course and give one activity in a module that trains each.

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Practical skill (baking the recipe, practical assessment); baking science (lessons and science units); experimental thinking (X1- experiments, hypothesis first); professional workflow (mise en place, formula sheets, DDT, sanitation, bake log).

</details>

2. What are the concept threads T1–T12 for, and why are Stage 1 IDs "append-only"?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Threads are the lines of understanding that run through the whole course; every lesson is tagged with them, and Stages 2 and 3 extend the same threads. IDs are append-only so that later stages add new IDs (`R2-`, `X2-`, `stage-2/`) without renaming anything in Stage 1, keeping links, notes and progress valid.

</details>

3. List at least eight fields of the bake log.

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Date/bake ID; formula; actual weights; ingredient lot/brand; room temperature; water temperature; DDT target vs actual; times; observations; photos; sensory evaluation; what to change.

</details>

4. Why is the oven "preheated" light not a reliable signal that you can load bread onto a stone?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

It reports the air temperature at the thermostat sensor. The walls and especially a stone (45–60 min) or steel (30–45 min) take much longer to reach temperature. Loading early gives weak bottom heat and poor oven spring.

</details>

5. What temperature adjustment do you make to bake a conventional-oven recipe in fan mode, and why does the fan make a difference at the same air temperature?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Set about 15–20 °C lower (or keep the temperature and shorten the bake). Moving air strips away the cooler boundary layer around the product, so heat transfer is faster at the same air temperature.

</details>

6. State the temperature danger zone and the two-stage cooling rule.

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

About 5–60 °C. Cool cooked perishables from 60 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours and to 5 °C within a further 4 hours.

</details>

7. Why should you not taste raw cookie dough even if it contains no egg?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Raw flour is not treated with a kill step and can carry pathogenic *E. coli* and *Salmonella*. Only baking (or commercially heat-treated flour) makes it safe.

</details>

8. What is the difference between cleaning and sanitising, and which removes allergens?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Cleaning (detergent, water, friction) removes soil and protein, including allergen protein. Sanitising reduces microorganisms on an already clean surface. Sanitiser alone does not remove allergens.

</details>

9. Why does whole-wheat flour need colder storage than white flour?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

It contains germ and bran, with unsaturated fats and active enzymes that oxidise and break down (rancidity) within a few months at room temperature. Cold slows these reactions.

</details>

10. What does FIFO mean, and what must a storage label contain?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

First in, first out: use the oldest stock first by placing new stock behind old. Label: contents (with brand/type), date opened or made, use-by date, allergens for prepared items, and lot/batch where relevant.

</details>

## B. Formulation exercises

**B1. Correcting for your oven.** Your oven map (conventional mode) gives:

| Dial (°C) | Measured average (°C) |
|---|---|
| 180 | 171 |
| 200 | 188 |
| 230 | 216 |

(a) What dial setting gives a true 190 °C? (b) The recipe says 190 °C conventional, but you want to use fan mode. What true temperature do you aim for?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) Between the 180 and 200 settings the measured temperature rises 17 °C for 20 °C on the dial (0.85 °C per dial degree). True 190 °C needs 19 °C above 171 °C: 19 ÷ 0.85 = 22.4 dial degrees above 180, so set about **202 °C**. (Assuming a constant −9 °C or −12 °C offset gives 199 °C or 202 °C; interpolating between measured points is the better method.)

(b) About 170–175 °C true, i.e. 15–20 °C below 190 °C. You would need to map the fan mode separately to know the dial setting for that.

</details>

**B2. Cooling log.** You cooked 2 kg of crème pâtissière, spread it 3 cm deep in two trays with film on the surface, and logged the centre temperature:

| Time | 14:00 | 14:30 | 15:45 | 16:15 | 19:30 | 20:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| °C | 85 | 60 | 24 | 19 | 6 | 4.5 |

Does this meet the two-stage cooling rule?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Stage 1 starts at 60 °C (14:30) and must reach 21 °C by 16:30. Interpolating between 15:45 (24 °C) and 16:15 (19 °C), it passed 21 °C at about 16:03 — **pass** (about 1 h 33 min). Stage 2 must reach 5 °C within 4 h of that, by about 20:03; it was 4.5 °C at 20:00 — **pass**, but only just. Improve with an ice bath under the trays or shallower trays.

</details>

**B3. Back-scheduling.** You want to load a lean loaf onto a preheated stone at 14:00. Times: mise en place 20 min; mixing 20 min; bulk fermentation 2 h; dividing and shaping 15 min; final proof 60 min; stone preheat 60 min. When does each step start?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Work backward from 14:00. Final proof 13:00–14:00; shaping 12:45–13:00; bulk 10:45–12:45; mixing 10:25–10:45; mise en place 10:05–10:25. Oven and stone on at **13:00**. Start at **10:05**. (A wise planner adds 10–15 min of margin.)

</details>

## C. Troubleshooting case

**Case 1.** A learner reports: "My bread comes out pale on the bottom and dark on top, and the bake time in the recipe never works — sometimes it's done early, sometimes it needs 10 more minutes. I preheat until the light goes off, then put the stone in with the loaf on it." What is going on, and what should they do?

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Two problems. (1) The stone goes in cold with the loaf, so the bottom gets little conducted heat while the top receives radiant heat: pale bottom, dark top. Fix: stone in from the start, preheated 45–60 min, on a lower shelf. (2) The oven is loaded when only the air is hot (light off after 10–15 min); the walls are not soaked, so recovery after loading varies with how long they happened to wait, and the true temperature is unknown. Fix: map the oven with a thermometer (X1-02), preheat 30+ min (60 with stone), correct the dial for the measured offset, and bake to internal temperature (96–99 °C) and colour rather than the clock.

</details>

**Case 2.** A cream-filled cake served at a party left two guests unwell. The cream was made in the morning, the cake was assembled at noon and left on the table from 15:00 to 21:00 in a 26 °C room. Identify the failures.

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

Six hours at room temperature is well beyond the 2-hour limit for a perishable, egg- and dairy-based filling in the danger zone. Also check: how the cream was cooled after cooking (likely slowly in the pot), whether piping tools were clean, and whether leftover cream was returned to stock. Correct: cook to endpoint, cool fast in a shallow tray, keep ≤ 5 °C, assemble cold, serve from the refrigerator and return it within 2 hours.

</details>

## D. Experimental design

**Hypothesis:** "Preheating my baking stone for 60 minutes instead of 20 minutes gives a loaf with more oven spring and a darker bottom crust." Design an experiment to test it.

<details class="answer"><summary>Answer</summary>

A good design:

- **Independent variable:** stone preheat time, 20 vs 60 min at the same dial setting (e.g. 240 °C).
- **Controlled:** same dough batch divided into two equal pieces (e.g. 2 × 450 g from one mix), same shaping, same proof time and temperature, same scoring, same oven and shelf, same steam method, same bake time. Because you cannot bake both at once with different preheats, bake them on the same day in sequence: keep the second piece in the refrigerator during the first bake so it is not over-proofed, or better, mix two identical doughs offset by the time difference, each hitting the same DDT.
- **Measurements:** stone surface temperature at loading (IR thermometer); loaf height and width, or volume by seed displacement; bottom crust colour against a photo scale; internal temperature at the end; bake loss.
- **Replication:** repeat on a second day with the order swapped (to cancel order and proof effects).
- **Expected result** (to be verified): the 60-minute stone is substantially hotter at the surface, giving faster bottom heat, more oven spring and a darker, crisper bottom.

</details>
