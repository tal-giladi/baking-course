# M04 · Heat and the oven

## Purpose

In M03 you learned to make a lean dough and bring it to the oven. This module is about what the oven does to it. You will build a working model of how heat moves from the oven into dough (conduction, convection, radiation, and the latent heat of condensing steam), what happens inside the loaf at each temperature (oven spring, starch gelatinization, protein set, crust formation and browning), and what happens after the bake (cooling, staling, moisture migration and storage). With it you can choose a baking surface, a steaming method and a bake profile on purpose, judge doneness by measurement, and store bread according to mechanism rather than habit. Everything here returns in every baked product in the course.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Explain how heat reaches the base, sides, top and centre of a loaf in a home oven, and choose between a stone, a steel and a covered pot.
- Set up home steam safely and explain, with numbers, why it increases oven spring and gloss.
- Describe the sequence of oven spring, crumb set, crust formation and browning with the temperatures at which each occurs.
- Judge doneness from internal temperature, bake loss, sound and colour, knowing what each can and cannot tell you.
- Distinguish staling, crust softening, drying and mould, and store and freeze bread accordingly.
- Bake pita that puffs reliably, and explain why it does or does not.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Conduction | Heat transfer by direct contact; how a stone, steel or pot heats the base of a loaf, and how heat moves from crust to centre. |
| Convection | Heat transfer by a moving fluid (air or steam); increased by an oven fan. |
| Radiation | Heat transfer by infrared emission from hot surfaces such as the oven roof, walls and elements; a major source of top heat at home. |
| Thermal mass | The amount of heat a baking surface stores (mass × specific heat); why a stone needs 45–60 min to preheat and keeps bottom heat steady at loading. |
| Thermal effusivity | How quickly a surface can push heat into something touching it (√(conductivity × density × specific heat)); much higher for steel than for ceramic stone. |
| Thermostat cycling | The on/off swing of a domestic oven around its set point, commonly ±5–15 °C. |
| Latent heat of vaporization | Energy released when steam condenses (about 2,260 kJ/kg for water); the reason steam heats the dough surface so effectively. |
| Oven spring | The rapid expansion of a loaf in the first 8–12 min of baking, from gas expansion, CO₂ release, vaporization and final yeast activity. |
| Starch gelatinization | Swelling and loss of crystalline order of starch granules heated with water (about 60–75 °C in bread dough); sets the crumb. |
| Crust | The dried outer layer of a loaf, formed once the surface loses its water and rises above 100 °C. |
| Maillard reaction | Browning reaction between reducing sugars and amino groups, rapid on dry surfaces above about 140 °C; gives crust colour and roasted flavour. |
| Caramelization | Browning of sugars heated alone; sucrose from about 160 °C, fructose from about 110 °C. |
| Bake loss | (dough weight − baked weight) ÷ dough weight × 100; typically 10–20 % for lean hearth bread. |
| Internal temperature | Centre temperature at the end of the bake: 96–99 °C for lean bread, 88–93 °C for enriched bread. |
| Staling | Firming of bread crumb during storage, caused mainly by amylopectin retrogradation, not by drying. |
| Retrogradation | Re-crystallization of gelatinized starch on cooling and storage: amylose within hours, amylopectin over days; fastest at refrigerator temperatures, reversed by heating above about 60 °C. |
| Moisture migration | Movement of water from the moist crumb to the dry crust, softening the crust during storage. |
| Flying crust | A top crust that separates from the crumb, leaving a void underneath. |
