# M16 · Chocolate fundamentals

## Purpose

Chocolate is a suspension of sugar and cocoa particles in a fat that can crystallize in six ways, and every chocolate technique follows from that fact. This module gives you the composition model (what the percentage on a label does and does not tell you, couverture versus compound, natural versus Dutch cocoa), the rules for storing and melting chocolate without seizing or scorching it, ganache as an engineered emulsion whose texture you set by the ratio of chocolate to cream, and tempering as controlled crystallization of cocoa butter into form V. The practicals are a set of ganaches from glaze to truffle, tempered chocolate used for dipping, discs and a moulded bar, and a brownie that shows chocolate working inside a baked batter. After this module you can make the chocolate components of the core French pastry repertoire.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Read a chocolate label and nutrition panel, estimate fat, non-fat cocoa solids and sugar, and choose a chocolate or cocoa for a job.
- Store chocolate at 15–18 °C, melt it within its temperature limits, explain seizing and rescue seized chocolate.
- Formulate a ganache by ratio for glazing, piping and truffles in dark, milk and white chocolate, make it at controlled temperatures, and diagnose and rescue a split or grainy ganache.
- Temper chocolate by seeding, prove temper with a test strip, keep it in temper while working, and use it for dipping, discs and a moulded bar.
- Explain cocoa butter polymorphism and tell fat bloom from sugar bloom.
- Bake a fudgy brownie and move it towards cakey by changing one variable.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Cocoa liquor (cocoa mass) | Nibs ground to a fluid paste of cocoa particles in melted cocoa butter; contains no alcohol. |
| Cocoa butter | The fat of the cocoa kernel: solid at room temperature, melting just below body temperature, and able to crystallize in six forms. |
| Non-fat cocoa solids | The dry, fat-free particles of the kernel that carry flavour, colour and bitterness and absorb water. |
| Conching | Prolonged heated kneading of chocolate paste that removes moisture and harsh volatiles, coats particles with fat and lowers viscosity. |
| Cocoa percentage | The total of cocoa-derived ingredients in a chocolate (liquor + added cocoa butter + cocoa powder); not its cocoa butter content. |
| Couverture | Chocolate with a high cocoa butter content (≥ 31 % under EU rules), fluid when melted, used for tempered work and fine pastry. |
| Compound coating | A chocolate-flavoured coating in which cocoa butter is replaced by other vegetable fats; sets without tempering. |
| Dutch-processed cocoa | Cocoa treated with an alkali: darker, milder, pH ~7–8, brings no acid to react with baking soda. |
| Seizing | Sudden thickening of melted chocolate into a grainy paste when a small amount of water glues its sugar and cocoa particles together. |
| Ganache | An emulsion of fat droplets and a suspension of cocoa particles in a sugar-water phase, made from chocolate and cream (or another liquid). |
| Split (broken) ganache | A ganache whose fat has coalesced out of the water phase: oily, curdled or greasy. |
| Water activity (a<sub>w</sub>) | A 0–1 measure of how available the water in a food is to microbes and reactions; lowered by sugars, glucose and invert sugar. |
| Polymorphism | The ability of a fat to crystallize in several forms; cocoa butter has six (I–VI). |
| Form V | The cocoa butter crystal form (melting ~33–34 °C) that gives gloss, snap, contraction and bloom resistance. |
| Tempering | Controlled crystallization of chocolate so that only form V crystals are present when it sets. |
| Seeding | Tempering by adding 20–25 % finely chopped, already tempered chocolate to melted chocolate and stirring down to working temperature. |
| Working temperature | The temperature at which tempered chocolate is used: dark 31–32 °C, milk 29–30 °C, white 28–29 °C (or the manufacturer's curve). |
| Test strip | A knife tip or parchment strip dipped in chocolate and left at ~20 °C; setting glossy in 3–5 min proves temper. |
| Fat bloom | Grey, greasy surface haze or streaks from unstable crystals or temperature swings; unaffected by a drop of water. |
| Sugar bloom | Dry, white, dusty surface from moisture dissolving and recrystallizing surface sugar; dissolved by a drop of water. |
