# Stage 1 capstone · Formulation development project

<div class="prereq">

**Requires:** [19.1 Designing experiments and sensory tests](stage-1/module-19/lesson-01.md) · walkthrough in [19.2 The capstone project](stage-1/module-19/lesson-02.md)

**Uses:** [Report template](stage-1/capstone/report-template.md) · [Standardized recipe template](templates/recipe-template.md) · [Experiment template](templates/experiment-template.md) · [Lab notebook](templates/lab-notebook.md) · [SC-14 Measurement, sensory evaluation and variability](stage-1/science/sc-14-measurement-sensory.md)

**Threads:** T12 Experimental method & R&D, T1, T11, and every thread your product touches  **Time:** 3–6 weeks, about 25–40 h

</div>

## What

You take one product through a complete development cycle: choose it, set a measurable target, establish a replicated baseline, identify and rank the variables, test hypotheses in at least three rounds of controlled experiments (one of them a two-factor design), and finish with a validated standardized recipe and a report that explains the science and the failures along the way.

## Why

Stage 1 taught you to reproduce, explain and diagnose. The capstone asks you to *change a product on purpose and prove the change*. That is the core skill of pastry R&D, and it is the bridge to Stage 2 (product lines produced to specification) and Stage 3 (formulation and development). It is also the best test of whether the course worked: someone who can do this has stopped following recipes and started reasoning about baking systems.

## The ten steps

1. Choose a product.
2. Establish a baseline formulation.
3. Identify the important variables.
4. Form hypotheses.
5. Run controlled experiments.
6. Record results.
7. Modify the formulation.
8. Produce a final standardized recipe.
9. Explain the scientific reasoning behind it.
10. Document failures and improvements.

[19.2](stage-1/module-19/lesson-02.md) explains what each step needs; the [report template](stage-1/capstone/report-template.md) has a section for each.

## Rules

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Product | One product (or one component measured in its real context) built on Stage 1 systems. Base it on a course recipe, or on your own formula documented in the course format. |
| Target | A written specification before round 1: at least one primary instrumental response, at least one guard response, and one sensory criterion, each with a target and tolerance. |
| Baseline | The unmodified base formula baked **3 times on 3 different days**, all responses measured; batch-to-batch SD and CV reported. |
| Rounds | At least **3 rounds** of experiments. At least **one 2 × 2 factorial** with **2 or more replicate batches per cell**, blocked by day. |
| Controls | The baseline is baked in every session as a same-day control. |
| Bias control | Randomized bake order and oven position; coded samples for all sensory work and, where possible, for measurements. |
| Sensory | At least one blind test (triangle or directional paired comparison) with 6 or more tasters (12 recommended), read against the critical values in [SC-14](stage-1/science/sc-14-measurement-sensory.md) and [19.1](stage-1/module-19/lesson-01.md). |
| Validation | The final recipe is baked **3 times from the written recipe alone**, and every batch meets the specification. |
| Batch size | Small batches are allowed for screening; the confirmation round and validation are at the real product size. |
| Safety | Allergen briefing for every taster; no raw dough, batter or raw-egg preparations served; creams and custards held at ≤ 5 °C and discarded after 2 h at room temperature ([FDA-FoodCode], [FDA-flour], [FDA-eggs]). |
| Honesty | Every batch is reported, including failed and discarded ones, with the reason. Never adjust, drop or invent a number to make a result look cleaner. An inconclusive result reported honestly scores higher than a clean result that is not true. |

## Deliverables and submission

Submit two things, both kept in your lab notebook (paper or digital):

1. **The capstone report**, written in the [report template](stage-1/capstone/report-template.md): specification, baseline and noise, ranked variables, dated hypotheses, a record for every round (plan, data, analysis, decision), the final recipe summary, the scientific explanation, failures and improvements, and the Stage 2 bridge. Include photos of cross-sections with a ruler and a colour reference for the baseline and the final product.
2. **The final standardized recipe**, in the [recipe template](templates/recipe-template.md), tagged <span class="tag experimental">Experimental formulation</span>, with its revision history from the baseline and the validation data (mean, SD, CV of the primary responses across the three validation batches).

If you study with a mentor or a group, present a 10-minute summary with the two products side by side, coded, and run your sensory test on the audience.

## Grading rubric

Each criterion is scored at one of four levels. **Pass** requires at least *Competent* in every row. *Distinction* requires Distinction in at least five rows and nothing below Proficient.

| Criterion | Not yet | Competent | Proficient | Distinction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Product and specification | Vague goal; no measurable target | Measurable primary response with target and tolerance; one guard response; one sensory criterion | Several responses chosen to cover the product's real quality attributes | Specification justified by a real use (customer, shelf life, cost) and protocols fixed before round 1 |
| 2. Baseline and noise | Fewer than 3 baseline bakes, or noise not calculated | Baseline 3× on 3 days; SD and CV for primary responses | Measurement repeatability checked separately from batch noise | Noise used explicitly to choose step sizes, replicate numbers and decision thresholds |
| 3. Variables and hypotheses | Variables chosen without reason; hypotheses written after the data | Ranked variable table with mechanisms; dated hypotheses with direction | Expected magnitudes compared with noise; untested variables justified | Decision rules written in advance for every hypothesis and applied as written |
| 4. Experimental design | Fewer than 3 rounds, or no two-factor design, or no controls | 3 rounds; one replicated 2 × 2; same-session control each round | Randomized order and position, blocked by day, coded samples | Representative batch sizes justified; confounders identified and controlled in each plan |
| 5. Records | Missing batches or conditions; data written from memory | Notebook sheet per batch; summary table per round; photos | Failed and discarded batches recorded with reasons | Records complete enough for someone else to repeat any round |
| 6. Analysis | No calculation, or effects claimed below noise | Cell means, main effects and interaction calculated and compared with noise | Each hypothesis judged supported / not supported / inconclusive with reasons | Interaction explained mechanistically; direction checked across blocks |
| 7. Final standardized recipe | Incomplete or not validated | Full recipe in course format; formula adds up; validated 3× within spec | CCPs and tolerances drawn from project data | Validation CV at or below baseline CV; someone else reproduced one batch from the written recipe |
| 8. Scientific explanation | Missing or incorrect mechanisms | Correct mechanism for every formulation and process choice | Mechanisms linked to own data | Predictions for further changes, consistent with the data and the science units |
| 9. Sensory evaluation | Unblinded, or result misread | One blind test, coded, allergen briefing, correct critical value | Test chosen to match the question (difference vs direction vs profile) | Two complementary tests (e.g. triangle then paired) and their limits (panel size, power) discussed |
| 10. Failures and improvements | Omitted or superficial | Every failure listed with its cause and what changed | Failures turned into new hypotheses and tested | Clear next-step programme including a Stage 2 bridge with specific instruments and questions |

## Worked example: improving the 3-day softness of milk bread (R1-07)

<div class="callout warn">

**Illustration only.** This example shows the shape, depth and reasoning of a strong capstone. **All numbers in its data tables are illustrative example data, not real results**: they were chosen to be realistic and internally consistent so that the calculations can be followed. The final formula is an illustration of how a result is written up; it has not been validated as a course recipe. Do not copy the conclusions: run your own project.

</div>

### Step 1 · Product and specification

Product: [R1-07 Milk bread with tangzhong](recipes/R1-07-milk-bread.md). Use: a sandwich loaf for a café that bakes three times a week, so the bread must still be soft on day 3 without toasting.

| Response | Type | Protocol | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day-3 compression % | Primary | 2.0 cm centre slice, 200 g load on a 6 cm lid for 30 s, two points averaged; loaf stored sealed at room temperature ([X1-16](experiments/X1-16-tangzhong.md)) | ≥ 1.15 × baseline |
| Specific volume (day 0) | Guard | Seed displacement, 3 readings averaged ÷ loaf weight | Not more than 5 % below baseline |
| Crust colour | Guard | Printed 10-shade card, same light | Baseline shade ±1 |
| Gumminess (day 3) | Guard, sensory | 0–10 descriptive line, 4 briefed tasters | ≤ 4 |
| Perceived softness (day 3) | Sensory | Blind directional paired comparison, 12 tasters | Final softer, significant at α = 0.05 |

### Step 2 · Baseline (illustrative data)

R1-07 at half size (150 g total flour, small 16 × 8 cm pans, as in X1-16) for screening, baked on three days.

| Baseline batch | Day-3 compression % | Specific volume cm³/g |
|---|---|---|
| Day A | 22 | 4.9 |
| Day B | 24 | 5.1 |
| Day C | 23 | 5.0 |
| **Mean / SD / CV** | **23.0 / 1.0 / 4.3 %** | **5.0 / 0.10 / 2.0 %** |

Noise threshold for comparing single batches: 2 × 1.0 = 2 percentage points of compression.

### Step 3 · Variables ranked

| Variable | Mechanism | Expected effect vs noise | Risk | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tangzhong flour 7 → 10 % | More pre-gelatinized starch binds more water; crumb holds moisture and firms more slowly ([06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md), [SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md)) | Large | Denser crumb, stiffer handling | 1 |
| Butter 10 → 15 % | Fat interferes with starch–starch association and softens the crumb ([Gray 2003]) | Medium | Lower volume | 2 |
| Sugar 10 → 14 % | Sugar holds water and slows retrogradation | Small–medium | Darker crust, slower rise | 3 |
| Milk → water + milk powder | Standardization only | Small | — | Not tested |
| Pullman lid | Shape change, not staling | Not relevant to target | — | Not tested |

### Step 4 · Hypotheses (dated before round 1)

- H1: Tangzhong at 10 % (milk reduced so true hydration stays 74 %) raises day-3 compression by at least 3 points, because more gelatinized starch holds more water in the crumb. Decision rule: difference ≥ 2 points on both days = supported.
- H2: Butter at 15 % raises day-3 compression by at least 2 points, because fat slows starch retrogradation; volume may fall.
- H3: Sugar at 14 % raises day-3 compression by at least 2 points; crust will darken by 1–2 shades.

### Step 5–6 · Round 1: screening (illustrative data)

Four small loaves per day (baseline + three variants), order and pan positions randomized, repeated on two days. Values are means of the two days.

| Variant | Day-3 compression % | Difference vs control | Specific volume | Crust shade | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (control) | 23.5 | — | 5.0 | 5 | — |
| Tangzhong 10 % | 28.0 | +4.5 | 4.9 | 5 | H1 supported (both days ≥ +4) |
| Butter 15 % | 26.5 | +3.0 | 4.8 | 5 | H2 supported, volume down 4 % |
| Sugar 14 % | 24.5 | +1.0 | 5.0 | 7 | H3 not supported; crust out of spec |

Noise for a mean of 2 vs a mean of 2 is σ × √(1/2 + 1/2) = 1.0 point, so the threshold is 2 points. Sugar is dropped; tangzhong and butter go forward.

### Round 2: 2 × 2 factorial (illustrative data)

Tangzhong (7 %, 10 %) × butter (10 %, 15 %), one loaf per cell per day, two days (blocks), order and positions randomized each day, compression slices coded.

| Day-3 compression % | Butter 10 % | Butter 15 % | Mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tangzhong 7 %** | 23.0 | 26.0 | 24.5 |
| **Tangzhong 10 %** | 28.0 | 29.0 | 28.5 |
| **Mean** | 25.5 | 27.5 | |

| Specific volume cm³/g | Butter 10 % | Butter 15 % |
|---|---|---|
| **Tangzhong 7 %** | 5.0 | 4.8 |
| **Tangzhong 10 %** | 4.9 | 4.6 |

- Main effect of tangzhong = 28.5 − 24.5 = **+4.0** points (noise 0.71 × σ = 0.7): real.
- Main effect of butter = 27.5 − 25.5 = **+2.0** points (noise 0.7): real, smaller.
- Interaction contrast = (29.0 − 26.0) − (28.0 − 23.0) = 3.0 − 5.0 = **−2.0** points (noise 2σ ÷ √2 = 1.4): about 1.4 × noise, **inconclusive**. The direction was the same on both days: the tangzhong gain was smaller at 15 % butter. A plausible mechanism is that both act on the same process (the firming of the starch in the crumb), so their benefits overlap.
- Guard response: tangzhong 10 % with butter 15 % drops specific volume to 4.6, 8 % below baseline: **out of specification**.

Decision: keep tangzhong 10 %; test butter 12 % as a compromise.

### Round 2b: extra round (illustrative data)

Tangzhong 10 % / butter 12 % vs baseline, two days: compression 28.5 vs 23.5, specific volume 4.8 vs 5.0 (−4 %, within specification). Candidate final formula chosen.

### Round 3: confirmation at full size (illustrative data)

Full 22 × 11 cm loaves (300 g flour), final vs baseline, one of each on each of three days.

| Day | Baseline compression % | Final compression % | Difference | Baseline SV | Final SV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 25 | 31 | +6 | 5.2 | 5.0 |
| B | 27 | 32 | +5 | 5.3 | 5.1 |
| C | 26 | 30 | +4 | 5.1 | 5.0 |
| **Mean (SD)** | **26.0 (1.0)** | **31.0 (1.0)** | **+5.0** | **5.20** | **5.03** |

Full-size loaves are softer on day 3 than the small ones in both versions: less surface per gram, so they lose less moisture and stale more slowly. That is why the confirmation round runs at full size. Final ÷ baseline = 31 ÷ 26 = 1.19 (target ≥ 1.15, met). Specific volume −3 % (limit −5 %, met). Crust shade 5 for both.

**Sensory, day 3 (illustrative):** triangle test, 12 tasters, balanced orders, 9 correct (critical value 8): the loaves are perceptibly different. Directional paired comparison "which is softer?", 12 tasters, 11 chose the final formula (critical value 10): significantly softer. Gumminess: baseline 2, final 3 on the 0–10 line (limit 4).

### Step 7–8 · Final formula (illustration)

Changes from R1-07: tangzhong 7 → 10 % of flour (water kept at 5× the tangzhong flour), butter 10 → 12 %, milk reduced from 30 % to 12 % so that true hydration stays at 74 %, milk powder 3 → 5 % to replace most of the milk solids removed with the milk.

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| **Tangzhong** | | |
| Bread flour | 10.0 | 30 |
| Water | 50.0 | 150 |
| **Final dough** | | |
| Bread flour | 90.0 | 270 |
| Whole milk | 12.0 | 36 |
| Whole egg, beaten | 15.0 | 45 |
| Sugar | 10.0 | 30 |
| Milk powder (nonfat) | 5.0 | 15 |
| Fine salt | 2.0 | 6 |
| Instant yeast | 1.0 | 3 |
| Unsalted butter, soft | 12.0 | 36 |
| **Total** | **207.0** | **621** |

- **True hydration:** 150 + 36 × 0.87 + 45 × 0.75 + 36 × 0.16 = 150 + 31.3 + 33.8 + 5.8 = 220.9 g ÷ 300 g = **74 %**, the same as R1-07 (221.9 g).
- **Milk solids:** removing 54 g of milk removes about 5 g of nonfat milk solids; 6 g of extra milk powder replaces them (rounded up to 5 %).
- **Sugar in the water phase:** 30 ÷ (30 + 220.9) ≈ 12 %, unchanged, so yeast activity and proof times stay close to R1-07.
- **Yield:** 621 g → 4 × 153 g pieces in a 22 × 11 cm pan. Tangzhong cooked to 65–70 °C and topped up to 180 g. Procedure as R1-07; the stiffer tangzhong makes the dough feel slightly firmer at the end of mixing, and the butter goes in at the same point.
- **Validation (illustrative):** three validation loaves, day-3 compression 31, 30, 32 % (mean 31.0, SD 1.0, CV 3.2 %, below the 4.3 % baseline CV).

### Step 9 · Scientific reasoning (summary)

The larger tangzhong gelatinizes more starch before mixing; gelatinized starch holds water the crumb would otherwise lose or move to the crust, so the crumb starts softer and stays softer as retrogradation proceeds ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md), [04.3](stage-1/module-04/lesson-03.md), [Gray 2003]). Butter slows the firming further by interfering with starch–starch association, but above about 12 % in this soft dough it also shortens the gluten network enough to cost volume, the guard response that set the limit. Sugar at 14 % had a small effect on firming in this formula but browned the crust through extra Maillard and caramelization reactions ([SC-07](stage-1/science/sc-07-browning.md)), so it failed the specification. Keeping true hydration constant meant that the gains came from the *state* of the water (bound by gelatinized starch), not from simply adding more of it.

### Step 10 · Failures and improvements (examples of what to record)

- Round 1, first tangzhong-10 % dough: the paste lost 12 g to evaporation and was not topped up. The dough was stiff and the loaf small. Discarded, recorded, rerun; weighing and topping up the paste became a CCP.
- Round 2, day 2: one loaf probed off-centre and pulled at 86 °C; gummy centre. Rebaked the next day with a same-day control; noted that the block was split.
- H3 (sugar) not supported: the mechanism was right in direction but the effect was below noise at this step, and the crust colour limit made a larger step impossible.
- Interaction inconclusive: would need 4 replicates per cell to test properly.

### Stage 2 bridge (example)

With a texture analyser, measure crumb firmness curves over 5 days instead of a single coin-load reading; with a water activity meter, check whether the final formula changes aw and so the mould-free shelf life; test a scald (yudane) and an enzyme approach as alternatives to the larger tangzhong.
