# M19 · Capstone: formulation development project

## Purpose

Every module so far gave you a system, a recipe and an experiment someone else designed. M19 hands the design to you. Lesson 19.1 turns the measurement and variability tools of [SC-14](stage-1/science/sc-14-measurement-sensory.md) and [X1-44](experiments/X1-44-batch-consistency.md) into a working experimental method: hypotheses with decision rules, response variables you can measure at home, replicated and blocked 2 × 2 factorial designs, and blind sensory tests read against critical values. Lesson 19.2 applies that method to a product of your choice over 3–6 weeks, from baseline to a validated standardized recipe. The [capstone](stage-1/capstone/README.md) is the bridge between learning baking and formulation work, and the end of Stage 1.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Turn a product goal into a specification and into hypotheses with a direction, a mechanism and a decision rule.
- Choose and run home instrumental proxies (specific volume by seed displacement, spread ratio, colour card, compression, snap, crumb image analysis) and sensory tests (triangle, paired comparison, ranking, descriptive profile).
- Design a replicated, randomized, blocked and blinded 2 × 2 factorial, and calculate main effects and the interaction from the cell means.
- Decide whether a difference is real by comparing it with the batch-to-batch noise you measured.
- Take a product through three or more rounds of experiments to a validated standardized recipe, and explain its science and its failures.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Specification | The measurable targets and tolerances a product must meet, written before development starts. |
| Hypothesis | A testable prediction: if X changes from a to b, response Y moves in direction d by about m, because of mechanism M. |
| Decision rule | The result, fixed in advance, that will count as supporting or not supporting a hypothesis. |
| Response variable | A measured outcome of an experiment; primary responses define success, guard responses must not get worse. |
| Factor / level | A variable deliberately changed in an experiment / the values it takes (e.g. oven 170 °C and 190 °C). |
| One-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) | Changing one factor while holding all others, as in most X1 experiments; cannot detect interactions. |
| Factorial design | An experiment that runs every combination of the levels of two or more factors (2 × 2 = four cells). |
| Main effect | Mean response at a factor's high level minus mean at its low level, averaged over the other factor. |
| Interaction | When the effect of one factor depends on the level of another; measured as the difference of differences between cell means. |
| Replicate | An independent repetition of the whole process (a separately mixed and baked batch) that captures batch-to-batch variation. |
| Pseudo-replication | Treating sub-samples of one batch (six cookies from one dough) as independent replicates. |
| Randomization | Assigning bake order and oven position by chance so drifting conditions do not line up with a factor. |
| Blocking | Running every treatment within each group of similar conditions (e.g. each day), so differences between groups cancel out. |
| Confounder | A second variable that changed along with the one being tested and could explain the result. |
| Blinding | Hiding sample identity from tasters and measurers with random three-digit codes. |
| Triangle test | Sensory difference test: two identical samples and one different; chance of a correct pick is 1/3. |
| Paired comparison | Sensory test asking which of two samples has more of an attribute; chance 1/2. |
| Specific volume | Product volume divided by its weight, in cm³/g; measured at home by seed displacement. |
| Spread ratio | Cookie diameter divided by centre thickness. |
| Validation | Baking the final written recipe several times (three in the capstone) to show it meets the specification reproducibly. |
