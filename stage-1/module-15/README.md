# M15 · Laminated doughs

## Purpose

This module teaches the last and most demanding dough system of Stage 1: **lamination**, in which a sheet of plastic butter is folded into a dough to build a stack of alternating fat and dough layers that the oven separates with steam. You learn it as a controllable system with two governing quantities: the **number of layers**, which you calculate from the turn schedule, and **temperature**, which decides whether those layers survive lamination, proof and bake. You apply it to three products that span the family: puff pastry (steam only, hundreds of fine leaves), croissant (a laminated bread with an open honeycomb) and Danish (a richer laminated dough carrying fillings). The module ends with a lamination clinic: reading the tray, the cross-section and the log to locate a fault in the process timeline.

## Objectives

By the end of M15 you can:

1. Calculate fat and dough layers for any lock-in and turn schedule (F = 3^s × 4^d, D = F + 1) and the nominal layer thickness at a given sheet thickness, and use it to explain why too many turns reduce lift.
2. Shape a butter plaque to specified dimensions and keep butter and dough within their working windows (plaque 12–15 °C at lock-in, block 10–15 °C during turns) by measurement and rests.
3. Make classic puff pastry (six single turns) and blitz puff, sheet and cut them to rise straight, and bake them with a lift-then-dry profile.
4. Make croissants and pains au chocolat over two days: a moderately developed détrempe at DDT 22–24 °C, overnight retard, 1 double + 1 single, hand-sheeting to 4 mm, proof at 24–27 °C to 2–2.5×, and a deep-brown bake with an open honeycomb.
5. Make Danish pastries in four shapes with bake-stable fillings and apricot nappage.
6. Diagnose laminated faults from the tray, the cross-section and the process log, and design a one-variable test to confirm the diagnosis.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Lamination | Building alternating layers of dough and solid fat by enclosing a fat sheet in dough and repeatedly rolling and folding it. In the oven, steam separates the layers. |
| Détrempe | The base dough of a laminated pastry (flour, liquid, salt, and in yeasted versions sugar, yeast and a little butter), before the roll-in fat is added. |
| Beurrage | The roll-in fat, usually butter, shaped into a plaque and locked into the détrempe. Puff 70–80 % of flour, croissant 50–55 %, Danish 55–65 %. |
| Plaque | A slab of butter beaten and rolled to exact dimensions and uniform thickness (for example 19 × 19 cm, 8 mm) for locking in. |
| Pâton | The assembled block of détrempe and beurrage during and after lamination. |
| Lock-in | Enclosing the plaque in the détrempe. Envelope and open (butt-joint) lock-ins give one fat layer; the English lock-in gives two. |
| Single turn (letter fold, *tour simple*) | Rolling the block to about three times its length and folding it in three. Multiplies the fat layers by 3. |
| Double turn (book fold, *tour double*) | Rolling the block to about four times its length, folding both ends to meet near the centre and then folding in half. Multiplies the fat layers by 4. |
| Fat-layer count | F = 3^s × 4^d after a one-layer lock-in (s single turns, d double turns); dough layers D = F + 1. Nominal: real distinct layers are fewer. |
| Plastic range (of butter) | The temperature window, about 13–16 °C, in which butter bends and spreads as a continuous sheet without cracking or smearing. |
| Firmness match | The rule that dough and butter must resist deformation about equally during lamination, usually with the dough a few degrees colder than the butter. |
| Blitz (rough) puff | Puff pastry made by folding cold butter chunks mixed into the dough, without a plaque. Faster; 60–80 % of the lift of classic, less even. |
| Inverted puff (*feuilletage inversé*) | Puff pastry with the butter (mixed with some flour) on the outside and the détrempe locked inside. Very tender and high-rising; Stage 2. |
| Proof ceiling | The maximum proofing temperature for laminated yeast dough, about 27–28 °C, set by the butter softening and seeping, not by the yeast. |
| Honeycomb crumb | The croissant quality target: large, elongated cells separated by thin, continuous walls, open from the outside to the centre. |
| Docking | Pricking a pastry sheet all over so steam escapes and it bakes flat. |
| Rognures | Puff pastry scraps, stacked flat and re-rolled; they rise less and are used for straws and bases. |
| Bake-stable filling | A filling that keeps its shape in the oven, such as pastry cream with its starch raised to about 11 % of the milk, or crème d'amande. |
| Nappage | A thin, hot apricot glaze (jam thinned with about 20 % water, boiled and sieved) brushed on baked pastries for shine and to seal fruit. |

## Time estimate

About 2 weeks at the course pace (weeks 20–21 of the schedule). Reading: about 4 × 45–50 min. Bench: practice block (1.5 h), R1-33 classic (about 4 h over one or two days) and blitz (about 2 h), R1-34 (two days: about 1 h on day 1, 6 h on day 2 of which 1.5 h hands-on), R1-35 (two days, similar). Experiments: X1-39 (about 3 h plus a bake), X1-40 Part A (alongside or instead of a second croissant bake), Part B optional (two days). Clinic and assessment: [M15 quiz](stage-1/assessments/module-15-quiz.md), about 1.5 h plus the practical bake.
