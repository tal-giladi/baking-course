# M10 · Cakes: batter and foam systems

## Purpose

A cake is a foam that the oven expands and then sets, built from ingredients that pull in opposite directions: flour and egg build structure, sugar and fat tenderize it, liquids moisten it and flour and starches dry it. This module gives you that balance as a working model, with the traditional formula-balance rules professionals use to check a formula before baking, and the timing idea underneath them: a cake succeeds when it sets neither too early (domes, peaks, tunnels) nor too late (sinks, coarse, gummy). You will build aeration two ways, by creaming fat (pound cake) and by whipping eggs (génoise, biscuit, chiffon), measure it with batter specific gravity, and finish with pans, doneness, cooling, soaking syrups, storage and a cake clinic. After this module you can bake the core cakes of pastry reliably and diagnose the ones that fail.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Classify cake ingredients as builders or tenderizers and moisteners or driers, and check a formula against the regular and high-ratio balance rules in baker's percentages.
- Explain how sugar raises the setting temperature of a batter and predict the effect of more sugar, liquid, fat or leavener on rise, set and crumb.
- Cream butter and sugar within a temperature and time window, add eggs without breaking the emulsion, and choose between creaming and reverse creaming.
- Whip a warm whole-egg foam to the ribbon stage, fold with a measured loss of air, and build a chiffon from a fluid batter and a meringue.
- Measure batter specific gravity and use it as a control point.
- Convert formulas between pan sizes by area, judge doneness with three indicators, cool and unmould each cake type, and dose a soaking syrup by weight.
- Diagnose failed cakes from symptoms using the cake troubleshooting database.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Structure builder (toughener) | An ingredient that forms or strengthens the set structure of a cake: flour, egg whites, whole egg, milk solids, cocoa, starches. |
| Tenderizer | An ingredient that weakens or delays the structure, making the crumb softer: sugar, fat, egg yolk, chemical leaveners. |
| Moistener | An ingredient that adds water or holds it: water, milk, eggs, liquid sugars; sugar holds water without adding it. |
| Drier | An ingredient that absorbs water: flour, starches, milk powder, cocoa powder. |
| Formula balance | The traditional rules for checking a cake formula in baker's %: for regular cakes sugar ≤ flour, eggs ≥ fat, liquids (incl. eggs) ≈ sugar. |
| High-ratio cake | A cake with sugar above flour weight (typically 110–140 %) and liquids above sugar; requires chlorinated or heat-treated flour and emulsified shortening. |
| Chlorinated flour | Cake flour treated with chlorine gas, lowering its pH and modifying starch so it sets high-sugar batters; not permitted in the EU and UK. |
| Specific gravity (SG) | Weight of a volume of batter ÷ weight of the same volume of water; a measure of aeration (pound cake ~0.8–0.9, sponge ~0.4–0.5, indicative). |
| Creaming | Beating plastic fat with sugar crystals to incorporate air cells stabilized by fat crystals; butter at 18–20 °C, 4–6 min. |
| Reverse creaming | Mixing fat into the dry ingredients before the liquids; limits gluten, gives a finer, more tender crumb and flatter top. |
| Curdled batter | A broken fat-continuous emulsion in which egg water separates, usually from cold eggs, fast additions or butter that is too warm. |
| Ribbon stage | The endpoint of a whipped egg-and-sugar foam: it falls from the whisk in a ribbon that holds on the surface for 3–5 s. |
| Folding | Combining a foam with other ingredients by cutting, sweeping and turning to minimize loss of air. |
| Génoise | A cake made from a warm whipped whole-egg foam with flour and a little melted butter; no chemical leavener. |
| Biscuit (separated sponge) | A sponge made from separately whipped yolks and meringue, with flour; pipeable (ladyfingers). |
| Chiffon | A cake made from an oil-and-yolk batter with baking powder, lightened with meringue, baked in an ungreased tube pan and cooled inverted. |
| Tempering (butter into foam) | Mixing melted butter with a small part of the batter before folding it in, so it disperses instead of sinking. |
| Baking strips | Wet fabric strips around a pan that hold its wall near 100 °C, reducing doming and edge browning. |
| Soaking syrup | A sugar-water syrup (1 : 1 or 1.35 : 1 by weight) brushed on sponge layers for moisture and flavour, dosed at about 25–40 % of layer weight. |
| Cake doneness temperature | The centre temperature at which most cakes are set, about 93–99 °C, used with springback and skewer tests. |
