# M00 · Orientation: the kitchen as a laboratory

## Purpose

Before you bake anything, set up the three things every later module depends on: a way of working (the study loop and the lab notebook), a kitchen whose tools are known quantities (equipment, oven behaviour, workstation, mise en place), and the safety rules that protect you and the people you feed. This module is short on baking and long on habits. The habits are what make the rest of the course produce data instead of anecdotes.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

1. Explain the four dimensions of the course, the three-stage structure, threads T1–T12 and the append-only ID scheme.
2. Use the site's progress, notes and backup features, and keep a lab notebook with every bake-log field.
3. Equip and lay out a home kitchen lab, and describe your own oven's real behaviour.
4. Prepare full mise en place and a timed plan from any recipe.
5. Apply food-safety rules for raw flour, raw eggs, the danger zone, cooling, allergens and ingredient storage.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Four dimensions | The course's training axes: practical skill, baking science, experimental thinking, professional workflow. |
| Concept thread | One of twelve lines of understanding (T1–T12) that run through all stages; every lesson is tagged with the threads it advances. |
| Append-only IDs | The rule that Stage 1 IDs and paths are never renamed; later stages add new prefixes (`R2-`, `X2-`, `stage-2/`). |
| Lab notebook | The bench record of every bake and experiment: formula, actual weights, temperatures, times, observations, decisions. |
| Bake log | The per-bake entry in the lab notebook, with fixed fields (date, formula, actual weights, lots, temperatures, DDT, times, observations, photos, sensory, changes). |
| Mise en place | Having every ingredient weighed and labelled, every tool out and every pan prepared before the process starts. |
| Thermostat swing | The oscillation of oven air temperature around the set point as the thermostat cycles the element on and off. |
| Hot spot | A zone of the oven that runs hotter than the average, typically near elements, walls or the rear fan. |
| Oven map | Your measured record of real oven temperature versus dial, swing, time to stable temperature and hot-spot locations. |
| Fan offset | The 15–20 °C reduction applied when baking a conventional-oven recipe in a fan (convection) oven. |
| Soak (oven) | Holding the oven at temperature long enough for walls, stones and steels to reach equilibrium — 45–60 min for a stone. |
| Temperature danger zone | About 5–60 °C, where most pathogenic bacteria multiply fastest. |
| Two-stage cooling | Cooling cooked perishables from 60 °C to 21 °C within 2 h and to 5 °C within a further 4 h. |
| Cross-contact | Transfer of an allergen into a food that should not contain it, via tools, surfaces, hands or dust. |
| FIFO | First in, first out: the oldest stock is used first. |
| Rancidity | Off-flavours from oxidation or enzymatic breakdown of fats, typical of whole-grain flour and nuts stored warm. |
| Clean vs sanitise | Cleaning removes soil and protein with detergent; sanitising reduces microorganisms on an already clean surface. |
