# M05 · Preferments and sourdough

## Purpose

M03 taught you to control fermentation in a single dough; this module splits fermentation into stages. You ferment part of the flour in advance, as a yeasted preferment or as a wild sourdough culture that you establish and keep alive yourself, and you learn what that time does to flavour, dough extensibility and strength, crust colour and keeping quality. You also learn to write and scale formulas that contain a preferment, which is where most errors in bread formulation happen. The module ends with the sourdough loaf and a bread clinic, where you diagnose failed loaves from M03–M05 using their symptoms and bake records. After M05 you can bake lean yeasted, poolish and sourdough breads to a professional standard at home.

## Objectives

By the end of M05 you can:

1. Explain what a preferment does (acids and aroma, amylase and protease activity, extensibility or strength, keeping quality, schedule) and give the specifications of poolish, biga, sponge and pâte fermentée.
2. Write a formula in three columns (total formula, preferment, final dough), calculate prefermented flour (PFF), and convert a straight dough to a preferment dough with a correct check-sum.
3. Plan a poolish bake around its ripeness window, judge ripeness from observable cues, and shape, score and bake bâtards and 35 cm baguettes with steam.
4. Describe the sourdough ecosystem (lactic acid bacteria and wild yeasts, lactic and acetic acid, pH), establish a starter in 7–14 days, and prove it is ready by rise, time to peak and pH.
5. Run a sourdough loaf over two days with a DDT of 25–26 °C and a cold retard, and adjust its sourness deliberately.
6. Diagnose a failed bread from its symptoms and production record, and propose a one-variable test to confirm the cause.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Preferment | Part of the dough's flour, water and leavening culture fermented before the final dough is mixed. |
| Prefermented flour (PFF) | The percentage of total flour that is in the preferment. The least ambiguous way to specify a preferment. |
| Total (overall) formula | The whole dough expressed in baker's % of total flour, including the preferment's flour and water. |
| Final dough | The ingredients added on the day, with the ripe preferment as one of them. Equals the total formula minus the preferment. |
| Poolish | Liquid preferment at 100 % hydration with ~0.1 % yeast, ripened 12–16 h at ~20–22 °C. Gives extensibility and a sweet, wheaty flavour. |
| Biga | Stiff Italian preferment at 50–60 % hydration, fermented cool. Gives strength and a fuller, slightly tangy flavour. |
| Sponge | Short, warm preferment that usually carries all the yeast; used for pan and enriched breads. |
| Pâte fermentée | "Old dough": a piece of finished lean dough, including salt, added to a new batch. |
| Alpha-amylase | Enzyme that cuts starch chains internally, creating dextrins and new chain ends. Usually the rate-limiting amylase in wheat flour. |
| Beta-amylase | Enzyme that cuts maltose from the ends of starch chains. |
| Protease | Enzyme that cuts proteins, including gluten; over long preferments it increases extensibility. |
| Autolyse | A rest of flour and water, without salt and traditionally without yeast, before mixing, which improves hydration and extensibility and shortens mixing. |
| Starter / levain | The wild culture you maintain (starter), and the preferment built from it for a particular bake (levain). |
| Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) | Acid-tolerant bacteria that ferment sugars mainly to lactic acid; the majority population in a sourdough. |
| Heterofermentative | LAB that produce lactic acid plus CO₂ and ethanol or acetic acid. Homofermentative LAB produce mainly lactic acid. |
| Feeding ratio | Starter : flour : water by weight at a feed, e.g. 1:2:2. Lower inoculation (1:5:5) peaks later and starts less acidic. |
| Peak | The maximum rise of a starter or preferment after feeding; the usual point of use. |
| False rise | Vigorous activity on day 2–3 of a new starter from non-sourdough bacteria that are then eliminated by rising acidity. |
| Hooch | Alcohol-rich liquid on a hungry starter. A sign of starvation, not spoilage. |
| Total titratable acidity (TTA) | The total amount of acid, including buffered acid. Tracks perceived sourness better than pH alone. |

## Time estimate

About 2 weeks at the course pace (weeks 7–8), with the starter running daily throughout. Start [R1-05](recipes/R1-05-levain.md) on the first day of the module, since establishment takes 7–14 days. Meanwhile, do 05.1–05.2, R1-04 (evening + 5 h) and X1-13 (evening + one long day, plus a day-2 tasting). Once the starter is ready: X1-14 (one day, alongside other work) and at least two bakes of R1-06 (two days each, about 1.5 h of hands-on time). Reading: about 4 × 50 min, plus 60 min for the bread clinic. Assessment: [M05 quiz](stage-1/assessments/module-05-quiz.md), about 90 min, plus the practical bake.
