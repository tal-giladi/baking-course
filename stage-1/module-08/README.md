# M08 · Fats, chemical leavening and mixing methods

## Purpose

Up to M07 your products were leavened by yeast and structured by gluten, and fat was an enrichment. This module makes fat and chemical leavening the main actors. You will build a working model of fat as a material whose physical state — liquid, soft-plastic, cold-plastic or hard — is set by temperature and decides which job it does: tenderizing, aerating, flaking or layering. You will treat baking soda and baking powder as a small chemical reactor with a gas schedule, calculate acid–base balance, and predict what a formula will taste and look like from its leavening. Then you apply both through the three mixing methods of quick breads: the muffin method, the creaming method and the rubbing-in method. After this module you can bake muffins, quick loaves and scones to a specification and diagnose their failures, and you have the foundation for cakes (M10), short and flaky pastry (M11) and lamination (M15).

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- State the composition of butter, shortening, lard, margarine and oil, and choose fat type and temperature for a given job; convert between fats while keeping fat and water constant.
- Write the soda–acid reaction, calculate the soda needed to neutralize a given acid ingredient, and predict the effects of excess soda.
- Explain single- and double-acting baking powder, fast and slow leavening acids (including aluminium-free SAPP powders), and bench tolerance.
- Carry out the muffin method to a counted, lumpy endpoint and explain tunnels, peaks and domes.
- Cream butter at 18–20 °C to a measured batter specific gravity and add eggs without curdling.
- Rub cold butter into flour to a stated piece size, handle minimally, build layers by fold-and-pat, and bake scones that rise straight and tall.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Triglyceride | The main molecule of fats and oils: three fatty-acid chains on a glycerol backbone. |
| Solid fat content (SFC) | The percentage of a fat that is crystalline at a given temperature; decides whether it is brittle, plastic or liquid. |
| Plasticity | The ability of a fat to hold its shape under light pressure yet deform without cracking; roughly 15–40 % solid fat. Butter is plastic at about 13–20 °C. |
| Shortening | (1) A 100 % fat, usually vegetable-based, with a wide plastic range and often emulsifiers. (2) The tenderizing action of fat, which coats flour and shortens gluten strands. |
| Creaming | Beating plastic fat (butter at 18–20 °C) with granulated sugar so that sugar crystals cut air cells into the fat-crystal network. |
| Specific gravity (SG) | Mass of a batter ÷ mass of the same volume of water; a lower value means more trapped air. |
| Baking soda | Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃); releases CO₂ when it reacts with an acid; leaves alkaline sodium carbonate if heated without acid. |
| Baking powder | Soda plus one or more leavening acids plus starch; a self-contained gas system, conventionally yielding at least ~12 % of its weight as CO₂. |
| Leavening acid | An acid or acid salt chosen to react with soda at a specific time: fast (monocalcium phosphate, cream of tartar) or slow and heat-activated (SAPP, sodium aluminium sulfate). |
| Double-acting | A baking powder with a fast acid (gas at mixing) and a slow acid (gas in the oven). |
| SAPP | Sodium acid pyrophosphate (diphosphates, E450); the slow acid in most aluminium-free baking powders. |
| Neutralizing value (NV) | Grams of soda neutralized by 100 g of a leavening acid. |
| Bench tolerance | How long a mixed batter can wait before baking without losing too much leavening gas. |
| Baker's ammonia | Ammonium bicarbonate; decomposes completely into ammonia, CO₂ and water; only for thin, dry products. |
| Muffin method | Dry and wet ingredients (with liquid fat) mixed separately, then combined in a few folds to a lumpy batter. |
| Tunnels | Long, vertical holes in muffins and quick breads caused by gas travelling through a gluten network developed by overmixing. |
| Rubbing-in (biscuit) method | Cold solid fat worked into flour to crumbs and pea-size pieces before minimal liquid and handling; used for scones, biscuits and flaky pastry. |
| Fold-and-pat | Patting a scone or biscuit dough flat and folding it on itself 2–3 times to flatten fat pieces into layers. |
