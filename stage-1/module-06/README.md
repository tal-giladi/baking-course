# M06 · Enriched doughs

## Purpose

This module takes the lean-dough system from [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md)–[M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) and adds the four enrichments: sugar, fat, eggs and milk. Each one changes fermentation, gluten development, crumb, crust colour and shelf life at the same time. You learn to predict those changes from baker's percentages, compute true hydration when most of the liquid is milk and eggs, and run three benchmark enriched breads that move along the spectrum from soft to rich: a tangzhong milk bread, a braided challah and a 50 %-butter brioche. Two ideas introduced here come back in pastry. Pre-gelatinized starch is the mechanism of pastry cream and fillings, and fat plasticity is what governs lamination.

## Objectives

By the end of M06 you can:

1. Place a yeasted formula on the lean-to-rich spectrum from its sugar, fat and egg percentages, and predict its fermentation speed, mixing requirements, oven temperature and internal doneness (88–93 °C).
2. Explain osmotic stress in sweet doughs, compute the sugar concentration in the dough water, and choose yeast type (standard or osmotolerant) and dose for 0–20 % sugar.
3. Compute true hydration for doughs containing milk, eggs, yolks, butter and honey.
4. Make and use a tangzhong or yudane while keeping total flour and water constant, and measure its effect on softness and staling.
5. Braid 3-, 4- and 6-strand loaves from positional notation, and control shine and colour with a double egg wash and oven settings.
6. Mix a 50 %-butter brioche with controlled temperatures (base ≤24 °C, butter 13–16 °C, final ≤24–26 °C, proof ≤27 °C), retard it, shape it à tête and Nanterre, and bake it to specification.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Enriched dough | A yeast dough containing significant sugar, fat, eggs and/or milk in addition to flour, water, salt and yeast. Examples range from soft rolls (5–10 % sugar and fat) to brioche (50–100 % butter). |
| Lean-to-rich spectrum | The classification of yeast doughs by their sugar, fat and egg percentages. Moving towards rich means slower fermentation, longer mixing, softer crumb, faster browning and lower oven temperatures. |
| Osmotolerant yeast | A strain of instant yeast, usually sold in a gold-labelled package, selected to keep fermenting in doughs with more than about 10 % sugar. |
| Water-phase sugar concentration | Sugar ÷ (sugar + total dough water). It predicts osmotic slowdown better than sugar % of flour. |
| Shortening (effect) | The action of fat in coating and interrupting gluten strands, making the crumb tender and easily torn rather than chewy. |
| Tangzhong | A roux of flour cooked with about five times its weight of water or milk to 65–70 °C, typically 5–10 % of total flour. It pre-gelatinizes starch so the dough holds more water. |
| Yudane | Flour scalded with an equal weight of boiling water, typically 10–25 % of total flour, cooled (often overnight) before use. |
| Pre-gelatinized starch | Starch that has been heated in water until its granules swell and lose their crystalline order before it goes into the dough. It binds much more water than native starch. |
| Scalding milk | Heating milk to about 82–85 °C, then cooling, to inactivate a heat-sensitive whey-protein component that weakens dough. Less critical with pasteurized milk; unnecessary with UHT milk or high-heat milk powder. |
| Plasticity | The property of a fat that is solid under small stresses but can be spread and shaped under larger ones. Butter is plastic at about 13–16 °C. |
| Broken (greasy) dough | A high-fat dough in which butterfat has melted and separated as free oil. The dough is shiny, curdled and weak. Usually caused by a final dough above about 26 °C. |
| Rubaud | A hand-mixing technique for soft doughs: rapid scooping and lifting of the dough in the bowl with cupped fingers to develop gluten. |
| Positional braid notation | Braiding instructions in which "*a* over *b*" moves the strand at position *a* (counted from the left) to position *b*, re-numbering after every move. |
| Double egg wash | Brushing egg wash once after shaping and again just before baking, for an even, deep, glossy crust. |
| Brioche à tête | Individual brioche baked in a fluted mould, with a small ball (the head) set into a deep well in the body. |
| Brioche Nanterre | Brioche loaf made of several rounded balls baked side by side in a loaf pan. |
| Pareve | In Jewish dietary law, food containing neither meat nor dairy. The reason challah is traditionally made with oil rather than butter. |

About 2 weeks at the course pace (weeks 9–10 of the schedule). Reading: about 4 × 45–50 min.
