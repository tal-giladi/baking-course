# M17 · Finished pastries: components and assembly

## Purpose

Everything you have made since M10 is a component: a shell, a cream, a sponge, a syrup, a buttercream, a ganache. This module teaches you to design, schedule and assemble them into finished pastries that are right at the moment they are eaten, not just at the moment they are made. The model has four parts: the job of each component (base, filling, accents, finish); the balance of textures and flavours; the structural logic of what holds what; and the moisture migration between components, which follows water activity and decides how long a product lasts. You apply it to the two canonical French tarts (a fruit tart with a wet filling that must be protected and timed, and a chocolate tart whose filling protects itself) and to a layered génoise cake, finished on a turntable or in a ring with acetate as a first simple entremet. The module is integrative: it introduces no new component, and it is the bridge to production planning (M18) and to Stage 2 entremets.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Break a finished pastry into components by role, and specify each by weight and percentage of the finished product.
- Predict moisture migration between components from their water activity, and protect a crisp component with a bake, a seal (egg wash, cocoa butter, chocolate), a matched filling or a schedule; quantify the effect of each seal in a controlled test.
- Build a production timeline backward from service, sorting components into make-ahead, same-day and at-assembly work.
- Assemble a fruit tart and a chocolate tart to specification: fully baked and sealed shell, cream at the right set, fruit and glaze applied at the right time and temperature, ganache poured at 35–40 °C or a chocolate custard baked to a just-set end-point.
- Split, soak (by weight), fill, crumb coat and finish a layered cake to a level, straight-sided, crumb-free result, or assemble it in a ring with acetate.
- Portion, hold and serve assembled products safely (cream fillings ≤ 5 °C, eaten within 24–48 h), and compose a simple plated dessert.

## Troubleshooting

[Assembled pastries](troubleshooting/assembled-pastries.md): soggy tart base, fruit weeping and glaze sliding off, cloudy or thick glaze, tart cream too runny to slice, cracked or grainy chocolate tarts, sliding layers, dry or soggy cakes, crumbs in the finish, leaning or bulging cakes, condensation, and melting decorations.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Component | A separately made preparation (shell, cream, sponge, syrup, glaze) with its own recipe, shelf life and working temperature, combined with others in a finished product. |
| Assembly | Putting components together in a set order, at set temperatures and times, to a weight specification. |
| Water activity (aw) | How available the water in a food is, from 0 to 1; water moves between components from high aw to low aw, and aw, not moisture %, governs microbial growth. |
| Moisture migration | Movement of water between components (or through the air) toward equal water activity; makes crisp components soft and can dry moist ones. |
| Seal (barrier) | A layer that slows moisture migration into a shell: baked egg wash (protein film, slows), cocoa butter or chocolate (fat film, largely blocks), jam (intermediate). |
| Full bake | Blind baking a shell until the centre of the base is golden, dry and matt, for fillings that will not be baked. |
| Nappage | A glaze (apricot or neutral, pectin- or carrageenan-based) brushed on fruit to give shine and limit drying, oxidation and weeping. |
| Ganache | An emulsion of cream's water phase in chocolate's fat phase with suspended cocoa solids; at 1:1 with dark chocolate, the filling of a set chocolate tart. |
| Pour temperature | The temperature at which a ganache or glaze is poured (35–40 °C for tart ganache; 32–35 °C for a pouring glaze); controls levelling, gloss and cracking. |
| Soaking syrup | A flavoured sugar syrup (typically 40 °Brix) brushed onto sponge layers by weight, about 25 % of the sponge's weight. |
| Dam | A piped ring of firm buttercream (or stiff cream) at the edge of a cake layer that contains soft filling and fruit. |
| Crumb coat | A thin first coat of buttercream that glues loose crumbs; chilled until firm before the final coat. |
| Final coat | The finishing layer of buttercream (4–5 mm), smoothed with a bench scraper on a turntable to vertical sides and a sharp top edge. |
| Acetate | A clear, flexible plastic strip lining a cake ring, giving smooth, straight sides that release cleanly after setting. |
| Entremet | A layered cake assembled in a ring or frame from several components (sponge, creams, mousses, inserts), set, unmoulded and glazed or decorated. |
| Backward timeline | A production schedule built from the service time backward, placing each component according to its setting time and shelf life. |
| Portion marker | A guide pressed onto a round cake or tart to mark equal portions for cutting and decoration. |
| Dew point | The temperature at which water in the air condenses; a chilled cake below the dew point of the room gathers condensation. |
| Plated dessert | A dessert composed on the plate at service from several components (main element, sauce, crisp, fresh element), often with temperature contrast. |

## Time estimate

About **1 week** (week 22 of the suggested schedule, shared with M16): 2.5 h reading and 1 h planning; one session for X1-43 (about 3 h, plus checks at 1 h, 6 h and 24 h); two sessions for the tarts (components the day before, about 2 h each for assembly); two sessions for the layer cake (about 3 h and 2.5 h). Total roughly **14–17 hours**, much of it overlapping with components you also practise from earlier modules.
