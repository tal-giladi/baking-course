# M14 · Choux pastry

## Purpose

Choux is the one pastry leavened by steam alone. A cooked flour paste (the panade) is diluted with egg until it is soft enough to stretch and firm enough to hold a shape; in the oven its water turns to steam, inflates it into a hollow shell, and egg protein and gelatinized starch set the walls. This module brings together threads from earlier modules: starch gelatinization ([SC-02](stage-1/science/sc-02-carbohydrates.md), [06.2](stage-1/module-06/lesson-02.md)), egg coagulation and emulsification ([M09](stage-1/module-09/lesson-01.md)), steam and oven heat ([M04](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md)), pastry cream ([12.2](stage-1/module-12/lesson-02.md)) and fondant ([13.1](stage-1/module-13/lesson-01.md)). You will learn to make the paste to measured endpoints, pipe to weight, choose an oven profile for your oven, and finish éclairs and profiteroles to a specification, then diagnose choux failures from the shell and the batch record.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

- Explain how choux rises and sets, and predict the effect of changing egg quantity, panade drying, liquid, flour and oven profile.
- Make pâte à choux to measured endpoints: butter melted at the boil, panade dried to a film, 75–80 °C and a recorded weight loss, eggs added at ≤ 60 °C and stopped at the V test.
- Pipe éclairs and choux balls to within ±10 % of a target weight, and control the surface with a French star tip, fork marks or craquelin.
- Bake choux to dryness (colour in the cracks, rigidity, 35–45 % bake loss) with a profile you have tested in your own oven.
- Fill by weight, glaze with fondant at 35–37 °C, and schedule assembly so shells are crisp when eaten.
- Diagnose collapsed, flat, cracked, lopsided, wet or soggy choux and faulty glazes, and name the correction.

[Choux](troubleshooting/choux.md): collapsed after baking, did not rise, cracked irregular tops, small hollow or wet interior, paste too runny, paste split or greasy, lopsided rise, soggy after filling, pale shells, fondant dull or cracked, fondant too runny or thick, misshapen shells.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Pâte à choux | Cooked paste of liquid, butter and flour, diluted with egg; leavened only by steam into a hollow shell. |
| Panade | The cooked flour paste before the eggs are added: liquid and butter brought to a boil, flour added at once and cooked. |
| Dessèchement | Drying the panade over heat for 1–3 min to complete gelatinization and drive off water (15–35 g for R1-31), so the paste can absorb more egg. |
| Steam leavening | Rising caused by water vaporizing inside a product; at 100 °C and atmospheric pressure steam occupies roughly 1,600–1,700 times the volume of the liquid water. |
| Starch gelatinization | Swelling of starch granules with water on heating, with loss of their ordered structure; in the panade it lets the paste hold about three times the flour weight in water. |
| Egg coagulation | Heat-setting of egg proteins (from about 62–65 °C for white, higher for yolk and for whole egg in mixtures); sets the expanded choux walls. |
| V test (bec d'oiseau) | Paste endpoint: lifted on a spatula, it falls slowly and leaves a hanging V-shaped tail of 3–5 cm that holds without dripping. |
| Trench test | A trench drawn through correct choux paste closes slowly, over several seconds. |
| Craquelin | Thin disc of butter, brown sugar and flour (about 1 : 1.2 : 1), rolled 1–2 mm, frozen and laid on raw choux to give an even, round rise and a crisp crackled top. |
| French star tip | Piping tip with many narrow teeth; its ridges make éclairs crack finely and evenly along their length. |
| Éclair | Long choux shell, typically piped 12–14 cm, filled with pastry cream and glazed. |
| Profiterole | Small round choux (piped 3–4 cm), filled with cream, pastry cream or ice cream. |
| Bake loss | (raw weight − baked weight) ÷ raw weight × 100; for well-dried choux typically about 35–45 %. |
| Venting | Letting steam escape: propping the oven door open slightly at the end of the bake, and piercing the shells after baking. |
| Fondant | Mass of very small sucrose crystals in saturated syrup, used as a glaze at 35–37 °C; overheated, it sets dull and brittle. |
| Crème diplomate | Pastry cream lightened with whipped cream, usually set with a little gelatin; a lighter choux filling. |
| Moisture migration | Movement of water from a high-water-activity filling into a dry shell, which makes filled choux soggy over hours. |
