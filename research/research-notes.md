## 1. Institutions and qualifications

### 1.1 Le Cordon Bleu Paris: Diplôme de Pâtisserie (H)
- URL: https://www.cordonbleu.edu/paris/patisserie-diploma/en
- Structure: 3 certificates, each a prerequisite for the next. Basic → Intermediate → Superior. Standard 9 months, or intensive 6 months (Basic + Intermediate in 3 months, Superior 3 months). Demonstration by chef, then individual supervised practical.
- **Basic**: workstation organisation, food safety, French terminology, equipment, ingredient handling. Core techniques: piping, glazing, doughs, creams. Classics: Pithiviers, Paris-Brest, éclairs, fraisier, macarons. Intro viennoiserie (croissant, pain au chocolat, brioche). Decoration basics. Ingredient theory.
- **Intermediate**: génoise mastery, colour/flavour harmony, contemporary tarts, pulled sugar, chocolate (crystallisation/tempering, moulding, dipping, candies), croquembouche, confectionery, plated desserts, petits fours, entremets. Theory: ice cream/sorbet, chocolate.
- **Superior**: chocolate and sugar showpieces (poured, pulled, blown), modern dessert creation with portfolio, contemporary entremets, intro to cost control.
- Notable: science is "ingredient theory" only; no formal food-science unit.

### 1.2 Le Cordon Bleu Paris: Diplôme de Boulangerie (H)
- URLs: https://www.cordonbleu.edu/paris/bakery-diploma/en ; https://www.cordonbleu.edu/paris/basic-boulangerie-certificate/en ; https://www.cordonbleu.edu/paris/advanced-boulangerie-certificate/en (last two M)
- Structure: 3 months total. Basic Boulangerie Certificate → Advanced Boulangerie Certificate (prerequisite: Basic).
- **Basic**: hygiene, manual and mechanical kneading, French flour types, fermentation types, basic and complex breads, viennoiserie.
- **Advanced**: same competency list at more depth. Focus on fermentation development, aroma, natural leaven (levain).
- Assessment: written/oral theory exams, continuous practical assessment, final practical, portfolio.

### 1.3 ENSP Yssingeaux (École Ducasse): French Pastry Arts Diploma, CAP Pâtissier (H/M)
- URLs: https://www.ecoleducasse.com/en/programs/french-pastry-arts-diploma (H) ; https://www.ecoleducasse.com/en/campuses/ecole-nationale-superieure-de-patisserie (H, overview only) ; https://www.ecoleducasse.com/en/cap-in-pastry (M; fetch returned bachelor content, CAP figures from search snippet)
- **French Pastry Arts Diploma**: 22 weeks, about 80% hands-on (~29 practical h/week), optional 8-week internship. 10 modules in order:
  1 Fundamentals (doughs, creams, baking, assembly, decoration) → 2 Tea-time/travel cakes and petits fours → 3 Bread and viennoiserie (fermentation, lamination) → 4 Entremets (biscuits, mousses, inserts, glazes) → 5 Natural/healthy desserts → 6 Plated desserts → 7 Chocolate (tempering, ganache, bonbons) → 8 Confectionery (jellies, marshmallow, nougat, caramel, praline) → 9 Ice creams and sorbets (mix balancing) → 10 Croquembouche/buffet (sugar work).
  Plus **food science 22 h**, photography 8 h, French 44 h.
- **CAP Pâtissier prep** (M): 8 months / 1,073 h or 9 months / 1,233 h; 70% practical, 30% theory; two 4-week internships.
- Also offers a 3-year Bachelor and Diplôme Supérieur des Arts Pâtissiers (M, not fetched).

### 1.4 French national reference: CAP Pâtissier (RNCP35316) and CAP Boulanger (RNCP37537) (H/M)
- CAP Pâtissier: https://www.francecompetences.fr/recherche/rncp/35316/ (H). Official référentiel PDF (arrêté 6 March 2019): https://eduscol.education.fr/referentiels-professionnels/CAP_patis/Annexes_CAP_patis.pdf (M, seen in search, not fetched)
  - **Bloc 1, Tour, petits fours secs et moelleux, gâteaux de voyage**: receiving and storing goods, stock tracking, workstation organisation and hygiene, creams, making, baking and finishing doughs (pâtes), dry and soft petits fours, meringues, quality evaluation.
  - **Bloc 2, Entremets et petits gâteaux**: production planning, fillings and garnishes, bases for entremets, assembly/filling/glazing, decoration, presentation and **cost calculation**.
  - Blocs 3–7: French, history-geography, maths-**sciences (physique-chimie)**, PE, prevention-health-environment, foreign language (A2).
- CAP Boulanger: https://www.francecompetences.fr/recherche/rncp/37537/ (M, from search)
  - BC01 procurement, communication, food safety and hygiene; BC02 production and presentation of breads and viennoiserie; BC03–07 general education incl. **maths + physics-chemistry**.
- Relevance: the CAP is the French legal/professional baseline and a good benchmark for "Stage 1 = employable production worker".

### 1.5 FERRANDI Paris (H)
- Pastry: https://www.ferrandi-paris.com/en/french-pastry-programs/intensive-professional-program-french-pastry
  - 16 weeks × 35 h (~560 h) plus optional 3-month internship. Tart crusts, puff pastry, choux, entremets biscuits, creams, icings/glazes, decorating. Tarts, cakes, individual pastries, chocolates, confectionery, frozen desserts. Also applied art (drawing), dessert–beverage pairing, a creativity project judged as the final exam.
- Bread: https://www.ferrandi-paris.com/en/bread-baking-programs/intensive-professional-program-bread-baking
  - 12 weeks plus optional 2-month internship. Traditional French breads and methods, regional breads, rye/bran, international breads (focaccia, naan). Viennoiserie (croissant, brioche, Danish). Explicit **fermentation science, hydration, gluten development, temperature control**.
- Advanced Professional Program in French Pastry also exists: https://www.ferrandi-paris.com/en/french-pastry-programs/advanced-professional-program-french-pastry (M, not fetched)

### 1.8 Johnson & Wales University: Baking & Pastry Arts A.S. (H)
- URLs: https://catalog.jwu.edu/programsofstudy/food-innovation-technology/baking-pastry-arts-as/ ; course list https://catalog.jwu.edu/courses/bpa/ (M)

Major (24 cr, 8 × 3 cr): BPA1701 Foundations of Baking and Pastry → BPA1710 Principles of Cake Production and Design → BPA1720 Plated Desserts → BPA1730 Artisan Bread & Viennoiserie I → BPA1740 Artisan Bread & Viennoiserie II → BPA1750 Chocolate, Confections & Mignardise → BPA2710 Advanced Pastry Applications → BPA2720 Artisan Café.

### 1.9 San Francisco Baking Institute (SFBI): Bread & Viennoiserie Training Series (H)
- URLs: https://sfbi.com/classes/ ; 2025 packet https://sfbi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Bread-Viennoiserie-Training-Series-2025.pdf (H, text extracted)
- Founded 1996 by Michel Suas (author of *Advanced Bread and Pastry*). Max 16 students; full days; production-scale volume.
- **Series I, Fundamentals (10 days, no prereq)**: mixing, dividing, pre-shaping, shaping, baking; equipment (spiral mixer, oven loader). Baguette used as the test medium for mixing methods and flours. **Function of flour, water, salt and yeast; the mixing–fermentation relationship**; preferments (poolish, sponge, pâte fermentée); whole wheat, rye, enriched doughs (challah); shaping (boule, bâtard, braids, regional shapes); intro to sourdough.
- **Series II, Sourdough, Whole Grain & Alternative Grains (10 days, prereq I)**: building and maintaining starters (hydration, temperature, feeding); **retarding**; whole-wheat percentages; Kamut, spelt, buckwheat, millet, teff; German ryes. Group practical exam.
- **Series III, Viennoiserie (10 days)**: laminated and non-laminated doughs, sheeter, lamination and retarding methods, Danish/croissant/puff, fillings; panettone with Italian levain, stollen, kouign-amann, inverted puff. Practical exam.
- **Series IV, Bakery Business (5 days, prereq I–III)**: production scheduling, food/labour cost, equipment, bakery design.
- Stated objectives include **baker's math**, ingredient function, formula design, and troubleshooting.
- Older "18-week full-time / 24-week part-time" professional program (Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Baking_Institute) appears to have been replaced by the Series format (M).

### 1.10 Bread Bakers Guild of America (BBGA): certification (H)
- URL: https://www.bbga.org/Get-Certified
- Levels: Certified Bread Baker (CBB) → Certified Viennoiserie Baker (CVB) → Certified Master Artisan Baker (CMAB; requires CBB + CVB).
- Prereqs: 1 year professional experience, ServSafe Food Handler.
- Knowledge: **milling, flour functions and standards, preferments and fermentation, mixing and baking, baker's percentage, sanitation**; fermentation control, shaping, scoring, time management.
- Exams: 100-question written (70%), plus a 2-day practical with a timed baker's-percentage quiz.

## 2. Synthesis

**(1) Common subjects (present in almost all programs)**
Food safety/hygiene (always first, often a certified exam: ServSafe, C&G 202, CAP bloc 1). Tools, terminology, mise en place. Ingredient function (flour, sugar, fat, eggs, dairy, leaveners). Pastes/doughs (shortcrust, sweet, puff, choux). Creams and custards (pâtissière, anglaise, crème mousseline, ganache). Sponges, cakes and biscuits. Yeast breads and viennoiserie. Plated/frozen desserts. Chocolate tempering and basic confectionery. Decoration. Costing.

**(2) Beginner → advanced progression**
Consistent across LCB, ENSP, CIA, JWU and C&G L2→L3:
- **Foundation**: safety, tools, weighing/baker's %, ingredient function. Then base preparations (pastes, creams, meringues, basic cakes, lean yeast doughs, basic viennoiserie).
- **Intermediate**: assembly of components into composite products (entremets, tarts, laminated doughs), preferments and sourdough, tempering and moulding, plated desserts, confectionery, ice cream balancing.
- **Advanced**: showpieces (sugar/chocolate), contemporary/creative design, production management, costing, supervision, menu development.

Complexity rises by number of components and control variables, not by switching to new product families. The same families recur at L2 and L3 (C&G 213–216 vs 321–326).

**(3) Prerequisite relationships (observed)**
- Food safety → any production.
- Ingredient function + baker's math → formula scaling and troubleshooting.
- Lean yeast dough and fermentation control → enriched doughs → laminated/viennoiserie (SFBI I → III; LCB Basic Boulangerie → Advanced).
- Straight/yeasted doughs → preferments → sourdough/levain → retarding (SFBI I → II).
- Base creams + sponges → entremets/mousses (ENSP modules 1 → 4).
- Sugar cooking stages → confectionery → pulled/blown sugar.
- Chocolate tempering → moulding/bonbons → showpieces (LCB Intermediate → Superior).
- Egg/starch/emulsion basics → ice cream mix balancing, plated desserts.

**(4) Practical competencies by level**
- **Level 1 / Stage 1** (CAP bloc 1, C&G L2, LCB Basic, SFBI I, JWU BPA1701, CIA BAKE-105/110): scale and read formulas; organise the station; receive and store goods. Make shortcrust, sweet and puff pastry, choux, pâtissière, anglaise, meringues (3 types), génoise/biscuit, travel cakes, cookies/petits fours secs. Make lean bread (baguette), a basic preferment, brioche, and intro croissant. Pipe, glaze, basic finishing. Evaluate own product quality.
- **Level 2**: entremets and small cakes (assemble, glaze, decorate, cost: CAP bloc 2); sourdough maintenance; retarding; lamination at volume; tempering, moulding, dipping; plated desserts; confectionery; ice cream.
- **Level 3**: sugar and chocolate showpieces, contemporary design/portfolio, production scheduling, supervision, business.

**(5) Food science taught in culinary programs**
Typically applied and ingredient-level: ingredient functions, gluten development and hydration (Ferrandi bread), fermentation types (LCB), preferments and sourdough factors (SFBI), cocoa-butter crystallisation (Callebaut), and sugar stages. Formal science hours are small: ENSP 22 h; CIA 3 cr culinary science + 1.5 cr ingredients/equipment technology + BAKE-251 Advanced Baking Principles; JWU only a nutrition course. LCB covers it as "ingredient theory". The CAP adds general physics-chemistry in general education.

**(6) R&D-relevant subjects commonly omitted by culinary programs**
Present at KSU, AIB and in cereal-science texts, but absent or thin in LCB/ICE/Ferrandi/JWU:
- Milling and flour specification (extraction rate, ash, protein, falling number, damaged starch).
- Flour and dough rheology/testing (farinograph, alveograph, extensograph, mixograph; GRSC 625).
- Carbohydrate and protein chemistry: starch gelatinisation/retrogradation, gluten polymer chemistry.
- Enzymes: amylases, xylanases, lipases, and enzyme-based anti-staling.
- Water activity, moisture migration, shelf-life modelling, and staling kinetics.
- Microbiology: spoilage moulds, rope (*Bacillus*), preservatives, sourdough microbial ecology.
- Emulsifiers, hydrocolloids, and dough conditioners/oxidants.
- Process calculations and heat/mass transfer (dough temperature, ovens).
- Frozen and par-baked dough, and scale-up to industrial lines.
- Sensory evaluation methods, statistics and experimental design.
- HACCP/preventive controls, labelling/regulatory, and nutrition reformulation (salt, sugar, fat reduction; gluten-free).

**(7) Culinary-school pastry vs artisan-bread training**
- Pastry programs (LCB, Ferrandi, ICE, ENSP) are broad in products and focus on composition, decoration and plating. Bread is usually one module.
- Bread programs (SFBI, BBGA, LCB Boulangerie, Ferrandi Bread) are narrow in products but deep in process control: fermentation time and temperature, preferments, levain management, retarding, flour choice, hydration, baker's %, production scheduling at volume. They also use more explicit process science and put more weight on **timed production and troubleshooting**. BBGA even tests baker's % under time.
- Viennoiserie sits between the two and is taught in both.

**(8) Foundation vs advanced placement**
- **Foundation (Stage 1)**: safety; equipment; weighing and baker's %; ingredient functions; the basic "mother" preparations (pastes, creams, meringues, sponges, lean dough, straight-dough fermentation, basic enriched dough); intro to preferments; intro to lamination; sensory/quality evaluation vocabulary; intro-level science (gluten, starch gelatinisation, Maillard/caramelisation, leavening gases, emulsions).
- **Advanced**: sourdough ecology and management; retarding and production scheduling; entremets engineering (texture layering, gelling agents); chocolate showpieces; sugar art; ice-cream mix formulation (PAC/POD); plated-dessert design; costing and management; R&D topics from (6).
