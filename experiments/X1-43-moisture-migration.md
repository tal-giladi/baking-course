# X1-43 · Moisture migration: sealed vs unsealed tart shells

## Question

How long does a fully baked sucrée shell stay crisp under crème pâtissière in the refrigerator, and how much longer does it last when sealed with a baked egg wash or with a thin layer of cocoa butter (or chocolate)?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Before baking, write down for each of the three treatments (**A** unsealed, **B** egg-wash sealed, **C** cocoa butter layer):

- A crispness score from 1 to 5 (scale below) that you expect at **1 h**, **6 h** and **24 h** after filling.
- The weight of water (in grams) you expect each shell to gain by 24 h.
- Which part of the shell will soften first: base centre, base–wall corner, walls or rim?
- For B, whether the seal *stops* migration or only *slows* it, and why you think so.
- What you expect for an **unfilled** shell stored in the same refrigerator for 24 h.

Commit to numbers. The point is to compare your mental model with the result.

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Barrier between shell and cream: **A** none; **B** egg wash (yolk + 10 % cream) brushed on the hot shell and baked 2–3 min; **C** thin film of melted cocoa butter (or tempered dark chocolate) brushed on the cooled shell |
| **Dependent** | Crispness score (sensory, 1–5) at 1 h, 6 h and 24 h; break force of the base (g, kitchen-scale press test); weight gain of the shell (g and % of shell weight); appearance of the base (dark, translucent wet band); sound on breaking |
| **Controlled** | One batch of dough ([R1-21](recipes/R1-21-pate-sucree.md)) and one batch of pastry cream ([R1-26](recipes/R1-26-creme-patissiere.md)); rolled thickness (3 mm guides); identical 8 cm tartlet rings; same bake (all shells baked together, including the 2–3 min "seal" bake for all three); cooling time; cream temperature (≤ 5 °C) and re-texturing; cream weight per tartlet (40 g); filling time (all within 10 min); storage (same refrigerator shelf, same loosely covered box, 2–5 °C); test method and tester |

**Controls:** one **unfilled** shell of each treatment is stored in the same box. It tells you how much softening comes from the refrigerator's humid air rather than from the cream.

**Equipment note:** you need 12 tartlet rings or tins of 8–10 cm (or bake in batches with the oven measured each time). If you have none, use the strip version under Procedure.

## Procedure

**Day 1 (evening):** make the sucrée and let it rest overnight. Make the pastry cream, spread it 2 cm thick under film in contact, chill to ≤ 5 °C.

**Day 2:**

1. **Label** 12 rings or a sheet of parchment under them: A1–A4, B1–B4, C1–C4 (position 4 = unfilled control). Spread the three treatments across the tray so no treatment occupies only the hot corner.
2. **Roll** the dough to **3 mm** between guides; cut 12 cm discs; line the 8 cm rings; dock; trim flush. Weigh 3 lined rings to check consistency (target 40 ± 3 g). Freeze **20 min**.
3. **Blind bake** all 12 together at **165–170 °C (330–340 °F)**: weighted 12–14 min, then without weights 6–10 min to a **full bake** (golden centre of the base).
4. **Seal bake.** Take the tray out. Brush only **B1–B4** with a thin, complete coat of egg wash. Return **all 12** shells to the oven for **2–3 min** (A and C unbrushed), so all have the same total bake.
5. **Cool** 30 min on a rack; unmould. **Weigh each shell** (m0, to 0.1 g if possible).
6. **Cocoa butter.** Melt the cocoa butter to **35–40 °C** (or temper the chocolate). Brush **C1–C4** with a thin, complete film, starting with the base–wall corner, then base and walls. Check against the light for bare spots. Set 10 min at ≤ 20 °C. **Weigh C1–C4 again** (m1; for A and B, m1 = m0).
7. **Fill.** Re-texture the cold pastry cream **20–40 s**. At **t = 0**, fill tartlets 1–3 of each treatment with **40 g** (weighed) in rotating order A1, B1, C1, A2, B2, C2, A3, B3, C3; finish within 10 min. Record the time of each fill. Leave the number 4 shells empty.
8. **Store** all 12 on one refrigerator shelf at **2–5 °C**, in one loosely covered box (same humidity for all). Record the refrigerator temperature.
9. **Test** at **1 h** (A1, B1, C1), **6 h** (A2, B2, C2) and **24 h** (A3, B3, C3, plus all three controls). Offset each test by the same interval as the fills so every tartlet has the same contact time.

**Strip version (no rings):** roll the dough to 3 mm and cut 12 strips of 4 × 10 cm; bake flat on parchment with the same schedule (no weights; about 12–15 min to golden). Apply the treatments to the top surface. At t = 0 spread 25 g cream in a 5 mm layer on each strip, 5 mm from the ends. Everything else is identical.

## Measurements

| What | How | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight gain** | At the test time, scrape the cream off with a small offset spatula, wipe once with a paper towel folded to the same size each time, and weigh (m2). Gain = m2 − m1; % gain = gain ÷ m1 × 100. Some cream residue remains; treat the same way every time | Scale (0.1 g ideal, 1 g acceptable) |
| **Break force (press test)** | Put the emptied shell, base down, on the scale on a small flat support; tare. Press the round end of a chopstick (or wooden spoon handle) vertically on the centre of the base, slowly and steadily (about 2 s), until it breaks. Record the peak reading (g) and whether it broke suddenly (snap) or bent and crushed | Kitchen scale; chopstick |
| **Crispness score** | Break a piece of base by hand, then bite it. **5** snaps cleanly and loudly, like a fresh shell; **4** snaps, slightly less crisp on the top surface; **3** bends a little before breaking, dull sound; **2** bends, crumbles softly, damp top layer; **1** soft, cakey, no break | Hands, ears, mouth |
| **Appearance** | Cut through the centre of the base; note a darker, translucent damp layer under the cream and its depth (mm); photograph | Ruler, camera |
| **Where it softened** | Score base centre, corner, walls and rim separately (1–5) | Fingers, bite |

If a helper serves you the samples without labels, your sensory scores are less biased (the chocolate variant will still be recognizable by colour; note it).

## Analysis questions

1. Plot crispness score (and break force) against time for each treatment. Which treatment keeps a score of 4 or better for longest? Estimate, for each, the time at which it drops to 3.
2. Rank the weight gains at 24 h. Does the rank match the crispness rank? If a shell gained little weight but still went soft, what might explain it?
3. Compare the unfilled controls with the filled 24 h shells. How much of the softening is due to the cream, and how much to the refrigerator's air?
4. Where did each shell soften first? What does that tell you about where to concentrate the barrier?
5. Did the egg wash stop migration or slow it? Explain with what the seal is made of.
6. A café fills fruit tartlets at 07:00 and sells them until 18:00. Using your data, which seal do they need, and what else would you change?
7. How did your results compare with your hypotheses? Which part of your model was wrong?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Typical pattern** (your times will depend on how fully the shells were baked, the cream and your refrigerator):

| Treatment | 1 h | 6 h | 24 h |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Unsealed | 3–4: top of the base already soft and damp under the cream; lower part still snaps | 2: bends before breaking, visible dark wet layer | 1–2: soft, cakey; the largest weight gain, typically several percent of the shell's weight |
| B Egg wash | 4–5 | 3: noticeably softer, still breaks | 2: soft; gain smaller than A but clearly present |
| C Cocoa butter | 5 | 4–5 | 4 (often 5 in the centre); little weight gain; any soft spot is where the film was thin or missing, usually the base–wall corner |
| Controls (unfilled) | — | — | 4–5; slight softening of rims in a humid refrigerator |

**Why.** A baked sucrée shell is at about aw 0.3; pastry cream at about 0.97 ([SC-01](stage-1/science/sc-01-water.md)). Water moves from high to low water activity, not toward equal moisture content, and it moves fastest where a mobile water phase touches a porous, dry solid. The shell's starch–sugar–protein matrix is glassy when dry; absorbed water plasticizes it, and only a small gain (1–3 % moisture) takes it past the point where it snaps. That is why the change feels sudden and why weight gain and crispness do not scale linearly.

**Egg wash** is a thin film of coagulated egg protein. Proteins are hydrophilic: the film absorbs water and lets it diffuse through, and it cracks and has pinholes. It *slows* migration (fewer open pores at the surface, a longer diffusion path) but does not stop it. It buys hours, not a day.

**Cocoa butter** is a continuous fat film. Water is almost insoluble in fat, so it crosses the film only very slowly, and the film blocks the capillary entry of cream into the pores. Its weakness is coverage: wherever the film is thin, missing or cracked (the corner, the rim of the base, a pinhole), water enters locally. Tempered chocolate gives a firmer, less brittle film than cocoa butter brushed too thick and cooled fast.

**The controls** show that the refrigerator itself is humid: an unfilled shell stored uncovered or loosely covered picks up some water from the air (the air in a closed box comes toward equilibrium with the cream, too), mostly at the rim and edges. Most of the softening of the filled shells, however, comes through the contact face.

**For the café:** a complete cocoa butter (or chocolate) film over a fully baked shell, plus drying fruit and glazing; or fill in two waves. The egg wash alone will not last 11 h under pastry cream.

</details>

## Going further

- **Barrier thickness:** brush cocoa butter once, twice and three times (weigh each); find the least that gives full protection at 24 h.
- **Other barriers:** a thin layer of apricot jam; white chocolate; a baked layer of crème d'amande ([R1-23](recipes/R1-23-creme-damande.md)); a dusting of cake crumbs.
- **Filling aw:** repeat treatment A with a 1:1 dark ganache ([R1-36](recipes/R1-36-ganache.md)) instead of pastry cream and compare 24 h and 72 h (connects to [R1-40](recipes/R1-40-chocolate-tart.md)).
- **Bake degree:** a par-baked vs fully baked unsealed shell under cream.
- **Stage 3 extension:** with a water-activity meter, measure the aw of each component and of shells over time, and build a simple sorption curve for the shell ([S3-M05 Shelf-life engineering](extension/stage-2-3-architecture.md)).
