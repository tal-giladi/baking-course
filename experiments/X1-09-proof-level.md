# X1-09 · Proof level: under, optimal, over

## Question

With the same dough, shape and bake, how does the length of final proof change oven spring, crumb structure and the way the score opens? And what does the poke test feel like at each stage?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Sketch the cross-section you expect for each loaf: height, shape, where the score opens, and where the big holes are. Predict the poke-test response at 30, 60 and 120 min. Write down which loaf you expect to have the palest crust, and why.

## Batch and scale

One dough, divided into three pieces:

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams (batch) | Per variant (≈) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 100 | 600 | 200 |
| Water | 68 | 408 | 136 |
| Salt | 2 | 12 | 4 |
| Instant yeast | 0.8 | 4.8 | 1.6 |
| **Total** | **170.8** | **1024.8** | **341.6** |

Divide into **3 × 340 g**. The remaining ~5 g allows for dough lost in the bowl.
**Scale-up (×2.5):** use a full [R1-01](recipes/R1-01-lean-loaf.md) dough per variant (three separate loaves of ~864 g), or a 1500 g flour batch divided into three 850 g pieces.

## Procedure

1. Mix to a **DDT of 24 °C** following [R1-01](recipes/R1-01-lean-loaf.md), Steps 1–4 (scaled). Bulk at 24–25 °C with 3 folds to **+75 %** (about 1.75–2.25 h).
2. Divide into 3 × 340 g ±2 g. Pre-shape into rounds. Bench-rest 20 min.
3. Shape three small **bâtards** (about 15 cm) with equal tension. The same person should shape all three, one after the other, within 5 min. Place each seam-up in a floured small oval banneton or cloth-lined bowl. Label them A, B, C. **t = 0** is when the last one is shaped.
4. Proof all three side by side at **24 °C**, covered.
5. **Preheat** a covered pot at 250 °C (480 °F) from t = 0 (it will be ready by ~t = 30–35 min). If you only have a stone, preheat it for 45+ min, which means starting before you shape.
6. **Bake each loaf at its time:**
   - **A at 30 min**, **B at 60 min**, **C at 120 min**.
   - Just before loading: poke test (on the side, same place each time) and record the response. Photograph the loaf from the side.
   - Turn it onto parchment and score one cut, 45°, ~6 mm deep, full length.
   - Bake covered for 15 min at 250 °C (480 °F), uncover, lower to 230 °C (445 °F), and bake 8–12 min more to ≥96 °C. Use exactly the same times for all three.
   - Return the empty pot and lid to the oven straight away so they reheat before the next loaf. At 22–27 min per bake, the pot is free and back at temperature before the next slot.
7. Cool each loaf for 2 h. Weigh it and measure height and width. Cut all three lengthwise through the score and photograph them side by side with a ruler.

**Alternative staging by poke test (option B):** proof one bâtard until the poke test reads *under* (fast, full spring-back), one to *ready* (slow, partial), and one well past (no spring-back), recording the minutes. This calibrates your finger instead of the clock. It is better if your room temperature is unstable.

## Analysis questions

1. Rank the loaves by height/width ratio. Which had the most oven spring, and why?
2. Where did each loaf expand? Did any of them tear somewhere other than the score?
3. Describe the crumb of each. Where are the largest cells, and where are the dense zones?
4. Which crust is palest? Connect it to what the yeast was doing during proof ([03.1](stage-1/module-03/lesson-01.md)).
5. Match each poke-test response to its outcome. Which response will you now treat as "ready" in your kitchen? Would you move B earlier or later next time?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**A: under-proofed (30 min).** Poke: fast, full spring-back. The loaf is small and tight going in. In the oven it springs *violently*: the score opens into a wide, deep, often ragged gash, and the loaf may also **burst along the side or base** (a blowout) because the score couldn't release all the expansion. It comes out tall but lopsided or split. The crumb is **tight and dense, especially at the bottom**, often with a few large irregular tunnels near the score where the expansion tore through. The crust is well coloured.

**B: optimal (60 min).** Poke: slow, partial spring-back. It springs strongly and evenly: the score opens into a clean bloom with a raised **ear** and there is no tearing elsewhere. It has the best height/width ratio. The crumb is moderately open and fairly even, with cells getting smaller toward the crust. The crust is deep golden-brown. (If A and B look similar, your optimum is later than 60 min. If B looks like C, it is earlier. Adjust your notes.)

**C: over-proofed (120 min).** Poke: the dent stays, and the dough may sigh. It may stick to the banneton, spread when turned out, and deflate slightly when scored. There is little oven spring: the score barely opens and has no ear, and the loaf is **flat and wide** (lowest height/width ratio). The crumb is either coarse and irregular with cells merging, or has a band of **large holes just under the top crust** with a denser zone below, because the stretched network collapsed as the gas cells coalesced. The crust is **paler**: the yeast used up more of the residual sugar during the long proof. It may taste slightly boozy or sour.

**The lesson:** proof is a window. Under-proofing wastes spring by releasing it in the wrong place. Over-proofing uses up the spring before the oven. The poke test tells you roughly where you are, but your own calibrated response (answer 5) is what you will use from now on.

</details>
