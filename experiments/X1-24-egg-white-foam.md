# X1-24 · Egg-white foam: sugar timing, acid and a trace of fat

## Question

How do cream of tartar, a trace of egg yolk, and the timing of sugar addition change the time an egg white takes to reach stiff peak, the amount of air it holds (specific gravity and volume), and how long the foam stays stable (drainage)?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Write in your notebook, before whipping:

- Rank the five variants below by time to stiff peak, fastest first.
- Rank them by specific gravity (most air first). Will adding 60 g sugar make the foam lighter or heavier per cup?
- Which will drain the most liquid in 30 minutes, and which the least?
- What do you expect half a gram of yolk to do to 60 g of white? Estimate it as a percentage change in volume.

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Five treatments: **A** plain white; **B** + cream of tartar (1 % of white); **C** + sugar (1:1) added at the start; **D** + sugar (1:1) added gradually from soft peak; **E** + 0.5 g yolk |
| **Dependent** | Time to soft peak and to stiff peak; specific gravity (SG) at stiff peak; foam volume and overrun (calculated); drainage after 30 min (g and %); appearance (gloss, bubble size, graininess); optional: baked meringue shape |
| **Controlled** | Egg source and age (one carton, whites pooled and divided); white weight (60 g); white temperature (20 ± 2 °C); bowl (same bowl, cleaned and degreased between variants); mixer, beaters and speed setting; person whipping; sugar type (caster); endpoint definition; measuring cup; room temperature |

The comparisons are: **A vs B** (acid), **A vs E** (fat), **C vs D** (sugar timing). A vs D shows the overall effect of sugar.

## Batch and scale

**Per variant:**

| Ingredient | A Plain | B Acid | C Sugar at start | D Sugar at soft peak | E Yolk trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Egg white, pooled (g) | 60 | 60 | 60 | 60 | 60 |
| Cream of tartar (g) | — | 0.6 | — | — | — |
| Caster sugar (g) | — | — | 60 | 60 | — |
| Egg yolk (g) | — | — | — | — | 0.5 |
| **Total (g)** | **60** | **60.6** | **120** | **120** | **60.5** |

**Total ingredients:** about **340 g egg white** (12 whites from ~56 g eggs: 300 g for the variants, up to 18 g for the yolk dilution below, plus a margin), 0.6 g cream of tartar, 120 g caster sugar, 1 yolk (you use 0.5 g; keep the rest for other use). A 0.1 g scale is needed for the cream of tartar and the yolk. If you have only a 1 g scale: make 1 g of cream of tartar into a 10 % mixture with 9 g sugar and use 6 g of that mixture (containing 0.6 g) in B, noting the 5.4 g sugar as a deviation; for the yolk, stir 2 g yolk into 18 g white and use 5 g of that mixture plus 55.5 g white for E (together 60 g white and 0.5 g yolk).

**Scale ×2.5** (for a stand mixer, which will not whip 60 g well): 150 g white per variant (750 g total, plus margin), 1.5 g cream of tartar, 150 g sugar each for C and D, 1.25 g yolk. Times will be longer; the rankings should not change.

**Equipment:** electric hand mixer (or stand mixer at ×2.5); one deep stainless or glass bowl about 15 cm across; a timer; a 100 ml cup (or a ramekin whose volume you have measured by filling it with water and weighing); scale (0.1 g); straight spatula to level the cup; 5 small funnels (or one funnel used in rotation, see Procedure) and 5 narrow glasses or jars that fit them; paper towel; white vinegar; labels.

## Procedure

1. **Prepare whites.** Separate the eggs cold, using the three-bowl method ([09.1](stage-1/module-09/lesson-01.md)); discard any white with a trace of yolk. Pool the whites, stir gently with a fork (do not whip), and divide into five labelled cups of 60.0 g (for E, 55.5 g if you are using the yolk dilution). Cover and leave at room temperature until the whites read **20 ± 2 °C** (30–45 min). Record temperatures.
2. **Prepare the bowl.** Wash bowl and beaters in hot soapy water, rinse, dry with a clean paper towel, then wipe with a paper towel moistened with vinegar. Repeat between every variant.
3. **Calibrate the cup.** Weigh the empty 100 ml cup; fill it level with water, weigh; the water weight in grams is its volume in ml. Record it.
4. **Order:** **A, B, D, C, E.** E is last so that no yolk contaminates the others.
5. **Whip each variant** at one fixed speed (e.g. hand mixer on medium-high, the same setting throughout), moving the beaters around the bowl in the same way. Start the timer when the mixer starts.
   - **A:** whip until stiff peak.
   - **B:** add the cream of tartar at the start, whip until stiff peak.
   - **C:** add all the sugar to the white before starting; whip until stiff, glossy peak.
   - **D:** whip to soft peak; note the time; add the sugar about 10 g every 15 s with the mixer running; continue to stiff, glossy peak.
   - **E:** add the 0.5 g yolk and stir before starting; whip until stiff peak. **Stop at 8 minutes** if it has not reached stiff peak, and record the stage reached.
   - For each variant record the time at **soft peak** (peak bends over and sinks back) and **stiff peak** (peak stands straight when the beater is lifted and turned up). Stop the timer and the mixer at stiff peak; do not keep whipping.
6. **Measure SG immediately** (within 1 min): spoon foam into the calibrated cup without pressing, tap it once on the bench to remove large voids, level with the spatula, weigh. SG = foam grams ÷ cup volume in ml.
7. **Start drainage:** within 2 min of stopping, weigh exactly **20.0 g** of foam into a funnel set in a tared glass. Label it and note the clock time. After **30 min**, lift out the funnel and weigh the liquid in the glass. (If you have one funnel, move the foam into a lidded clear cup instead, and after 30 min carefully tip off and weigh the liquid that has collected at the bottom.)
8. **Photograph** the remaining foam in the bowl next to its label, and note gloss, bubble size and any graininess.
9. **Optional window test (A and B only):** whip the remaining foam for 60 s more and describe it (glossy, dull, grainy, lumpy, liquid appearing).
10. **Optional bake (C and D):** pipe or spoon three 3 cm kisses of each onto parchment. Bake at **100 °C (210 °F)**, fan off, for 60–90 min until they lift cleanly off the paper, then cool in the switched-off oven. Compare shape, spread, cracking and beading.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** These foams are raw egg. Do not taste them unless you used pasteurized whites. Clean surfaces and wash hands after handling shells ([FDA-eggs]). Keep the hand mixer cord away from the bowl and the sink.

</div>

## Analysis questions

1. How much longer did the sugar-at-start foam (C) take than D to reach stiff peak? Why does sugar slow foaming?
2. Compare SG of A and D. Did adding sugar make the foam lighter or heavier per cup? Which held more air per gram of *white*? (Hint: calculate foam volume per 60 g white.)
3. Rank the five variants by drainage. What does that ranking say about the roles of sugar and acid?
4. By what percentage did 0.5 g yolk (under 1 % of the white) change E's volume compared with A? What does this mean for separating eggs in production?
5. In the window test, how did A and B differ after 60 s of extra whipping? Relate it to protein bonding in the films.
6. If you baked kisses: which held its shape better, C or D? Which was more likely to bead syrup?
7. Which variant would you use to fold into a chiffon batter, and which for piped meringue? Justify with your numbers.

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

Absolute numbers depend on your mixer, bowl size and eggs; the **rankings** are the result. Typical values for 60 g whites at 20 °C with a hand mixer:

| | A Plain | B Acid | C Sugar start | D Sugar soft peak | E Yolk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to stiff peak | ~2–3 min | ~2.5–4 min (slightly slower) | ~6–10 min (slowest) | ~4–6 min (soft peak as fast as A) | ~4–8 min, or never stiff |
| SG at stiff peak | ~0.13–0.18 | ~0.12–0.17 | ~0.30–0.45 | ~0.20–0.30 | ~0.20–0.40 |
| Drainage in 30 min | High, often 15–35 % | Lower, ~5–15 % | Very low, ~0–3 % | Very low, ~0–5 % | Highest, and the foam visibly collapses |
| Appearance | White, dry-looking, coarsens | Whiter, finer | Very glossy, dense, marshmallow-like | Glossy, stiff, fine | Creamy-tinged, soft, coarse |

**Sugar timing (C vs D).** Sugar at the start makes the water phase viscous before any foam exists, so proteins reach and unfold at the bubble surfaces slowly: whipping takes much longer and the foam holds less air. Added after soft peak, sugar thickens lamellae around bubbles that already exist: nearly as much air, far more stability. Both sugared foams are heavier per cup than plain white (the sugar itself weighs something and holds no air), but D gives the most volume of the two and is the standard method.

**Acid (A vs B).** Cream of tartar slows foaming a little, gives similar or slightly more volume, a finer and whiter foam, and markedly less drainage. In the window test, A turns dull and grainy and starts to separate within 30–60 s past stiff peak, while B stays smooth. Acid moderates protein–protein bonding in the films, so they stay flexible instead of over-bonding and squeezing out water.

**Fat (A vs E).** Well under 1 % yolk typically cuts volume by a quarter or more, slows foaming and may stop the foam at soft or firm peak. Yolk lipids occupy the bubble surface and break up the protein films, so bubbles coalesce. In production, a white with any yolk goes to another use.

**Uses.** For folding into a chiffon or soufflé batter: B, or D with part of the sugar, taken to firm peak. For piped meringue: D (or C if maximum stability matters more than volume). Baked, C and D kisses both hold shape; C is denser and more likely to show beads of syrup if the sugar did not fully dissolve, D is lighter.

</details>

## Going further

- **Temperature:** repeat A with whites at 4 °C and 20 °C.
- **Sugar ratio:** D with 30 g, 60 g and 120 g sugar (0.5:1, 1:1, 2:1); plot SG and drainage against sugar ratio. This leads into French meringue in [13.2](stage-1/module-13/lesson-02.md) and [X1-35](experiments/X1-35-meringue-stability.md).
- **Other acids:** lemon juice (1 g) and vinegar (1 g) vs cream of tartar (0.6 g).
- **Pasteurized carton whites** vs fresh whites, same method.
- **Stage 3:** aquafaba and plant-protein foams measured with the same sheet; add a hydrocolloid (xanthan 0.1 %) and measure drainage.
