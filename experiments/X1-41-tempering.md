# X1-41 · Tempered vs untempered chocolate

## Question

What does tempering actually change? If you take one chocolate and make identical discs and bars three ways (properly tempered, simply melted and cooled, and tempered then warmed to 36 °C), how do they differ in set time, gloss, snap, release from acetate and mould, and bloom over a week? And can you tell a tempered chocolate from an untempered one by its temperature alone?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Write in your notebook, before you start:

- Rank A, B and C by set time, gloss and snap.
- Will C (tempered, then warmed to 36 °C, then cooled back to the same working temperature as A) behave like A or like B? Why?
- On which day do you expect to see bloom on each, if at all?
- Which will release from the mould without help?

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Crystal state at deposit: **A** tempered by seeding ([R1-37](recipes/R1-37-tempering.md)); **B** untempered: melted to 45–50 °C and cooled to 31–32 °C with no seed; **C** lost temper: tempered as A, then warmed to **36 °C** for 2 min, then cooled to 31–32 °C with no seed |
| **Dependent** | Test-strip set time at ~20 °C (min); gloss (0–3); snap (0–3); contraction/release from acetate and mould (0–3); fingerprint resistance; bloom score on days 1, 3 and 7 (0–3); photos |
| **Controlled** | One chocolate product, one lot; deposit temperature (31–32 °C for all); disc weight (4 g) and bar weight; same acetate and same mould; same setting place and temperature (15–20 °C); same storage (the same box, side by side, room temperature); same evaluator and light for photos |

## Batch and scale

Dark couverture 55–70 %.

| Variant | What you prepare | Chocolate |
|---|---|---|
| **A + C** | One tempered batch by seeding (R1-37): 225 g melted + 75 g seed (25 %) | 300 g |
| **B** | Melted to 45–50 °C, cooled unseeded | 150 g |
| **Total chocolate** | | **450 g** |

Each variant yields: 1 test strip, **8 discs** of 4 g (32 g), and **one bar** of 60–80 g in a polycarbonate mould (or a 10 × 3 cm rectangle 4–5 mm thick spread on acetate, if you have no mould). The tempered batch is split: ~130 g for A, the rest (~150 g after losses) for C.

**Scale ×2.5:** 750 g for A + C (560 g melted + 190 g seed) and 375 g for B; 20 discs per variant and a full mould. The larger mass holds temperature better, and the tempered batch then behaves more like a professional one.

## Procedure

Label everything before you start: three acetate sheets marked **A**, **B**, **C**; mould cavities marked with tape; three parchment test strips.

1. **Set up** at a room temperature of 18–22 °C, no steam. Record room temperature (and humidity if you can).
2. **B first (it takes longest to cool).** Melt 150 g to **45–50 °C** in a small bowl. Leave it on the bench, stirring gently for a few seconds **only once every 5 min**, until it reaches **31–32 °C** (15–30 min depending on the room). Do not add any chocolate to it; do not spread or table it.
3. **Meanwhile, temper A + C** by seeding (R1-37, steps 1–4): 225 g melted to 45–50 °C, 75 g seed, stirred down to **31–32 °C**; test strip must set in 3–5 min. Record the times.
4. **Deposit A** (at 31–32 °C): test strip, 8 discs on acetate A, the bar. Tap the mould 30 s. Note the time (**t = 0 for A**).
5. **Make C.** Warm the remaining tempered chocolate to **36 °C** (microwave 5 s bursts or bain-marie, stirring; probe). Hold it at 35–36 °C, stirring, for **2 min**. Then let it cool on the bench with the same occasional stirring as B until it is back at **31–32 °C**. Note: it looks exactly like A.
6. **Deposit C** at 31–32 °C: test strip, 8 discs, bar. Note the time (**t = 0 for C**).
7. **Deposit B** when it reaches 31–32 °C: test strip, 8 discs, bar. Note the time (**t = 0 for B**).
8. **Set** all three side by side at 15–20 °C (not the fridge). Record test-strip set times: touch each strip lightly every minute; "set" = no longer tacky and releases from the parchment.
9. **At 1 h:** try to peel discs off the acetate; turn the mould over and tap once. Record release.
10. **At 2–24 h:** score gloss, snap and fingerprint resistance; photograph all three on the same background in the same light.
11. **Store** one disc and part of each bar in a box at room temperature for **7 days**. Photograph and score bloom on **days 1, 3 and 7**. If your room is steady at 16–18 °C, also keep a second set somewhere with temperature swings (a kitchen shelf near the oven) to accelerate bloom.

<div class="callout safety">

**Safety:** Chocolate at 45–50 °C can burn; the microwave heats unevenly: stir between bursts. Samples are edible if handled with clean hands and stored covered, but they have been handled repeatedly for scoring: eat only the untouched ones.

</div>

## Analysis questions

1. All three were deposited at the same temperature. Which ones behaved alike? What does that tell you about using a thermometer as your only check of temper?
2. How much longer did B take to set than A? Relate this to the kinetics of unstable versus stable crystal forms.
3. Did release from acetate and mould correlate with gloss and snap? Explain the link through crystal density and contraction.
4. Did bloom appear on A? If it did, what does your storage log say?
5. What was the practical difference between C and B, if any? What would a professional have done at step 5 instead of cooling C unseeded?
6. How would you redesign the test to measure snap more objectively?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**A (tempered)** sets in **3–5 min** on the test strip; discs peel off the acetate cleanly with a mirror-glossy underside; the bar shrinks visibly from the mould edges and drops out with a tap. Gloss 3, snap 3 (a clean, audible crack at room temperature), no fingerprints after a brief hold. No bloom at 7 days in stable storage (it may show slight bloom if the room swung widely).

**B (untempered)** takes far longer to set: typically **15–30 min or more** at 20 °C, sometimes remaining tacky for an hour. It sets soft, with a matte or satin surface and often greyish streaks or swirls within hours. Discs stick to or tear on the acetate; the bar does not release, or comes out with a dull face and broken edges. Snap 0–1: it bends. Fingerprints appear immediately. Fat bloom (grey, greasy, unaffected by a water drop) is usually visible by day 1–3 and extensive by day 7. It melts in the mouth with less clean feel.

**C (lost temper)** looks exactly like A while liquid at 31–32 °C, but behaves **like B**, or only slightly better: slow set, streaky, poor release, early bloom. At 36 °C the form V seed crystals (melting ~33–34 °C) melted; cooling back to 31–32 °C without new seed restores the temperature but not the crystals. If a few seed crystals survived, C may set a little faster and bloom a little less than B, but it is not reliably tempered.

**Why.** Tempering is about which crystals exist, not about the final temperature ([16.3](stage-1/module-16/lesson-03.md), [SC-13](stage-1/science/sc-13-crystallization.md)). Left alone, cooling chocolate nucleates unstable forms III and IV first; they set slowly into loose, irregular crystals: soft, dull, no contraction, and they convert over days with fat migrating to the surface: bloom. Seeded chocolate contains form V nuclei that template the whole mass into small, dense, uniform crystals: gloss, snap and contraction. Heating above ~34 °C destroys those nuclei. The practical lessons: the test strip is the only proof of temper; never let working chocolate drift above its working temperature; and if it does, re-seed (add fresh tempered chocolate while cooling) instead of just cooling it back.

</details>

## Going further

- Add a **variant D**: untempered chocolate set in the **refrigerator** (fast, cold setting). It usually sets faster than B and can look glossy at first, but it has no snap and blooms fast once back at room temperature.
- **Rescue C** by adding 20 % fresh seed at 34 °C and stirring to 31–32 °C; confirm with a test strip.
- **Milk chocolate** version, working at 29–30 °C: compare bloom timing with dark.
- **Stage 2** replaces the scoring with a temper meter (cooling curve and temper index) and compares seeding with tabling and machines. **Stage 3** measures crystal forms by DSC or X-ray diffraction (in a lab) and runs accelerated bloom tests with temperature cycling.
