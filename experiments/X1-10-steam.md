# X1-10 · Steam: open oven vs added steam vs covered pot

## Question

How much does steam during the first part of the bake change a lean loaf's volume, shape, score opening, crust colour, gloss and crust thickness, and is a covered pot equivalent to added steam in an open oven?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

In your notebook, before baking, write down for each of the three variants (no steam, added steam, covered pot):

- Which will be tallest? Widest? By roughly what percentage will the best differ from the worst?
- Which will have the glossiest crust, and which the thickest?
- Will the covered-pot loaf be closer to the no-steam or the added-steam loaf? Why?

Commit to numbers. The value of the experiment is the gap between your prediction and the result.

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Steam condition in the first 20 min: A no steam (open oven, stone); B added steam (stone + preheated steam tray + 150 g boiling water); C covered pot (preheated cast-iron pot, lid on) |
| **Dependent** | Height, width, height:width ratio, score opening ("ear"), crust colour, gloss, crust thickness, bake loss, crumb notes |
| **Controlled** | Same dough batch; piece weight (340 g); shaping (boule); proof (cold retard, same duration); score (one cut, same depth and angle); oven set temperature and schedule; total bake time; internal temperature at removal; cooling |

## Batch and scale

One batch, divided into three identical pieces. The dough is a lean dough at 68 % hydration.

**Per-variant formula (one piece, 200 g flour basis):**

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams per variant |

| Bread flour (12–13 % protein) | 100 | 200 |

| Fine salt | 2 | 4 |

Divide into **3 × 340 g**; the ~5 g remainder allows for losses to the bowl and bench.

**Scale-up ×2.5** (full-size 850 g loaves, results closer to R1-01): flour 1,500 g, water 1,020 g, salt 30 g, yeast 12 g; total 2,562 g → 3 × 850 g. Bake time increases to about 40–45 min (20 min steam phase, 20–25 min open).

## Procedure

**Day 1 (evening)**

1. Mix to a DDT of 24–25 °C ([01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)): 2 min low, 4–5 min medium in a mixer, or 8–10 min by hand, to moderate development (thin windowpane).
2. Bulk ferment at 24–26 °C for about 2 h with one set of stretch-and-folds at 45 min, until about 1.5–1.75× in volume.
3. Divide into 3 × 340 g (weigh each, record exact weights), pre-shape, rest 15–20 min, shape into tight boules ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)).
4. Place each in a floured basket or a bowl lined with a floured towel. **Label A, B, C.** Cover and refrigerate at 3–5 °C for 12–16 h. Cold retard is what makes this experiment fair: the three loaves are baked up to an hour apart, and in the cold their proof barely changes.

**Day 2**

5. **Oven setup.** Stone on the lower-middle rack. Cast-iron pot (with lid) on the same rack beside the stone if both fit, otherwise on the rack above. Steam tray (heavy pan with lava rocks or a cast-iron pan) on the lowest rack. Preheat at **250 °C (480 °F)** for **60 minutes**. Stone and pot within 10 °C of set point by IR thermometer. Fan off throughout.
6. **Bake order and timing** (so the oven condition is clean for each):

| Bake | Variant | Setup | Schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **C covered pot** and **A no steam**, together | C in the pot, lid on; A on the stone. Steam tray empty and dry | Load both. 20 min at 250 °C; then remove the lid of C; 20 min at 230 °C (445 °F) for both |
| 2 | **B added steam** | B on the stone; pour **150 g boiling water** into the steam tray immediately after loading; close the door | 20 min at 250 °C with steam; then open the door for 5 s and remove the steam tray (mitts); 20 min at 230 °C |

   A and C can share the oven because the pot's steam stays inside the pot. Before bake 2, reheat the oven at 250 °C for 20 minutes after removing the pot, and check the stone temperature again.

7. **Loading each loaf.** Take it from the refrigerator immediately before loading. Turn out onto parchment. Score one straight cut, 1 cm deep, blade at about 30° to the surface, the same length on each loaf (for example 10 cm, marked with a ruler). Record dough surface temperature (IR).
8. **Removal.** At 40 min, check internal temperature (target 96–99 °C). If any loaf is below 96 °C, extend all variants by the same time in their next run; record this.
9. **Cool** on a rack for 2 hours. Weigh each.

## Analysis questions

1. Rank the loaves by height and by H:W ratio. Calculate the percentage difference between the tallest and the shortest.
2. Where did the no-steam loaf expand? Did the score open, or did the loaf burst elsewhere? Relate this to when the crust set.
3. Which loaf had the thickest crust, and on which surface (top, side, base)? Explain using surface drying.
4. Was gloss related to steam? What does gloss tell you about the surface starch?
5. Was the covered pot closer to A or B? What does that tell you about where its steam came from?
6. Was bake loss different between variants? Does a steamed loaf lose more or less water, and why?
7. What would you change to make the added-steam method (B) as effective as the pot in your oven?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Volume and shape.** B and C are clearly taller and rounder than A; in a typical home oven the difference in height is about 10–25 %, with a higher H:W ratio. A often spreads relatively more sideways, its score opens less and may tear at the side or base ("burst") because the dry surface set while the interior was still expanding. C is usually at least as good as B, and often better, especially in gas ovens or vented electric ovens that lose added steam quickly.

**Score and ear.** B and C show a clean, open score and, with a 30° blade angle, a raised ear. A shows a narrower, rough-edged opening.

**Colour and gloss.** A is dull and matt with a floury look, and its crust often browns earlier (the surface dried from the start) but less evenly. B and C are glossy (score 1–2), with a thinner, crackly crust that "sings" as it cools. The gloss is the gelatinized surface starch dried into a thin film; it only forms if the surface was wet and hot.

**Crust thickness.** A usually has the thickest crust, especially on the top and sides (often 3–5 mm versus 2–3 mm), because its drying front started moving inward from minute one instead of minute 20. The bases are similar, being set by the stone or pot base.

**Covered pot.** C behaves like B, not A. The pot's steam comes from the loaf itself: in 20 minutes it releases enough water to saturate the small volume. No added water is needed.

**Bake loss.** Differences are small (often within 1–2 percentage points). A may lose slightly more because it dried from the start; steamed loaves regain a little water by condensation early, then lose it after venting.

**If your results differ.** If B was no better than A, your oven likely vents steam quickly (common in gas ovens and some electric ovens with fans or vents), the steam tray was not hot enough (water should flash to steam audibly), or the door stayed open too long. If C was pale, you may have kept the lid on too long or reduced the temperature too much.

</details>

## Going further

- **Steam duration:** repeat B with steam removed at 5, 12 and 20 min. Where does extra steam stop increasing volume, and when does it start to hurt colour?
- **Loading temperature:** bake one retarded loaf straight from the refrigerator and one that warmed for 60 min at room temperature, both with steam. Compare spring and blistering.
- **Scoring:** repeat A and B with and without a score to separate the effect of steam from the effect of the cut.
- Stage 2 extends this to baguettes in a home oven with combined stone-and-steam setups; Stage 3 to logging surface temperature with a thermocouple to measure when the crust sets in each condition.
