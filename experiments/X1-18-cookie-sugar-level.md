# X1-18 · Sugar level in cookies: −20 % / baseline / +20 %

## Question

When you change the total sugar in the R1-11 cookie dough by 20 % in either direction, keeping everything else the same, how do spread, thickness, colour, texture (on the day and after two days) and perceived sweetness change? Is the effect symmetrical?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Write in your notebook, before mixing:

- Predict the spread ratio (diameter ÷ thickness) of each variant relative to the baseline: higher, lower, by roughly how much?
- Predict which variant is darkest and which is palest at the same bake time.
- Predict the texture of each on day 0 and on day 2 (crisp, chewy, cakey, dry, hard).
- Predict whether the −20 % cookie will taste 20 % less sweet.

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Total sugar as % of flour: **L** 80 % (−20 %), **B** 100 % (baseline), **H** 120 % (+20 %); brown : white ratio held at ~56 : 44 |
| **Dependent** | Diameter, centre thickness, spread ratio; edge and centre colour; texture on day 0 and day 2 (bend/snap test, bite); weight loss in baking; sweetness ranking |
| **Controlled** | Flour, butter, egg, soda and salt weights; butter temperature (18–20 °C); creaming time and speed; mixing time after flour; dough rest (same time, 3–5 °C); portion weight (45 g); tray type and lining; oven temperature, rack and bake time (fixed at 11 min); storage for day 2; evaluator |

**Why no chocolate:** chocolate pieces interrupt spread unevenly and add their own colour and sweetness. Leaving them out makes the dough itself the thing you measure. Add them back once you know the dough effect.

**Portions:** 6 × 45 g per variant (18 cookies), with 10–40 g left over per variant (more for H) that you bake as testers and exclude from the data.

**Scale ×2.5:** each variant becomes 250 g flour, 190 g butter, 95 g egg, 2 g soda, 4 g salt, with sugars L 112.5 + 87.5 g, B 140 + 110 g, H 167.5 + 132.5 g. That is the full R1-11 batch without chocolate; bake 12 cookies per variant for a more reliable average.

## Procedure

**Day 1: mix and rest** (about 1 h)

1. Label three bowls and three containers **L**, **B**, **H**. Bring butter to 18–20 °C (probe the block) and eggs to 18–22 °C. Beat the eggs and weigh 38 g per variant.
2. For each variant, whisk flour, soda and salt in a small bowl.
3. Mix the variants **one after the other, in the order B, L, H**, with the same tool (a hand mixer is best at this size):
   - cream butter + both sugars, medium speed, **2 min** exactly (timer), scrape once;
   - add the egg in two parts, 30 s each;
   - add the dry mix and mix on low for **20 s**, then finish with a spatula until no dry flour shows.
4. Probe each dough and record its temperature (target 20–23 °C).
5. Portion 6 × 45 g ± 0.5 g per variant, roll into balls, then shape each slightly taller than wide. Put each variant in its labelled container, cover, and refrigerate at 3–5 °C for **18–24 h**. Record the time.

**Day 2: bake** (about 1.5 h)

6. Preheat to **180 °C (355 °F)**, no fan, rack in the middle, for 30 min; check with an oven thermometer.
7. Prepare three light aluminium trays with parchment. On each tray, place **2 balls of each variant** (6 per tray) in two rows of three, rotating positions from tray to tray so every variant appears at the front, middle and back:

| Tray | Front row (left → right) | Back row (left → right) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | L · B · H | H · L · B |
| 2 | B · H · L | L · B · H |
| 3 | H · L · B | B · H · L |

Mark the parchment under each ball with the variant letter in pencil.

8. Weigh each loaded tray (with parchment). Bake one tray at a time, straight from the refrigerator, for **11 min exactly**. Do not adjust time by variant: colour is one of the things you are measuring.
9. Leave on the tray 5 min, then weigh the tray again (for bake loss), then move cookies to a rack **keeping them on their labelled parchment**.
10. Let the oven recover 5 min between trays, and use a cool tray each time.

**Day 2 evaluation** (after 30 min cooling)

11. Measure every cookie (see Measurements). Photograph all 18 on white paper with a ruler, grouped by variant.
12. From each variant, use **2 cookies** for the day-0 bend test and tasting. Put **2 cookies** of each variant into one airtight container per variant for day 4 (day 2 after baking). The remaining 2 per variant are spares.

**Day 4** (48 h after baking)

13. Repeat the bend test and tasting on the stored cookies.

## Measurements

| What | How | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter (mm) | Two readings at right angles; average | Ruler or calliper |
| Thickness (mm) | At the centre; for a ruler, lay a card on top and measure its height at the centre | Calliper or ruler + card |
| Spread ratio | Mean diameter ÷ thickness | Calculation |
| Bake loss (%) | (Tray weight before − after) ÷ dough weight × 100, per tray | Scale |
| Edge and centre colour | Score 1 (pale cream) to 5 (dark brown) against your photo scale; same light | Eye, photo |
| Bend test | Hold the cookie at both ends and bend gently: **snaps** / **bends then breaks** / **bends and stays** / **crumbles** | Hands |
| Texture | Bite at edge and centre: crisp, chewy, cakey, dry, hard | Mouth |
| Sweetness | Rank 1–3 blind (ask someone to code the samples); drink water between samples | Mouth |

## Analysis questions

1. Plot mean spread ratio against sugar % (80, 100, 120). Is the relationship roughly linear, or does one step matter more than the other?
2. Compare the range of spread ratios within each variant with the differences between variants. Are the differences real, or within the noise?
3. Which variant was darkest? Which of the browning levers from [07.2](stage-1/module-07/lesson-02.md) did the sugar change?
4. Describe the change in texture from day 0 to day 2 for each variant. Which staled fastest, and why, in terms of water and sugar?
5. Did the blind sweetness ranking match the sugar order? Was L obviously "20 % less sweet"?
6. You portioned by weight (45 g). How much flour does each L, B and H cookie contain? How could that difference affect your comparison?
7. If you had to make a 20 % reduced-sugar cookie that spreads like B, which two changes would you test first?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Spread.** Spread increases with sugar. H is visibly wider and thinner than B, with a flatter profile and more crackled top; L is narrower and thicker, more domed, with a smoother, sometimes matt surface. A typical outcome is a spread ratio for L around 15–25 % lower than B and for H 10–25 % higher, but your numbers depend on flour, oven and rest. The step down (B → L) often looks larger than the step up, because L runs short of dissolved sugar to liquefy the dough while H is already fluid and its edges begin to brown and set.

**Colour.** H is darkest at the edges and on the base; L is palest. More sugar at the surface feeds Maillard browning and caramelization, and H's thinner edges dry and heat faster.

**Texture.** Day 0: L is soft but cakey or bread-like, with a fine, even crumb; B has crisp edges and a chewy centre; H has crisper, sometimes lacy edges and a chewy-to-sticky centre, and is more fragile. Day 2: L has become dry and firm (it bends little and crumbles), because it has more free water available for starch to retrograde and less sugar to hold moisture. B has firmed somewhat but still bends. H's edges remain crisp and the centre is still chewy or has become harder where sucrose recrystallized.

**Sweetness.** The blind ranking usually follows the sugar order, but tasters rarely perceive L as 20 % less sweet: perceived sweetness is not proportional to concentration, and salt, butter and browning flavours mask the difference. That gap between "chemical" and "perceived" change is exactly why sugar is reduced in industry with sensory testing, not arithmetic.

**Flour per cookie.** At 45 g portions, L has 15.2 g flour per cookie, B 14.2 g and H 13.4 g (45 × 100 ÷ total %). Part of L's thickness and cakiness comes from carrying more flour per cookie. This is a real confound of portioning by weight; the alternative (portioning by equal flour content) would give different cookie sizes. Neither is wrong; state which you used.

**If your results differ.** Little difference between variants: check that the soda and sugars were weighed to 0.1 g and that the doughs had the same temperature at oven entry. Large scatter within a variant: tray position or uneven ball shapes. Compare front-row and back-row cookies in your table.

</details>

## Going further

- Extend the series to 60 % and 140 % sugar and find where the H end starts to lose shape (lacy, holes) and where the L end becomes a scone-like biscuit.
- Repeat L with the missing sugar replaced by 10 g glycerol or 10 g invert syrup and see which of B's properties return.
- Bake B and H at 170 °C and 190 °C to see whether temperature can make H spread like B.
- Stage 3: measure water activity and break force over 7 days, and run a proper triangle test for sweetness.
