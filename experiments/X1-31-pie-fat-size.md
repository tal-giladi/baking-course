# X1-31 · Fat piece size in pie dough

## Question

In an all-butter pie dough with a fixed formula, how does the size of the butter pieces left after mixing (fine breadcrumbs, pea-sized, large flattened flakes) change flakiness, height, tenderness, shrinkage and butter leakage?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Before mixing, write down:

- Rank the three variants by baked height. Estimate the height of each from a 3 mm rolled sheet.
- Which will show distinct layers when broken? Which will be the most tender?
- Which will leak the most butter onto the parchment? Which will shrink most?
- Which would you use for the bottom crust of a custard pie, and which for the top crust of an apple pie?

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Butter piece size at the end of cutting in: **F** fine (rubbed to breadcrumbs, no piece > 2 mm); **P** pea-sized (5–8 mm, flattened); **L** large (15–25 mm, walnut-sized pieces pressed into flat flakes) |
| **Dependent** | Baked height (mm); number of visible layers; flakiness and tenderness (panel); shrinkage of squares (%); butter leakage (grease ring on parchment); bake loss (%); handling |
| **Controlled** | Formula and ingredient lots; butter temperature at start (4–7 °C); water amount (28 %) and temperature (1–4 °C); one letter fold for all; rest (2 h); rolled thickness (3 mm guides); cut size (8 × 8 cm); chill before baking; oven, tray, bake time |

## Batch and scale

Mix each variant separately in its own bowl (dry mix can be made as one batch of 459 g flour + salt and divided into 3 × 153 g).

**Scale-up ×2.5** (one full double-crust pie per variant, as in R1-22): per variant 375 g flour, 262.5 g butter, 7.5 g salt, 105 g ice water (750 g).

## Procedure

**Mixing (about 40 minutes)**

1. Cut all the butter: for F and P, 1.5 cm cubes; for L, 2.5 cm cubes. Refrigerate or freeze briefly to **4–7 °C**. Record temperature.
2. **F (fine).** Rub the butter into the flour and salt with fingertips until the mixture looks like fine breadcrumbs with **no visible butter pieces larger than 2 mm**. Work quickly; chill 10 min if the butter becomes soft.
3. **P (pea-sized).** Toss the cubes in the flour and press each flat between fingers, working until the pieces are **5–8 mm**, flattened.
4. **L (large).** Toss the cubes in the flour and press each one once between palms into a flat flake about 2–3 cm across. **Pieces 15–25 mm.**
5. Photograph each bowl next to a ruler before adding water.
6. Add **42 g ice water** to each, tossing with a fork. Squeeze-test. **Do not adjust water** unless a variant will not come together at all; if you must, add 5 g, record it and treat it as part of the result.
7. Gather each into a rough rectangle, roll to about 1.5 cm, give **one letter fold** (all three variants), flatten to a 2 cm square, wrap and **label F, P, L**. Stagger variants by about 10 minutes; note wrapping times.
8. Refrigerate each **2 hours from its own wrapping time**.

**Rolling, cutting, baking**

9. Roll each to **3 mm with guides** on a lightly floured bench. Score handling. Cut **4 squares of 8 × 8 cm** with a ruler and knife (straight down, no dragging). Weigh the four squares together (raw weight).
10. Place each variant's squares on its own labelled strip of parchment on one tray (or two trays on the same rack). Freeze **20 minutes** once all are cut.
11. Preheat the oven to **200 °C (390 °F)** still, 190 °C fan, rack in the middle, with the tray preheated if possible.
12. Bake all together **20–25 minutes**, rotating at 12 minutes, until the P squares are evenly golden. Remove all at the same time.
13. Cool on the parchment 10 minutes (so the grease ring can be measured), then on a rack 30 minutes.

## Analysis questions

1. Plot mean height and layer count against butter piece size. Is the relation linear, or does it level off?
2. Rank the variants by tenderness (panel) and by flakiness. Where is the trade-off?
3. Which variant shrank most? Relate that to how much flour was left uncoated by fat.
4. Which variant leaked most butter? What does that suggest about how the largest pieces behave in the oven?
5. Which variant would you use for (a) a custard pie base, (b) a fruit pie top, (c) an all-purpose double crust? Would a hybrid (half F, half P) combine the advantages?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Height and layers.** F rises least (typically only to about 4–5 mm from a 3 mm sheet) and shows no real layers: it breaks with a fine, even, sandy crumb. P rises clearly (often 6–9 mm) with several irregular layers. L rises most in places (often 8–12 mm or more) but unevenly, with large blisters and pockets and a few thick layers.

**Tenderness and flakiness.** F is the most tender and crumbly (mealy); P is tender and flaky, usually the panel favourite; L is the flakiest but slightly tougher and crunchier between the layers.

**Shrinkage.** F usually shrinks least. L tends to shrink most, because large butter pieces coat less flour, so more flour meets the water and forms gluten, which recoils in the oven. Differences of a few percent (for example 2–3 % vs 5–8 %) are typical.

**Butter leakage.** L leaks most: large pieces melt into pools that run out of the pastry before the surrounding dough sets. F leaks least, because its fat is dispersed and held in the fat-coated crumb.

**Why.** Solid pieces of butter act as barriers between thin sheets of dough. In the oven the dough sets while the butter melts and its water, plus water from the dough, flashes to steam that separates the sheets: the bigger and flatter the pieces, the fewer and thicker the barriers and the more steam in each pocket. Fine butter coats the flour instead: very tender, but there is nothing to separate layers ([11.3](stage-1/module-11/lesson-03.md)).

**If your results differ.** If P and L look similar to F, the butter softened and smeared during mixing or rolling (check your temperatures). If F would not hold together, fat-coated flour absorbs less water; that is expected, and a mealy dough is normally made with 20–25 % water.

</details>

## Going further

- **Wet-base test:** bake 8 cm squares of F and P under 30 g of a thickened fruit filling in small foil cups; compare sogginess after 4 hours.
- **Folds:** repeat P with 0, 1 and 3 letter folds.
- **Fat type:** P with all butter vs 50 % butter and 50 % shortening (wider plastic range).
- Stage 2 carries this into rough puff and blitz puff pastry; Stage 3 uses image analysis of cross-sections to quantify layer count and thickness.
