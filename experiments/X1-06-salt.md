# X1-06 · Salt: 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 %

## Question

How does salt level change dough strength and stickiness, fermentation speed, loaf volume, crust colour and flavour?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Before mixing, write down:

- Which variant will rise fastest in the container, and which slowest.
- Which loaf will be largest on a fixed schedule — and whether that is the same as the fastest-rising dough.
- How crust colour will change from 0 % to 3 %.
- The lowest salt level you think you will find acceptable to eat.

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| Independent | Salt: 0, 1, 2, 3 % of flour weight |
| Dependent | Dough feel immediately after mixing (stickiness, strength); container rise over time; shaping behaviour; loaf volume and shape; crust colour; crumb; flavour in blind tasting |
| Controlled | One flour; hydration 65 %; instant yeast 1 %; same salt (fine, non-iodized, one packet); dough temperature 25 ± 1 °C; mixing method and time; one fold at 45 min; bulk 90 min at 24–26 °C; shaping; proof 45 min; pans; oven and bake time |

## Equipment

0.1 g scale; four bowls; four identical 1 L straight-sided containers with tape marks; four identical small loaf pans about 15 × 8 cm (or parchment for boules); probe thermometer; timer; ruler; seeds for volume (optional); four small plates and a helper for the blind tasting.

## Procedure

Start variants **5 minutes apart** and keep the offset for every step. All four fit in most home ovens in small pans.

1. **Label** bowls, containers and pans S0, S1, S2, S3. Have a helper also write a random code (e.g. 17, 42, 58, 93) on the underside of each pan and keep the key hidden until after tasting.
2. **Mix:** whisk flour, salt and yeast together; add water (temperature computed for 25 °C, [01.3](stage-1/module-01/lesson-03.md)); stir 1 minute until no dry flour remains.
3. **Knead** by push-and-fold on an unfloured bench for **8 minutes** at a steady rhythm (or, at ×2.5, 2 min low + 4 min medium-low in a stand mixer).
4. **Immediately:** measure dough temperature; score stickiness and strength; do the 20 g windowpane test.
5. **Bulk 90 minutes** at 24–26 °C in the marked container; **one stretch-and-fold set at 45 minutes**. Mark height every 30 minutes.
6. **Shape** into a log with the same number of movements; place in a greased pan. Note how the dough behaves (tension, tearing, stickiness).
7. **Proof 45 minutes**, covered, at 24–26 °C. Record the poke test for each: pressed 1 cm, does the dent spring back quickly (under-proofed), slowly and partly (ready), or not at all (over-proofed)? Preheat oven to 220 °C (430 °F).
8. **Bake** all together at 220 °C for 25–30 minutes to an internal temperature ≥ 96 °C. Unmould, weigh, cool 1 hour.
9. **Measure** height and volume; score crust colour against each other in daylight; cut and photograph.
10. **Blind tasting:** helper serves a 1 cm slice of each, identified only by code, in a random order. Taster rates saltiness (1–5) and overall flavour (1–5), and ranks preference. Drink water between samples. Then reveal the key.

## Analysis questions

1. Plot container rise at 60 and 90 minutes against salt %. Describe the shape of the curve.
2. Was the fastest-rising dough also the largest loaf? Use the poke test results to explain.
3. Relate dough stickiness and strength right after mixing to the gluten mechanism in [02.2](stage-1/module-02/lesson-02.md). Why did this effect appear within minutes, while the fermentation effect needed an hour to see?
4. Explain the order of crust colours.
5. Where did your blind tasting place the acceptable range? How does it compare with the 1.8–2.2 % professional standard?
6. If you had to reduce salt to 1.5 % for a customer, which two process changes would you test to recover strength and flavour?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

| | S0 | S1 | S2 | S3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dough after mixing | Slack, sticky, extensible, weak | Slightly slack | Smooth, balanced | Tight, dry-feeling, strong, less extensible |
| Container rise | Fastest | Fast | Standard | Slowest — noticeably behind |
| Proof at bake (fixed schedule) | Often over-proofed: weak poke recovery | Ready to slightly over | Ready | Under-proofed |
| Loaf | Can be large but may flatten, collapse at the top or have a coarse, irregular crumb | Good volume | Good volume, domed, even | Smallest, dense, tight |
| Crust colour | Palest | | | Darkest |
| Flavour | Flat, bland, "cardboard" | Under-seasoned | Balanced | Noticeably salty |

**Gluten effect (fast).** Salt ions screen charges on the gluten proteins, so they associate more closely; the network tightens and becomes less sticky within a minute or two of mixing. Without salt the dough is slack and sticky from the start.

**Fermentation effect (slow).** Dissolved salt pulls water out of yeast cells by osmosis and slows their metabolism. The difference accumulates, so it becomes obvious only after 30–60 minutes. Between 0 % and 3 % the rise at 90 minutes can differ by a factor of about two, depending on your yeast and temperature.

**Fastest ≠ largest.** On a fixed schedule, S0 reaches and passes optimal proof first and has the weakest network to hold the gas, so it often loses volume or shape; S3 is under-proofed and tight. S2 (and often S1) hit the best combination of strength and proof.

**Crust colour.** Slower fermentation leaves more residual sugar in the dough at baking time, feeding browning reactions ([04.2](stage-1/module-04/lesson-02.md)); faster fermentation consumes it. So the order usually runs palest at 0 % to darkest at 3 %.

**Flavour.** Most tasters find 0 % unacceptable and 3 % too salty; preference clusters around 1.8–2.2 %, which is why it is the professional standard. For salt reduction, test a longer, cooler fermentation or a preferment for flavour, and an extra fold or slightly lower hydration for strength ([02.4](stage-1/module-02/lesson-04.md)).

</details>

## Going further

- Proof each variant to the same container rise (e.g. +75 %) instead of the same time and record the time; this separates the fermentation effect from the gluten effect.
- Compare fine sea salt, kosher salt and iodized salt at 2 % by weight (blind taste).
- Salt timing: 2 % added at the start vs after a 30-minute autolyse vs after the first fold.
