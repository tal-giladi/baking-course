# X1-11 · Bake time and internal temperature: crust and crumb series

## Question

As bake time increases for identical pieces of lean dough, how do internal temperature, bake loss, crust colour, crust thickness and crumb texture change, and at what point is the bread "done"? Is internal temperature alone a sufficient doneness test?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Write in your notebook, before baking:

- Sketch a graph of internal temperature (y) against bake time (x) for these pieces. Where will it flatten, and at what temperature?
- Sketch bake loss (%) against bake time on the same x-axis. Will it flatten at the same time?
- At which pull time do you expect the crumb to stop being gummy? At which will the crust first look "done"?

## Variables

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Bake time: pieces pulled at 12, 16, 20, 25 and 32 min |
| **Dependent** | Internal temperature at removal; weight out of oven and after 1 h; bake loss %; crust colour; crust thickness; crumb gumminess; crumb springback; sound |
| **Controlled** | One dough batch; piece weight (200 g); shape (small boule); proof (same time, same place); oven temperature schedule; steam method and duration; position on the stone (record it); thermometer; cooling conditions |

## Batch and scale

Lean dough at 68 % hydration, divided into five identical 200 g pieces.

**Scale-up ×2.5:** flour 1,500 g, water 1,020 g, salt 30 g, yeast 12 g (2,562 g) → 5 × 500 g loaves. Pull at 20, 26, 32, 40 and 50 min. Larger pieces show the temperature lag and the gap between "set" and "baked" even more clearly.

## Procedure

1. **Mix** to a DDT of 24–25 °C, moderate development (as [X1-10](experiments/X1-10-steam.md) step 1).
2. **Bulk** 1.5–2 h at 24–26 °C until about 1.5–1.75× volume, one set of folds at 45 min.
3. **Divide** into 5 × 200 g on the scale, recording each exact weight to 0.1 g if possible. Pre-shape, rest 15 min, shape into tight small boules.
4. **Label.** Place each on its own square of parchment labelled **P12, P16, P20, P25, P32** (pull time in minutes). Arrange them on a peel or rimless tray in a pattern you record, e.g. a row front to back, so each piece's oven position is known.
5. **Proof** covered at 24–26 °C for 45–60 min, until a floured finger dent springs back slowly and incompletely ([03.4](stage-1/module-03/lesson-04.md)). All pieces proof together.
6. **Oven.** Stone on the lower-middle rack, steam tray on the lowest rack, preheat at **240 °C (465 °F)** for 60 min. Fan off.
7. **Load** all five at once, 5 cm apart, and score each with the same 5 cm cut. Pour **100 g boiling water** into the steam tray. Close the door and start a timer. Reduce to **230 °C (445 °F)**.
8. **Vent steam at 8 min:** open the door for 5 s, remove the steam tray.
9. **Pull pieces** at 12, 16, 20, 25 and 32 min. Work fast: door open under 10 s each time. For each piece, immediately:
   - Insert a probe thermometer into the centre through the base and record the peak reading (within 20 s).
   - Weigh it (weight out of oven).
   - Tap the base and note the sound (dull / slightly hollow / hollow).
   - Put it on a rack.
10. **Cool** all pieces for **1 hour** from their own pull time, then weigh again.
11. **Cut** each piece through the centre after 1 hour of cooling. Photograph all five cut faces side by side, with the pull time label.

## Measurements

| What | How | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Internal temperature | Centre, through the base, peak reading | Probe thermometer, ±1 °C (calibrated in [X1-02](experiments/X1-02-calibration.md)) |
| Weight (dough, out of oven, after 1 h) | On the scale, on its parchment (tare the parchment) | Scale, 0.1–1 g |
| Bake loss | (dough − 1 h weight) ÷ dough × 100 | Calculation |
| Cooling loss | (out-of-oven − 1 h weight) ÷ dough × 100 | Calculation |
| Crust colour | Match to a 1–5 colour reference (1 = pale cream, 3 = golden brown, 5 = deep mahogany); photograph all pieces together in daylight | Photo + reference card |
| Crust thickness | On the cut face, top and base | Calipers or ruler, mm |
| Crumb gumminess | Press a 2 cm cube of centre crumb between fingers: does it spring back, or stay compressed and sticky? Score 0 (clean, springy) to 3 (wet, dense, sticky) | Fingers, consistent pressure |
| Taste | Crust and crumb notes: raw flour, wheaty, toasty, bitter | Tasting, record words |

## Analysis questions

1. At what time did internal temperature stop rising meaningfully? What value did it plateau at?
2. Did bake loss plateau at the same time? What was it doing while the temperature was flat?
3. Which was the first piece with a non-gummy crumb (score 0–1)? What was its internal temperature?
4. Which piece would you call done? Justify it with at least three measurements.
5. Which piece had a crust colour you would call ideal? Was it the same piece as in question 4? What would you change (temperature or time) to make colour and doneness coincide?
6. How much weight did the pieces lose during cooling alone? Was it larger for the earlier or the later pulls?
7. Could you have judged doneness by sound? Where did it mislead you?

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Internal temperature** rises fast at first, then plateaus: typically the 12-minute piece is somewhere around 88–94 °C, the 16-minute piece around 95–97 °C, and from 20 minutes on all pieces read 97–99 °C. Small pieces reach the plateau early because the distance from surface to centre is short. The exact numbers depend on piece shape and your oven.

**Bake loss keeps climbing** after temperature plateaus. Typical small-piece values: roughly 7–10 % at 12 min, 10–13 % at 16, 12–15 % at 20, 14–18 % at 25 and 17–22 % at 32. The flat temperature curve hides steady evaporation: the energy goes into turning water into steam, not into heating the crumb.

**Crumb.** P12 is gummy and compresses into a sticky wad (score 2–3); P16 is borderline (1–2), especially near the base; P20 and later are clean and springy (0). P32 may taste and feel slightly dry.

**Crust.** Colour runs from pale (1–2) at 12 min to deep brown (4–5) at 32 min. Crust thickness grows with time, often from about 1 mm to 3–4 mm. Sound is hollow for all pieces from about 16 min, so it cannot separate a borderline piece from a good one.

**The lesson.** A small lean piece is "set" (crumb above 96 °C) at around 16–20 min but its crust and flavour are better at 20–25 min. Internal temperature is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one: combine it with bake loss (≥ ~12–13 % for pieces this size) and crust colour. If colour arrives before bake loss is sufficient, lower the oven temperature and bake longer; if the crumb is set long before the crust has colour, raise the temperature or check for overproof (low sugar).

**Cooling loss** is usually 1–3 % of dough weight in the first hour, larger for the earlier pulls because their crumb still held more water to evaporate.

</details>

## Going further

- Repeat with the oven at 210 °C and 250 °C and compare the times at which colour and doneness coincide.
- Repeat with an enriched dough (after M06) and find where its temperature plateau and gumminess threshold lie; compare with the 88–93 °C enriched target.
- With a leave-in probe, log one full-size loaf's centre temperature every minute and fit the curve. Stage 3 uses this kind of log to model heat penetration and set bake profiles by loaf mass.
