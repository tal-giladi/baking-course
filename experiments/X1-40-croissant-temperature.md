# X1-40 · Croissant proof temperature and butter temperature

## Question

Croissant butter must stay solid and plastic through the proof and continuous through the lamination. This experiment has two parts, each with one independent variable:

- **Part A (core):** from one batch of laminated dough, how does proofing at **24–26 °C** versus **30–32 °C** change proof time, butter leakage, height, and the honeycomb crumb?
- **Part B (optional, second session):** from one détrempe, how does the **butter temperature at lock-in** (about 7 °C, 14 °C and 20 °C) change the lamination and the baked croissant?

## Before you start: write your hypothesis

Before you begin, write down:

- Part A: how much faster you expect the warm proof to be; which group will leave more fat on the tray, by roughly how much; how the cross-sections will differ.
- Part A: at what temperature butter begins to visibly soften and seep, from [SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md) and [15.3](stage-1/module-15/lesson-03.md).
- Part B: what you expect to see and hear during the lock-in and first turn for each butter temperature, and which variant will have the best honeycomb.
- Part B: whether cold butter or warm butter will leak more, and why they might both leak.

## Variables

**Part A**

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Proof temperature: **P25** (24–26 °C) vs **P31** (30–32 °C), both at 75–85 % relative humidity |
| **Dependent** | Proof time to endpoint; butter visible on the foil after the proof; fat on the foil after baking (g); baked height, length and weight; bake loss (%); cross-section honeycomb score; panel: flakiness, greasiness |
| **Controlled** | One batch of dough, laminated once; same triangles (dimensions and weight ± 3 g); same shaping; same egg wash; **same proof endpoint** (2–2.5× volume with jiggle), not the same time; same humidity method; same oven, temperature, time and tray positions |

**Part B**

| Type | Variable |
|---|---|
| **Independent** | Butter plaque temperature at lock-in: **B7** (6–8 °C, straight from the refrigerator after beating), **B14** (13–15 °C, control), **B20** (19–21 °C) |
| **Dependent** | Behaviour during lock-in and turns (cracking, smearing, butter showing through); block temperatures; cross-section of the raw block; baked height; fat on the foil; honeycomb score |
| **Controlled** | One détrempe divided in three; détrempe temperature at lock-in (4–8 °C); plaque size and weight; lock-in method; 1 double + 1 single; identical rests (30 min after the double, 1 h after the single); sheet thickness; triangle size; proof at 24–26 °C to the same endpoint; bake together |

Proofing to the same **endpoint** isolates the effect of temperature on the butter. If you proofed both groups for the same time, P31 would also be more proofed, and you would be testing two things at once.

## Procedure

### Part A (two days)

1. **Day 1.** Make the détrempe and plaque as R1-34 steps 1–4, at ×0.5. Prepare two proofing boxes (lidded plastic boxes) and test them tonight or tomorrow morning:
   - **P25:** room or box at 24–26 °C, with a cup of warm water for humidity.
   - **P31:** oven off with the light on, or a box with a hot-water bottle, and a cup of hot water. Adjust until it holds **30–32 °C** for an hour. Put a thermometer in each (a hygrometer too if you have one).
2. **Day 2.** Laminate as R1-34 steps 5–8 (1 double + 1 single), recording block temperatures. Rest 1 h.
3. Sheet the whole ×0.5 block to about 26 × 52 cm, 4 mm; trim to 25 cm wide; cut 9 triangles. **Weigh every triangle**; allocate them so the two groups have the same mean weight (alternate heavier and lighter). Shape all 9 identically.
4. Place each croissant on its own **pre-weighed foil square** (record foil weights to 0.1 g; write the ID on the foil corner with a marker). Put the foils on two trays: **P25** and **P31**. Photograph each group from the side with a ruler (proof start).
5. **Stagger the start.** Put P25 in its box immediately. Keep P31 covered in the refrigerator and start its proof **45–60 minutes later**, so the two groups reach their endpoint at about the same time. Record start times.
6. Egg-wash all lightly at the start of their proof.
7. Check every 20 minutes after the first hour. **Endpoint for each group:** 2–2.5× volume (compare with the start photo), jiggle, layers visible. Record the time. Photograph the foil around each P31 piece at the endpoint: look for a sheen or droplets of butter.
8. Preheat the oven (R1-34 step 14) to reach temperature 20 minutes before the first group is ready. If both groups finish within 15 minutes of each other, hold the earlier group at room temperature (about 22 °C) and bake together, alternating P25 and P31 positions on each tray. If not, bake each group when it is ready, at identical settings, and note it.
9. Second egg wash; bake at 190 °C fan (200 °C conventional) for 18 minutes; remove all at the same time.
10. Cool 10 minutes on the foils. Lift each croissant off its foil with a spatula, brushing any crumbs back onto the croissant, not the foil. **Weigh each foil** (fat collected = foil after − foil before). Weigh each croissant.
11. After 30 minutes, measure height and length; cut two croissants from each group lengthwise and photograph the cross-sections with a ruler. Score honeycomb (below). Panel-taste the other two from each group, blind.

### Part B (two days; optional)

1. **Day 1.** Mix one détrempe (793 g) and divide into three 264 g pieces; flatten each to 12 × 15 cm, 1.5 cm, and retard. Make three 82.5 g plaques, 11 × 11 cm, labelled **B7, B14, B20**.
2. **Day 2.** Prepare the plaques to temperature, checking with the probe pushed into the edge of the plaque:
   - **B7:** straight from the refrigerator after beating; 6–8 °C.
   - **B14:** 10–20 minutes at room temperature; 13–15 °C (bends without cracking).
   - **B20:** 30–60 minutes at room temperature, or a few minutes in a warm place; 19–21 °C (soft, starting to look shiny).
3. Laminate the three variants **one after the other**, each with a détrempe straight from the refrigerator (4–8 °C): roll to 11.5 × 23 cm, open lock-in, **double turn** (roll to about 11 × 40 cm), rest 30 minutes, **single turn** (to about 11 × 33 cm), rest 1 hour. Record for each: sound and feel during the first roll (cracking, snapping, butter smearing or squeezing out of the edges, butter showing through the dough); block temperature before and after each turn.
4. Before sheeting, cut a 1 cm slice off the end of each block and photograph the cut face (raw cross-section).
5. Sheet each to 4 mm (about 25 × 33 cm), cut triangles, keep **4 per variant**, weigh them, shape, place on pre-weighed foils.
6. Proof all together at **24–26 °C** to the endpoint (they may differ slightly: record each).
7. Bake together, positions alternated, as in Part A steps 9–11.

## Analysis questions

1. **Part A.** How much faster was P31? Is the ratio of proof times consistent with what you know about yeast activity between 25 and 31 °C ([03.3](stage-1/module-03/lesson-03.md))?
2. How many times more fat did P31 leave on the foil? When did it leave: during the proof, the bake, or both? What does this say about the butter's state at 31 °C?
3. Compare the cross-sections. Where in the croissant are the differences largest: outer layers or core? Why?
4. A baker says: "My croissants leak because the oven is too hot." Using your data, what would you ask them to measure first?
5. **Part B.** Describe the failure mode of B7 and of B20 in one sentence each. Did they both leak? Through which mechanism in each case?
6. The control B14: did its block stay within 10–15 °C during the turns? If not, was its result compromised?
7. Combine A and B: sketch a "temperature budget" for a croissant, from lock-in to oven, listing the maximum butter temperature allowed at each stage.

<details class="answer"><summary>What you should expect to see</summary>

**Part A: proof time.** P31 typically reaches the endpoint in roughly half to two-thirds of the P25 time (for example 70–90 minutes against 2–2.5 hours from cold dough). Yeast is considerably more active at 31 °C.

**Part A: butter.** P31 usually shows a sheen or small droplets of butter on the foil **before** baking, especially at the ends and along the cut edges of the steps. After baking, P31 leaves clearly more fat on the foil: often several times as much as P25. P25 should leave little more than a thin film. At 30–32 °C, much of the butterfat is liquid ([SC-04](stage-1/science/sc-04-lipids.md)); the butter layers lose their plastic solidity, merge with the dough and seep out through the open edges. The effect is strongest in the thin outer layers and the tips, which warm first.

**Part A: structure.** P31 croissants are usually lower and slightly wider (they spread), with less distinct steps. Their cross-section is finer, with smaller cells and bready or cottony zones, especially towards the centre, where layers that fused in the proof bake as one dough. Their bases are greasier and sometimes darker (fried in the leaked fat). The panel usually finds P31 less flaky and greasier. P25 shows the open honeycomb of R1-34.

**Part B.** **B7:** the butter cracks audibly and breaks into plates during the lock-in and the double turn; you can see or feel pieces under the dough, and the raw block's cut face shows broken, discontinuous butter lines with gaps. Baked: irregular rise, some big holes next to dense fused zones, leakage through the gaps. **B20:** the butter squeezes out of the ends during lock-in, sticks, and shows through the dough; the block feels soft and greasy and warms above 16 °C during the first turn. The raw cut face shows smeared, blurred lines. Baked: lower, bready crumb with few distinct layers, and leakage (free fat that was never held in layers). **B14** gives the best honeycomb and least leakage. Both extremes leak, by different mechanisms: B7 through broken barriers, B20 through fat already worked out of its layers.

**The lesson.** Butter temperature has a narrow window at every stage: about 12–15 °C at lock-in, a 10–15 °C block during turns, and a proof ceiling of about 27–28 °C. Leaving it on either side destroys layers. Leakage alone does not tell you which side you left: the lamination notes, the raw cross-section and the proof log do.

**If your results differ.** If P31 and P25 look the same, check the P31 box really held 30–32 °C (many ovens with the light on reach only 27–29 °C) and that humidity was similar. If everything leaked, check the oven temperature with a thermometer; a cool oven makes all groups leak and masks the proof effect.

</details>

## Going further

- Add a third proof temperature (27–28 °C) to locate the threshold more precisely.
- Proof to equal **time** instead of equal endpoint, and separate the proof-level effect from the butter effect.
- Repeat Part A with laminating butter (beurre de tourage) or a higher-fat butter and test whether the threshold moves.
- Stage 2 extends this to retarded proofs and freezing; Stage 3 measures butter solid fat content across temperature and relates it to leakage and layer retention.
