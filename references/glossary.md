# Glossary

Every key term defined in the Stage 1 modules, alphabetically, with the module where it is taught. Where a term is defined in more than one module, the first definition is shown and all modules are linked.

<div class="bk-filter" data-placeholder="Search the glossary..."></div>

| Term | Definition | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | How close a measurement is to the true value; never better than resolution. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Active dry yeast** | Dried yeast in larger granules, traditionally rehydrated in warm water first. About 0.4–0.5 of the fresh-yeast weight. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Albumen** | Egg white: about 88 % water and 10–11 % protein, roughly two thirds of the edible egg. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Alpha-amylase** | Enzyme that cuts starch chains internally, creating dextrins and new chain ends. Usually the rate-limiting amylase in wheat flour. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Amorphous sugar** | Sugar in a non-crystalline state (syrup or glass). Plasticized by water it is chewy; dry it is glassy and crisp. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Append-only IDs** | The rule that Stage 1 IDs and paths are never renamed; later stages add new prefixes (`R2-`, `X2-`, `stage-2/`). | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Ash** | The mineral residue after burning flour; a measure of refinement. The basis of French T-types (T55 ≈ 0.55 % ash). | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Autolyse** | A 20–60 minute rest of flour and water only, before adding salt and yeast, to hydrate the flour and develop gluten passively. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Bain-marie (water bath)** | Water surrounding a baking mould; it stays around 80–90 °C in a 150 °C oven and limits the edge temperature of a custard. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Bake log** | The per-bake entry in the lab notebook, with fixed fields (date, formula, actual weights, lots, temperatures, DDT, times, observations, photos, sensory, changes). | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Bake loss** | Weight lost in the oven as a percentage of dough weight; about 10–20 % for bread. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Baked custard** | A custard set undisturbed in the oven into a continuous protein gel; crème brûlée, crème caramel. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Baker's ammonia** | Ammonium bicarbonate; decomposes completely into ammonia, CO₂ and water; only for thin, dry products. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Baker's percentage** | Each ingredient's weight as a percentage of total flour weight; flour = 100 %. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Baking powder** | Soda plus one or more leavening acids plus starch; a self-contained gas system, conventionally yielding at least ~12 % of its weight as CO₂. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Baking soda** | Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃); releases CO₂ when it reacts with an acid; leaves alkaline sodium carbonate if heated without acid. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Baking strips** | Wet fabric strips around a pan that hold its wall near 100 °C, reducing doming and edge browning. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Base temperature method** | Water temperature = DDT × N − (sum of other temperatures), with N = 3 for straight doughs, 4 with a preferment. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Bassinage** | Adding part of the water late in mixing, after gluten has developed, to reach high hydration. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Bench rest** | 15–30 min rest after pre-shaping so the gluten relaxes enough to be shaped without tearing. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Bench tolerance** | How long a mixed batter can wait before baking without losing too much leavening gas. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Beta-amylase** | Enzyme that cuts maltose from the ends of starch chains. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Biga** | Stiff Italian preferment at 50–60 % hydration, fermented cool. Gives strength and a fuller, slightly tangy flavour. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Biscuit (separated sponge)** | A sponge made from separately whipped yolks and meringue, with flour; pipeable (ladyfingers). | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Blind baking** | Baking a shell empty, usually with parchment and weights, before or instead of baking it with a filling. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Bran** | The outer layers of the kernel (~13–15 %): fibre, minerals, pigments; absorbs water and cuts gluten. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Brioche à tête** | Individual brioche baked in a fluted mould, with a small ball (the head) set into a deep well in the body. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Brioche Nanterre** | Brioche loaf made of several rounded balls baked side by side in a loaf pan. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Broken (greasy) dough** | A high-fat dough in which butterfat has melted and separated as free oil. The dough is shiny, curdled and weak. Usually caused by a final dough above about 26 °C. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Brown sugar** | Sucrose with ~3–7 % molasses: brings moisture, invert sugar, minerals and slight acidity. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Bulk fermentation** | The first rise, with the dough still in one mass. It is where most of the flavour and dough strength develop. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Cake doneness temperature** | The centre temperature at which most cakes are set, about 93–99 °C, used with springback and skewer tests. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Caramelization** | Browning of sugars heated alone; sucrose from about 160 °C, fructose from about 110 °C. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Chiffon** | A cake made from an oil-and-yolk batter with baking powder, lightened with meringue, baked in an ungreased tube pan and cooled inverted. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Chlorinated flour** | Cake flour treated with chlorine gas, lowering its pH and modifying starch so it sets high-sugar batters; not permitted in the EU and UK. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Clean vs sanitise** | Cleaning removes soil and protein with detergent; sanitising reduces microorganisms on an already clean surface. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Coagulation** | Bonding of unfolded proteins into a network that traps water, turning liquid egg into a gel; irreversible. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Coefficient of variation (CV)** | Standard deviation ÷ mean × 100 %; a unit-free measure of repeatability. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Concept thread** | One of twelve lines of understanding (T1–T12) that run through all stages; every lesson is tagged with the threads it advances. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Conduction** | Heat transfer by direct contact; how a stone, steel or pot heats the base of a loaf, and how heat moves from crust to centre. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Continuous phase** | The liquid that surrounds the droplets in an emulsion and determines how it behaves (water in O/W, fat in W/O). | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Convection** | Heat transfer by a moving fluid (air or steam); increased by an oven fan. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Cream of tartar** | Potassium bitartrate, a dry acid used at about 1 % of white weight to give a finer, more stable egg-white foam. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Creaming** | Beating plastic fat (butter at 18–20 °C) with granulated sugar so that sugar crystals cut air cells into the fat-crystal network. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Creep (auto-zero tracking)** | A scale's tendency to ignore small, slow additions by treating them as drift. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Crémage** | Mixing soft butter with sugar, then egg, then flour; gives a smooth, cohesive dough that is tougher if overmixed. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Crème anglaise** | Stirred custard of milk and/or cream, yolks and sugar (standard ratio 100 : 20 : 20), cooked to 82–84 °C. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Crème Chantilly** | Cream (35–40 % fat) whipped with 8–10 % icing sugar and vanilla. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Crème d'amande** | Almond cream of equal weights butter, sugar, almond flour and eggs, baked in tarts. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Crème diplomate** | Pastry cream lightened with whipped cream, often set with a little gelatin. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Crème mousseline** | Pastry cream enriched with a large amount of beaten butter (treated in M13). | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Crème pâtissière** | Pastry cream: milk, yolks, sugar and starch (about 8–10 % cornstarch on milk), boiled 1–2 min, enriched with butter. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Cross-contact** | Transfer of an allergen into a food that should not contain it, via tools, surfaces, hands or dust. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Crust** | The dried outer layer of a loaf, formed once the surface loses its water and rises above 100 °C. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Curdled batter** | A broken fat-continuous emulsion in which egg water separates, usually from cold eggs, fast additions or butter that is too warm. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Curdling** | Over-coagulation of egg proteins into clumps that expel water: grain, then visible curds in thin liquid; above about 85 °C in anglaise. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Custard** | Egg proteins dispersed in dairy and sugar, thickened or set by heat-induced coagulation. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Damaged starch** | Starch granules broken in milling; absorbs much more water than intact starch and is readily attacked by amylase. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Deglazing** | Adding liquid to a hot caramel or pan to stop cooking and dissolve it into a sauce. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Denaturation** | Unfolding of a protein's folded shape by heat, acid, salt, air–water interfaces or mechanical stress. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Desired dough temperature (DDT)** | The target temperature of the dough at the end of mixing, set so that fermentation times are predictable. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Dextrose equivalent (DE)** | A measure of how far starch has been broken down in a glucose syrup (pure glucose = 100). Higher DE = sweeter, more hygroscopic, less viscous. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Disulfide bond** | A strong covalent sulfur–sulfur link between protein chains; the "welds" of the gluten network. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Docking** | Pricking the base of a shell so trapped steam and air escape and it does not dome. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Double egg wash** | Brushing egg wash once after shaping and again just before baking, for an even, deep, glossy crust. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Double-acting** | A baking powder with a fast acid (gas at mixing) and a slow acid (gas in the oven). | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Drainage** | Liquid flowing down between bubbles of a foam under gravity; seen as liquid collecting under a meringue or mousse. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Drier** | An ingredient that absorbs water: flour, starches, milk powder, cocoa powder. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Dry caramel** | Caramel made by melting sugar alone in the pan. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Ear** | The raised, crisp flap of crust along a shallow-angled score. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Egg-wash seal** | A thin coat of egg baked onto a shell to slow moisture moving from a filling into the pastry. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Elasticity** | A dough's tendency to spring back after being deformed. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Emulsifier** | A molecule or particle that sits at the oil–water interface, lowers interfacial tension and stops droplets merging (yolk lecithin, lipoproteins, proteins). | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Emulsion** | Droplets of one liquid dispersed in another it does not mix with; oil-in-water (O/W: mayonnaise, cream) or water-in-oil (W/O: butter). | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Endosperm** | The starchy inner part of the wheat kernel (~80–85 % of its weight); the source of white flour's starch and gluten-forming proteins. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Enriched dough** | A yeast dough containing significant sugar, fat, eggs and/or milk in addition to flour, water, salt and yeast. Examples range from soft rolls (5–10 % sugar and fat) to brioche (50–100 % butter). | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Extensibility** | How far a dough can be stretched before it tears. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Extraction rate** | Kilograms of flour obtained per 100 kg of wheat; ~70–75 % for white flour, ~100 % for whole wheat. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Factorial design** | An experiment that combines every level of two or more factors, so interactions between them can be measured. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Falling number** | A test of amylase activity in flour (seconds); low values mean sprouted wheat and a risk of gummy crumb. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **False rise** | Vigorous activity on day 2–3 of a new starter from non-sourdough bacteria that are then eliminated by rising acidity. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Fan offset** | The 15–20 °C reduction applied when baking a conventional-oven recipe in a fan (convection) oven. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Feeding ratio** | Starter : flour : water by weight at a feed, e.g. 1:2:2. Lower inoculation (1:5:5) peaks later and starts less acidic. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Fermentation** | Yeast breaking down sugars without oxygen into CO₂, ethanol and flavour compounds. In a bakery it also means the whole period the dough spends rising. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **FIFO** | First in, first out: the oldest stock is used first. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Final dough** | The ingredients added on the day, with the ripe preferment as one of them. Equals the total formula minus the preferment. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Final proof** | The last rise, after shaping and before baking. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Flaky dough** | Pie dough with butter left as flattened pieces that become steam-separated layers. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Flying crust** | A top crust that separates from the crumb, leaving a void underneath. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Foam** | Gas dispersed as bubbles in a liquid or solid, separated by thin liquid films (lamellae). | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Fold (stretch and fold)** | Stretching the dough and folding it over itself during bulk. It builds strength, evens out temperature and redistributes gas. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Fold-and-pat** | Patting a scone or biscuit dough flat and folding it on itself 2–3 times to flatten fat pieces into layers. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Folding** | Combining a foam with a heavier mixture using cutting and lifting strokes, after lightening the base with a sacrificed third of the foam. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Fonçage** | Lining a ring or pan with rolled dough, easing it into the corner without stretching. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Formula balance** | The traditional rules for checking a cake formula in baker's %: for regular cakes sugar ≤ flour, eggs ≥ fat, liquids (incl. eggs) ≈ sugar. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Four dimensions** | The course's training axes: practical skill, baking science, experimental thinking, professional workflow. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Fraisage** | Smearing dough once across the bench with the heel of the hand to homogenize it without developing gluten. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Frangipane** | Crème d'amande combined with crème pâtissière, usually 2 : 1. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Fresh (compressed) yeast** | Moist block of live yeast cells, about 70 % water. Perishable; keep it refrigerated and use it within 2–3 weeks. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Friction factor** | The temperature rise caused by mixing, determined empirically for a given mixer, speed, time and batch. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Génoise** | A cake made from a warm whipped whole-egg foam with flour and a little melted butter; no chemical leavener. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Germ** | The embryo (~2–3 %): oil, vitamins, enzymes and glutathione; makes whole-grain flour perishable. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Gliadin** | Smaller gluten proteins; give dough extensibility and viscosity. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Glucose syrup** | A syrup of glucose, maltose and longer chains made by partly breaking down starch; used to prevent crystallization and add body. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Glutathione** | A small reducing molecule (from wheat germ and dead yeast cells) that breaks disulfide bonds and weakens dough. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Gluten** | The viscoelastic protein network formed from glutenin and gliadin when flour is hydrated and worked. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Glutenin** | Large polymeric gluten proteins linked by disulfide bonds; give dough elasticity and strength. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Hard / soft wheat** | Kernel hardness: hard wheat is usually higher in protein and mills into granular flour with more damaged starch; soft wheat into fine, low-protein flour. | [M02](stage-1/module-02/README.md) |
| **Heterofermentative** | LAB that produce lactic acid plus CO₂ and ethanol or acetic acid. Homofermentative LAB produce mainly lactic acid. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **High-ratio cake** | A cake with sugar above flour weight (typically 110–140 %) and liquids above sugar; requires chlorinated or heat-treated flour and emulsified shortening. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Hooch** | Alcohol-rich liquid on a hungry starter. A sign of starvation, not spoilage. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Hot spot** | A zone of the oven that runs hotter than the average, typically near elements, walls or the rear fan. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Hydration** | Water as a percentage of flour weight. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Hygroscopicity** | How strongly a substance attracts and holds water. For sugars: fructose > invert/honey > glucose > sucrose > lactose. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Ice point** | 0 °C in an ice–water slurry; the most reliable home thermometer reference. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Ingredient cost per unit** | Sum of (grams × price per gram) for all ingredients, divided by the number of saleable pieces. | [M01](stage-1/module-01/README.md) |
| **Instant yeast** | Dried yeast in fine granules that you can mix straight into flour. About ⅓–0.4 of the fresh-yeast weight gives the same activity. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Interaction** | When the effect of one factor depends on the level of another (e.g. resting reduces spread more for melted-butter dough). | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Interfering agent** | An ingredient (glucose syrup, invert, acid, fat) that hinders sucrose from crystallizing. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Internal temperature** | Centre temperature at the end of the bake: 96–99 °C for lean bread, 88–93 °C for enriched bread. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Invert sugar** | Sucrose split into equal parts glucose and fructose, by acid, heat or the enzyme invertase. Sweeter, more hygroscopic and faster-browning than sucrose. | [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Lab notebook** | The bench record of every bake and experiment: formula, actual weights, temperatures, times, observations, decisions. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Lactic acid bacteria (LAB)** | Acid-tolerant bacteria that ferment sugars mainly to lactic acid; the majority population in a sourdough. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
| **Latent heat of vaporization** | Energy released when steam condenses (about 2,260 kJ/kg for water); the reason steam heats the dough surface so effectively. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Lean-to-rich spectrum** | The classification of yeast doughs by their sugar, fat and egg percentages. Moving towards rich means slower fermentation, longer mixing, softer crumb, faster browning and lower oven temperatures. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Leavening acid** | An acid or acid salt chosen to react with soda at a specific time: fast (monocalcium phosphate, cream of tartar) or slow and heat-activated (SAPP, sodium aluminium sulfate). | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Lecithin** | Common name for phosphatidylcholine and related phospholipids in egg yolk; molecules with a water-loving head and fat-loving tails that emulsify oil in water. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Line-spread test** | Viscosity test: a fixed amount of sample is released at the centre of concentric circles and its spread measured after a set time. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Maillard reaction** | Browning reaction between reducing sugars and amino groups, rapid on dry surfaces above about 140 °C; gives crust colour and roasted flavour. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) [M07](stage-1/module-07/README.md) |
| **Mealy dough** | Pie dough with butter rubbed in fine; tender, few layers, resists soaking; used under wet fillings. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Mise en place** | Having every ingredient weighed and labelled, every tool out and every pan prepared before the process starts. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Moistener** | An ingredient that adds water or holds it: water, milk, eggs, liquid sugars; sugar holds water without adding it. | [M10](stage-1/module-10/README.md) |
| **Moisture migration** | Movement of water from the moist crumb to the dry crust, softening the crust during storage. | [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Muffin method** | Dry and wet ingredients (with liquid fat) mixed separately, then combined in a few folds to a lumpy batter. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Nappe** | The stage at which a custard coats the back of a spoon and a finger line drawn through it stays clean; about 82–84 °C for anglaise. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Neutralizing value (NV)** | Grams of soda neutralized by 100 g of a leavening acid. | [M08](stage-1/module-08/README.md) |
| **Osmotic stress** | Water pulled out of yeast cells by high concentrations of dissolved sugar or salt around them. It slows or stops fermentation. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) |
| **Osmotolerant yeast** | A strain of instant yeast, usually sold in a gold-labelled package, selected to keep fermenting in doughs with more than about 10 % sugar. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Ovalbumin** | The main egg-white protein (about half the white's protein); sets fully around 80 °C, giving a firm white. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Oven map** | Your measured record of real oven temperature versus dial, swing, time to stable temperature and hot-spot locations. | [M00](stage-1/module-00/README.md) |
| **Oven spring** | The rapid rise in the first 10–15 min of baking, from gas expansion, steam and a last burst of yeast activity. | [M03](stage-1/module-03/README.md) [M04](stage-1/module-04/README.md) |
| **Overrun** | Increase in volume from incorporated air, as a percentage of the unwhipped volume. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Ovotransferrin (conalbumin)** | Egg-white protein that begins to set around 62 °C, giving the first milky, tender gel; binds copper, which stabilizes foams whipped in copper bowls. | [M09](stage-1/module-09/README.md) |
| **Par-bake / full bake** | Par-baked: set and dry but pale, for shells baked again with a filling. Fully baked: evenly golden, for unbaked fillings. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Pareve** | In Jewish dietary law, food containing neither meat nor dairy. The reason challah is traditionally made with oil rather than butter. | [M06](stage-1/module-06/README.md) |
| **Partial coalescence** | Sticking together of partly crystalline fat globules without full merging; forms the fat network that holds whipped cream. | [M12](stage-1/module-12/README.md) |
| **Pâte brisée** | Lean French short dough: flour 100, butter 50–60, water 20–25, salt ~2, optional yolk; for quiche and savoury or rustic tarts. | [M11](stage-1/module-11/README.md) |
| **Pâte fermentée** | "Old dough": a piece of finished lean dough, including salt, added to a new batch. | [M05](stage-1/module-05/README.md) |
